## Changelog

`node_state` reads a stored projection kept current on write: migration 060's `ob1_ticket_head` (each ticket's head) and `ob1_superseded_by` (each thought's newest successor), fed by statement triggers on `thoughts`, so `search_thoughts`' `prefer_current` costs a lookup, not the brain — signatures and rows unchanged, the capture group gains the writes on both tables, and a write that moves a ticket's key, status or pointer is refused under REPEATABLE READ (SMD-2256).

## FORK

node_state reads two tables kept current on write, so prefer_current costs its window, not the corpus (SMD-2256)

**What changed.** `db/migrations/060_node_state_projection.sql`:
`ob1_ticket_head` (keyed by `md5(issue)::uuid`, the issue beside it: the head
row, its status, status_type and watermark) and `ob1_superseded_by` (each
superseded thought's newest successor), no foreign keys. The rules once each —
`ob1_ticket_heads_of(text[])`, `ob1_superseders_of(uuid[])`, NULL for every
key, a one-time-filtered branch for NULL beside an `unnest()`-keyed one — and
`ob1_node_projection_reconcile` (superseders, then heads, since the head rule
reads "nothing supersedes it" from the table). Three statement triggers on
`thoughts` (AFTER INSERT / UPDATE / DELETE, transition tables) and a fourth on
TRUNCATE: a statement touching no row with an issue key or a pointer returns at
once; otherwise the keys it moved — both sides of a key's move, a pointer's
targets and their issues, a successor's `created_at` — are locked and
reconciled. The locks: a shared global, then buckets of the keys' hashes, 256
per class — the superseded thoughts and the rows whose head fields moved, then
the issues, the targets' issues read once the pointer buckets are held — so a
transaction holds at most 513. REPEATABLE READ is refused for such a statement;
SERIALIZABLE is left to SSI. `ob1_node_projection_drift()` (058's formulas
verbatim) and `ob1_rebuild_node_projection()` (the seed, and the repair after a
write with triggers disabled). `node_lifecycle()` and `node_state()` keep their
signatures and rows and read the tables; `node_state` has no top-level WITH, so
a caller's planner pulls it up and drops the dependency joins it does not read.
059's wrapper is re-created in plpgsql (its plan cached), body unchanged, and
`search_thoughts_hybrid` is estimated at 100 rows. Grants: the capture group
gains SELECT, INSERT, UPDATE, DELETE on both tables; preflight says, by
privilege, what a role without them breaks. The tool's description and grant
hint, both READMEs, graph-centrality's and config's comments, the ADR's
`node_state` row and step 3, and the bench's arm follow.

**Why.** At 059 `prefer_current` cost `node_state`'s whole-brain read per
call — +10.7 ms at 10,000 thoughts, +129 ms at 100,000 — over the budget it
was pre-registered against. A narrowing that kept the reads met the budget and
was declined for a stored projection, SMD-1997's direction (the ticket holds
its numbers). Fed by the row store, not the log: every writer reaches
`thoughts`, raw ones included, and the log carries no `created_at` move; the
fold can later feed the heads' status, not the superseders.

**Held.** test-schema 1862 — new [56]: the catalog; two seeded sequences of
150 raw statements each (thirteen kinds of write, each counted only when it
wrote a row — multi-row and whole-table, rollbacks, successors competing for

to 058's own text after each; the plan; the grant; the replays; the rebuild;
REPEATABLE READ refused and SERIALIZABLE run; six hundred keys in one

same-ticket writers serialise and the later recomputes from the earlier's

**Measured after.** `db/bench-hybrid.ts`'s arm, the order alternating round
by round, the pre-registered estimator (difference of medians) beside the
paired one: `prefer_current` adds +0.84 to +1.06 ms at 10,000 thoughts, inside
the budget (the hybrid's own median, 1.09 to 1.19) — as sql the wrapper was
+1.11 against 1.10 — with +0.6 of it the hybrid at the wider window; about +2.9
ms at 100,000, +2.1 of it the window. A whole-brain read of every lifecycle
still reads every row (7.5 ms at 10,000, 93 ms at 100,000; 058's about 125): a
narrow read is what got cheaper. A writer, triggers on against off: a plain
capture +0.03 to +0.06 ms (budget 0.1), a ticket's status update +0.31 to +0.49
ms (budget 0.5), stamping 40% of the rows +1% to +7% (budget +20%). The dogfood brain (a read-only dump in a throwaway
Postgres, 1,001 thoughts, migrated to main's 059, then 060 applied at commit
057a7ee7: 449 heads, 75 superseders, drift 0): graph-centrality in 300 modes,
`node_state()` and `node_lifecycle()` byte for byte the same, the dependency
read 34 → 3–4 ms. Mutants, each harness first run clean on the unmutated tree:
nineteen, eighteen killed — [56] kills every rule, trigger, isolation and
bucket mutant (a trigger dropped, TRUNCATE's included; the old key or pointer
skipped; the target lookup; the created_at and watermark moves; the pointer
target; the head rule's superseded term; the keyed delete; heads before
superseders; the REPEATABLE READ refusal; the buckets), test-live [28] the
four concurrency ones (no row locks, no issue locks, no superseder locks, NULL

**Not taken.** The narrowing (an ids overload of `node_lifecycle`). A
thought_audit fold (the log misses `created_at` moves and trigger-less loads).
One row per thought (a status sync would rewrite every note under the ticket).
SECURITY DEFINER triggers (the repo's policy). One global lock for every ticket
writer (considered at pass 1; the maintainer kept per-key buckets).
