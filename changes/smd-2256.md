## Changelog

`node_state` reads a stored projection kept current on write: migration 066's `ob1_ticket_head` (each ticket's head) and `ob1_superseded_by` (each thought's newest successor), fed by statement triggers on `thoughts`, so `search_thoughts`' `prefer_current` costs a lookup, not the brain — signatures and rows unchanged, the capture group gains the writes on both tables, a write that moves a ticket's key, status or watermark, a pointer or a successor's created_at is refused under REPEATABLE READ, and preflight fails a connection whose default is REPEATABLE READ; roles granted before 066 need `migrate.ts --grant` again (SMD-2256).

## FORK

node_state reads two tables kept current on write, so prefer_current costs its window, not the corpus (SMD-2256)

**What changed.** `db/migrations/066_node_state_projection.sql`:
`ob1_ticket_head` (keyed by `md5(issue)::uuid`, the issue beside it: the head
row, its status, status_type and watermark) and `ob1_superseded_by` (each
superseded thought's newest successor), no foreign keys. The rules once each —
`ob1_ticket_heads_of(text[])`, `ob1_superseders_of(uuid[])`, NULL for every
key, a one-time-filtered branch for NULL beside one whose keys are an index
condition (`= ANY`), the head rule's "nothing supersedes it" a probe per
candidate — and `ob1_node_projection_reconcile` (superseders, then heads;
internal: it takes no lock). Three statement triggers on `thoughts` (AFTER
INSERT / UPDATE / DELETE, transition tables; an UPDATE's old and new images
paired whole, the filter on the pair) and a fourth on TRUNCATE: a statement
touching no row with an issue key or a pointer returns at once; otherwise the
keys it moved — both sides of a key's move, a pointer's targets and their
issues, a successor's `created_at` — are locked and reconciled. The locks: a
shared global, then buckets of the keys' hashes, 256 per class — the
superseded thoughts, the rows whose head fields moved and a DELETE's issue rows
and superseded rows (what its cascade can need), then the issues on their md5,
the targets' issues read once the pointer buckets are held — so a transaction
holds at most 513. The trigger and the reconcile plan every statement that
takes the keys afresh. REPEATABLE READ is refused for such a statement;
SERIALIZABLE is left to SSI. `ob1_node_projection_drift()` (058's formulas
verbatim) and `ob1_rebuild_node_projection()` (the seed, and the repair after a
write with triggers disabled; it locks `thoughts` first, refuses outside READ
COMMITTED, and `db/migrate.ts` now runs every file under READ COMMITTED).
`node_lifecycle()` and `node_state()` keep their signatures and rows and read
the tables; `node_state` has no top-level WITH, so a caller's planner pulls it
up and drops the dependency joins it does not read. 059's wrapper is
re-created in plpgsql (its plan cached), body unchanged, and
`search_thoughts_hybrid` is estimated at 100 rows. Grants: the capture group
gains SELECT, INSERT, UPDATE, DELETE on both tables (re-run `migrate.ts
--grant`); preflight says, by privilege, what a role without them breaks, and
fails a connection whose default isolation is REPEATABLE READ, naming where it
was set. The tool's description and grant hint, both READMEs,
graph-centrality's and config's comments, the ADR's `node_state` row and step
3, and the bench's arm follow.

**Why.** At 059 `prefer_current` cost `node_state`'s whole-brain read per
call — +10.7 ms at 10,000 thoughts, +129 ms at 100,000 — over the budget it
was pre-registered against. A narrowing that kept the reads met the budget and
was declined for a stored projection, SMD-1997's direction (the ticket holds
its numbers). Fed by the row store, not the log: every writer reaches
`thoughts`, raw ones included, and the log carries no `created_at` move; the
fold can later feed the heads' status, not the superseders.

**Held.** test-schema 2213 — new [60]: the catalog; two seeded sequences of 220
raw statements each (fourteen kinds of write, each counted only when it wrote a
row — multi-row and whole-table, a writable CTE deleting a thought and updating

**Measured after.** `db/bench-hybrid.ts`'s arm on pass 3's code (one run; the
order alternating round by round, the pre-registered estimator — the difference
of medians — beside the paired one): `prefer_current` adds +0.50 ms at 10,000
thoughts with no needle and +0.66 with one, inside the budget (the hybrid's own
median, 0.88 and 1.14), and +1.11 and +1.34 at 100,000. With no needle, the
hybrid asked for its window of 40 costs +0.44 at 10,000 and +0.99 at 100,000 on
its own — about all of the no-needle addition; the two differences do not
subtract. (As sql, one earlier run: +1.11 against 1.10.) A whole-brain read of
every lifecycle still reads every row (6.3 ms at 10,000, 78 at 100,000): a
narrow read is what got cheaper. A writer, against the triggers dropped
(disabling them still fills their transition tables — pass 2): a plain capture
+0.03 ms at both sizes (budget 0.1); a ticket's status update +0.24 ms at 10,000
and +0.45 at 100,000 (budget 0.5; pass 2's one run had +0.61 at 100,000, and the
per-row probe since saves about 0.15 ms at 5,000 superseded thoughts, within the
spread); stamping 40% of the rows +2% at both (budget +20%). Pass 4 changed only
the trigger's UPDATE and DELETE branches; its full run was on a machine at load
7–9 and is not quoted. Timed instead against pass 3's trigger on one database,
blocks alternating, a status update moved −0.08 ms at 10,000 and −0.01 to +0.23
at 100,000 over three runs: within the spread. The dogfood brain (a read-only
dump in a throwaway Postgres, 1,001 thoughts, migrated to 059, then this file
(as 060) at commit 057a7ee7: 449 heads, 75 superseders, drift 0):
graph-centrality in 300 modes, `node_state()` and `node_lifecycle()` byte for
byte the same, the dependency read 34 → 3–4 ms. Mutants, each harness first run
clean on the unmutated tree: twenty-four, twenty-two killed — [60] kills every
rule, trigger, isolation and bucket mutant (a trigger dropped, TRUNCATE's
included; the old key or pointer skipped; the target lookup; the created_at and
watermark moves; the pointer target; the head rule's superseded term; the keyed
delete; heads before superseders; the REPEATABLE READ refusal, the trigger's and
the rebuild's; the buckets; an UPDATE filtered per side; a DELETE locking every
row), test-live [33] the concurrency ones (no row, issue or superseder locks;
NULL for "no keys"; a DELETE skipping its superseded rows). Two survive: the md5
recheck, reachable only through a collision; and the head rule's plain join in
place of the per-row probe, which the planner probes either way (the review row,
pass 3).

**Not taken.** The narrowing (an ids overload of `node_lifecycle`). A
thought_audit fold (the log misses `created_at` moves and trigger-less loads).
One row per thought (a status sync would rewrite every note under the ticket).
SECURITY DEFINER triggers (the repo's policy). One global lock for every ticket
writer (the maintainer kept per-key buckets, pass 1). **Follow-ups.** SMD-2267:
the dependency columns as a stored fold.
