`server-portable/test-preflight.ts` matches every age and next try it reads back from a heartbeat as a range that holds a late read, so a busy runner no longer fails a case though preflight is right (SMD-3569).

## FORK

test-preflight's heartbeat ages held to a range — a late read is not a failure (SMD-3569)

**What changed.**
- **Every seconds age** preflight reads back is matched as a range: `1\d s ago` for a row planted 10 s back, `2\d`, `3\d`, `4\d` and `6\d` likewise, 30 forms on 30 assertions. This is the convention SMD-2679 PR 4b-2 began with `3\d s ago`. An age only grows when the read lands late.
- **The next try** "in 80 s" is `(?:7\d|80) s`. A time still to come only shrinks.
- **A minute form** stays exact. `ago()` (brain-info.ts) rounds minutes over 30 s, so a form planted on a whole minute holds for 29 s of lateness. One sat on the edge:
  - the held scheduler's next try, `in_s` 240 from a stamp 30 s old, is 210 s, or 3.5 min, and a read 3 s late said "3 min";
  - `in_s` is now 270, so the next try is 240 s, 4 min whole.
- **A comment beside the suite's regex helper** says why, and how to write the next one.

**Why.** `ago()` rounds to the second, so a read half a second late turns a planted 20 into 21. Under the load of SMD-3468's three background legs, the rest shard failed on one of these in 4 of 7 Linux rehearsals ("21 s ago", "ended 60 s ago", "11 s ago", "61 s ago"), and the first shard once ("stamped 20 s ago"). Earlier, SMD-3485's and SMD-3486's runs saw "79 s", "31 s ago" and "11 s ago", and CI failed SMD-2679 PR 4b-1 once on "30 s ago".

**Held.**
- **No exact seconds pin is left:** a scan of the file finds no `<digits> s ago` or `in <digits> s` outside a range. The one left, "within 5 s", is a configured limit, not a reading.
- **A read 3 s late,** simulated by planting every time 3 s further back (the heartbeats, the sleep marks, the literal intervals):
  - main's file failed 30 cases;
  - this file failed 1 (the held scheduler's 3.5 min) before its next try moved, and none after: 994 passed.
- **Teeth:**
  - every seconds age printed a minute off (`ago()` + 60) fails 35 cases;
  - a read 10 s late fails 33, since each range holds 9 s of lateness at most, as `3\d` did.

**Verify.**
- [x] No exact pin left: the scan above.
- [x] Under load: test-preflight passed 20 runs in a row on this laptop, two at a time, each beside another. Three Linux rehearsals of the data layer's three legs (`oven/bun:1.4.3`, 4 CPUs) passed rest's 553 and test-upgrade's 735, first failing only the 11 rows any container fails. Those three ran quicker than the loaded ones that had failed (rest 86–94 s against 138–195 s), so the 3 s late read above is the stronger evidence.
- [x] Teeth: a mutant printing an age a minute off still fails the cases it touches (35).
