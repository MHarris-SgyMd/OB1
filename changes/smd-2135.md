## FORK

test-live.ts stops waiting on clocks it does not test — CI's live suite spends its time on what it asserts (SMD-2135)

**What changed.** Waits in `db/test-live.ts` that are not what their section tests. There are two cuts, #169 and this pull request.
- **The deadlock timer (#169).** `racer()`, defined just above [6d], opens every racing session of [6d], [6f], [6g] and [6i] with `deadlock_timeout = 50ms` as a startup parameter.
  - Postgres checks a lock wait for a cycle only once it has lasted that long, 1 s by default, so each deadlock a section provoked on purpose cost up to a second. The arms that must not deadlock use the same sessions, so the arms differ only in the code under test.
  - The setting moves when a wait is checked, and so which side of a cycle is broken. Where the other side has already waited past 50 ms, as behind [6i]'s 400 ms probes, the session that closes the cycle is broken — in [6i] arm 3 `update_thought`, where it was the delete.
  - Every deadlocking arm accepts either side. Every call that could close a cycle is caught, so a regression fails its assertion rather than ending the suite; [6i] arm 2's `update_thought` is wrapped for that.
  - Waits probed before any cycle closes are untouched: [6i]'s 400 ms probes and arm 1's `d.ms >= 400`. Every other bound on a wait is 8 s or more.
  - The setting is superuser-only by default. It is sent only when the role is one (CI's service and `with-postgres.sh` connect as `postgres`); any other role races at the default, only slower.
- **The lease clock (#169).** [8]'s (c) and (e) waited out real leases: 2.2, 4.5, 1.0 and 5.3 s.
  - `claim_thoughts` reaps a lease whose `ttl_expires_at < now()` (015), and `renew_claims` moves it to `GREATEST(ttl_expires_at, now() + the lease)` (031). Neither compares against the time any other way.
  - So `elapse(key, s)`, which moves one job key's claimed deadlines back `s` seconds, is the same to both as waiting `s` seconds. Each sleep is an `elapse` of the same length, and the leases, steps and assertions are unchanged.
- **[9]'s slowed stub (this pull request).** The stub provider's `slowMs` (600 ms an embedding) served two runs, about 41 of the section's 54 s on CI by the stub's arithmetic: 42 rows at 610 ms for the thief, 26 in the heartbeat run's longer lane. The provider probe is never slowed now.
  - **The thief run.** One worker, with a batch of 4, re-embeds 42 thoughts while the test steals the batch it holds. `slowMs` is cleared as soon as the theft lands, and the rows after it run at full speed. The row in hand's release is refused because `release_thought` matches the holder; the 600 ms on the first batch is what makes the theft land inside it, a 100 ms poll against a 610 ms row. The other stolen rows, fast now, are all released before the worker's first 1 s beat, so every one learns it at release, and no run reaches `reembed.ts`'s lost-at-beat skip, which nothing asserted before either (SMD-2190).
  - **The heartbeat run.** Three workers with batches of 15, in place of two with 16. They claim 15, 15 and 12 in one round, where two rounds took some sixteen seconds. The two 15-row batches still outlast the 6 s lease, by about 3 s. The worker with twelve finishes at about 7.4 s (7.3 s by the stub's arithmetic), and its next claim reaps any lease past its deadline. So a beat that renewed nothing now hands it the others' last rows every time, where the two-worker shape caught that only when a claim happened to race a lapse. Fifteen is the one batch with margin both ways for 42 thoughts and three workers: the short batch must run past 6 s and finish before the long ones, and 14 (14, 14, 14) finishes all three together while 16 (16, 16, 10) ends the short one at 6.1 s.

**Not taken.**
- Leases of one second where they are two to five, as the ticket proposed. That keeps 3 of the 13 s and brings back the cliff (c)'s comment chose two seconds to avoid; the clock costs nothing.
- An assertion that `racer()` took effect. Losing it costs time, not correctness, and CI's step time shows it, as with SMD-2097's loads.
- Driving [9]'s passes in-process, as the ticket proposed. `reembed.ts` and `preflight.ts` export nothing: they parse `argv` at module scope and exit through `process.exit`. Every spawn asserts its exit code or printed text, and at about 0.12 s a start, by CI's arithmetic (about 0.07 s locally), all 86 cost about 10 s, of which the thirteen preflights and the killed run would stay subprocesses.
- A shorter tarpit timeout. It fires once, and its 2 s lands on one of two workers while the other goes on, so it costs well under 2 s; the slowed runs share the environment it is set in.
