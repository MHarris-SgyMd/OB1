## FORK

test-live.ts stops sleeping out deadlock_timeout and lease deadlines — about 30 s of CI's live suite (SMD-2135)

**What changed.** Two waits in `db/test-live.ts`, neither the thing its section tests:
- **The deadlock timer.** A helper, `racer()`, opens each racing session of [6d], [6f] and [6g], both arms, with `deadlock_timeout = 50ms` as a startup parameter. Postgres checks a lock wait for a cycle only once it has lasted that long, 1 s by default, so every deadlock a first arm provokes cost a second. The setting is a superuser's; the helper sends it only when the role is one (CI's service and `with-postgres.sh` connect as `postgres`), and any other role races at the default, only slower.
- **The lease clock.** [8]'s (c) and (e) waited out real leases: 2.2 s, then 4.5, 1.0 and 5.3 s, 13 s in all. `claim_thoughts` reaps a lease whose `ttl_expires_at < now()` (015) and `renew_claims` moves it to `GREATEST(ttl_expires_at, now() + the lease)` (031), and neither reads the time any other way. So `elapse(key, s)` moves the key's claimed deadlines back `s` seconds, which is the same to both functions as waiting `s` seconds, and each sleep is now an `elapse` of the same length. The leases (2 s and 5 s), the order of every step and every assertion are unchanged; (e)'s message no longer prints wall-clock milliseconds, which now measure nothing.

The suite stays at 703 assertions; `db/README.md` is untouched.

**Why.** Main's run on a6bfe4e3 (36131497058) spent 188 s in "Run the live suite". Timed by section from the job log, [9] took 53.9 s, [5d] 27.5, [5f] 20.2, [8] 19.9 and [6g] 16.9. [6g]'s first arm deadlocked 15 times in 40 on that run, 15 s of the section at one second each; locally it deadlocks 2 or 3 times in 40, so the cost shows only on CI. [8]'s sleeps were 13 of its 19.9 s.

The ticket's other levers are separate pull requests (see Follow-ups): [9]'s time is two runs against a stub slowed to 600 ms (about 41 of its 54 s), not its 86 subprocesses, and [5d] and [5f] can share one corpus.

**Held.** Each mutant below was run before this change (a6bfe4e3) and after it, stopping at the section's end, and fails the same assertions both times:

| Mutant | Fails, before and after |
| --- | --- |
| `delete_thought` without its supersession lock | [6g] arm 2's three (14 and 13 deadlocks in 40) |
| [8]'s time never passes (sleeps, then `elapse`, at zero) | six: (c)'s four after expiry, (e)'s inherited three and its final count |
| `claim_thoughts` never reaps an expired lease | the same six |
| `renew_claims` does not move the deadline | (e)'s four: the claim after the original deadline takes the beating worker's rows |
| `claim_thoughts` stamps twice the lease asked for | (c)'s four after expiry |

So [6g]'s no-lock arm still deadlocks at 50 ms, and [8]'s expiry assertions still fail when a lease does not expire. Ten runs of the changed suite, two at a time from two checkouts, all passed 703/703.

**Measured after.** On an 18-core Mac through `with-postgres.sh`, one run each: the suite 130.6 → 113.5 s, [8] 15.2 → 2.5 s, [6g] 2.5 → 0.6 s, [6d] and [6f] 1.1 → 0.2 s each.

**Not taken.**
- Leases of one second where they are two to five, as the ticket proposed: it keeps 3 of the 13 s and brings back the cliff (c)'s comment chose two seconds to avoid. The clock costs nothing and keeps the numbers.
- `SET deadlock_timeout` in each transaction: the helper sets it once per session, for every transaction the session runs.
- [6i] (3.1 s): its time is its 400 ms "still waiting" probes, not a deadlock, and it is the same locally and on CI.

**Follow-ups.** SMD-2135 stays open for its next cuts, each its own pull request: [9]'s two slowed runs (the thief's stub back to full speed once the theft lands, the heartbeat run in one round), then one corpus for [5d] and [5f], then the two-database split only if those leave the step over 120 s. SMD-2155: [6g]'s first arm deadlocked 29 times in 480 tries over twelve local runs (6%, where CI's 15 in 40 fits the comment's "roughly half"). At that rate a run sees no deadlock, and fails, about one time in twelve. This predates the change: the baseline saw 2 in 40.
