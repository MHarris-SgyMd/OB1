## FORK

test-live.ts stops sleeping out deadlock_timeout and lease deadlines — about 30 s of CI's live suite (SMD-2135)

**What changed.** Two waits in `db/test-live.ts`, neither the thing its section tests:
- **The deadlock timer.** A helper, `racer()`, opens each racing session of [6d], [6f] and [6g] with `deadlock_timeout = 50ms` as a startup parameter: the first arms, which deadlock on purpose, and the second arms, which show the shipped code does not, so the two arms still differ only in the code under test. Postgres checks a lock wait for a cycle only once it has lasted that long, 1 s by default, so every deadlock a first arm provokes cost a second. The setting moves when a wait is checked, and so which side of a cycle is the victim, not which waits are cycles; no assertion names a victim or reads a duration, and every bound on a wait is 8 s or more. It is superuser-only by default; the helper sends it only when the role is one (CI's service and `with-postgres.sh` connect as `postgres`), and any other role races at the default, only slower.
- **The lease clock.** [8]'s (c) and (e) waited out real leases: 2.2 s, then 4.5, 1.0 and 5.3 s, 13 s in all. `claim_thoughts` reaps a lease whose `ttl_expires_at < now()` (015) and `renew_claims` moves it to `GREATEST(ttl_expires_at, now() + the lease)` (031), and neither compares against the time any other way; the stamps they write from it (`claimed_at`, `finished_at`) are read by nothing in [8]. So `elapse(key, s)` moves the key's claimed deadlines back `s` seconds, which is the same to both functions as waiting `s` seconds, and each sleep is now an `elapse` of the same length. The leases (2 s and 5 s), the order of every step and every assertion are unchanged; (e)'s message no longer prints wall-clock milliseconds, which now measure nothing.

The suite stays at 703 assertions; `db/README.md` is untouched.

**Why.** Main's run on a6bfe4e3 (36131497058) spent 188 s in "Run the live suite". Timed by section from the job log, [9] took 53.9 s, [5d] 27.5, [5f] 20.2, [8] 19.9 and [6g] 16.9. [6g]'s first arm deadlocked 15 times in 40 on that run, 15 s of the section at one second each; locally it deadlocks 1 to 5 times in 40, so the cost shows only on CI. [8]'s sleeps were 13 of its 19.9 s. On that log the four sections' waits come to about 30 s: 15 for [6g], about 1 each for [6d] and [6f], and 13 for [8]. That figure is arithmetic on one run; the pull request's own run is the measurement.

The ticket's other levers are separate pull requests (see Follow-ups): [9]'s time is two runs against a stub slowed to 600 ms (about 41 of its 54 s), not its 86 subprocesses, and [5d] and [5f] can share one corpus.

**Held.** Each mutant below was run before this change (a6bfe4e3) and after it, stopping at the section's end, and fails the same assertions both times:

| Mutant | Fails, before and after |
| --- | --- |
| `delete_thought` without its supersession lock | [6g] arm 2's three (14 and 13 deadlocks in 40) |
| [8]'s time never passes (sleeps, then `elapse`, at zero) | six: (c)'s four after expiry, (e)'s inherited three and its final count |
| `claim_thoughts` never reaps an expired lease | the same six |
| `renew_claims` does not move the deadline | (e)'s four: the claim after the original deadline takes the beating worker's rows |
| `claim_thoughts` stamps twice the lease asked for | (c)'s four after expiry |

Review pass 1 ran four more on both trees:
- `renew_claims` renewing to `now()` plus 1 s: (e)'s four fail, before and after.
- `LEAST` in place of `GREATEST`: the same four fail.
- A reaper that waits until 3 s past the deadline: the same six expiry assertions fail.
- `GREATEST` dropped: passes on both trees. [8] never tested the never-backward rule; test-schema.ts [30] holds it.

So [6g]'s no-lock arm still deadlocks at 50 ms, and [8]'s expiry assertions still fail when a lease does not expire. Ten runs of the changed suite, two at a time from two checkouts, all passed 703/703.

**Measured after.** On an 18-core Mac through `with-postgres.sh`, one run each: the suite 130.6 → 113.5 s, [8] 15.2 → 2.5 s, [6g] 2.5 → 0.6 s, [6d] and [6f] 1.1 → 0.2 s each.

**Not taken.**
- Leases of one second where they are two to five, as the ticket proposed: it keeps 3 of the 13 s and brings back the cliff (c)'s comment chose two seconds to avoid. The clock costs nothing and keeps the numbers.
- Long leases on the lease clock (60 s, `elapse` by 60.2): they would remove the cliff (c)'s "before it expires" claim stands on, landing inside a 2 s lease, and shorten nothing further. That cliff predates this change, and the numbers stay as the sections chose them.
- `SET deadlock_timeout` in each transaction: the helper sets it once per session, for every transaction the session runs.
- [6i] (3.1 s): its time is its 400 ms "still waiting" probes, not a deadlock, and it is the same locally and on CI.
