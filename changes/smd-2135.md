## FORK

test-live.ts stops waiting out deadlock_timeout and sleeping out lease deadlines — 35 s of CI's live suite (SMD-2135)

**What changed.** Two waits in `db/test-live.ts`, neither the thing its section tests:
- **The deadlock timer.** A helper, `racer()`, defined just above [6d], opens every racing session of [6d], [6f], [6g] and [6i] with `deadlock_timeout = 50ms` as a startup parameter. Each section has an arm that provokes a deadlock on purpose, and Postgres checks a lock wait for a cycle only once it has lasted that long, 1 s by default, so every provoked deadlock cost up to a second. The arms that must not deadlock use the same sessions, so the arms differ only in the code under test. The setting moves when a wait is checked, and so which side of a cycle is broken: where the other side has already waited past 50 ms, as behind [6i]'s 400 ms probes, it is the session that closes the cycle — in [6i] arm 3 `update_thought` where it was the delete. Every deadlocking arm accepts either side, and every call that could close a cycle is caught, so a regression fails its assertion rather than ending the suite ([6i] arm 2's `update_thought` is wrapped for that, as arm 3's was). A wait probed before any cycle closes ([6i]'s 400 ms "still waiting" probes, arm 1's `d.ms >= 400`) is untouched, and every other bound on a wait is 8 s or more. It is superuser-only by default; the helper sends it only when the role is one (CI's service and `with-postgres.sh` connect as `postgres`), and any other role races at the default, only slower.
- **The lease clock.** [8]'s (c) and (e) waited out real leases: 2.2 s, then 4.5, 1.0 and 5.3 s, 13 s in all. `claim_thoughts` reaps a lease whose `ttl_expires_at < now()` (015) and `renew_claims` moves it to `GREATEST(ttl_expires_at, now() + the lease)` (031); neither compares against the time any other way, and the stamps they write from it (`claimed_at`, `finished_at`) are read by nothing in [8]. So `elapse(key, s)` moves the claimed deadlines of one job key (`work_type`) back `s` seconds, which is the same to both functions as waiting `s` seconds, and each sleep is now an `elapse` of the same length. The leases (2 s and 5 s), the order of every step and every assertion are unchanged; (e)'s message no longer prints wall-clock milliseconds, which now measure nothing.

The suite stays at 703 assertions; `db/README.md` is untouched. [6g]'s comment on its one stochastic assertion now gives the measured rates (SMD-2155).

**Not taken.**
