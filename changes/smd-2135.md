## Changelog

`db/test-live.ts` [5d] loads 15,000 rows where it loaded 25,000, after a `VACUUM` that keeps the rows an earlier section deleted out of its gate's sample, and [5f] loads 6,000 where it loaded 12,000, the smallest table on which each plan it asserts is the one it names; [5f]'s user triggers stay off until its cleanup has emptied the table (SMD-2135).

## FORK

test-live.ts sizes [5d]'s and [5f]'s tables to what their planner decisions need — the two sections stop spending a third of CI's live step on rows and index builds (SMD-2135)

**What changed.** The third cut of SMD-2135, after #169 (the deadlock timer, the lease clock) and #176 ([9]'s slowed stub). The ticket asked whether [5d] and [5f] could share one corpus, and whether 25,000 and 12,000 rows are what the planner needs or margin. They are margin, and the corpus is not where the time goes.
- **[5f]: 12,000 rows → 6,000.** Its time was the two HNSW builds over the loaded rows and their chunks, 18 of its 22 s locally, and a build grows faster than its rows: the thoughts index took 9.4 s over 12,000 rows, 3.3 s over 6,000 and 1.1 s over 3,000, with 0, 2 or 4 parallel maintenance workers alike. Every assertion holds down to 2,000 rows, the mutant's buffer excess thousands of pages over the heap's size at each count. 6,000 is the smallest count at which each mutant plan is the one the section's header names: at 5,000 and under, the unfiltered call's parent lookup becomes a Hash Join over the heap rather than a Merge Join over the whole primary key.
- **[5d]: 25,000 rows → 15,000, after a `VACUUM`.** The skip needs the sample's scaled estimate at ten times `v_exact`, 10,000 here. Every full page holds the same number of rows (about 70), so a draw of d distinct pages reads at least (d − 1)/d of the table, short only by the last, partly filled page: at 15,000 five distinct pages with an empty last page among them still read 12,000. Measured over 5,000 draws of the deployed statement each: 11,000 missed 4.9% (its last page held 10 rows); 12,000 missed none, only because its last page happened to hold 30.
  - The `VACUUM` between the section's `DELETE` and its load is what makes the smaller table safe. [5b]'s 2,000 rows are dead after the `DELETE`, and the load went in after their 28 pages, which the sample draws and counts as seen with no hit (041's LEFT join, on purpose). At 15,000 without it, 49 of 5,000 draws missed — about one call in a hundred, so about one run in six would have failed (twenty broad calls a run) the section's exact band. At 25,000 the same draws missed none.
- **[5f]'s triggers.** It disabled user triggers for the load and re-enabled them straight after, so its cleanup's `DELETE` wrote an audit row per loaded row, the rows [5d]'s comment says a load must not leave to the sections after. They stay off until the cleanup has emptied the table, as in [5d] and [5e]. Nothing after reads `thought_audit`'s delete rows by count ([25] reads capture rows only).

No assertion is added or removed (733, main's count), and `db/README.md`'s two lines naming the row counts follow.

**Why.** On main's push run for #176 (36178807862) and the runs since, "Run the live suite" took 129–134 s, [5d] 27.4 s and [5f] 22.4 s of it — the two largest sections once #176 cut [9].

**Held.** The sections' own teeth are their mutants and run on the smaller tables unchanged: [5d]'s 020 arm makes one more GIN scan per call on the broad filter, and [5f]'s pin `RESET` turns every branch's join into a Merge or Hash Join touching at least the heap's pages more. The section comments carry the arithmetic above, so a later change to either count has to answer it.

**Measured after.** Main (82655a02) and this change, 733/733 on every run:

| Where | Main: [5d] / [5f] / suite | This change: [5d] / [5f] / suite |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Through `with-postgres.sh` | 15.7 / 22.2 / 103 s, one stamped run | 6.5–7.0 / 5.3–6.5 / 71–75 s, ten runs |
| CI-shaped containers (`oven/bun:1.4.0` at `--cpus 4`, `pgvector:0.8.6-pg16` at 2), alternated | 12.0–12.5 / 12.0–16.6 / 80–87 s, three runs | 5.9–6.8 / 4.7–6.2 / 65–71 s, three runs |

[6g]'s first arm deadlocked 2–14 times in 40 over the ten local runs and 7–15 in the containers, the rates SMD-2155 records.

**Not taken.**
- One corpus for [5d] and [5f], as the ticket proposed. The two need different tables — [5d] no HNSW index, no chunks, one row in 250 thin; [5f] both indexes, a chunk for every second row, the first 500 thin — and [5e], between them, loads its own 3,000. Sharing [5d]'s 15,000 rows would give [5f] a larger build, the cost this cut removes.
- Keeping each broad filter's exact answers across [5d]'s two bodies. The table does not change between them, but at 15,000 rows it saved about 0.4 s.
- [5f] at 3,000 rows (1.6 s less again). The unfiltered mutant becomes a Hash Join, and the section's text and messages would stop describing the plan they catch.
- The two-database split of the suite the ticket held in reserve, until CI says whether the step is under 120 s without it.
