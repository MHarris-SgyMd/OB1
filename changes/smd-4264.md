## Record

Within one level the agenda puts the item that unblocks more first — the unblocks count, highest first, after the sooner due time and before kind and key (SMD-4264)

**Why.** 122's unblocks term steps at 1 open item blocked (+0.1 since 127) and at 3 (+0.2), and stops. Past three the count is reported, to its cap (50 by default), but never ordered on. The agenda (129) broke a tie by the sooner due time, then kind and key, so among items of one level blocking three or more, the key decided. On the dogfood board of 2026-11-12 eleven Medium tickets tied at 2.2, and SMD-3887, blocking 50 open items within three levels, came last, behind four blocking exactly 3.

**What changed.** The maintainer chose (2026-11-12) a tie-break over a graded term. Migration 137 replaces 129's ten-argument agenda, its order gaining `COALESCE(unblocks, 0) DESC` after the due time and before kind and key. It applies under `priority` and `due` alike (a tie on the moment and on effective). The nine-argument wrapper and `plan_context`'s board section call it, so they take it too. The function's comment says the order. `docs/priority.md` (the Unblocks row says why the term stops at 3; a bullet under "What this reverses or amends"), `db/README.md`, `server-portable/README.md` and the agenda tool's description say it; the REST client is regenerated for the description.

**Not changed.** Every score: `effective_priority`, its weights, search's `priority_weight` blend and `min_priority`, and 127's level first. Two items at or past the cap (50 by default) count the same and still fall to the key. A due time still comes before the count: urgency near it moves the score, and a due time with no urgency yet (more than a week off) still sorts ahead of the count in the tie-break, so a Medium due in months that blocks 3 comes before an undated Medium that blocks 40, as the ticket set the order.
