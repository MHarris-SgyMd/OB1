## Changelog

test-live runs in six shards, not five: `claims` takes [10] from the rest and [38b] from long, in Brain Checks and in `db/ci-parity.sh`, and a row ci-parity stops at its limit says the section it was in and what it had passed (SMD-4366). No behaviour changes: the suite's shards and the maintainer's script.

## Record

test-live's rest shard cut in two, and a stopped ci-parity row says how far it got — the rest 562 → 308 s (SMD-4366)

**What changed.**
- **A sixth shard, `claims`.** `db/test-live.ts`'s `SHARDS` lists it with [10], extraction through the claims, which was the rest's, and [38b], which was long's. Brain Checks' db-live matrix has its cell, `db/ci-parity.sh` its lane (`OB1_LIVE_SHARD=claims run db test-live.ts`), and `db/README.md` its line. No section is edited; each runs once, as before.
- **A stopped row says how far it got.** ci-parity's `reached` adds to the verdict of a suite its limit stopped: the last section header it printed, and the assertions it had passed and failed by its rows. A `(doc)` row is not counted, as `createAssert` counts it apart, and a failed row printed on both streams counts once. A suite with no header says its counts alone; one with no row adds nothing.

  ```
  ✗ OB1_LIVE_SHARD=rest db/test-live.ts   exit 124, stopped at its 45 s limit in [16], 453 passed and 0 failed by then · 45 s
  ```
- **The limit stays 600 s.** Its comment says why it is not raised: a shard that nears it is cut or its sections moved.

**Why.** ci-parity stops a row at 600 s and fails the run (SMD-3647). The rest shard ran 515–578 s there on the maintainer's Mac and was stopped in seven runs of twenty, with nothing failed; a stopped row said only that it stopped.
- **On CI the rest was the longest cell too.** Over main's 15 runs to 42ab662a, each cell's live step:

  | Cell | Median | Max |
  |---|---|---|
  | rest | 571 s | 604 s |
  | long | 460 s | 474 s |
  | sweep, since [38p] joined it | 378–382 s | |
  | extract | 340 s | 341 s |
  | sleep | 319 s | 322 s |
- **Two sections were four fifths of it.** By the medians of main's runs 44529853317, 44538524681 and 44557642841, of the rest's 545 s of sections [10] was 238 s and [16] 200 s; the other 67 were 108 s together.
- **Both are mostly real clocks.** Waits of 2 s or more before a row are 211 s of [10] and 161 s of [16], the outage, pause and poll cases. Across the three runners [10] moved 0.4 s and [16] 2.5 s. On the Mac the rest's process and its children used 31 s of CPU in 562 s.
- **The Mac matches CI, section for section:** [10] 238 s and [16] 202 s in a run alone, the shard 562 s. So ten lanes' load, not a slower machine, took it to the limit.

**Held.**
- **Check 35** holds the new row and the new cell to each other: `claims` out of ci-parity's rows fails it, and out of the workflow's matrix fails it (mutants, both run). With `claims` out of both and still in `SHARDS` the checker passes: SMD-4439.
- **No row lost in the move.** The rest's 1,430 assertions are 1,238 now and [10]'s 192; long's 386 are 337 and [38b]'s 49; claims has those 241 and its own [end].
- **`reached`,** twelve fixtures under macOS's bash 3.2, kept outside the tree as ci-parity has no suite: a stopped suite, one not stopped, failed and `(doc)` rows, no header, no row, a bare header, a long name, a raw array that is no header. And the run above, the rest's lane under `OB1_PARITY_ROW_LIMIT=45`.
- **shellcheck** v0.10.0 and stable, each after a positive control: ci-parity.sh's findings are main's six, none added.

**Measured after.** On the maintainer's Mac, each shard alone on a Postgres of its own, two or three side by side:

| Shard | Before | After |
|---|---|---|
| rest | 562 s, 1,430 assertions | 307 and 308 s, 1,238 |
| long | 455 s, 386 | 359 s, 337 |
| claims | | 350 s, 242 |

One full `ci-parity` run, every row passing, 477 s: rest 311 s, sleep 301, long 361, extract 331, sweep 377, claims 352. The longest live row is 63% of the limit, where the rest was 86–96% of it. On CI, by the section medians above, the rest ~308 s of sections, claims ~338 and long ~347, against sweep's ~365; a live step is 10–27 s more for the module's load.

One flake, once: the rest's first run alone failed [5e]'s JIT bound with five shard runs going at once, SMD-2246's row. [5e] runs before [10] did, and the shard passed alone straight after and in the full run.

**Not taken.**
- **Raising the limit.** It would have passed the rest at 578 s and hidden the next growth, and CI's run would still have waited on a 594 s job.
- **Moving the rest's small sections out.** The 67 are 108 s together; with all of them gone [10] and [16] alone are 438 s, still the longest cell, and a section no shard lists would no longer land in the shortest.
- **[16] in the new cell beside [10].** 438 s, longer than any cell left. The rest keeps [16], and at ~308 s the most room for the sections that land there unlisted.
- **Cutting the timers in [10] and [16].** They are most of the 438 s, and SMD-3734 did the like for long and the rest. A change to what the rows wait for, not to where they run.

**Follow-ups.**
- SMD-4439: nothing holds `SHARDS` to the db-live matrix.
- SMD-4440: ci-parity's database-free lane, 476 s of rows in the run above, sets a run's length now that the rest does not.
