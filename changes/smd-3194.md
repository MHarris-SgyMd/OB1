## Changelog

Every healthcheck in the stack's compose files probes once a second while its service starts, until it first passes. Docker probed every 5 s, so a service is now healthy about 4 s sooner on Docker. Podman, which already probes at once, turns healthy no sooner.

Postgres's healthcheck now asks over TCP. The first `up` after this recreates Postgres and the other services whose healthcheck changed, a brief restart. Building the images from a checkout needs Docker Engine 25 or podman 5.1 or later (SMD-3194).

## FORK

Compose healthchecks probe every second until a service is up (SMD-3194)

**What changed.**
- **`start_interval: 1s` on every healthcheck:**
  - in `deploy/compose.yaml` (13);
  - in `deploy/compose.tiers.yaml` (7, which check 27 holds to compose.yaml's);
  - in `deploy/edge/compose.yaml` (2).

  Where a healthcheck had no `start_period`, it gets one: 30 s for postgres and ollama, 10 s for proxy, forwarder, orchestration-runner, edge and edge-host. The start interval applies only within it. A comment on postgres's healthcheck says why, for the file.
- **Postgres is probed over TCP:** `pg_isready -h 127.0.0.1 …`, in both files. On a fresh volume the image runs a temporary server on the Unix socket alone while it creates the database. A probe a second in could pass against it, and `migrate`, which waits on postgres being healthy and dials 5432 once, met a refused connection. The temporary server has no TCP listener.
- **The server's image HEALTHCHECK** gets `--start-interval=1s` (`server-portable/Dockerfile`; compose does not override the server's). Older builders refuse the flag, so `deploy/README.md` names the floor for building from a checkout: Docker Engine 25 or podman 5.1. jev's compose healthcheck overrides its image's, so its Dockerfile is left alone.
- **A new check, 40, holds every healthcheck** in the three files, each service's and each `x-` anchor's, to a `start_period` longer than zero and `start_interval: 1s`. A new healthcheck written without them would sit out Docker's 5 s again. A disabled one (`disable: true`, or a test of `NONE`) is passed over. Its probe pins what it refuses and what it passes.
- **Pinned to the new values:** check 28's PROXY_SERVICE, FORWARDER_SERVICE, MCP_SERVICE, APP_SERVICE, LGTM_SERVICE and PG_EXPORTER_SERVICE; check 36's edge compose; `deploy/auth/provision.ts`'s auth healthcheck.

**Why.** Docker's start interval defaults to 5 s, and a healthcheck with no start period waits its interval, 5 s here. So every service waited about 5 s for its first probe, however soon it was up. In Fork Checks run 39951449029, every Started→Healthy wait in the four full-stack jobs took 5.0–5.6 s: 75 of them, about 400 s in all, many in series behind an `up --wait` or a `service_healthy` dependency.

Measured on Docker Engine 28.0.4 with Compose 2.38.2, the runner's versions, in a throwaway `docker:dind`:
- each wait falls to about 1.1 s, for the stack's own postgres and traefik images among others;
- a service ready at 2 s turns healthy at 2.1 s;
- a container restarted gets the start period again, so it is healthy 1.1 s after the restart, where it was 5.1 s.

**What else moves.**
- A service that never comes up is called unhealthy later, since failures within the start period do not count: proxy, forwarder and edge at about 30 s rather than 25 s, postgres at about 86 s rather than 60. No CI step or deploy script waits on that; the `--wait` timeouts are 60–300 s.
- None gets meaningfully longer. Past its start period a service waits for the next probe of its interval, as before: one ready at 15 s, past its 10 s start period, was healthy at 15.5 s where it was 15.2 s, which is where the probes fell.
- Probes run one at a time, each after the last ends, so a timeout longer than 1 s (lgtm's 8 s, postgres's 5 s) does not stack them. The extra probing ends at the first pass.
- The first `up` after this recreates every service whose healthcheck changed. That includes Postgres: a stop and a start, its data on its volume.

**Podman.** The maintainer's podman (6.0.2, compat API 1.44, compose 5.6.1) takes `start_interval` without complaint and drops it from the container; it keeps the new start periods. Podman runs a container's first probe at once. On a throwaway project, two services already up when started were healthy in 0.7 s with or without the key. A service still starting at that first probe waits its interval on podman, as before.

**Not taken.**
- A 1 s `interval`, the ticket's fallback. It would probe every second for the life of every container.
- The compose files under evals/ (the authorization server POC's, probing every 2 s; the orchestration kit's), which are not the stack's.
