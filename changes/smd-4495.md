## Changelog

Under the sleep scheduler, consolidation and a model subject sweep now yield to extraction where their calls would take its turn on the model server. That is where, on a local model server, the judge's model is not extraction's, so each turn between them can load the other model, or where extraction's background share of its model is one slot. They wait between calls while an extraction call is asked for or running, and a second after, at most `OB1_SLEEP_MAX_WAIT` (60 s) in one stretch of extraction's calls. They yield from consolidation's start to the period's end. Consolidation judges while writes that pause nothing keep arriving, and on such a server its calls had extraction fall behind them. The trade: extraction keeps up, and consolidation judges less while captures come — on a 7B extracting and a 27B judging on one Ollama, under a capture every 6 s, extraction's median wait fell from 122 s to 23 s and consolidation's rose from 394 s to 599 s. With one model and slots to spare, or on a hosted provider, the judge still runs beside extraction. No migration. **Upgrading:** the start says `yield:` and why where the rule applies; there is no switch of its own (one judge model with extraction's, or a share of two or more, turns it off). An Ollama on the host answering one call at a time per model (its `OLLAMA_NUM_PARALLEL` unset) while the brain counts four is not seen: raise the host's to `OB1_MODEL_SLOTS`, 2 or more (SMD-4495).

## Record

The judge model's passes yield to extraction where they share its slot or load another model beside it — consolidation joined during an inflow no longer slows extraction on such a server (SMD-4495)

**What changed.**

- `server-portable/model-call.ts`: each pass's model calls in the process are counted from asked to settled, a wait for a slot included, and `watchPassCalls(pass, fn)` tells a watcher the count at once and at each change.
- `db/sleep.ts`:
  - **The rule** (`YIELD_WHY`), read once at the start from the configuration: on a local chat endpoint the judge's model is not extraction's (both call `cfg.chat.base`), or extraction's background share of its model is one slot, as `OB1_BACKGROUND_SLOTS` and `OB1_MODEL_SLOTS` count it. Where it applies, the start says `yield:` and why.
  - **The gate:** a third gate beside the live gate and re-embed's turn gate, one a period, in the admit of consolidation and of a model subject sweep (`judgeAdmit`). It is closed while an extraction call is asked for or running, and for `YIELD_GRACE_MS` (1 s) after the last one ends, which covers the writes and the next claim between two of a worker's thoughts. A stretch of extraction's calls (less than the grace apart) closes it for `--max-wait` at most, the CLI's under `off` too; it then opens for a turn of the grace, the passes waiting at it let through, and closes again while extraction's calls go on, the cap counted afresh. A yield's closure has the claim in hand read again before the next call, as a pause's does.
  - **When:** from consolidation's start in a period, however it joined, begun before its first call, to the period's end however it ends; said in the log as it first holds consolidation back (`consolidation and a model subject sweep now yield to extraction's calls, for the rest of the period`). A drain between two captures ends nothing.
- `db/README.md` and `deploy/README.md` describe the rule, the lines, the cap, and a host Ollama the brain counts wrong.
- `db/test-live.ts`: a new section **[38y]** in the sleep shard — the rule's cells by dry run, then four runs — and the scaffold's stub serves a second model and records each call's end.

**Why.** SMD-4468 let consolidation join while writes kept arriving. Its review pass 2 measured, on a stub server of one slot, extraction falling behind captures every 4 s that main kept up with, and consolidation finishing no sooner. This record's stub runs extended that harness: the brain's own slot share installed as the CLI does, a judge model of its own, and a load's cost at the server. The maintainer's choices (2026-11-21):
- **The mechanism.** "Extraction first" in the slots' order changed nothing: 65.6 s. With one worker per pass, extraction never has a call queued at the moment its own call frees the slot. A gate on extraction's calls in hand was prototyped and measured instead.
- **The rule:** one slot, or two models. Always yielding gave back SMD-4468's gain where slots are spare (consolidation's median 420 s against 254 s); yielding only where told one slot left the two-model swaps, the canary's shape (a 7B extracting, a 27B judging).

**Beyond the decision, as built.** The first build yielded from the backlog join until extraction's pool drained. A brief drain between captures ended it for the rest of the period, and started the subject sweep, which asks the judge's model too: extraction's median rose to 22 s, with 37 loads in the swap case. So the yield runs from consolidation's start to the period's end and holds the subject sweep as well, and the cap keeps a thought stalled on long calls, or captures extraction never catches up with, from holding either for good.

**Limits.**
- A host Ollama answering one call at a time while the brain counts four is not seen with one model: compose's note on `OB1_MODEL_SLOTS` already asks for the host's `OLLAMA_NUM_PARALLEL` to be raised to it.
- Decide mode (`OB1_CONSOLIDATE_DECIDER`) is not read: its verdicts go to the decide tier, another server, and are held all the same.
- Two or more consolidation workers are all let through at each turn, so the judge's share past a cap grows with them.
- A pass's start-up probe of its model, and its probes after a failed call, are not held.
- The rule reads the brain's own slot count, not the host's slot broker's: under the broker (SMD-3379) it applies as it would without one, and the broker's residency rule orders loads on its own.
- Under captures extraction never catches up with, the judge gets a turn of a second per `--max-wait`, or the gaps.
