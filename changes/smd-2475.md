## Changelog

test-live's two races of one page slug no longer depend on timing to collide: the first create is held open until the second is seen waiting on it, so the second is refused by name every run. The two-title race had failed about 1 run in 5 under ci-parity (SMD-2475).

## FORK

test-live [32]'s two creates of one slug are held, not timed — the second create meets the first every run and is refused by name (SMD-2475)

**What changed.** `db/test-live.ts` [32] gains `heldRace`, and both slug races use it:
1. A runs its `upsert_page` inside an open transaction on `cA`.
2. B's is dispatched on `cB`, whose pid is read first for the poll.
3. A commits once B's `wait_event_type` reads `Lock`, or after 100 polls (about 5 s). `wait_event_type` is read by B's pid from the section's `sql` connection, which holds no transaction, so its view is current (SMD-2742).

Each race asserts the lock B waited on, by its `wait_event`, and the one message B is refused with:
- **One title:** B waits on 033's fingerprint lock (`advisory`), merges into A's thought, and is refused as "another writer created with this text meanwhile". The assertion used to accept "holds this page's exact text" too, the message of a B that read the slug before A committed and the thought after.
- **Two titles:** B waits on the slug's unique index, that is on A's transaction (`transactionid`), and is refused as "another writer created page … meanwhile", its thought rolled back.

A failure message now says A's outcome too. A create that fails in A's transaction releases its locks, and B then has nothing to wait on.

**Why.**
- Under ci-parity on 10-02, the two-title race read "neither refused", 1 run in 5.
- Both creates were dispatched at once, so when A committed before B looked the slug up, B found the page and updated it. The one-title race had the same timing and could fail the same way.
- On 10-29, main's timed races passed 40 of 40 runs of [32] alone under load. The flake is rare outside ci-parity, so the case rests on the mechanism, and on the mutant below that makes it certain.

**Held.**
- Each race's `wait_event` and refusal message, and A's created page.
- Mutants, each failing every run it was given:
  - 064 without its unique-violation clause around the page insert: the two-title race reads the constraint's raw error, 3 of 3;
  - 064 without the refusal of a merged capture: the one-title race reads the two-title message, 3 of 3;
  - A committing before B is dispatched, the old flake made certain: both races read that B waited on nothing and was not refused, 2 of 2;
  - the poll reading a wrong pid: both races fail on their `wait_event` alone, their refusals passing, 1 of 1.

**Measured after.** [32] alone, in a copy with `runsHere` admitting only `"32"`:
- **With the `wait_event` pin:**
  - unloaded, 30 of 30;
  - under load, 15 of 15 that reached [32], two at a time beside eight CPU burners and two other sessions' ci-parity runs; 5 of the 20 starts lost their port before podman bound it.
  - Every B was seen on `advisory`, then `transactionid`, by the second poll.
- **Before the pin, with the same polling:**
  - 50 of 51 starts reached [32] and passed; the 39th lost its port;
  - under load, 40 of 40, B seen within 61 ms.

test-live's `rest` shard: 1023 of 1023.

**Not taken.**
- **An advisory lock or `pg_sleep` standing in for A's hold,** as the ticket suggested. Holding A's real transaction makes B meet the lock under test; a stand-in lock would not, and a sleep is still timed.
- **Polling from `cA`,** as SMD-2742's sites do. It works here too, because `cB`'s session predates `cA`'s transaction and the wait columns are read live: two mutants polling from `cA`, with the clear and without, passed 4 of 4 each. But `sql` holds no transaction, so its poll needs neither `pg_stat_clear_snapshot()` nor that argument. A B connected fresh and counted from inside `cA`'s transaction would be missed: a mutant of that shape saw nothing in 100 polls.
