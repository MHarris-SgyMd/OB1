## Changelog

The sleep scheduler's own stops of a thought it holds back — setting extraction aside, among others — are counted as cuts, apart from the stops, which now say the wakes (migration 100), so through a judge outage of hours preflight's `sleep` row no longer reads hundreds of wakes, and the cuts no longer decide which held thoughts it names (SMD-3060). The row and `--status` say both counts. After migrating, restart the sleep service; counts made before stay as they were.

## FORK

The sleep scheduler's own cuts of a stalled thought are counted apart from its stops, so the held word says the wakes and orders by them (SMD-3060)

**What changed.**
- **Migration 100, `extraction_cuts`:**
  - `extraction_progress.cuts` counts the scheduler's own hard stops of a claimed thought, written by `note_extraction_cut` under 093's holder rule.
  - A cut adds one to `cuts` and moves nothing else. It is no wake, so it neither stalls the thought nor ends its gaining.
  - 093's `note_extraction_stop` and `save_extraction_window` are redefined to set `cuts` back to 0 when either replaces a row of other text or windowing, as they do the stops.
  - The table's and `stops`' comments say the cuts are counted apart.
  - It refuses to apply without 093.
- **`db/lease.ts`:** `PassStop` takes `{ cut }`, a hard stop of the caller's own. Extraction counts it as a cut; the other engines read it not.
- **`db/sleep.ts`:** the scheduler's own hard stops pass the cut: `setAside` (the join's set-aside, the restart, the let-out's set-aside), and its stop of the passes when one ends by itself, a follower is found outside or the sleep throws. A wake and the operator's second signal do not, so `stops` counts those alone.
- **`db/extract-entities.ts`:**
  - The hard stop counts a cut through `note_extraction_cut`.
  - On a brain before 100 it counts nothing, never a stop instead, and says so once a run. Each set-aside starts a run of its own, so through an outage that is about once a minute until 100 is applied. The windows are still kept.
- **The held word** (sleep.ts's `heldBack`, `server-portable/brain-info.ts`'s `heldWords`) and extraction's `--status` add ", and N cut(s) of the scheduler's own".
  - The order stays the most stalled, then the most stopped, so it is now by the wakes.
  - `cuts` is read only where the column is there: the scheduler probes once at its start, `--status` at each run. So the sleep service is restarted after 100: a scheduler of an earlier tree counts its own stops as stops, and one of this tree started before 100 names the held thoughts without their cuts. Counts made before 100 stay as they were.
  - A row an earlier scheduler stamped, with no `cuts`, reads as before.
  - A worker's claim between the scheduler's read and its stop can put a fresh capture in hand: it gets a cut and no stop, so it is neither named nor dated behind the next sleep's captures, as one stop used to date it.

**Why.**
- 093's `stops` counted every hard stop that took a claim, and meant the wakes that held a thought back (SMD-2694).
- The scheduler's own hard stops are the same call as a wake's: the join's set-aside (SMD-2694), and the restart and the let-out's set-aside (SMD-3008). Through a judge outage of hours with captures arriving, the let-out cut a stalled thought about once a minute, its `stops` climbing some 60 an hour.
- The `held` word on heartbeat:sleep read that as wakes ("no window kept over 240 stop(s)"), and its tie-break by `stops` let the cuts pick the three thoughts named.
- Found by SMD-3008's post-merge cold read. Not measured on a real brain: no stalled thought has met a long judge outage there yet.
