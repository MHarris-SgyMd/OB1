## Changelog

The weekly digest no longer reads `importance`, so ten captures from a capture-only key carrying a high value can no longer rank every other key's thought out of it; it reads a share of each day and writer in the window instead (SMD-2653). **Upgrading:** `db/weekly-digest.ts` drops `--min-importance`; a scheduled run that passes it is refused as an unknown flag, so drop the flag.

## FORK

The weekly digest reads a share of each day and writer — a capture-only key's `importance` no longer decides what it reads (SMD-2653)

**What changed.** `db/weekly-digest.ts` stops reading `importance`, the native column and `metadata.importance` both, as SMD-3661 decided (`docs/priority.md`, decision 8: the key is retired):
- **The pick.** `pickForDigest` replaces `thoughtImportance` and `rankAndTrim`. `readThoughts` numbers each (day, `metadata.actor_name`) group's rows newest first, as turns, and reads in turn order up to its 400. The pick takes the prompt's 80 a turn at a time: the newest of every group, then each group's second, and so on. It returns them newest first. A quiet day keeps its share beside a busy one, and a key that floods the window gets one row per day per turn, as every other writer does. The server stamps `actor_name` and refuses it from a caller (`RESERVED_META`).
- **Gone.** The threshold, the widening below ten rows, `--min-importance`, and the probe for an `importance` column.
- **The prompt.** Each row in the synthesis prompt loses its `importance` field, and the prompt calls the rows "a share of each day and writer in the window", not "high-signal" ones.
- **Times compare as instants,** and an unreadable one sorts last. The old recency tiebreak compared `String(created_at)`, and a row from the database carries a Date, whose string leads with its weekday. With no importance anywhere (stable held none on 2026-11-02), that tiebreak was the whole order: a quiet week's widened pool of 60 began with the window's Wednesdays, not its newest rows.

`--min-importance` was a flag of maintainer tooling (`db/`, outside the version contract), so its removal is a `minor`.

**Why.** Any key could set `metadata.importance` on its own rows, a capture-only key included. SMD-2617's review pass 3 showed it on the real REST door: with a write key's 15 captures and a capture key's 10 carrying `importance: 9`, the pool held the capture key's 10 and none of the writer's. Only that key's text reached the synthesis model and the Telegram sink. Without its rows the pool held all 15 of the writer's.

**Why the spread.** This change first ranked by recency alone, and its review found that too narrow. Stable took 453 thoughts in the 7 days to 2026-11-02, from 10 to 93 a day. The newest 80 covered a day and a half, and the read's cap of 400 dropped the oldest day. A capture key's 80 captures just before a run would have filled the prompt, as its 10 high-importance ones had.

**Held.**
- `test-weekly-digest.ts`:
  - Dates across three weekdays order by the instant, ISO strings too, and an unreadable time sorts last.
  - A quiet day's first row comes before a busy day's third.
  - The repro, pure: a writer's 15 newer rows and a capture key's 10 older ones carrying importance 9 in metadata and as a column. The writer's lead.
  - The cap of 80, and a prompt that carries a row's type and text but no `importance`.
- Seven mutants, each refused by that suite:
  - turns ignored;
  - the string sort;
  - the unreadable-time guard dropped;
  - importance ranked first;
  - `importance` back in the prompt;
  - the cap dropped;
  - the pick left in turn order.
- `test-live.ts` [36] runs the CLI without `--min-importance`, and its stub keeps the synthesis prompt. What reaches it:
  - today's three rows, each carrying an importance, and one row from each of three earlier days;
  - beside them, a newer burst of 100 from one writer, and that writer's row from six days ago;
  - no row's `importance`.
- Two live failures prove [36] can fail:
  - With the day dropped from the read's groups, the writer's older row is its 101st and falls out.
  - With every row under one writer (the payload's `actor_name`, which the stamp drops), three of the six fall out.

**Review pass 1** (the code-review skill, eight findings):
- **Changed the mechanism:** recency alone made a weekly digest of the last day and a half, and a burst of 80 captures could fill it. That is the spread above.
- **Tests:** [36]'s `importance` check now reads the key alone, not the word in any row. It plants earlier days and a burst, which also showed that a raw write's `actor_name` comes from the `ob1.actor` envelope, not the payload. The repro now exceeds the cap; under it, the importance-first mutant had survived.
- **Smaller fixes:** the guard for an unreadable time, the dead cap of 200, and `docs/vendored-disposition.md`'s row.
- **Declined:** keeping `--min-importance` (below).

**Not taken.**
- Keeping `--min-importance` as an ignored flag for a release. Nothing in the tree schedules the digest (the stack, its runner image), and nothing on the maintainer's machine does. The changelog's Upgrading line names the change.
- Refusing `importance` to a capture-only key at capture (the ticket's option 1). Once the digest stops reading it, nothing in the core or `db/` ranks by the key, and the vendored workers may still write it as data. Once SMD-3667 lands, the digest ranks by the effective priority.
