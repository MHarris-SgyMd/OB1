## Changelog

The H2O-Lightning-4B decision tier (`jev/h2o/serve.py`, the tier decide mode calls) can read two-way: `H2O_ATTENTION=two-way`, on a revision trained for it (H2O's are from v1.3), reads each question with the model's full-attention layers attending in both directions, the mode H2O recommends. At start it checks itself against the answers H2O recorded for three probes and does not serve if they differ. Nothing changes until the setting is given: the tier still reads one-way, at `v1.2.3`, with the same `rules` value in `/info`. A two-way read shares no prefix, so each question of a request reads its record again, and a context over `H2O_TWO_WAY_MAX_TOKENS` (8000) is refused with a 422 (SMD-4369).

## Record

The decision tier reads H2O-Lightning-4B two-way on this Mac, through its own adapter, checked at start against H2O's recorded answers; the pin stays at v1.2.3 until it is measured on our question (SMD-4369)

**Why.** The tier loads `h2oai/h2o-lightning-4b` at `v1.2.3` (2026-10-09). H2O has published through `v1.4.2` (2026-10-29), with new weights at v1.3, v1.3.1 and v1.4.0. From v1.3 the weights answer under two attention patterns, and the gains H2O reports are in the new one: two-way, where the answer slot sees the record, the question and the options in both directions. H2O serves two-way through its own build of vLLM on CUDA, which this Mac does not run. Read one-way, the way this adapter read, v1.4.2 scores no higher than v1.2.3 on H2O's public set by the cards' own counts (202 of 231 against 205; two-way 206). So an upgrade is worth measuring only two-way, and the maintainer chose to get two-way on this Mac (2026-11-17).

**What changed (PR 1).** `jev/h2o/serve.py` gains a reading mode, `H2O_ATTENTION` (one-way, the default, or two-way; H2O's own words `causal` and `bidirectional` are read too), and a length limit for it, `H2O_TWO_WAY_MAX_TOKENS` (8000).

- **The read.** Qwen3.5-4B has 32 layers: 8 full-attention, every fourth, and 24 linear-attention (Gated DeltaNet). `transformers`' text model takes `attention_mask` as a dict by layer type and uses it as given, so a two-way read is one forward pass with an additive mask of zeros for `full_attention` and none for `linear_attention`, which is causal by construction. Nothing in `transformers` is patched, and no engine is added.
- **The check at start.** H2O's shim refuses an engine whose answers to three fixed probes, read two-way at temperature 1, are more than `two_way.probe_tolerance` (0.05) from the ones `serve_config.json` records for the release weights, or less than 0.05 from its one-way answers on every question. The adapter applies both rules before it serves, to the same probes rendered as the shim renders them. A revision whose `serve_config.json` has no `two_way.probe_reference` is refused under two-way: weights from before v1.3 were not trained for it.
- **No shared prefix.** One-way, the decisions of a request that share a context read it once. A two-way prefix is not reusable, so under two-way every prompt is a whole pass, whatever `H2O_SHARE` says.
- **The limit.** A two-way read's attention grows with the square of its length. The question and its options are part of that length, so under two-way the whole prompt is counted: one over `H2O_TWO_WAY_MAX_TOKENS` gets the contract's 422, naming the setting, and `/info`'s `max_tokens` says that limit. Over the model's own limit, the refusal is the one-way one. One-way counts the record alone, as before.
- **Provenance.** `/info`'s `rules` is the hash of a rules string, which differs by mode: the one-way string is unchanged, so its hash (`198e4c78db4c23dd`) is what every earlier lineage row carries, and a two-way judgement's row carries `72fa9c992f907dc0`. Under two-way the temperatures are the `two_way` block's own, shipped equal to the one-way ones.

**Measured** on this Mac (64 GB), v1.4.2 (`839c5c95`, weights sha256 `c9b6290d…f8a377`), float16 on MPS with decider's kernels:

- **H2O's probes:** the largest gap between a two-way answer and H2O's recorded one is 0.0012 (tolerance 0.05); the two modes' answers are up to 0.0967 apart (at least 0.05). The third-party MLX build H2O's card links reports 0.014 on the first.
- **Time and memory by length,** one binary question over made-up text, a whole pass each: 1,037 tokens 0.74 s one-way and 0.74 s two-way; 1,997 tokens 1.46 s and 1.61 s; 4,237 tokens 3.14 s and 3.26 s; 7,821 tokens 5.94 s and 6.73 s. The MPS driver held 9.2 GB after the shortest and 11.3 GB after the longest. Not measured past 7,821 tokens, which is where the default limit comes from.
- **Served:** started with `H2O_REVISION=v1.4.2 H2O_ATTENTION=two-way` on a spare port, ready in 27 s, the start line naming the probes' match. A made-up pair's verdict and two yes/no questions answered in 1.23 s, each result `truncated: false`.

**Not measured, and why the pin does not move here.** Whether v1.4.2 read two-way judges our pairs better than v1.2.3 read one-way. That needs pairs with labels, and decide mode's cut at P(outdates) of 0.5 was set on v1.2.3's calibration. It is this ticket's next piece of work.

**Not taken.** **The MLX build** (NetProjects, 8-bit, 4.8 GB): it reads two-way through its own engine repository under H2O's shim and matches the probes to within 0.014, but it serves H2O's own request format, not `ob1-jev/1`, runs quantized weights, and adds a third party's engine code to a tier that reads brain text. **H2O's cut of an over-long record** (BM25 selection, from v1.4.1): this adapter still refuses a record it would read in part, as decided for SMD-3326. **A mode by request:** the mode is the tier's, set at start, so one tier's judgements share one `rules` value.

**Held.** `test_serve.py` [13] to [17], 74 assertions in all, with no model: the setting's words and its refusal; the limit; the rules string by mode, the one-way one as it was; the `two_way` block read or refused (absent, another probe count, a probe question without an answer per option, a tolerance outside (0, 0.5)); the guard's two rules, each turning at its own number; a two-way read as one whole pass a prompt though sharing is on; the guard reading every probe question once each way, unshared, at temperature 1, a structured record as compact JSON; the 422's words under each limit. Twenty-eight mutants, each killed: twenty-five on the row that names its rule, three by the test crashing where a refusal was removed (a word that is no mode, a missing block, a question that cannot be encoded).

**Review pass 1** (cold-read of the diff; then run-it on the real tier). Nine findings fixed, one declined, one left for the measurement:

- The two-way limit counted the record alone, and the mask is the whole prompt's square: 24 options of 20,000 characters under a short record made a prompt of 100,000 tokens that passed. The prompt is counted under two-way; on the real tier a 14,064-token prompt over a short record is now a 422, and the same record under a short question is read. (caught: cold-read; held: test_serve [17])
- The guard took its two numbers with `max`, which passes over a NaN, so a read that overflowed on one probe counted for nothing in either rule and could serve. An answer that is not a finite probability per option fails the guard, naming its question. (caught: cold-read; held: test_serve [16])
- A revision not trained for two-way was refused only after its weights were downloaded and hashed, under a comment promising the start stops at once. `serve_config.json` is read alone first: on `v1.2.3` the refusal takes 0.26 s. (caught: cold-read)
- `two_way.temperature` giving one type alone, or null, was a bare KeyError or TypeError. One reader, `temperatures_of`, serves both blocks and refuses by the field's name; zero and negative values are refused too, where `answer` read them as its floor. (caught: cold-read; held: test_serve [16])
- With a model that sets no limit of its own (`max_state_tokens` 0), a context over the two-way limit was told it was over the model's. (caught: cold-read; held: test_serve [17])
- The two-way rules string was the one-way one with a clause replaced, so it still said the temperatures were the top block's, and a reworded `RULES` would have left both modes one string and one hash. Each replaced clause must be in `RULES` exactly once or the start stops, and the string names the `two_way` block's temperatures and the whole-prompt limit. Its hash moved from `7a682316774558f0` to `72fa9c992f907dc0`; no judgement carried the first. (caught: cold-read; held: test_serve [13], [16])
- The refusal of a revision named its commit's sha, which names no setting. It names the revision as set, with the commit: `v1.2.3 (acaf0d4e)`. (caught: cold-read; held: test_serve [16])
- The temperatures' parsing was written twice, and `handle` read the mode through `getattr` for the test's fake reader alone. One helper, and the fake reader states its mode. (caught: cold-read)
- The PR's description said the two-way 422 was held by the stubbed test alone, the contract's longest context coming to under 8,000 tokens. The first bullet's run exercises it on the real tier. (caught: run-it)
- **Declined:** batching a request's two-way prompts into one padded pass. It would cut the time a request of several questions holds the read lock, but a padded two-way batch is a different read from the one H2O's probes hold, and decide mode asks one question a pair.
- **Left for the measurement:** the two-way read is held by three probes of about a hundred tokens each, and `conformance.py` compares one-way reads only, since H2O's published per-question reads are one-way at v1.2.3. Before Work 3 scores anything, the read needs a check at our pairs' length, against H2O's public two-way rows if they carry probabilities.
