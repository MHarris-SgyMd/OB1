## Changelog

The H2O-Lightning-4B decision tier (`jev/h2o/serve.py`, the tier decide mode calls) can read two-way: `H2O_ATTENTION=two-way`, on a revision trained for it (H2O's are from v1.3), reads each question with the model's full-attention layers attending in both directions, the mode H2O recommends. At start it checks itself against the answers H2O recorded for three probes and does not serve if they differ. Nothing else changes until the setting is given: the tier still reads one-way, at `v1.2.3`, with the same `rules` value in `/info`. A two-way read shares no prefix, so each question of a request reads its record again, and a prompt (the record, the question and its options) over `H2O_TWO_WAY_MAX_TOKENS` (8000) is refused with a 422. In both modes, a lone surrogate character in a proposition or an option is now refused with a 422 for that pair, as one in the record always was; the one-way tier answered a 500 for it, which a caller retries without end (SMD-4369).

## Record

The decision tier reads H2O-Lightning-4B two-way on this Mac, through its own adapter, checked at start against H2O's recorded answers; the pin stays at v1.2.3 until it is measured on our question (SMD-4369)

**Why.** The tier loads `h2oai/h2o-lightning-4b` at `v1.2.3` (2026-10-09). H2O has published through `v1.4.2` (2026-10-29), with new weights at v1.3, v1.3.1 and v1.4.0. From v1.3 the weights answer under two attention patterns, and the gains H2O reports are in the new one: two-way, where the answer slot sees the record, the question and the options in both directions. H2O serves two-way through its own build of vLLM on CUDA, which this Mac does not run. Read one-way, the way this adapter read, v1.4.2 scores no higher than v1.2.3 on H2O's public set by the cards' own counts (202 of 231 against 205; two-way 206). So an upgrade is worth measuring only two-way, and the maintainer chose to get two-way on this Mac (2026-11-17).

**What changed (PR 1).** `jev/h2o/serve.py` gains a reading mode, `H2O_ATTENTION` (one-way, the default, or two-way; H2O's own words `causal` and `bidirectional` are read too), and a length limit for it, `H2O_TWO_WAY_MAX_TOKENS` (8000).

- **The read.** Qwen3.5-4B has 32 layers: 8 full-attention, every fourth, and 24 linear-attention (Gated DeltaNet). `transformers`' text model takes `attention_mask` as a dict by layer type and uses it as given, so a two-way read is one forward pass with an additive mask of zeros for `full_attention` and none for `linear_attention`, which is causal by construction. Nothing in `transformers` is patched, and no engine is added.
- **The check at start.** H2O's shim refuses an engine whose answers to three fixed probes, read two-way at temperature 1, are more than `two_way.probe_tolerance` (0.05) from the ones `serve_config.json` records for the release weights, or less than 0.05 from its one-way answers on every question. The adapter applies both rules before it serves, to the same probes rendered as the shim renders them. A revision whose `serve_config.json` has no `two_way.probe_reference` is refused under two-way: weights from before v1.3 were not trained for it.
- **No shared prefix.** One-way, the decisions of a request that share a context read it once. A two-way prefix is not reusable, so under two-way every prompt is a whole pass, whatever `H2O_SHARE` says.
- **The limit.** A two-way read's attention grows with the square of its length. The question and its options are part of that length, so under two-way the whole prompt is counted too: one over `H2O_TWO_WAY_MAX_TOKENS` gets the contract's 422, naming the prompt and the setting. The model's limit on the record stays what it is, with its own refusal, in both modes, and `/info`'s `max_tokens` is the smaller of the two.
- **Provenance.** `/info`'s `rules` is the hash of a rules string, which differs by mode: the one-way string is unchanged, so its hash (`198e4c78db4c23dd`) is what every earlier lineage row carries, and a two-way judgement's row carries `72fa9c992f907dc0`. Under two-way the temperatures are the `two_way` block's own, shipped equal to the one-way ones.

**Measured** on this Mac (64 GB), v1.4.2 (`839c5c95`, weights sha256 `c9b6290d…f8a377`), float16 on MPS with decider's kernels:

- **H2O's probes:** the largest gap between a two-way answer and H2O's recorded one is 0.0012 (tolerance 0.05); the two modes' answers are up to 0.0967 apart (at least 0.05). The third-party MLX build H2O's card links reports 0.014 on the first.
- **Time and memory by length,** one binary question over made-up text, a whole pass each: 1,037 tokens 0.74 s one-way and 0.74 s two-way; 1,997 tokens 1.46 s and 1.61 s; 4,237 tokens 3.14 s and 3.26 s; 7,821 tokens 5.94 s and 6.73 s. The MPS driver held 9.2 GB after the shortest and 11.3 GB after the longest. Not measured past 7,821 tokens, which is where the default limit comes from.
- **Served:** started with `H2O_REVISION=v1.4.2 H2O_ATTENTION=two-way` on a spare port, ready in 27 s, the start line naming the probes' match. A made-up pair's verdict and two yes/no questions answered in 1.23 s, each result `truncated: false`.

**What changed (PR 2).** `jev/h2o/conformance.py --two-way <results.jsonl.gz>` checks the two-way read against H2O's own published two-way reads, at length. PR 1's review left this open: the start-up guard holds the read on three probes of about a hundred tokens, and `conformance.py` compared one-way reads only, since H2O's routing set is one-way at v1.2.3.

- **The reference.** H2O's per-row results on the Decision Index 0.3 public suite (`h2oai/h2o-lightning-4b-decision-index`, `runs/h2o-lightning-4b/results.jsonl.gz`): 140,620 requests, each with the request as sent and a probability per option, every one read two-way on vLLM at v1.4.1, whose weights v1.4.2 keeps. Public benchmark rows, read from a file the caller fetches; nothing of it is in the repository.
- **The sample.** A row's first choice question, the rows shuffled by seed 4369 and taken until each of four strata of prompt length holds 60: under 500 tokens, 500 to 2,000, 2,000 to 4,300, 4,300 to 8,000. Yes/no questions are left out, since that run applied the shim's yes/no floor, which the port never applies. A prompt over the two-way limit is counted and not read.
- **The bar,** fixed on the ticket before the run, is the one-way arm's: at least 98% of top answers agree and the median largest gap is at most 0.01, over the whole sample and in each of the two long strata on its own. A long stratum with no question read fails.
- **Measured** (2026-11-18, v1.4.2, float16 on MPS): top answers agree on 240 of 240. The median gap is 0.0011 over all, 0.0029 at 2,000 to 4,300 tokens and 0.0003 at 4,300 to 8,000; the largest is 0.0506, one question in the longest stratum. H2O's own two passes over one server differ by a median 0.0005.
- The one-way arm reads as before; both arms share the bar's one function.
- **Held,** with no model: `test_serve.py` [18], four rows on a results file of eight made-up rows (which rows are compared and why the others are not, a structured record's rendering, the bar's two numbers and its refusal of an empty sample, the strata running to the adapter's limit with no hole); 88 assertions in the file. Fourteen mutants of `conformance.py`, each killed. The arm run from the repository gives the first run's 240 questions and the same numbers.

**Not measured, and why the pin does not move here.** Whether v1.4.2 read two-way judges our pairs better than v1.2.3 read one-way. That needs pairs with labels, and decide mode's cut at P(outdates) of 0.5 was set on v1.2.3's calibration. It is this ticket's next piece of work.

**Not taken.** **The MLX build** (NetProjects, 8-bit, 4.8 GB): it reads two-way through its own engine repository under H2O's shim and matches the probes to within 0.014, but it serves H2O's own request format, not `ob1-jev/1`, runs quantized weights, and adds a third party's engine code to a tier that reads brain text. **H2O's cut of an over-long record** (BM25 selection, from v1.4.1): this adapter still refuses a record it would read in part, as decided for SMD-3326. **A mode by request:** the mode is the tier's, set at start, so one tier's judgements share one `rules` value.

**Held.** `test_serve.py` [13] to [17], 84 assertions in all, with no model: the setting's words and its refusal; the limit; the rules string by mode, the one-way one as it was; the `two_way` block read or refused (absent, another probe count, a probe question without an answer per option, a tolerance outside (0, 0.5)); the guard's two rules, each turning at its own number; a two-way read as one whole pass a prompt though sharing is on; the guard reading every probe question once each way, unshared, at temperature 1, a structured record as compact JSON; the 422's words under each limit. Thirty-six mutants, each killed: thirty-one on the row that names its rule, five by the test crashing or stopping where a refusal or a parse was changed.

- **A limit, written down:** a two-way request of many questions takes a pass each, and the client's deadline of 30 s and a second a decision does not know it. Decide mode sends a few decisions a request and is inside it. A caller asking many must set its own deadline; the README's section says so. Sizing the client for two-way belongs with moving a tier to it.
- **Declined:** carrying the counted prompts into the read, to tokenize each once. Under two-way a prompt's count is milliseconds beside its pass. **Declined:** resolving the revision once for the early check and the snapshot. The second read is the snapshot's own file, and a revision that moves between the two is caught by the Reader.
