## Changelog

A thought can now be forgotten, not just deleted (SMD-1723, migration 139). `forget_thought(id, actor)` removes the thought and what in the database holds its text or id, in one transaction, and names what it can't reach. That covers the extraction's entity links and bare orphaned entities, the snapshot's vectors, other thoughts' `derived_from`, relation facets and expired citations, the page sections built on it, the priority statements and open items read from it, its deliverable's events, and the query log's references. Every event of it in the audit log is redacted in place, keeping who did what and when. A thought already deleted can be forgotten too, from what the log and the stores still hold. A ledger records each forgetting with the evidence; the same text is refused on capture unless reinstated, and the syncs leave out the record it was ingested as. A thought another cites is refused `CITED`, as a delete is, unless the caller detaches it. Since migration 144 `forget_thought` runs as its owner and is an operation over REST (`POST /v1/thoughts/{id}/forget`) and MCP for a write key, the id echoed in `confirm`; a forgotten text is refused `FORGOTTEN` before it is embedded, and preflight names a forgotten thought a store still holds. The session hook's fan-out (1.9.0) copies a forget to the other brains, a server from 144 on reaching a copy deleted there before by its key. **Upgrading:** the compose stack's servers connect as the database owner and need nothing. A self-hosted server role from `migrate.ts --grant` holds no `EXECUTE` on the new `forget_thought` until `--grant` is run again after migration 144; until then the forget tool fails and preflight's `forget definer` row says so. A fan-out hook pasted before 1.9.0 needs its matcher replaced to copy forgets.

## Record

forget_thought — a thought removed from every store in the database that holds its text, its events redacted in place, and the forgetting recorded (SMD-1723)

**Why.** `delete_thought` removes the row, but the brain keeps what it said.
- **The log:** every event carries the text: a capture's content, each edit's before and after, and the tombstone's previous content and metadata.
- **Everywhere else:** the entities, the snapshot's vectors, other thoughts' `derived_from`, the facets, the page sections, the priority statements, the open items, the deliverables and the query log keep the text or the id.
- **No removal path:** `thought_audit` refuses every DELETE. Nothing could answer "is this gone" with evidence.

**What changed.** The maintainer's choices (2026-11-13):
- three PRs;
- a cited thought refused;
- the fingerprint kept, so a forgotten text is refused on its way back;
- the page sections built on it deleted.

Migration 139:
- **`forget_thought(id, actor)`,** in one transaction, under READ COMMITTED alone, as every priority writer runs:
  - the supersession lock, the capture's fingerprint lock for every text it has had, the priority lock of each item whose statements it deletes (as the priority writers lock it, so one holding that lock and then the row's share lock does not deadlock with the forget), then the row — so a capture of one of its texts waits for the forget and is refused after (`OB005`);
  - the fingerprints read again under the row lock, so an edit that committed meanwhile gives its text to the ledger too;
  - a thought deleted before is forgotten from what remains: its snapshot vectors and every store below (`gone: true`);
  - every bare orphan entity (no mention, edge, priority statement or merge record) deleted, on every call: which orphans were the thought's can't be told once their mentions are gone;
  - a thought with an active citation refused `CITED`, unless the caller detaches (`forget_thought(id, actor, true)`): the citing rows are deleted with the rest; citations an earlier delete detached are deleted too;
  - every fingerprint the text had, read from the log first, less any a standing twin holds;
  - `rebuild_derived(id, 'forget', true, fingerprints)`;
  - relation facets targeting it and expired citations of it deleted;
  - its id stripped from other thoughts' `derived_from`;
  - page sections built on it (as evidence or pending evidence) deleted through `delete_page_section`, the page re-rendered; every page whose own log names it listed;
  - the query log's `result_ids` (each score with its id) and `result_items` (with its open items; the other items kept) cleared of it, and its action rows on it deleted;
  - the row deleted through `delete_thought`;
  - then, the row gone, priority statements on it, on its open items, or taking a due time from either, and 142's priority findings on them, deleted and re-projected under those item locks; those items, their events and close proposals deleted, as are proposals whose evidence it is (the proposals citing it locked first, so a decision in flight commits, and the reason an accepted one copied onto another item's status event blanked; their ids recorded for a later forget); its deliverable's events deleted;
  - every event of it redacted;
  - a ledger row written.
- **What it returns:**
  - each store's count;
  - `rebuild_derived`'s report;
  - `irreproducible` (thoughts derived from it) and `pages` (pages re-rendered, or whose own log names it), for a person to review;
  - `twins`;
  - `source`, the record it was ingested as (a Linear ticket or one of its sections, a note), as `{system, identity_sha256}`, its identity a sha256 as the fingerprints are, or null;
  - `outside`, the copies beyond the brain.
- **The log's `redact` amendment,** its third beside `backfill` and `payload`:
  - **What it changes:** an event's diff becomes `ob1_audit_redacted_diff(diff, action)`. Every text is the marker `[forgotten]` and every metadata object `{"forgotten": true}`; ids, fingerprints and times are kept. The `source` it copied from the metadata is blanked. `redacted_at` is stamped, once, and every other column stays byte-equal.
  - **When:** only under the forget's own setting, and only once the thought is deleted.
  - **Why `payload` can't undo it:** a capture always gains the marker as its content, and the `payload` amendment never changes a content once set.
- **`thought_forgotten`,** the ledger: append-only, one row per forgetting (when, by whom, the fingerprints, the evidence).
  - A capture or edit to a forgotten text is refused `OB005` unless `ob1.reinstate_forgotten` is on, or a standing thought already holds that text (reinstated, or a twin).
  - The whole record is forgotten (the maintainer, review pass 8), not its text alone; each writer leaves it out until a row stands at its identity again, edits at the source included:
    - `db/ingest-records.ts` counts a record the brain forgot, by its id or its identity, as `forgotten`, and goes on;
    - `db/sync-linear.ts` leaves a forgotten ticket out of its plan (neither missing nor fetched, no drift in `--audit`), `--only` included, and a forgotten section out of its parts; a part refused `OB005` is reported `forgotten`, not as an error.
  - Each role writing thoughts reads it: the capture group gains `SELECT`, and 139 grants it to each existing role with a write on the table, on one of its columns, or through PUBLIC.
- **The append-only guards of 116, 117 and 134** admit the forget's deletes of the rows that hold that thought's text, once it is deleted, and 117's also admits blanking the reason a status event copied from a close proposal citing it (or one an earlier forget of it took); nothing else, and the forget's setting never names a living thought.
- **A write racing a forget** (the maintainer, review pass 2: simplify): another store's write that lands beside a running forget — a statement restated, an item updated, a close proposal, a page section, a derived thought — may stay. A second `forget_thought` of the same id sweeps it, the items an earlier forget took included (the ledger's `item_ids`), and answers `FORGOTTEN` when there is nothing left. PR 2's preflight check names a forgotten id still held. Serialising every writer against a forget is SMD-4299.

**The limit, named.**
- **Prose the forget can't reach:** prose that absorbed the text, in a derived thought, a page's earlier render in its own log, or a section once built on it and since rewritten (revisions record no evidence; the maintainer: listed, not deleted), can't be removed mechanically. Those thoughts and pages are listed for review.
- **Another thought's own history:** a thought that once held the same text and was edited away keeps it in its log.
- **Edge cases not reached:** a twin whose key a raw write left stale is not read as a twin; a raw INSERT takes no fingerprint lock, so one racing the forget is not held.
- **A statement deleted whole:** a priority statement on another item that takes its due time from the thought, or from one of its items, goes with its level: the priority table lets a row change only by closing, and a closed row keeps the reference (the ticket's Work 8).
- **A forgotten record stays out of the syncs** until it is reinstated, later edits at its source included. A thought deleted before it is forgotten has lost its record (`thought_sources` goes with the row): only its text is refused. Forgetting a ticket leaves its sections, listed `irreproducible`; each is forgotten on its own.
- **Kept on purpose:**
  - an entity a surviving thought still mentions keeps its name and aliases;
  - other thoughts' events keep the id they named. An id is not the text.
- **Outside the brain:** sleep dumps, Linear comments already posted, digest files, extraction dumps and backups taken before the forget (`backup.ts` exports, `pg_dump`) are named, not reached.

**Held.**

## PR 2: the operation over REST and MCP

**What changed.** The maintainer's choice (2026-11-15): `forget_thought` runs as its owner, so a role needs `EXECUTE` on it alone.
- **Migration 144:** 139's body renamed `ob1_forget_thought_body`, and `forget_thought` a SECURITY DEFINER wrapper over it: its `search_path` pinned (the schemas the migration ran under, `pg_temp` last), every runtime `ob1.*` setting but `ob1.actor` emptied for the call and given back, so a caller's replay or amendment setting is not carried in as the owner; `EXECUTE` revoked from PUBLIC and the **server** grant group's, a grant 139's function held carried to the wrapper. `ob1_text_forgotten_at(text)`, 139's refusal rule asked before an embedding; `ob1_forget_leftovers()`, each forgotten thought a store still holds.
- **The operation,** `forget_thought` (write scope; `POST /v1/thoughts/{id}/forget`): `id` an id or a `client_key`, `confirm` the `id` echoed exactly (`REFUSED_CONFIRM`, before anything is read), `detach_citations`. It answers the evidence (`removed` by store, `irreproducible`, `pages`, `twins`, the hashed `source`, `outside`), or `NOT_FOUND`, `FORGOTTEN`, `REFUSED_CITED` (its way through the forget's) and `REPLAYING`. It writes no action-log row: that log is a store the forget clears.
- **A forgotten text** is refused `FORGOTTEN` on `capture_thought` and `update_thought` before the embedding call, so the provider never sees it again; the trigger's `OB005`, which reached the caller as a 500, is the same refusal. A capture-only key is not told when.
- **Preflight:** `forget definer` (a re-applied 139 resets the definer; the server's role lacks `EXECUTE`) and `forgotten` (the ticket's Work 3), each a warning: preflight gates the server's start, and the sweep, a second forget, runs through the server.

**Limits.**
- A forget called inside a replay runs as a live write: the replay's setting is pinned with the rest, so 139's `REPLAYING` now comes only from a brain before 144. Nothing that replays calls it. A re-applied 139 puts its body back under `forget_thought`, running as its caller; re-applying 144 restores the wrapper.
- `ob1_text_forgotten_at` reads the canonical fingerprint alone: a raw writer's stale key is refused by the trigger, after the embedding. Reinstating a forgotten text stays an operator's SQL setting (`ob1.reinstate_forgotten`); the GUI and the fan-out to other brains (PR 3) are not here.
- A capture-only key told `FORGOTTEN` learns the text was once in the brain, and can so confirm a guess: the trigger refused such a capture before 144 too. Whether it should be answered as a success is SMD-3934's. The `confirm` echo catches a malformed call, not a wrong choice of thought: a client copies the id. A forget leaves each emptied setting defined (`''`) for the rest of a pooled session; every reader takes `''` as unset.

**Held.**

Sixteen mutants of 144, each killed.

- **test-update-delete [12]:** the echo, `CITED` and its detach, the evidence, `FORGOTTEN` on capture and edit with the provider never asked, the trigger's `OB005` mapped, `FORGOTTEN` again and `NOT_FOUND`. **test-rest-sql:** REST and MCP give the same reply and the same refusals; **test-preflight:** both rows, ok and warned.

## PR 3: the session hook copies a forget

**What changed.** The write fan-out (SMD-3314) copies `forget_thought` (session hook 1.9.0): a forget through the connector's brain is repeated on each brain with a write key, by the id the first brain answered and `confirm` the same, always detaching citations there (the maintainer's choice: they copy the text), and again by the thought's own `client_key`, which the reply names, where a brain knows it by that alone. It drops that brain's queued and given-up copies of the thought. A copy forgotten there already is done; one the brain no longer knows is skipped, said so. A brain whose server is from before 144 holds the copy until it is upgraded; `--check` warns. On the server, a forget takes every thought the name is there: the id, standing or deleted, the thought keeping it as its key, and each deleted one whose tombstone kept it, the others listed (`alsoForgotten`). **Upgrading:** a fan-out hook pasted before 1.9.0 needs its matcher replaced.

**Held.** The hook's suite: the echo and forced detach, a deleted copy reached by its key, a queued capture dropped, a retry by the thought's own key, a repeat and a brain's own forget done, a refused forget not copied, an old brain held then drained, `--check`, the matcher; eight mutants, each killed. test-update-delete [12]: every thought a name is, the reply's key; three mutants killed.
