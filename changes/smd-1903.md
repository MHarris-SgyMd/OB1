## Changelog

An egress gate decides, before any model call, whether a thought's text may reach an endpoint not declared local: `OB1_EGRESS_POLICY` (deny by default, allow, off) with `OB1_EGRESS_ALLOW` / `OB1_EGRESS_DENY` terms over actor, source, type, topic and marker; `OB1_LLM_LOCAL` / `OB1_CHAT_LOCAL` declare an endpoint on the box. A refused capture lands without a vector and says why; the decision is recorded on the audit row; preflight reports the mode and what leaves per endpoint (SMD-1903). Every stack pointing at a local provider by address now sets `OB1_LLM_LOCAL=1`.

## FORK

Nothing decided which content may leave the box for a model call — a deterministic egress gate, deny by default, with "local" declared and never guessed, before any cloud tier (SMD-1903)

Every embedding and chat call sent a thought's full text to whatever
`OB1_LLM_BASE_URL` — and, since SMD-1902, `OB1_CHAT_BASE_URL` — named. With
a hosted provider that was every capture, every consolidation pair, every
entity pass, and the only policy was which URL the operator typed. For a
brain holding protected health information that is a compliance boundary, and
the routing ladder (SMD-1898) makes the gap concrete: a difficulty router says
"this item needs a stronger model"; nothing said "this item may not leave
regardless". Where both fire, a local-large tier is the only legal escalation
— which is the actual reason one is needed (SMD-1880), not a nicety.

The ordering rule, decided before the build and held: **the gate is
deterministic first.** It reads what is already known about the text — the
access key sending it, the row's own `source`, `type` and `topics`, a literal
marker in the text — against terms the operator wrote down. A classifier may
attach as a second opinion and can only make the answer stricter: a detector
that is right 98% of the time is a compliance failure two times in a hundred,
so it never gets to say yes. `mayLeaveBox` consults the hook only on an answer
that is already "allowed", a refused subject is never put to it, and a hook
that throws refuses. The Jev-class detector SMD-1897 spikes would attach
there, at most.

`server-portable/egress.ts` is the one rule: `mayLeaveBox(subject, endpoint,
policy, secondOpinion?) → { allowed, rule, reason }`, pure, over a subject
(the call's kind, the actor, the row's metadata, the text), an endpoint's
`base` and `local`, and a policy resolved from `OB1_EGRESS_POLICY` —
`deny` (the default: nothing reaches an endpoint not declared local unless an
`OB1_EGRESS_ALLOW` term matches), `allow` (everything does unless an
`OB1_EGRESS_DENY` term matches), `off`. Terms are comma-separated
`unit:value` over `actor`, `source`, `type`, `topic` and `marker`, matched
exactly and case-insensitively, the marker as a substring of the text. A knob
that does not parse fails CLOSED — the effective mode is deny whatever the
knob said, and preflight fails the row naming the entry — because a policy
that could not be read is not a policy that allows.

"Local" is **declared, not guessed**: `OB1_LLM_LOCAL=1` says the embeddings
endpoint is on this machine or its private network, `OB1_CHAT_LOCAL=1` says the
same of a chat endpoint of its own, and a chat endpoint at the same base is
the same box — either knob declares it, for both calls. A loopback address, the container-to-host
alias and the compose service name are all remote to the gate until the flag
says otherwise. Preflight already guesses "local" from the hostname to decide
whether a credential is needed (db/config.mjs `isLocalHostname`), and that
guess stays: an unneeded key is a warning. It is the wrong basis for "may this
text leave" — a hostname is whatever DNS says it is today — and the ticket's
own test is that a loopback base with the flag unset is treated as remote.
`ProviderEndpoint` carries `local`; `resolveProviderEndpoints` sets it; nothing
reads it off the address.

Every dialler asks before the request. `providerCall` takes the subject as a
fourth argument and throws a `ProviderError` of kind `egress` — no status,
nothing about the provider — before the request exists; `judgePair` refuses a
pair when EITHER row may not leave (the more restricted row decides for both,
which is why `PairSide` gained the row's `metadata`); `extractEntities` refuses
on the row's. The embedder threads the subject through every window and blurb,
and the whole-content fallback rethrows an egress refusal rather than letting
the head window stand in for text the windows are refused on the same rule.
So no call the fork makes is ungated, and TypeScript holds it: a dialler
without a subject does not compile.

The server asks first and skips the call, so a refusal costs no request and
the reply says why. `capture_thought` runs `decideCalls` once for both
endpoints and makes only the allowed calls: a refused embedding lands the
capture with its text and fingerprint and NO vector — `captureThought` takes
`embedding: number[] | null`, the only path that stores a row vectorless on
purpose — and the reply leads with "saved WITHOUT a vector", the rule, the
host, and the two ways the row is still reachable (exact text through
`search_thoughts_keyword`; a later `db/reembed.ts` pass against an endpoint the
gate allows). A refused tagging call lands the row with
`metadata_extraction_failed: egress_denied` and nothing else — no `type`, no
`topics`, not even the `uncategorized` placeholder: the call never happened, so
it produced none, and any of them would have been a tag set dressed as an
extraction — the ticket's "never a fabricated tag set". A refused search names
the keyword tool and the remedy for the rule that refused. An edit with new
text is judged on the row's own metadata (one read, so a `type:` or `source:`
term reaches it), stores it vectorless when refused and says so; a
metadata-only edit sends nothing and is not judged. A re-capture of text that
is already a thought keeps that thought's tags and vector — `upsert_thought`
merges and coalesces — and the reply says so rather than "no vector".

The decision is recorded as provenance on the write's audit row:
`thought_audit.actor_context.egress` — `{ policy, embeddings: { to, allowed,
rule }, chat: { … } }`, one entry per endpoint NOT declared local, allowed or
refused, nothing at all when both are (nothing was judged). The trigger keeps
every actor key it does not read by name, so this needed no migration; it
rides the actor envelope both stores already send, and it is where SMD-1729's
program will find "which rule fired, which provider was dialled or refused"
when it shapes the event. Declined: the thought's own `metadata` as the
record. That is the row's current state, merged by edits; the decision is an
event about one write, and the audit row is the fork's event log.

The three passes carry the same subject from the row: `reembed.ts` reads
`metadata` beside the text and a refused row is a failed claim naming the
rule, its text never sent, revisited by `--retry-failed` once the policy or the
endpoint changes; `consolidate.ts` records a refused pair on the claim as it
records a timeout and judges the next; `extract-entities.ts` fails the row.
Each banner prints an `egress:` line before claiming anything. The evals that
dial through the server's resolver pass a subject too.

`preflight` reports it in words before the first capture: an `egress policy`
row (the mode, "the default" when unset, the terms; `off` a warning, a knob
that does not parse a failure with the units) and an `embeddings egress` row —
a `chat egress` row beside it when chat has an endpoint of its own — saying
"declared local, the gate does not apply", or "looks local but is not declared
so — the gate treats it as remote" with the one-line fix (the upgrade case:
every stack from before the gate that points at Ollama by address), or under
deny with no terms "every call is refused" with the consequence and the three
ways out, or under terms what leaves and under what, or under `allow`/`off`
that the full text leaves to the host. `deploy/compose.yaml` forwards the five
knobs with no fallback, since the compose file must not declare local on the
operator's behalf; `deploy/.env.example` has the block; `SETUP.md` gains "What
may leave the box" and puts `OB1_LLM_LOCAL=1` in both local blocks, with the
paragraph the ticket asked for independent of the build: with PHI in the brain,
keep every endpoint on the box.

Deny by default is the decision the ticket left to stakeholders, made here and
stated: it is the shape SMD-1813 already chose for the connector path
("default-nothing"), it is the one that fails safe on the upgrade — a stack
that upgrades without reading the notes loses no text, captures land vectorless
and say so, preflight names the line — and `allow` and `off` remain one word
away for an operator who chooses them in words. The unit of decision is the
term vocabulary above, shared with SMD-1813 where the units coincide (actor,
type, topic); the operator override is the policy knob itself. Declined:
routing a refused call to a local tier instead (Work step 3's last clause) —
the fork has two endpoints, not a tier list; when SMD-1880's runtime stands
beside a hosted judge the gate already knows which endpoint is local, and the
route is SMD-1897's. Declined: a fallback in compose for `OB1_LLM_LOCAL` under
the `local-models` profile, which would have declared the stack's Ollama local
by default — right for that profile and a false declaration for anyone who
sets a hosted URL and forgets the flag. The profile now needs one line.

`test-egress.ts` holds the function ([1] parsing and fail-closed, [2] declared
not guessed over every guessable address, [3] every unit under both modes with
the reason naming knob and term, [4] the second opinion refusing and never
consulted on a refusal), the three diallers against a counting stub ([5]: kind
`egress`, zero requests; a pair with one refused side; declared or allowed,
every request), and the real server under the default with the stub NOT
declared local and one key allowed by name ([6]: the refused capture's reply,
row, metadata and audit record at zero requests; the refused search; the
keyword tool finding the row; the vectorless edit; the allowed key's capture
reaching the stub with the decision recorded as allowed). `test-preflight.ts`
[8] holds every row shape. Every other suite that boots the server against a
stub declares it local, which is the upgrade every stack makes. Mutants: drop
the gate from `providerCall` and [5] fails; consult the hook on a refusal and
[4] fails; read `local` off the hostname and [2] fails.

**First review pass** (one of my own reading, one an independent reviewer over
the commit). Found and fixed: `db/reembed.ts`'s provider probe called
`getEmbedding` without a subject — `db/` is not type-checked in CI (SMD-1932
files the step), so it compiled nowhere and crashed under any policy that read
the subject, blaming the provider; the probe is a gated constant now, skipped and said where the
row terms would refuse it. The refusal shape carried the `uncategorized`
placeholder as a topic, and `upsert_thought` merges a re-capture's metadata
over the row's, so every re-capture under a refusing policy would have
replaced a thought's real tags with the placeholder — the shape is the marker
alone now, the reply on a re-capture says the tags and vector stand, and the
suite re-captures a tagged row. An edit was judged on the capture's bare
`{source: "mcp"}` rather than the row's metadata, so a `type:` or `source:`
term never reached it; one read. The three passes would have marked a whole
pool failed one row at a time under a policy that refuses every row; they stop
before claiming, with the fix, while dry runs and status report. The search
refusal suggested an allow term whatever rule refused; the chunk-context note
sent the caller to check a model the gate never dialled; `OB1_CHAT_LOCAL` alone
was discarded on a shared endpoint; `localKnob` named the wrong knob for a
same-base chat endpoint declared by its own; preflight's "looks local" warning
omitted the consequence under deny with terms and fired unqualified under
`off`; terms in the knob the mode does not read were parsed and silently
ignored (warned now); `eval-graphrag.ts`'s community-summary call and the
chunking eval's child server were ungated and undeclared. Documented as
outside the gate: `evals/lib.ts`'s own dialler (`OB1_EVAL_BASE`, eval
corpora) and preflight's `--deep` probes (constants, no thought text).
