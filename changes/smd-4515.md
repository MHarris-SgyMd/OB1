## Changelog

A test-live row in the sleep shard that failed some runs now takes either line consolidation can join on at the end of extraction, and sets the order of extraction that makes that safe (SMD-4515). No shipped code changes.

## Record

test-live [38]'s "in the fixed order" row takes either join line, where a poll between a thought's entities and its claim's finish decided which was printed (SMD-4515)

**What changed.** In `db/test-live.ts`, section [38]'s one-period case:

- The row takes either line consolidation joins on: "extraction drained — consolidating beside it", or the inflow rule's "extraction has done the thoughts captured before the period began …".
- At the period's first extraction call, the two older notes' claims still to be taken are dated after today's note's, so a backlog note is the last extracted. The row holds that the dating moved a claim.
- The row's message names the line consolidation joined on and how many claims were dated.

No file a release ships changes, and `db/sleep.ts` is as it was.

**Why.** The row failed three times: twice in about 11 runs of `ci-parity`, and once in CI. Each time the first judge call came 1.5 s after the last extraction began (1541, 1533, 1540 ms): the timing half passed, and the line it asks for was not the one printed.

Measured on this branch:

- A thought's entities are written a moment before its claim is marked finished. Under the inflow rule (SMD-4468) a thought with entities is extracted whatever its claim says.
- A poll in that moment on the last thought reads no backlog left and a pool not drained. The scheduler then joins on the inflow rule's line; a poll a moment later reads the pool drained and joins on the other. Extraction is over either way.
- With each claim's finish held a second, the row failed the one run made, on that line, at 1677 ms. It was the only row of the shard to fail. With the row taking either line, four more runs with the finish held all joined on that line, and passed.

So the scheduler did what the inflow rule decided, and the row asked for one of two lines a few milliseconds apart.

**The order.** Taking either line is safe only while a backlog note is the last extracted. The period's first pool takes the three notes in one statement, so their claims' order is a tie, and today's note is no backlog. In every run seen the order was older note, today's note, deploy note. With today's note put last by hand, consolidation joined as the older two ended and judged 25 ms into its extraction, by design ([38c] tests that). The row's dating settles the tie, so the early line here can only mean the end of extraction.

**Held.** The row asks what it asked of the order: no judge call before the last extraction, a join after "working:", and the period's end. The fixture's notes, dates and pooling are untouched.

**Measured after.** The `sleep` shard four times at once: 4 of 4 pass. Two held each claim's finish a second and joined on the inflow rule's line; two ran plain and joined on "extraction drained". Before the row took either line, six runs at once passed with the dating alone, two of them from the order put wrong by hand.

The tie had never been seen to fall the other way; the 1.5 s in every failure was the sign, and a run put wrong by hand read 25 ms. | run-it | The cause measured as above, and the row takes either line. |
| 3 | Two counts in the record, the PR and the row's comment were wrong: the failures as "3 of about 12 under `ci-parity` and once in CI", which reads as four, and the held finish as failing "every run", of one. | cold-read | Three failures, two in `ci-parity` and one in CI; one failing run, and four that took the line. |

Three mutants, one shard run each, on the dating:

| Mutant | This row | The shard |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `db/sleep.ts` joins consolidation at the first poll, drained or not | passes: the first judge call still came 2056 ms after the last extraction began | 11 rows fail, the first the row before this one, with no proposal |
| The row's dating moves no claim | fails, "0 older note(s) dated" | that row alone |
| The row sets no hook | fails | that row alone |

So the row's timing clause does not catch a scheduler that joins early: today's note is then judged before the older is extracted, finds no candidate and makes no model call, and the loss shows in the row before it. The ticket's second Verify item holds for the pair of rows, not for this one.

**Not taken.**

- A scheduler that reads a thought with entities and a claim still in hand as drained. The line it prints in that moment says it extracts "the writes since" when none is left. That is `db/sleep.ts`, which another ticket's branch is changing now, and the row's meaning does not need it.
- Pooling the notes before the period, which makes today's note backlog. It would take the period's first pool out of the case.
- A stronger timing clause for this row. What an early join costs is the row before's to hold, and it does.

**Follow-ups.** Five other rows wait on the "extraction drained" line alone (one more in [38], three in [38b], one in [38s]). As read, not run: each can meet the same moment, a poll while a note that is no backlog has its claim in hand. None has been seen to fail.
