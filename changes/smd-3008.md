## Changelog

A capture made during a sleep — a sync write, which does not wake the brain — is no longer stuck behind a thought whose window outlasts every sleep: the sleep scheduler dates the stopped thoughts ahead of the sleep's own captures, and restarts extraction when one lands while such a thought is in hand, so the capture is extracted and consolidation joins past the stalled thought in the same sleep. While consolidation waits for a judge that does not answer, extraction takes the newer work instead of nothing, the stalled thought still waiting for consolidation (SMD-3008).

## FORK

A capture made during a sleep goes ahead of the stalled thought — dated after it, it waited out the window in hand, and with one worker held the join back for the sleep — and a judge that does not answer no longer leaves extraction idle (SMD-3008)

**What changed** (PR 1).
- **`db/sleep.ts`, the stopped thoughts dated forward:** as each sleep begins they are dated 30 days ahead (`FORWARD_DAYS`), not now, still the least stalled first. A capture made during the sleep is enqueued at its own `now()` by 016's trigger, so it goes ahead of them. `claim_thoughts` orders by `enqueued_at` and reads no clock, so the date is an order, not a delay. A wake's tail move dates the thought(s) in hand now; the next sleep dates every stopped thought forward again.
- **A restart for work behind a stalled thought in hand:**
  - Before consolidation joins, two polls in a row that read extraction with only stalled thoughts in flight and newer work behind them (a row pending that is not stalled, or a thought not yet in the pool) stop extraction hard. The stalled thought(s) are moved behind that work and extraction starts again.
  - With the forward date this is the backstop: for a capture that lands between the stalled thought's claim and the next poll, a thought not yet in the pool, or a stalled thought reclaimed from a lapsed lease with an old date.
  - At most once a minute (`RESTART_EVERY_MS`), and none once a move has failed, the sleep's first included, since such a thought would be claimed first again; none either where 093 cannot be read.
  - The stop counts on the thought as any hard stop does: a stall only past the floor.
  - The pool read gains `stalledPending`. The join's set-aside and the restart share one helper.
- **Known limits:**
  - Once consolidation has joined, a stalled thought in hand keeps its turn, and a capture made then waits for the next sleep, where it goes first.
  - A thought still gaining windows, or a stall or two short of the join, is not stopped for a capture, which waits for its window.

**What changed** (PR 2).
- **`db/sleep.ts`, extraction let out while the judge waits:**
  - Set aside past a stalled thought, extraction got nothing until consolidation's pool drained, which it cannot while consolidation waits for its provider.
  - While consolidation waits, extraction is let out for the newer work (a pending row that is not stalled) at most once a minute (the restart's bound), with one worker, so a stalled thought is not claimed beside the newer work. It is set aside again once it holds a stalled thought, or holds nothing and nothing newer is left. Its claims are read by this process's worker ids, claimed since this run began, so a lease another process left does not re-park it every minute.
  - It is let out only where the stalled thoughts can be told (093 readable) and moved behind the newer work. A move that fails ends the let-outs and PR 1's restarts for the sleep, by choice: a role that cannot move the rows would otherwise cut the stalled thought every minute for nothing. A stalled read that fails while extraction is out sets it aside.
  - The move covers a lapsed lease as well as the pending rows. A scheduler that has gone left the lease claimed, and the next claim reaps it with its old date, ahead of the sleep's captures. PR 1's restart reads this process's claims too.
  - The stalled thoughts still wait for consolidation, so the judge, on the same chat endpoint, does not queue behind their window when it answers. Claimed after the newer work by its place, a stalled thought is set aside at the next poll, its call cut within a poll.
  - If the judge answers and consolidation drains while extraction is out, extraction stays out under `--workers 1`, as after any drain. With more workers it is set aside once it holds nothing, so the drain's resume starts every worker, where the one let out ran for the rest of the sleep.
  - The wait read is either the start's (`startWait`) or a mid-pass one. The poll reads the claims only, skipping the scan for thoughts not yet in the pool.
- **`db/pass-stamp.ts` and `db/consolidate.ts`:** `PassStamper` gains `providerWait`, a pass's word that it waits mid-pass for a provider that stopped answering: after its workers' pauses for a 5xx, at once for a missing model or a call that timed out with no answer to a probe. consolidate.ts says it around `waitForProvider`, and the scheduler keeps it off the row, since the mid-pass `waiting` word is SMD-2905's.
- **The cases this covers:** the judge model failing while the extraction model serves (both are on the one chat endpoint): a judge that stops answering, or answers 5xx (a larger judge that will not load, a gateway routing by model to a backend that is down), or whose model goes missing mid-pass.
- **A judge answering 5xx** mid-pass is waited for only past the pauses (5, 15 and 45 s), a minute and more. Mid-pass a missing model is waited for at once, as a judge not answering at a sleep's start is; a model missing at the start is refused, as before (SMD-2752's start refusals).
- **Known limits:**
  - Each let-out that reaches a stalled thought cuts it, so through a long outage with captures arriving its `stops` climbs, about once a minute; a short stop, so `stalled` does not.
  - A sleep that joined past at its first pool, its extraction never started, can now meet extraction's start refusal at a let-out, as any sleep's start can.
  - A stale let-out after a drain (the judge answering while extraction is out) is ended only at the next poll, and a stalled thought claimed in between is cut once more.

**Why.**
- SMD-2694 moved the stopped thoughts behind new captures at each sleep's start, but dated them then. A capture made later in the sleep queued behind them, and nothing wakes the brain for a sync write.
- With one worker the pool then read {the stalled thought in hand, the capture pending}: not all stalled, so consolidation did not join past it either, and both waited out the window or the wake.
- Measured on the stable brain, read-only, over the 14 days to 2026-10-15: of 149 sleeps reconstructed from its logs at a 300 s quiet (the scheduler's own rule for what wakes it; whether it ran then was not read), 99 held an ingested capture or text edit, read from `thought_audit`'s diff rather than the trigger, about 4.8 such writes each. The output was not kept.

**What changed** (PR 3, after a post-merge review and a claims audit).
- **This process's claims are those made since this run began.** A container restarted in place keeps its hostname and pid, so its last run's lease, live for its TTL, matched this run's worker ids. For up to 15 minutes after such a restart, a let-out could be re-parked at once every minute, cutting the capture in hand. The pool reads `claimed_at` against the database's clock at the scheduler's start.
- **A forward date no longer outlives its stop record.** A thought finished since, its kept row deleted, and put back by a `--retry-*` flag or `retry_failed`, kept its forward date and went behind every capture for up to 30 days. Each sleep's move first dates now every forward-dated pending row that is no longer a stopped thought.
- **Docs and this record:** the audit's corrections. They cover the tail move, a missing model (refused at the start, waited out mid-pass), when the mid-pass wait begins, the restart's bound, the measurement's method, and the mutant lists.
