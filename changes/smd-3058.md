## Changelog

The decider-4b adapter (`jev/decider/serve.py`) reads a context's whole 32,768-token window. Before, it read only the first 1,536 tokens and reported nothing cut. And `evals/eval-judge-decide.ts` records why the consolidation judge keeps judging every pair itself (SMD-3058).

## FORK

The consolidation judge keeps every pair — decider-4b in front of it costs more than the 7B here and drops relations both judges see; its adapter now reads past 1,536 tokens (SMD-3058)

**What changed.**
- **`evals/eval-judge-decide.ts`** (new) asks decider-4b three questions of every pair in `eval-judge.ts`'s export, read as prompt 4 renders the two thoughts:
  - S, a binary "same subject?";
  - T, a choice among related, evolves, duplicate and outdates;
  - C, a choice among all five verdicts.
- **Scoring.** It scores a cascade in which the decider settles a pair only when it is confident the verdict is not `outdates`, and the saved answers of an LLM judge (`eval-judge.ts --out`) stand in for every other pair.
  - Bands are picked from the data, then checked on held-out halves.
  - It reports supersessions lost, accuracy on what is settled, agreement with the judge, relations dropped and model time.
- **`jev/decider/serve.py` passes its 32,768-token window to every `decide()` call,** and reports the real token count and whether the context was cut. `jev/decider/README.md` says so.

**Why.** Every candidate pair costs the judge one LLM call with both thoughts' text. Beside a live call, consolidation is the costliest pass (SMD-3024: a capture took 3.4 times as long), and nearly every pair ends in a verdict that proposes nothing. The question was whether the typed-decision tier could settle those pairs cheaply.

**Measured.** On 444 pairs exported from stable (62 true supersessions, 120 candidates), each judge one pair at a time on the dogfood Mac:
- **Behind the 7B, every cascade costs more model time than the 7B alone,** 4.4–4.7 s a pair against 3.6 s. A decision costs a median 2.2 s on a 2,200-token pair (PyTorch over the GPU), 60% of a judge call.
- **Behind the 27B, the two-pass band saves 28%** (9.4 s against 13.0) and loses none of its 23 supersessions; one held-out half lost one.
- **Both ways, it changes what the pass writes.**
  - Its confident verdicts are mostly "unrelated". On the candidates it settles it gives the 7B's verdict 71–81% of the time and the 27B's 38–49%, dropping 14–59 relations per 120 candidates.
  - The labels side with the judges. On linked ticket pairs the 27B is right on 124 of 135, while the decider calls 27 of the 39 relates_to pairs unrelated.
- **Declined.** `evals/README.md` has the tables, and what would reopen it: a faster runtime, and a "same subject" read as the judge reads it.

**Held.**
- `bun eval-judge-decide.ts --self-check`, run in fork-checks.yml ("Judge cascade rules") and `db/ci-parity.sh`, covers:
  - the settle rules: `outdates` never settled, an abstention or the middle band sent on, the two-pass time paid only past the band;
  - the pair context, cut from prompt 4's own rendering even when a thought quotes its rules;
  - the scoring: a settled supersession is lost, and a settled candidate is compared with the judge's answer.
- The adapter fix was checked by hand: a 2,730-token context whose one decisive sentence came last answered "insufficient evidence" (p 0.985) before the fix, and the right answer (p 0.997, 2,731 tokens read) after. 354 of the 444 pairs (80%) are longer than 1,536 tokens.

**Not taken.**
- **Building `consolidate.ts --decide`.** The spike declined it.
- **A clipped context to make the decider cheap.** Unmeasured, and the decider's errors are in how it reads "same subject", not in what it is shown.

**Follow-ups.** SMD-2265 (deploying decider-4b) takes the adapter fix as given, and a runtime faster than PyTorch on the GPU is its question.
