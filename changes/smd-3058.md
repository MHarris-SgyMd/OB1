## Changelog

The decider-4b adapter (`jev/decider/serve.py`) reads a context's whole 32,768-token window. Before, it read only the first 1,536 tokens and reported nothing cut. With `DECIDER_PACK=1` it answers several decisions over one context in a single call. `jev/h2o/serve.py` serves H2O-Lightning-4B behind the same contract. And `evals/eval-judge-decide.ts` records why the consolidation judge keeps judging every pair itself, with either model in front of it (SMD-3058).

## FORK

The consolidation judge keeps every pair — decider-4b in front of it is right 18–53 points less often than the judge it replaces, and H2O-Lightning-4B, nearly as right as the judge, saves no time behind the 7B; decider's adapter now reads past 1,536 tokens (SMD-3058)

**What changed.**
- **`evals/eval-judge-decide.ts`** (new) asks a typed-decision model three questions of every pair in `eval-judge.ts`'s export, read as prompt 4 renders the two thoughts:
  - S, a binary "same subject?";
  - T, a choice among related, evolves, duplicate and outdates;
  - C, a choice among all five verdicts.
- **Two ways to ask:** `--packed` asks S and T in one request, and `--clip N` cuts each thought to its first N characters.
- **Scoring.** It scores a cascade in which the decider settles a pair only when it is confident the verdict is not `outdates`, and an LLM judge's saved answers (`eval-judge.ts --out`) stand in for every other pair.
  - Bands are picked from the data, then checked on held-out halves.
  - It reports supersessions lost, model time, agreement with the judge and relations dropped.
  - On the labelled pairs it settles, it reports how often the decider is right beside how often the judge it replaces was.
  - **The reopen rule**, fixed before H2O's run: a band at least as accurate as the judge on the labelled pairs it settles (20 or more), losing no supersession, and cheaper on the candidates than the judge alone. It is swept over 6,096 bands, some settling only some verdicts, with the widest passing band checked on held-out halves.
  - **Beyond the cascade**, as exploration: the decider's top verdict as the judge itself, and P(outdates) as a supersession flag, with each judge reading only the pairs it raises.
- **`jev/decider/serve.py`** passes its 32,768-token window to every `decide()` call, and reports the real token count and whether the context was cut. With `DECIDER_PACK=1` it answers the decisions of one request that share a context in one call. `jev/decider/README.md` says both.
- **`jev/h2o/serve.py`** (new) serves H2O-Lightning-4B v1.2.3 (Apache-2.0) behind `ob1-jev/1` on PyTorch MPS. It is a port of the text path of H2O's shim, which needs vLLM. It reads the same prompt and the label logits at the answer slot, at the shipped temperatures, without the yes/no floor. A shared context is read once per request, and a start-up check holds that to whole reads. `jev/h2o/conformance.py` checks the port against H2O's own published log-probabilities, and `jev/h2o/README.md` gives the mapping.

**Why.** Every candidate pair costs the judge one LLM call with both thoughts' text. Beside a live call, consolidation is the costliest pass (SMD-3024: a capture took 3.4 times as long), and nearly every pair ends in a verdict that proposes nothing. The question was whether the typed-decision tier could settle those pairs cheaply.

**Measured.** On 444 pairs exported from stable (62 true supersessions, 120 candidates), on the dogfood Mac:
- **The judges.** The 7B catches 2 of 62 at 3.6 s a pair, the 27B 23 at 13.0 s. They give the same verdict on 50% of the candidates.
- **Reading the pair is the cost,** about 1 ms a token for decider-4b and the 7B alike (Ollama's timing: the 7B reads judge prompts at 0.84 ms a token). At full length, a decision costs what the 7B spends reading the same pair, so behind the 7B the cascade costs 4.7 s a pair against 3.6 s.
- **Packing** S and T took 1.97 s against 4.38 s. **Clipping** to 1,500, 600 or 300 characters brings a decision to 0.8, 0.5 or 0.4 s, and the two-pass cascade to 2.7, 2.2 or 1.9 s behind the 7B, and 8.4, 7.5 or 7.1 s behind the 27B.
- **For decider-4b, accuracy is the binding problem.** On every variant, on the labelled pairs it settles, it is right 18–30 points less often than the 7B it replaces, and 35–53 less often than the 27B. Clipping widens the gap. Its errors are "unrelated" for pairs the board links (27 of 39 relates_to at full length) and "duplicate" for true supersessions. No variant meets the reopen rule.
- **H2O-Lightning-4B, for cost.** Its port matches H2O's own top answers on all 302 sampled questions, with a median probability gap of 0.0009. It reads at 0.69 ms a token in float16 with decider's MPS kernels.
  - **Accuracy comes close.** On the 170 labelled pairs its loosest one-pass band settles (60% of the candidates), it is right on 140, against the 7B's 143 and the 27B's 156.
  - **The rule fails behind the 7B.** 67 bands are as accurate as the 7B, and none saves time: a read costs 1.41 s a candidate against 3.76, and the accurate bands settle at most 18%.
  - **The rule passes behind the 27B, by 7%.** The widest band settles only evolves, related and duplicate; it costs 10.56 s a candidate against 11.30, and it holds on both held-out halves.
- **Declined.** Behind the 7B, which both dogfood brains run, neither model saves time at the judge's accuracy. `evals/README.md` has the tables.
- **H2O as the judge itself** (exploration after the run). It is right on 259 of 324 labelled pairs (the 7B 239, the 27B 263). It proposes 52 of the 62 true supersessions (2 and 23), with 32 false proposals (3 and 17), at 1.41 s a candidate. On pointer pairs whose newer thought names no supersession, it still answers outdates on 36 of 38.

**Held.**
- `bun eval-judge-decide.ts --self-check`, run in fork-checks.yml ("Judge cascade rules") and `db/ci-parity.sh`, covers:
  - the settle rules: `outdates` never settled, whatever the band; an abstention or the middle band sent on; a band settling only some verdicts;
  - the time each framing pays, packed or not, and on the candidates;
  - the pair context, cut from prompt 4's own rendering even when a thought quotes its rules, and `--clip`;
  - the scoring: a settled supersession is lost, and the judge's agreement, relations dropped and added, and the judge's own accuracy on the settled pairs are counted;
  - the reopen rule, each of its four conditions at its edge (a tie with the judge meets it, the judge's own cost misses it), and its sweep;
  - the decider as a judge, the flag at exactly its threshold, and the judge reading only what the flag raises.
- **Mutants:** twenty-eight, each undoing one of those, all fail it.
- **The adapter fix,** checked by hand: a 2,730-token context whose one decisive sentence came last answered "insufficient evidence" (p 0.985) before the fix, and the right answer (p 0.997, 2,731 tokens read) after. 354 of the 444 pairs (80%) are longer than 1,536 tokens.
- **Decider's adapter is deterministic:** C, asked alone in two runs, gave the same answers on all 444 pairs.
- **H2O's port:** `jev/h2o/conformance.py` against H2O's routing set, and the start-up check that a shared read gives the answers of whole reads.

**Not taken.**
- **Building `consolidate.ts --decide`.** The spike declined it for both models.
- **Packing all three questions.** C, packed after T, kept its answer on 5 of 10 trial pairs, so it is always asked alone.
- **Packing on by default.** It moves the second question's answers (T kept its answer on 63% of pairs), so it is opt-in.
- **Running H2O's own shim.** It needs vLLM, which does not run on the Mac; the port reads its prompt and checks against its answers instead.

**Follow-ups.** SMD-2265 (deploying decider-4b) takes the adapter fix and `DECIDER_PACK` as given. Its runtime question no longer decides this one. H2O's supersession recall, through a pass that writes the proposal for its call, is proposed on SMD-3058 and not yet filed.
