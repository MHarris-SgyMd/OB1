## Record

A listen stream whose client or key is gone: measured, and decided (SMD-4493)

**What changed.**

- `docs/mcp-listen-streams.md` has the measurements and the decisions, "A stream whose client or key is gone", and the paragraph that said of a stream that outlives its credential "This is accepted" points to it; and how each of the two decisions still to build is to be built, as the maintainer agreed it the same day.
- `deploy/README.md` says what a vanished and a frozen client leave in the log and at the bounds, with the numbers, and that nothing in the path hid a close or a kill.
- Nothing the stack runs.

**Why.** A listen stream keeps its place under the server's bound of 256 and a caller's of 32 until its connection ends. SMD-4458's third review found a frozen client holding its places for 680 s and a revoked key its 32, and SMD-4459's a relay that hid a client's leaving, its caller at its bound in 22 s. The maintainer set this ticket to come before SMD-4347 turns streams on, and decided on 2026-11-23, with the measurements below: a vanished client and a frozen one are accepted; a revoked caller's streams are ended, the MCP server asking the REST core every 60 seconds by each caller's digest, and holding no caller's credential to ask with; an operator's action, an operation of the REST core for the operator's key, ends one caller's streams. The last two are this ticket's next two PRs, and SMD-4347's PR 2b waits on all three.

**Measured after.** On a scratch stack on one Mac (a podman machine), its MCP server started holding streams by the suite's preload, with a probe client, each case once (the setting taken back, twice), by scripts outside the tree. Times are from what the client did to the server's line for the stream's end.

| What the client did | From the Mac, the published port | A container at the forwarder | A container at the MCP server | Through a quick tunnel |
| -- | -- | -- | -- | -- |
| Closed its stream | within 0.2 s | 7 ms | 1 ms | 40 ms |
| Was killed | within 0.2 s | within 0.2 s | within 0.2 s | within 0.2 s |

The 13 min 28 s is the Mac's VM's own network proxy giving up on a container inside it, not the tunnel: a tunnel vendor's edge with a client that vanishes was not measured. The quick tunnel answered four probe streams and carried one comment or two of each, 15 seconds apart, in the 45 s at most each was read, where its vendor's page says it does not support event streams.

**Not taken.**

- A shorter timeout on the forwarder: built, measured (1 min 55 s and 1 min 56 s for a container on its network), and taken back, above.
- A frozen client's stream is not ended by the server: a listen stream carries nothing from its client by which the server could tell it from a live one that is idle. Nor is a vanished client's: what faces it is seldom the stack, and gives up in its own time.
- Ending every stream at a set age, and closing a caller's oldest stream at its bound: `docs/mcp-listen-streams.md` says why not.
- A client that vanishes cannot be made on one machine through the published port, and none was run from another machine behind a tunnel: that is on the page's list for SMD-4347's PR 2b, with the real client and an hour's hold.
