## Record

A listen stream whose client or key is gone: measured, and decided (SMD-4493)

**What changed.**

- `docs/mcp-listen-streams.md` has the measurements and the decisions, "A stream whose client or key is gone", where it said of a stream that outlives its credential "This is accepted"; and what each of the two decisions still to build must settle.
- `deploy/README.md` says what a vanished and a frozen client leave in the log and at the bounds, with the numbers, and that nothing in the path hid a close or a kill.
- Nothing the stack runs.

**Why.** A listen stream keeps its place under the server's bound of 256 and a caller's of 32 until its connection ends. SMD-4458's third review found a frozen client holding its places for 680 s and a revoked key its 32, and SMD-4459's a relay that hid a client's leaving, its caller at its bound in 22 s. The maintainer set this ticket to come before SMD-4347 turns streams on, and decided on 2026-11-23, with the measurements below: a vanished client and a frozen one are accepted; a revoked caller's streams are ended by asking again; an operator's action ends one caller's streams. The last two are this ticket's next two PRs.

**Measured after.** On a scratch stack on one Mac (a podman machine), its MCP server started holding streams by the suite's preload, with a probe client, each case once, by scripts outside the tree. Times are from what the client did to the server's line for the stream's end.

| What the client did | From the Mac, the published port | A container at the forwarder | A container at the MCP server | Through a quick tunnel |
| -- | -- | -- | -- | -- |
| Closed its stream | within 0.2 s | 7 ms | 1 ms | 40 ms |
| Was killed | within 0.2 s | within 0.2 s | within 0.2 s | within 0.2 s |
| Was frozen | held for a minute | held after 21 min, read again when thawed | the same | not run |
| Vanished: its network taken away | cannot, on one machine | 16 min 0 s | 16 min 4 s | 13 min 28 s, the VM's proxy |

The 13 min 28 s is the Mac's VM's own network proxy giving up on a container inside it, not the tunnel: a tunnel vendor's edge with a client that vanishes was not measured. The quick tunnel answered four probe streams and carried their comment every 15 seconds for the 40 s at most each was read, where its vendor's page says it does not support event streams.

**Review passes.** One, of two independent readers, each with a copy of the commit: one on every number against the raw logs and every claim against the kernel's, the engines' and the tunnel vendor's own pages; one as the operator taking the upgrade, behind a proxy or a tunnel, and as the maintainer building on the decisions. Every number stood against its log; what two of them were numbers of did not.

| pass | finding | caught | fix |
| -- | -- | -- | -- |
| 1 | the build set the forwarder's kernel to give up on a silent peer after 8 retransmissions, and measured it from a container on the forwarder's own network: no client the documents describe is faced by that kernel, a tunnel, a TLS proxy or the engine's port relay facing each; the maintainer took the setting back, and a vanished client is accepted | cold read | pass 1's commit |
| 1 | the 13 min 28 s said of a quick tunnel was the Mac's VM's network proxy giving up on the client's container (15 retransmissions, 804.6 s by its source; 805.0 s in the log from the first frame not acknowledged); said so, and a tunnel's own time left as not measured | cold read | pass 1's commit |
| 1 | no case had used the stack's published port, the one path a local client takes, and the pages did not say where the clients were; a close, a kill and a minute frozen were run through it from the Mac, and each column says its path | cold read | pass 1's commit |
| 1 | the two decisions still to build were given with nothing of what their builds must settle (what the server keeps to ask with, a token caller, how one caller's streams are ended and the rest left); listed under each | walkthrough | pass 1's commit |

**Not taken.**

- A shorter timeout on the forwarder: built, measured (1 min 55 s for a container on its network), and taken back, above.
- A frozen or a vanished client's stream is not ended by the server: a listen stream carries nothing from its client by which the server could tell either from a live one that is idle.
- Ending every stream at a set age, and closing a caller's oldest stream at its bound: `docs/mcp-listen-streams.md` says why not.
- A client that vanishes could not be made on one machine through the published port, nor behind a tunnel from another: both are on the page's list for SMD-4347's PR 2b, with the real client and an hour's hold.
