## Record

A listen stream whose client or key is gone: measured, and decided (SMD-4493)

**What changed.**

- `docs/mcp-listen-streams.md` has the measurements and the decisions, "A stream whose client or key is gone", and the paragraph that said of a stream that outlives its credential "This is accepted" points to it; and under each of the two decisions still to build, what the maintainer agreed of it the same day, and what the reviews changed since, which they agreed after the third but for a revoked grant's streams.
- `deploy/README.md` says what a vanished and a frozen client leave in the log and at the bounds, with the numbers, and that nothing in the path hid a close or a kill.
- Nothing the stack runs.

**Why.** A listen stream keeps its place under the server's bound of 256 and a caller's of 32 until its connection ends. SMD-4458's third review found a frozen client holding its places for 680 s and a revoked key its 32, and SMD-4459's a relay that hid a client's leaving, its caller at its bound in 22 s. The maintainer set this ticket to come before SMD-4347 turns streams on, and decided on 2026-11-23, with the measurements below: a vanished client and a frozen one are accepted; a revoked caller's streams are ended, the MCP server asking the REST core every 60 seconds and holding no caller's credential to ask with; an operator's action, an operation of the REST core, ends one caller's streams. The last two are this ticket's next two PRs, and SMD-4347's PR 2b waits on all three. The reviews found the agreed design of those two could not be built as it was written: the page has what they changed, which the maintainer agreed after the third review, and a gate the second PR begins with, what the real client does when the server ends one stream. One thing is still theirs to decide, and that PR waits on it: the streams of a client whose grant is revoked at the authorization server.

**Measured after.** On a scratch stack on one Mac (a podman machine), its MCP server started holding streams by the suite's preload, with a probe client, each case once (the setting taken back, twice), by scripts outside the tree. Times are from what the client did to the server's line for the stream's end.

| What the client did | From the Mac, the published port | A container at the forwarder | A container at the MCP server | Through a quick tunnel |
| -- | -- | -- | -- | -- |
| Closed its stream | within 0.2 s | 7 ms | 1 ms | 40 ms |
| Was killed | within 0.2 s | within 0.2 s | within 0.2 s | within 0.2 s |
| Was frozen | held for a minute | held after 21 min, read again when thawed | the same | not run |
| Vanished: its network taken away | cannot, on one machine | 16 min 0 s | 16 min 4 s | 13 min 28 s, the VM's proxy |

The 13 min 28 s is the Mac's VM's own network proxy giving up on a container inside it, not the tunnel: a tunnel vendor's edge with a client that vanishes was not measured. The quick tunnel answered four probe streams and carried one comment or two of each, 15 seconds apart, in the 45 s at most each was read, where its vendor's page says it does not support event streams. Not measured: a client that vanishes behind a tunnel from another machine (it cannot be made on one machine through the published port), which is on the page's list for SMD-4347's PR 2b with the real client and an hour's hold.

**Not taken.**

- A shorter timeout on the forwarder: built, measured (1 min 55 s and 1 min 56 s for a container on its network), and taken back, above.
- A frozen client's stream is not ended by the server: a listen stream carries nothing from its client by which the server could tell it from a live one that is idle. Nor is a vanished client's: what faces it is seldom the stack, and gives up in its own time.
- Ending every stream at a set age, and closing a caller's oldest stream at its bound: `docs/mcp-listen-streams.md` says why not.
