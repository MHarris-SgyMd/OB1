## Record

A listen stream whose client or key is gone: measured, and decided (SMD-4493)

**What changed.**

- `docs/mcp-listen-streams.md` has the measurements and the decisions, "A stream whose client or key is gone", and the paragraph that said of a stream that outlives its credential "This is accepted" points to it; and under each of the three decisions still to build, what the maintainer agreed, when, and what the reviews found besides. Its paragraphs on a quick tunnel, the path, a stream that outlives its credential, the cuts and what is still to measure say what was measured since.
- `deploy/README.md` says what a vanished and a frozen client leave in the log and at the bounds, with the numbers, and that nothing in the path hid a close or a kill.
- Nothing the stack runs.

**Why.** A listen stream keeps its place under the server's bound of 256 and a caller's of 32 until its connection ends. SMD-4458's third review found a frozen client holding its places for 680 s and a revoked key its 32, and SMD-4459's a relay that hid a client's leaving, its caller at its bound in 22 s. The maintainer set this ticket to come before SMD-4347 turns streams on, and decided on the measurements below, 2026-11-23: a vanished client and a frozen one are accepted; a revoked caller's streams are ended; an operator's order ends one caller's streams; and the streams of a grant the operator revokes, or that is ended for a refresh token used twice, are ended. Those three are this ticket's second, third and fourth PRs. SMD-4347's PR 2b waits on the first three. The design page has, under each, what the maintainer agreed and what the reviews found besides.

**Measured after.** On a scratch stack on one Mac (a podman machine), its MCP server started holding streams by the suite's preload, with a probe client, each case once (the setting taken back, twice), by scripts outside the tree. Times are from what the client did to the server's line for the stream's end.

Not measured: a client that vanishes behind a tunnel from another machine (it cannot be made on one machine through the published port), which is on the page's list for SMD-4347's PR 2b with the real client and an hour's hold.

**Not taken.**

- A shorter timeout on the forwarder: built, measured (1 min 55 s and 1 min 56 s for a container on its network), and taken back, above.
- A frozen client's stream is not ended by the server: a listen stream carries nothing from its client by which the server could tell it from a live one that is idle. Nor is a vanished client's: what faces it is seldom the stack, and gives up in its own time.
- Ending every stream at a set age, closing a caller's oldest stream at its bound, and ending a token caller's stream at its token's lapse: `docs/mcp-listen-streams.md` says why not.
