**What changed.**

**Why.** A listen stream keeps its place under the server's bound of 256 and a caller's of 32 until its connection ends. SMD-4458's third review found a frozen client holding its places for 680 s and a revoked key its 32, and SMD-4459's a relay that hid a client's leaving, its caller at its bound in 22 s. The maintainer set this ticket to come before SMD-4347 turns streams on, and decided on the measurements below, 2026-11-23: a vanished client and a frozen one are accepted; a revoked caller's streams are ended; an operator's order ends one caller's streams; and the streams of a grant the operator revokes, or that is ended for a refresh token used twice, are ended. The last three are this ticket's second, third and fourth PRs. SMD-4347's PR 2b waits on this ticket's first three PRs. The second begins with a gate, what the real client does when the server ends one stream; on its result (below) the maintainer settled, 2026-11-24, that the ticket's Verify stands, the server ending a stream by a closing frame written by hand, and that the decisions stand, a client that stops asking after a refusal being accepted.

**Not taken.**

- A shorter timeout on the forwarder: built, measured (1 min 55 s and 1 min 56 s for a container on its network), and taken back, above.
- A frozen client's stream is not ended by the server: a listen stream carries nothing from its client by which the server could tell it from a live one that is idle. Nor is a vanished client's: what faces it is seldom the stack, and gives up in its own time.
- Ending every stream at a set age, closing a caller's oldest stream at its bound, and ending a token caller's stream at its token's lapse: `docs/mcp-listen-streams.md` says why not.
