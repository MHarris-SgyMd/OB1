## Changelog

The stack's forwarder gives up on a client that acknowledges nothing after 8 retransmissions, about 100 seconds, where the kernel's default is about 16 minutes: `net.ipv4.tcp_retries2` on its container (SMD-4493). A connection through the stack's port whose client has vanished is closed that much sooner. Upgrading: the first `up -d` recreates the forwarder, so the stack's port is away for a moment.

## Record

A listen stream whose client or key is gone: measured, decided, and a vanished client given up on in two minutes (SMD-4493)

**What changed.**

- `deploy/compose.yaml`: the forwarder's container sets `net.ipv4.tcp_retries2` to 8. Its kernel faces a client that reaches the stack's port, and is what gives up on one that has vanished. The break-glass listener, which carries no stream, keeps the default.
- `scripts/check-consistency.ts`, check 28: the forwarder's definition, which it holds whole, has the setting, and the break-glass listener's has none.
- `.github/workflows/brain-checks.yml`: the step that asks the running forwarder reads the setting in its container.
- `docs/mcp-listen-streams.md` has the measurements and the decisions, "A stream whose client or key is gone", in place of "This is accepted". `deploy/README.md` says what a vanished and a frozen client leave in the log, with the numbers, and that the stack's forwarder and proxy pass a client's leaving on.

**Why.** A listen stream keeps its place under the server's bound of 256 and a caller's of 32 until its connection ends. SMD-4458's third review found a frozen client holding its places for 680 s and a revoked key its 32, and SMD-4459's a relay that hid a client's leaving, its caller at its bound in 22 s. The maintainer set this ticket to come before SMD-4347 turns streams on, and decided on 2026-11-23, with the measurements below: a shorter timeout on the forwarder; a revoked caller's streams ended by asking again; an operator's action that ends one caller's streams; and a frozen client accepted. This PR builds the first.

**Held.** Check 28 fails a compose file whose forwarder has no such setting, or another's, or whose break-glass listener has one. CI's step "The proxy asks nothing outside the stack, and the forwarder dials it alone" fails where the running forwarder's kernel does not have the 8: an engine that did not take the setting would leave the default with nothing else to show for it.

**Measured after.** On a scratch stack on one Mac (podman), its MCP server started holding streams by the suite's preload, each client a container of its own, each case once, by scripts outside the tree; times are from what the client did to the server's line for the stream's end.

| What the client did | Through the forwarder and proxy | Direct | Through a quick tunnel in front |
| -- | -- | -- | -- |
| Closed its stream | 7 ms | 1 ms | 40 ms |
| Was killed | within 0.2 s | within 0.2 s | within 0.2 s |
| Vanished: its network taken away | 16 min 0 s | 16 min 4 s | 13 min 28 s |
| Vanished, the forwarder at 8 retransmissions | 1 min 55 s | | |
| Was frozen | held after 21 min, read again when thawed | the same | |

The quick tunnel answered a listen in 0.36 s and carried its comment every 15 seconds, where its vendor's page says it does not support event streams.

With the forwarder brought up from this change's own compose file: compose recreated it, and a second `up -d` left it running; its kernel read 8, the proxy's and the MCP server's 15; a client that vanished had its stream ended 1 min 56 s later; and a live client held beside it was still read every 15 seconds when the watch ended, five and a half minutes on.

**Not taken.**

- A frozen client's stream is not ended: it still acknowledges what it is sent, and a listen stream carries nothing from its client by which the server could tell it from a live one that is idle.
- Ending every stream at a set age, and closing a caller's oldest stream at its bound: `docs/mcp-listen-streams.md` says why not.
- Behind an operator's own TLS proxy or tunnel the forwarder's kernel does not face the client, and the setting changes nothing: that front's timeout decides.
- No row holds how long a vanished client's stream lasts: the measurement takes two minutes of a stack with a client taken off its network. CI holds the setting, and the measurement above what the setting does.
- The real client was not run, nor a stream held for an hour: both are on the page's list for SMD-4347's PR 2b.
