## Changelog

The supersession proposal listing pages. `list_supersession_proposals` (and `GET /v1/proposals`) takes `after` — the `cursor` the page before ended with — and answers `cursor` (null once no more follow) and `remaining` (how many follow), so any status or pass of more than 200 proposals can be walked page by page; `consolidate.ts --list <status> --after <cursor>` does the same, a capped page printing the line for the next. A cursor carries the filters it was made under and is refused with others. A server or CLI from before this release reads a brain at 110 as missing migration 109: upgrade it, and do not run the `--reapply` its preflight prints. A server left running across the migration fails its listings ("cached plan must not change result type") until it is restarted (SMD-3249, migration 110).

## FORK

The proposal listing pages — a keyset cursor on the listing's own order, with the rows that remain counted (SMD-3249)

**What changed** (migration 110).
- **`list_supersession_proposals`, redefined on 109's body** — its columns, order, cap and the status, lineage and judge-key selectors — plus:
  - an after-cursor of three trailing parameters, `p_after_confidence numeric`, `p_after_judged_at timestamptz`, `p_after_id uuid`, all defaulted: the last listed row's values on the listing's order (`confidence DESC, judged_at, id`), the term `confidence < c OR (confidence = c AND (judged_at > t OR (judged_at = t AND id > i)))` applied before the order and the cap. All three or none: a partial cursor is refused by name, where it would list from the top without a word.
  - two trailing columns: `page_key`, a JSON array of each row's three values as text (numeric(3,2) exactly, judged_at to the microsecond, the id), and `remaining`, the selection's rows from this page's first on (`count(*) OVER ()`, before the LIMIT).
  - LANGUAGE plpgsql (a sql function cannot raise), `#variable_conflict use_column`. The sentinel `ob1:listing-pages` beside 070's and 109's.
  - 109's four-argument form dropped first (a RETURNS TABLE cannot gain a column), 070's and 029's with it if a hand re-apply left them; a call of two to four arguments resolves through the defaults.
- **Seven arguments, always,** in every fork caller: the store, `consolidate.ts`, `evals/eval-consolidate.ts`. 029, 070 or 109 re-applied by hand lands its form beside 110's, and a call both take — under three arguments beside 029's, four beside 070's, five beside 109's — is ambiguous.
- **The cursor** (`core/list-cursor.ts`, beside list_thoughts'): base64url of `["proposals", status, lineage, judge_key, confidence, judged_at, id]` — the selection it was made under, then the place. The tag keeps one listing's cursor out of the other; the status and lineage are held to the inputs' words, the key to pass-key.ts's rule, the confidence to numeric(3,2)'s text, the time to Postgres's (the thoughts cursor's check, shared as `pgStamp`). One passed with other filters is `REFUSED_CURSOR` with `reason: "filters"` and `made`, the filters it was made under (list_thoughts' `sort` refusal's kind). A time of the right shape Postgres still cannot read is the caller's: `REFUSED_CURSOR`, as list_thoughts says it, and the CLI's `--after` refusal, exit 2.
- **The tool and `GET /v1/proposals`** take `after` and answer `cursor`, `remaining` (how many follow this page, counted off the listing before the writers' read drops a row gone in between) and `pastCursor` (whether the page followed a cursor). A page after a cursor says "past the cursor"; the text ends "N more follow — pass after: <cursor> (with the same filters) for the next page". An empty page after a cursor says the listing ends there, and one whose rows all went before the writers' read still carries the cursor. The value's guard keeps a proposals cursor by its own shape.
- **`consolidate.ts --list <status> --after <cursor>`**: a capped page's header says "the first N of R" ("past the cursor" on a later page) and the page ends with the line to run, `next page: --list <status> [--key <key>] --after <cursor>`; `capNote` points at it. `--after` is refused alone, with `--list lineage` (two listings, pending then stale) or `--list relations` (another listing), with another `--list` or `--key` than its page's, and when it is no cursor of the listing's.
- **A walk lists each row once while its status, judge key, confidence and judged_at hold.** A row that joins the selection or moves in it during the walk — a new proposal from a running pass, a decision, a stale mark, a re-judge (063, which also makes a stale row pending under the new key) — is listed if its place is past the cursor and missed if behind it; one that leaves it is not listed after. The tool's description says so.
- **Preflight's `lineage` check** reads 110's seven-argument form and its three sentinels; its arms are 109's, one argument list on.
- **An older server or CLI on a brain at 110** lists through its shorter call on a connection opened after 110; a connection that had already listed holds a prepared `SELECT *` whose result type 110 changes, and fails each listing ("cached plan must not change result type") until the process restarts — the release's `up` recreates the servers. Its preflight and `--status` probe 109's signature and read the listing as from before 109: preflight says every listing fails and prints `migrate.ts --reapply`, `--status` says to apply 109. The fix is the new server, never that `--reapply`, which stands 109's form beside 110's and makes the older tree's four-argument calls not unique.

**Why.** 070's listing caps a page at 200 with no cursor, and 109 kept that. A pending queue drains as it is reviewed, but every other listing stopped at 200: accepted, rejected, stale, all, and a look back at one pass's verdicts (SMD-3115's precision check is per pass). The canary on 2026-10-24 held 242 pending proposals under `@p4d1` alone.

**Why the values, not the row's id.** 063's recorder re-judges a stale row in place — `ON CONFLICT DO UPDATE`, the same id, `judged_at` now() and the new verdict's confidence — so a cursor naming the row would read its new place and jump the walk, skipping or repeating rows. (The plan on the ticket said the recorder deletes and re-inserts; test-schema [98] found it updates in place.)

**Not taken.**
- *Naming the listing's columns in this tree's callers instead of `SELECT *`.* Every other listing here reads `SELECT *` too, so it is its own change (SMD-3302).
- *Paging `--list relations`.* Another listing with its own cap of 50 and its own order (newest first); a cursor there is its own change (SMD-3304).
- *The operator GUI's Next link on `/proposals`.* SMD-2280's page, which can now pass `after` (SMD-3303).
- *A cursor naming the row (an id).* 063's in-place re-judge moves a row; a position does not.
- *Fetching one row past the cap to learn whether more follow.* The cap is 200 for every caller; `remaining` answers it without raising the cap or a second query.
