## Changelog

The version rules now say what a version speaks for: the release's two images and the files it attaches (`compose.yaml` with its profiles, `compose.release.yaml`, the settings `.env.example` names, and the four public overlays). A URL, route, port or setting removed from those is a major. Maintainer tooling and vendored code are outside the contract, so removing something there is a minor with an upgrade line. A function changed only additively (a trailing defaulted parameter, a trailing column, or a re-created form every caller resolves) is a minor. A command a shipped image runs that loses a flag or a named value is a major (`migrate.ts`'s flags and its `--groups` names), and the authorization server is inside the contract through `compose.yaml`'s `auth` profile (SMD-2687, SMD-2324).

## FORK

The version contract named, and the additive clause carried into the rules (SMD-2687 / 2324)

**Decided** by the maintainer on 2026-10-25, in the board review:
- The version contract covers every release asset: the two images and every file the release attaches. Maintainer tooling and vendored code are outside it, and a change there is a minor with an upgrade line.
- The same pass carries SMD-2324's additive clause.

Until now, FORK.md's MAJOR rule read a changed signature or a dropped URL literally, and each cut decided by hand when that didn't hold:
- v1.6.0 kept SMD-2294's dropped tier ports a minor.
- v1.7.0 kept SMD-1931's deleted vendored integrations a minor.
- Five migrations each argued MINOR for a change the rule's words called MAJOR.

**What changed.**
- **`FORK.md`, "Versioning":**
  - The MAJOR bullet says when a changed signature or return shape is a caller's problem: a positional call that resolves to another form or to none, or a reader that takes columns by position. A URL, route, port, transport or setting is held to the rule when something inside the version contract gives it.
  - The MINOR bullet carries the additive clause: a trailing defaulted parameter, a trailing output column, or an older form dropped and created anew, when every caller in the tree resolves the new signature and every reader keys by name. It cites the precedents 046, 058, 059, 061 and 070. SMD-2324 named 069 for 070's change; 069 is `069_jobs.sql`. A removal outside the contract is a minor too.
  - A new paragraph, "What the version contract covers":
    - inside: `ob1-server` and `ob1-migrate`, and the files `scripts/release-artifacts.ts`'s `ASSETS` attaches;
    - outside: maintainer tooling (`compose.host-ports.yaml`, the edge, the scripts under `deploy/`, and the `db/` scripts the migrator doesn't carry) and vendored code;
    - the three cuts that decided it by hand, SMD-2878's retirement among them.
- **`CONTRIBUTING.md`, "Changelog & versioning", and `changes/README.md`:** a fragment that removes or renames a URL, route, port or setting in a shipped file, or deletes vendored code, says in one sentence which side of the contract it falls on. Outside, it carries an **Upgrading:** line.

**PR 3** (2026-10-26): gaps the second-pass board review found in the text above.
- **`FORK.md`, the MAJOR list:** a command a shipped image runs that loses a flag or a named value, or whose flag changes meaning, is a major: `ob1-migrate`'s `migrate.ts` flags and its `--groups` names. SMD-3325's retirement of `--baseline` was argued a major on this ground before the rule said it, and so was SMD-3315's removal of the `sleep` grant group.

Each gains an **Upgrading:** line and its sentence on the contract's side. They said `patch`. v2.0.0 stays a major through SMD-2532 and SMD-3101, so no version moves.
