## Changelog

The MCP server can hold a 2026-07-28 `subscriptions/listen` stream open, and does not yet: its stop closes held streams last, each with its closing frame, a stream's log line is written as it opens, and one caller's name holds at most 32 and the server 256, all behind `tools.listChanged`, which still says `false` (SMD-4347). No migration, and nothing the brain serves changes yet.

## Record

A server that can hold the listen streams, and does not yet — its stop closes them last, each with its closing frame, and nothing it serves changes: PR 2a (SMD-4347)

**Why.** Change 545 decided, in `docs/mcp-listen-streams.md`, to hold the 2026-07-28 `subscriptions/listen` streams open and send nothing on them, so that a restart of the MCP server is what tells a connected client its tool list changed. It cut the build in two: 2a, what a held stream needs, with `tools.listChanged` still `false`; and 2b, the capability, the documents and a real client. This is 2a.

**What changed.**

- `mcp-server/listen-streams.ts`, new: `HOLD_LISTEN_STREAMS = false`, read by the entry alone; the bounds, 256 for the server and 32 for one caller's name; and the object the app and the entry share, a count of open streams and the app's close.
- `mcp-server/app.ts`: an app given streams to hold declares `tools.listChanged: true` on 2026-07-28. A request whose `Mcp-Method` header names `subscriptions/listen` then takes a place under the two bounds or is refused as the SDK refuses one past its own (a 200 carrying -32603; a notification bare), its line `refused` with a code that says which bound, `LISTEN_SERVER_FULL` or `LISTEN_CALLER_FULL`. A held stream's line is written as it opens, `ok`, its `ms` the time to open it; it has no stall bound, no keepalive frame of this server's and no abandonment warning; its place is given back when it ends, its client closes it, its connection drops or anything throws. Such an app also does the stop's cutting, on every route its first middleware sees, since its connections outlast the cut: after the cut nothing is answered and nothing more of an answer goes out, and after the drain has ended a request that arrives is not served. An app that holds none heeds neither flag.
- `server-portable/sse.ts`: `held`, a stream the wrapper neither keeps alive nor bounds; and `cut`, after which it passes nothing on and does not end the answer.
- `server-portable/shutdown.ts`: `drainOnSignal` takes `held`, `closeHeld` and `afterDrain`. Given them, it waits for the requests and the calls and not the held streams, tells the app as that wait ends, closes no connection at the bound, and after the flush and the pool's close it closes the streams, waits for their answers to have ended, then closes every connection. Its lines count the streams and say whether they were closed, or were not and were dropped, and why. The two new bounds, 100 ms each, come out of the flush's, so the latest a stop can end is unchanged at a grace of 2 s or more.
- `mcp-server/index.ts` gives the stop those three only where the server holds streams, so today's entry stops as it did.
- `mcp-server/test-listen-on.ts`, new: a preload that starts the suite's spawned entry with that one constant `true`. Its `test-` name keeps it out of the image, and `scripts/check-consistency.ts` names it among the suites not run, with why.

**Decided in the build, and written into `docs/mcp-listen-streams.md` under "As built in PR 2a".**

- The line at the open, not at the end with the stream's lifetime as the decision first had it. A record open at the stop must be ended before the flush, and the stream closed after it; ended at the open, the record goes out like any request's and `server-portable/telemetry.ts` is untouched. Nothing records when a stream ended.
- Inert where the capability is off, where the decision said it would not be.
- The caps are the route's count. The handler's `maxSubscriptions` was set to the same number, taken out as a bound no row could tell from the first, and put back by the second review as what a stream the count has lost runs into.
- For a request that is not a stream, three things differ on a server that holds them: its connection is kept, silent, from the cut to the stop's end; a connection with no whole request on it no longer holds the stop to its bound; and a request made whole on one after the drain has ended is dropped.

**Found by running it, before the reviews.** A call cut at the bound beside a held stream was answered 200 with an empty stream that ended whole, and no warning: closing the SDK's handler ends its calls too. With the exit straight after the streams' close, a closing frame was lost. Both are mended; the reviews below changed how.

**Left as it is.** A request whose header names the method, from a caller at its bound, is refused at the bound whatever else is wrong with it, where the SDK would answer 400, 413 or 415. A cut call's warning gives the time it was written, not the time on its line, and says "still running" of a call whose work ended between the cut and the connections' close. A request dropped after the drain's end is written `cut` with no caller's name, on stdout alone. A path holding an encoded line break, carriage return or line separator is still answered its 405 after the cut, as is what the runtime answers by itself. One person signed in through eight OAuth clients has eight names and could fill the server's 256; the two numbers are a first guess.
