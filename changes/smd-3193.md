## Changelog

The stack's forwarder runs under an init, so a stop, a recreate or a `down` ends it within about a second, not after compose's 10 s stop timeout. This applies to `deploy/compose.yaml`, the three-brain stack's `deploy/compose.tiers.yaml`, and so to `deploy/canary.sh down` and a canary `up` that moves its port (SMD-3193).

## FORK

The forwarder and the authorization server POC's containers stop on SIGTERM (SMD-3193)

**What changed.**
- **`deploy/compose.yaml`:** the `forwarder` service gets `init: true`, with a comment saying why. Bun as PID 1 ignores a signal it has no handler for, and `deploy/forwarder.ts` installs none. Under the init, bun is PID 2 and takes SIGTERM's default action: it exits.
- **`deploy/compose.tiers.yaml`:** the same key on its forwarder, which check 27 generates from compose.yaml's.
- **`scripts/check-fork-consistency.ts`:** check 28's `FORWARDER_SERVICE` holds `init: true`.
- **`.github/workflows/fork-checks.yml`:** the edge step already stops ob1b's forwarder. It now times that stop and reads its exit code: 143, SIGTERM, in under 5 s, or the step fails. The step's header names the check. That is the claim measured on Docker Engine's init, where everything else was measured on podman's.
- **`evals/auth/`:**
  - `init: true` on the `x-bun` anchor in `compose.yaml`, which covers proxy, resources, cimd and bait. None of their scripts handles SIGTERM.
  - The same on the runner-up's `auth` (`compose.better-auth.yaml`), which does not use the anchor and does not handle SIGTERM either.
  - The same on the winner's `auth` (`compose.oidc-provider.yaml`), for parity with `deploy/compose.yaml`'s `auth`, which runs that image under an init. Its `deploy/auth/server.ts` handles SIGTERM itself, so its stop time does not change.

**Why.**
- In Fork Checks runs 39707442097 and 39706512002, every compose stop or recreate that waited out the 10 s timeout was the forwarder's, at 10.13–10.20 s, eight times a run: about 81 s per run.
  - The next slowest was lgtm's stop, at 5.1 s. lgtm already runs under an init, so that is its image's own shutdown.
  - The two post-5b runs 39709635973 and 39718372494 show the same eight, at 10.13–10.17 s. The new `mcp` service handles SIGTERM and stops in under 0.1 s.
- The daemon-restart step adds about 10 s more: after the restart, each of its six services reported "started 11 s after the restart began".
- The authorization server POC's "Tear down" and "Runner-up torn down" steps took 10–11 s each. Each is a `down -v --rmi all`, so most of each step, about 9 s, was the timeout: about 18 s per run.

Expected per job, from the two post-5b runs:

| Job | Forwarder stops | Expected fall | Now |
|---|---|---|---|
| Full stack: a second brain, the tier refresh and the canary | 5: the edge step's, the canary step's three, the Tear down's | about 50 s | 287–292 s |
| Full stack, no Supabase | 1, plus the daemon restart's 10 s | about 20 s | 228–229 s |
| Full stack: the import runner and the authorization server | 1 | about 10 s | 203–207 s |
| Full stack: the observability profile | 1 | about 10 s | 202–204 s |
| Authorization server POC | none; its two `down`s | about 18 s | 64–74 s |

Each full-stack job's Tear down falls from 11 s to 1–2 s.

**Not taken.**
- A `process.on("SIGTERM", …)` in `deploy/forwarder.ts`. The forwarder has nothing to flush: its connections are byte pipes that a stop cuts either way, so the signal's default action is the whole job. `init` matches `sleep` and `auth`, and leaves the script, which check 28 holds byte for byte in two places, unchanged.
- `--timeout 1` on `eval-auth.ts --down`. It could kill the oidc-provider server 1 s into its own stop, which waits up to 5 s for requests in flight and then closes its store.
- A check on the POC's `init`. Nothing in `scripts/` reads `evals/auth/`. Losing the key costs seconds in an eval no live stack runs.

**Upgrading.** The first `up` after this recreates the forwarder because its configuration changed. The old container has no init, so that one stop still waits out the timeout, and that `up` takes about 10 s longer, once. The old forwarder serves the port until it is killed: compose creates the new container first and starts it after. So the port is down only while the new one starts. This applies to stable, the canary's `up` in either form, and the tier stack.
