## Changelog

An entity's priority statements now follow it (SMD-4241, migration 141). Extraction no longer prunes an entity someone stated a priority on, or one another item's due time comes from, when nothing mentions it any more: it stays in the graph until its statements are cleared, and a later capture of the name finds it, statements and all. Before, the prune closed them as `deleted`, and the name came back as a new entity with none. A deliberate delete, and forgetting a thought, still close them. A merge, the retype gate's included, now moves the loser's statements and restates each statement whose due time comes from the loser to take it from the survivor.

## Record

An entity's priority statements follow it — the prunes spare an entity with an open statement, a merge restates the due references naming its loser, and the retype gate's merge moves statements as merge_entities does; migration 141 (SMD-4241)

**Why.** 136 (SMD-3711) closes a deleted entity's open statements as `deleted`, and `merge_entities` restates a loser's on its survivor. Three entity paths still lost a stance no one withdrew. Extraction prunes an entity nothing mentions any more, when the thought's next extraction drops its name (or names it as another type); its statements closed, and a later capture of the name made a new entity with none. A re-extraction naming the same entity was never the loss: 061's writer upserts the entity by type and normalised name before it replaces the thought's mentions, so it keeps its id. 056's `apply_entity_type_gate` merges a retyped entity inline and deletes it, so its statements closed rather than moved. And a merge left other statements whose due reference names the loser, which then read no due time.

**What changed.** The maintainer's choices (2026-11-13): an entity with an open priority statement is no orphan; a merge restates the due references naming its loser.
- **The prunes.** Migration 141 carries 061's `record_thought_entities` and 016's `prune_orphan_entities`, each verbatim but for one more condition on its prune: `NOT ob1_entity_has_open_priority(id)`, true for an entity with an open statement or one an open statement takes its due time from (a new partial index on the open statements' due references serves it and the merge). `rebuild_derived` is not changed: its prunes run only for an input leaving, which is SMD-1723's forgetting (`forget_thought`, OB1 PR 776; `rebuild.ts --gone`), and erasing the thought's text wins. That new function is plpgsql and answers false for a role without SELECT on `priority_assertions`. That role prunes as before and leaves the statements to 136's sweep, rather than failing its whole extraction on a query naming the table. A deliberate delete still closes them as `deleted` (136's trigger): a merge, the gate's refusal of a name, an operator's DELETE.
- **The merge.** `ob1_priority_merge_entity(survivor, loser)` holds 136's restatement loop, moved out of `merge_entities` so the gate's merge shares it. Before that loop, it restates each open statement on another item whose due reference names the loser: same maker, level, times, reason, flag, tier and `asserted_at`, with the reference naming the survivor and the old statement closed as replaced by the new one. A statement on the survivor naming the loser loses its reference, since 116's CHECK refuses one naming its own item. A merge with nothing to move (no open statement on the loser, none taking its due time from it) returns before any lock, so the gate's many merges in one transaction take none for such entities. Otherwise every item it touches is locked first, in the order of its pool's key (133's `ob1_priority_lock_key`, read once, the order the projection's rebuild takes), and the references are read again under those locks, by pool, so one written meanwhile on another row of a locked ticket's pool is restated too. Each item is then re-projected. `merge_entities` calls it, and its answer gains `priority_refs_moved`. `apply_entity_type_gate` (056's, carried) calls it in its retype merge, and its answer gains `priorities_moved` and `priority_refs_moved`. Each counts the restatements still standing when it is done, from the ids the helper returns. A role that cannot restate, or a transaction above READ COMMITTED, moves nothing, as in 136.
- **Docs.** `db/README.md` and `docs/priority.md` decision 2 say it. test-schema's CARRIES gains `"141": ["061"]`, so a restore of the writer still re-applies the file it came from. Without that, [52]'s re-apply of 053 would keep 053's six-argument writer. [123] restores the shipped `merge_entities` after re-applying 136, so its merge rows hold 141's body.

**Not changed.** A restatement is the newest written within its tier on its item. So it may outrank another maker's statement written there after the original: 136 kept that for the survivor (merges being an operator's step), and it holds for an item whose due reference moved. Some writes can still slip past. A writer that read a due reference's item before a merge committed, and commits after the merge read the references, on an item the merge never locked, keeps naming the loser; the read finds no due time through it, as 136 left a reference to a deleted item. A writer stating a priority on an entity a prune is deleting at that instant writes on a deleted entity, for 136's sweep. An entity spared from the prune stays until its statements are cleared and then until `prune_orphan_entities` runs, which nothing schedules (as for what `delete_thought` leaves), or a later extraction names it and drops it again. A name a re-extraction gives another type leaves its statements on the old entity, where they were stated. The gate's locks pile up across its merges out of pool-key order, so a projection rebuild beside it can deadlock with it; Postgres aborts one.
