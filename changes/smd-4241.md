## Changelog

An entity's priority statements now follow it (SMD-4241, migration 139). Extraction no longer prunes an entity someone stated a priority on when nothing mentions it any more: it stays in the graph until its statements are cleared, and a later capture of the name finds it, statements and all. Before, the prune closed them as `deleted`, and the name came back as a new entity with none. A deliberate delete still closes them. A merge, the retype gate's included, now moves the loser's statements and restates each statement whose due time comes from the loser to take it from the survivor.

## Record

An entity's priority statements follow it — the prunes spare an entity with an open statement, a merge restates the due references naming its loser, and the retype gate's merge moves statements as merge_entities does; migration 139 (SMD-4241)

**Why.** 136 (SMD-3711) closes a deleted entity's open statements as `deleted`, and `merge_entities` restates a loser's on its survivor. Three entity paths still lost a stance no one withdrew. Extraction prunes an entity nothing mentions any more, when the thought's next extraction drops its name (or names it as another type) or the thought is deleted; its statements closed, and a later capture of the name made a new entity with none. A re-extraction naming the same entity was never the loss: 061's writer upserts the entity by type and normalised name before it replaces the thought's mentions, so it keeps its id. 056's `apply_entity_type_gate` merges a retyped entity inline and deletes it, so its statements closed rather than moved. And a merge left other statements whose due reference names the loser, which then read no due time.

**What changed.** The maintainer's choices (2026-11-13): an entity with an open priority statement is no orphan; a merge restates the due references naming its loser.
- **The prunes.** Migration 139 carries 061's `record_thought_entities`, 067's `rebuild_derived` (both of its prunes, where its input thought is gone) and 016's `prune_orphan_entities`, each verbatim but for one more condition on its prune: `NOT ob1_entity_has_open_priority(id)`. That new function is plpgsql and answers false for a role without SELECT on `priority_assertions`. That role prunes as before and leaves the statements to 136's sweep, rather than failing its whole extraction on a query naming the table. A deliberate delete still closes them as `deleted` (136's trigger): a merge, the gate's refusal of a name, an operator's DELETE.
- **The merge.** `ob1_priority_merge_entity(survivor, loser)` holds 136's restatement loop, moved out of `merge_entities` so the gate's merge shares it. Before that loop, it restates each open statement on another item whose due reference names the loser: same maker, level, times, reason, flag, tier and `asserted_at`, with the reference naming the survivor and the old statement closed as replaced by the new one. A statement on the survivor naming the loser loses its reference, since 116's CHECK refuses one naming its own item. Every item it touches is locked first, in the order of its pool's key (133's `ob1_priority_lock_key`, the order the projection's rebuild takes), and the references are read again under those locks. Each item is then re-projected. `merge_entities` calls it, and its answer gains `priority_refs_moved`. `apply_entity_type_gate` (056's, carried) calls it in its retype merge, and its answer gains `priorities_moved` and `priority_refs_moved`. A role that cannot restate, or a transaction above READ COMMITTED, moves nothing, as in 136.
- **Docs.** `db/README.md` and `docs/priority.md` decision 2 say it. test-schema's CARRIES gains `"139": ["061", "067"]`, so a restore of either writer still re-applies the file it came from. Without that, [52]'s re-apply of 053 would keep 053's six-argument writer. [123] restores the shipped `merge_entities` after re-applying 136, so its merge rows hold 139's body.

**Not changed.** A restatement is the newest written within its tier on its item. So it may outrank another maker's statement written there after the original: 136 kept that for the survivor (merges being an operator's step), and it holds for an item whose due reference moved. Some writes can still slip past. A writer that read a due reference's item before a merge committed, and commits after the merge read the references, on an item the merge never locked, keeps naming the loser; the read finds no due time through it, as 136 left a reference to a deleted item. A writer stating a priority on an entity a prune is deleting at that instant writes on a deleted entity, for 136's sweep. An entity spared from the prune stays until its statements are cleared and a later prune touches it, or `prune_orphan_entities` runs.
