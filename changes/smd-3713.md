## Changelog

`db/test-live.ts`'s [10h] runs about 35 s faster: its cases past a scheduler's budget wait out 750 ms, not 1,500, and hold their rows and tables by that budget and a margin, or to the run's end. [10h] had grown from 40 s to 122 s on CI with the subject sweep's scheduler cases. The hand-back case no longer fails when the run's own hand-back reaches the row before the stop's (SMD-3713, SMD-3712).

## FORK

test-live's [10h] ~35 s shorter: the budget cases at a 750 ms budget, their holds by it; the hand-back case's order flake fixed (SMD-3713, SMD-3712)

[10h] grew from 40 s to 122 s on CI with SMD-3306 PR 6a ([OB1 PR 615](https://github.com/MHarris-SgyMd/OB1/pull/615)), taking db-live's extract cell from ~245 s to 314–326 s, the run's longest beside the sleep cell.

**What changed.**
- **The budget.** `BUDGET_MS` (750) is the statement_timeout every [10h] case past a scheduler's budget runs under: the session's of 15 runs and the role's of the naming's write. It was 1,500 ms in each.
  - Each case waits it out once or more. A statement nothing holds ends far inside it.
- **The holds.** A hold that must outlast the budget is `BUDGET_MS` and a margin in milliseconds, each margin as it was: the claim's, release's, window's and stop's 1 s; the read's and the hand-back's 1.5 s. Load delays a step by milliseconds, not by a share of the budget.
  - Two holds that only had to outlast the run end with it: the hand-back's `past` row (4 s) and the every-kind case's (4 s).
  - The lost poll's hand-back row (4.5 s) is let go once the stop is sent. The case's poll sees only that hand-back, since the release before it is `release_thought`'s.
- **The bounds that follow.** The claim's gap is `BUDGET_MS + 1700`, its 300 ms slack kept. The put-back's provider pause is 2 s, still past the follow, and its gap `BUDGET_MS + 2000`; its line says "claims again in 2 s".
- **The provider-pause case** pauses 2 s, its abort 1 s in, and the calls at least 1.7 s apart (3 s, 1.5 s and 2.5 s before).
- **The judge timing out on every thought** times out three thoughts, not five: two counted as answers would trip the window of five.
- **The hand-back case (SMD-3712).** `within` took the stop's hand-back's count to be 1. The call's abort ends the run's loop at once, so the run's end hands back beside the stop, and either may wait on the row first; the one behind finds nothing, 0.
  - Under load the run's end came first in 4 of 30 local runs, the unchanged test's among them: 1 in 6 runs beside twelve CPU burners. That is the row SMD-3712's flake failed; its CI log kept only the row's first line.
  - The case now takes 1 or 0: each lands past the budget, not null, which review pass 8's mutant (a plain release under the budget) makes null.
  - `past` held its row 4 s, which raced the two hand-backs' bounds; it is held to the run's end.

**Not here.**
- Running [10h]'s cases side by side: they share the stub's state, the judge's mode and the pool, so none is independent.
- `follow` and `--timeout` are whole seconds in the engine, so the follow cases' 1 s polls and the 1 s limits stay.
- The cells' rebalance (the ticket's step 4) waits on [OB1 PR 627](https://github.com/MHarris-SgyMd/OB1/pull/627) (SMD-3714), which moves the sleep cell.

**Held.**
- **[10h] alone, side by side with the unchanged test under the same load.**
  - With twelve CPU burners (load ~25), the unchanged test took 168–177 s and failed once in six runs, on `within.hard`; the changed test took 130–144 s and passed six of six.
  - At load ~30–38 from other work on the host, before the hand-back fix, the changed test failed `within.hard` "0" in three of nine runs.
