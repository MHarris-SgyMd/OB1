## Changelog

`db/test-live.ts`'s [10h] runs about 25 s faster locally: most of its cases past a scheduler's budget wait out 750 ms, not 1,500, and hold their rows and tables by that budget and a margin, or to the run's end. [10h] had grown from 40 s to 122 s on CI with the subject sweep's scheduler cases. The hand-back case no longer fails when the run's own hand-back reaches the row before the stop's (SMD-3713, SMD-3712).

## FORK

test-live's [10h] ~25 s shorter locally: the budget cases at a 750 ms budget, their holds by it; the hand-back case's order flake fixed (SMD-3713, SMD-3712)

**What changed.**
- **The budget.** `BUDGET_MS` (750) is the statement_timeout of [10h]'s runs past a scheduler's budget: 13 session sites (14 runs, the hand-back loop's two among them) and the role's of the naming's write. It was 1,500 ms in each.
  - Most of these cases wait it out once or more. A statement nothing holds ends far inside it.
  - `WATCHED_MS` (1,500, twice `BUDGET_MS`) stays the budget of the three cases that stop the run once a poll every 250 ms sees a statement wait, inside the wait: the stop while a claim waits, and the hard and first stops while a thought's own write waits.
- **The holds.** A hold that must outlast the budget is the budget and a margin in milliseconds, each margin as it was: the claim's, release's, window's, put-back's and stop's 1 s; the read's and the hand-back's 1.5 s. Load delays a step by milliseconds, not by a share of the budget.
  - Two holds that only had to outlast the run end with it: the hand-back's `past` row (4 s) and the every-kind case's (4 s).
  - The lost poll's hand-back row (4.5 s) is let go once the stop is sent. The case's poll sees only that hand-back, since the release before it is `release_thought`'s.
- **The bounds that follow.** The claim's gap is `BUDGET_MS + 1700`, its 300 ms slack kept. The put-back's provider pause is 2 s, still past the follow, its line says "claims again in 2 s", and its gap is `BUDGET_MS + 2500`: the hold and the pause give 3,750 ms, the hold and the follow 2,750.
- **The provider-pause case** pauses 2 s, its abort 1 s in, and the calls at least 1.7 s apart (3 s, 1.5 s and 2.5 s before).
- **The judge timing out on every thought** times out three thoughts, not five: two counted as answers would trip the window of five.
- **The close while the door's lock is held** waits under a 1.5 s `lock_timeout`, not 3 s, and asserts its facet probe saw the close waiting and returned before the close did.
- **The hand-back case (SMD-3712).** `within` took the stop's hand-back's count to be 1. The call's abort ends the run's loop at once, so the run's end hands back beside the stop, and either may wait on the row first; the one behind finds nothing, 0.
  - The case takes 1 or 0, and reads the claim back in the pool as the stop's hand-back lands, 500 ms or more past the budget after the stop. SMD-3306 PR 6a review pass 8's mutant (a plain release under the budget) still lands null.
  - `past` held its row 4 s, which a slow host could spend before the two hand-backs' bounds ran out; it is held to the run's end, and its bound is `BUDGET_MS + 100`, as 1,600 was the old budget's.

- **SMD-3712:** under load the run's end hand-back came first in 4 of 30 local runs, the unchanged test's among them, 1 in 6 runs of it beside twelve CPU burners: the row SMD-3712's flake failed, whose CI log kept only the row's first line.

**Not taken.**
- **Running [10h]'s cases side by side** (the ticket's step 3): they share the stub's state, the judge's mode, the pool and two module-level settings (`RELEASE_WAIT`, `TRANSIENT_PAUSES_MS`), so none is independent.
- **A case at each timer's real length** (step 2's last clause): no case ran a scheduler's budget, a provider pause or a follow at its production length before this change either. The follow and `--timeout` stay at 1 s, the shortest the engine takes (whole seconds); the budget stays 1.5 s where a case must act inside its wait. No case waits out a lease: each sets one run out by its timestamp.
