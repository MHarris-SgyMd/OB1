## Changelog

Both servers' stop lines count their telemetry from the signal. An export lost while the stop drains its requests, or in its flush, makes it `telemetry not all sent`, where only the flush's own exports were counted. An export lost before the signal (`no answer in time` or any other loss) is still said only on its own line, and the stop can still say `telemetry sent`, even when the once-a-minute limit holds that line until the stop prints it. deploy/README.md says which (SMD-3297).

## FORK

The stop's telemetry word counts from its signal (SMD-3297)

**What changed.**
- **Decided** by the maintainer on 2026-10-24, in two steps:
  - at the ticket's start: the word is the stop's own, not every loss since the process started;
  - after review pass 1: its boundary is the signal, not the flush's start, so a loss in the drain, between `no longer accepting` and `stopped`, is the stop's.
- **`server-portable/otel-setup.ts`:** `Telemetry` gains `mark()`, each watched signal's `{ started, failed, pending }`, and `flush(since)` counts from it: `flushBatch` and `flushSignal` take the signal's mark in place of their own start.
- **`telemetry.ts`:** `markTelemetry()` keeps the mark, and `flushTelemetry()` flushes since it. preflight never marks, so its row still reads its own flush.
- **`shutdown.ts`:** `DrainOptions.mark`, told first as the signal comes, guarded as the cut's sink is. `api.ts` and the MCP server pass `markTelemetry`, and check 25's import list for api.ts names it.
- **Comments:** the stop's word, `flushResult` and `flushTelemetry` say what the word counts.
- **`deploy/README.md`:**
  - says the word counts from the signal, and `no telemetry left to send` is nothing exported since it;
  - its old "a stop after any request is `sent` … under delta it can be" now reads: with a metrics endpoint a stop is never `no telemetry left to send`, since the uptime gauge (SMD-3050) is read at every flush.
- **test-rest [12]:**
  - trickled's collector answers the metrics at once and trickles the rest, so its row pins `; telemetry sent; exit 0` beside the span's and log record's `no answer in time`, lost before the signal.
  - A new `drained` case trickles the first span and log export. A provider holds a search 5.5 s, so the stop drains it past their deadline, about 3 s after the signal, while the flush and metrics are answered. Its row pins `; telemetry not all sent; exit 0`.
  - The rejected row's comment names the metric export as what its flush lost.
- **test-server:**
  - a row holds `flushResult` reading only the exports since its mark: sent, none, or sent for one on its way at the mark;
  - another holds the stop taking the mark before its first line and its flush, and a mark that throws still stopping;
  - a real setup whose collector refuses one signal's export after the mark, then answers, reads it failed since the mark and sent from its own start — spans, log records and the metrics each alone;
  - a flush counts from the mark taken on its own SDK: `stopTelemetry` clears it;
  - a real `bun index.ts` (the MCP server) has its first span and log record trickle while a keyed tool call the REST core never answers holds the drain past their deadline, and its stop says `not all sent`.

**Why.** SMD-3263's review pass 2 found trickled's row asserting that the stop says not all was sent. That held only because its metric export trickled too and was cut at the flush's bound: with the metrics answered at once, the stop said `telemetry sent` beside the two lost exports. Every loss since the start was turned down because one collector blip days before would colour every later stop. Review pass 1 then ran a real stop whose drain outlasted an export's deadline: the loss came 3.5 s after `SIGTERM`, and the stop still said `sent`, hence the second step.

**Held.**
- test-server 1212, 5 more than main (1207); test-rest 485, 1 more (484).
- Each of these fails `drained`'s row: flushing from the flush's own start, taking the mark after the drain, or not passing it from api.ts. The late mark also fails test-server's row, and not passing it from the MCP server fails its twin there.
- A span, log or metric flush that ignores its signal's mark fails test-server's per-signal row; a mark kept past `stopTelemetry` fails its own.
- A watcher that does not free an export at its deadline fails trickled's row.
- A `flushResult` that reads every failure since the start fails test-server's row.
