## Changelog

The consolidation judge asks prompt version 4: five verdicts (unrelated, related, evolves, duplicate, outdates), with the words that show which thought is current quoted and checked. Only outdates and a duplicate become proposals, and a proposal's confidence is the model's token probability where the endpoint returns logprobs. On the dogfood brain the judge proposed again 2 of the 126 pairs a reviewer had rejected, where p3 proposed 119; `evals/eval-judge.ts` measures it on a brain's own labels (SMD-1873).

## FORK

The consolidation judge says something — five verdicts, a quoted direction, and a confidence from the model's own token probabilities (SMD-1873)

**What changed.**
- **The prompt** (`server-portable/consolidate.ts`, `CONSOLIDATE_PROMPT_VERSION` 4, so a new pool under `consolidate:<model>@p4`). Five verdicts where p3 had three:
  - `unrelated`;
  - `related`: compatible, neither continues the other;
  - `evolves`: one continues the other and both stand;
  - `duplicate`: the same claims;
  - `outdates`: one is no longer current, either a later state of the same thing or a reversal. This is p3's "conflict", renamed.

  Each word starts with a different letter, so the verdict's first token separates them. For a directed `outdates`, the judge copies the words from the current thought that show it is current. `judgePair` checks they are in that side's text (`evidenceIn`, a new `evidenceFound` on the judgement).
- **What becomes a proposal** (`proposalVerdict`): `outdates`, in the direction named or `conflict_undirected`, and a `duplicate`, with its reason starting `duplicate —`. A duplicate is recorded as `newer_supersedes_older`, except when the operator wrote the older thought and someone else the newer: then the operator's stands, SMD-1726's rule. The other three record nothing yet; SMD-1873's next PR stores them as relation edges, a duplicate included.
- **The confidence** (`proposalConfidence`). The pass asks for `logprobs` (`JUDGE_LOGPROBS`, 10). When the endpoint returns them, `valueDistribution` reads the model's probability over the five words at the token where the verdict starts. The proposal records the mass on `outdates` plus `duplicate`. It records the number the model wrote instead in three cases:
  - no distribution;
  - a token with no alternatives;
  - alternatives naming a verdict that hold under half the token's mass (`MIN_COVERED`).
- **The 400 rule.** An endpoint that refuses `logprobs` with a 400 and then answers without it is asked without it for the rest of the process. A 400 the retry gets too is the pair's own, so it doesn't change the scale for later pairs.
- **The record.** The proposal's recipe in `derivations` carries `judged`: the verdict word, `confidence_source` (`token` or `stated`), the stated number, `evidence_found`, and the token distributions. `--dump` lines carry `score`, `score_source`, `evidence` and `evidence_found`.
  - The run summary counts the five verdicts, and how many proposals were scored each way.
  - A verdict outside the five, such as p3's `conflict` from a model keeping to old words, is reported by name.
  - Evidence counts only when it is found in the named side and not the other.
- **`evals/eval-judge.ts`** (new). It reads a brain's reviewed proposals, the board's links between two tickets and the writers' `supersedes` pointers in one read-only transaction, along with a sample of today's candidates. It judges them offline and reports:
  - the verdicts by label;
  - the written confidence's spread;
  - the AUROC of each confidence against a right verdict, and of the recorded score against "is it a supersession", over all labelled pairs and, apart, among the pairs proposed (with their count);
  - how often a supersession names a side and quotes words that are there;
  - what the pass would record.

**Why.** On the dogfood brain, 120 of the 128 proposals carried exactly 0.80 confidence and 117 named no side. 126 were rejected, mostly two related tickets the judge called a conflict. Re-judged under p3, the 7B called 119 of those 126 a conflict again. 0.80 was the model's own answer (the parser's default is 0.5), and among the proposals it was a coin, AUROC 0.53. The pairs that do make a thought out of date are mostly a later state of the same thing, and the 7B reads "conflict" as contradiction only.

**Measured after** (434 pairs from the dogfood brain, qwen2.5:7b, temperature 0; evals/README.md has the tables):

| | p3 | p4 |
|---|---|---|
| rejected proposals proposed again | 119 of 126 | 2 of 126 |
| linked ticket pairs read as related | 46 of 126 | 106 of 126 |
| writer-superseded and accepted pairs proposed | 3 of 62 | 22 of 62 |
| supersessions the model directed | 14 of 128 | 4 of 4 outdates (the 20 duplicates are directed by rule) |
| AUROC, is it a supersession, over all 314 labelled pairs | 0.12 (written) | 0.91 (token) |

The 0.91 is mostly the verdict itself. Among the 24 pairs the 7B proposed, 2 were false, too few to say how well the token score ranks proposals.

On qwen3.8:27b, p4 proposes 60 of the 62 true pairs, with 6 of 40 rejected pairs proposed again, at about four times the time per pair. Ollama returns that model's first-token logprobs only, so its proposals record the written number. That number orders its 71 proposals (11 false) worse than chance, AUROC 0.35.

**Not taken.**
- **Byte-fallback tokens.** An endpoint that sends one, or U+FFFD, anywhere in the answer makes the tokens not spell the content, so the distribution is dropped and the written number is used. Ollama's qwen2.5:7b sent whole characters on every call measured.
- **Rounding at the floor.** The floor compares the two-decimal value 029 stores, so 0.495 passes 0.50.
- **The writer rule for "outdates".** An agent's newer thought outdating the operator's is held by the prompt's rule only: an agent may state a later fact. The dogfood set has no thought marked operator, so neither that rule nor the duplicate guard is measured there.
- **The table comment** on `supersession_proposals` (029, 067) still says "judged to CONFLICT". Those migrations are frozen; SMD-1873's next PR, which carries a migration, rewrites it.
- **One scale in the queue.** Token-scored and written-number proposals share one queue ordered by confidence; the recipe says which each is.
- **Asking the 27B by default:** its recall is the better one, but it costs four times the time per pair and records no ranking confidence. `OB1_JUDGE_MODEL` stays the operator's choice.
- **Dropping a direction whose quote was not found:** on the 27B the quote was found on 24 of 31 directed answers, and of those, 15 of 18 named the right side where labelled. That is too few labels to move the rule, so the recipe records the finding and the direction stands.
