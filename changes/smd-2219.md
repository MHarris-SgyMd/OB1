## Changelog

Fork Checks' "SQL data layer against real Postgres" job runs `db/test-upgrade.ts` and `server-portable/test-preflight.ts` side by side, each against its own database on the service container, where they were its two longest steps in series. What the two share across the cluster is scoped in the suites: preflight's repeatable-read default is set on its own database, and test-upgrade counts advisory locks in its own. test-upgrade's `reembed.ts --status` assertion also passes in a shell with `FORCE_COLOR` set (SMD-2219).

## FORK

The data-layer job runs test-upgrade and test-preflight side by side — the job's two longest steps overlap instead of adding up (SMD-2219)

**What changed.** `.github/workflows/fork-checks.yml`'s job `data-layer` loses its "Incremental upgrade" and "Preflight catches bad configurations" steps for one, "Incremental upgrade and preflight, side by side", at preflight's place.
- **The step.** It creates a second database, `ob1upgrade`, on the service container and runs `test-upgrade.ts` against it while `test-preflight.ts` runs against `ob1test`. The rest is SMD-2092's step for the schema legs: a log per run, both waited on and printed (a passing one folded, a failing one open), a 10-minute limit on each, and each suite's summary line at the end. The five steps that stood between the two now run before the pair. Each resets its own schema, and none read what test-upgrade left.
- **What the cluster shares.** A role and its settings, `pg_locks` and `pg_stat_activity` are the cluster's, not a database's. Both suites' headers and the step's comment now say each suite scopes them to its own database or names them for itself. Two fixtures reached across:
  - test-preflight [5] set `default_transaction_isolation = 'repeatable read'` on its role, which is the role test-upgrade connects as, for one preflight run. It is now set with `ALTER DATABASE` on its own database. Preflight's fresh session still inherits it, and the warning it asserts is unchanged. Not `ALTER ROLE … IN DATABASE`, which would outrank the `ALTER ROLE … SET` the warning names as the remedy. A new assertion beside it holds the scoping: a session as the same role in another database, `postgres`, is still at read committed. It skips when the role may not connect there or the suite's own database is `postgres`, and fails on any other error.
  - test-upgrade [12] and [13] assert that no advisory lock outlives a call by counting the cluster's. The count now takes the suite's own database only (`advisoryLocksHere`). Preflight's captures take the same lock in theirs. [12] counts while a session in `postgres` holds an advisory lock, so a count that reads the cluster's fails there every run.
- **The colour codes.** test-upgrade [7]'s `reembed.ts --status` assertion anchors `--reapply` at a line's end. In a shell with `FORCE_COLOR` set, which `migratorEnv` keeps, reembed's stderr ends in a reset code after it. That failed the assertion on every local run in such a shell and never on CI (SMD-2219's Notes). The output is stripped of colour codes before the regexes read it.

test-upgrade keeps its 315 assertions; test-preflight goes from 456 to 457 with that one. No production file changes. `db/ci-parity.sh`, which runs the suites in series, says CI runs these two side by side, and `db/test-support.ts`'s `report()` names the step among the readers of its summary line.

**Why.** After SMD-2135's two cuts (#169, #176) the live job's step took 129–134 s, and this job, 133–166 s over five runs, was the run's critical path. Its two longest steps were test-upgrade (41–45 s on CI) and test-preflight (32–50 s). Timed locally by line through `with-postgres.sh`:
- **test-upgrade: 43.9 s**, 30.2 s of it three 10.05 s waits in [7] on the migrator's `lock_timeout` (`LOCK_TIMEOUT_S`, config.mjs). A held `ob1_config` fails the checks, a held `thoughts` fails the re-run at 001 and the plain run at 021. The migrator runs themselves cost 0.1–0.3 s each.
- **test-preflight: 32.3 s**, [5] 20.8 of it with no single hotspot. [5] spawns about 180 preflight runs. The ticket's "62 schema resets" are 61 `applyMigrations` calls, 43 of them one file, and one `dropSchema`. A preflight run costs about 27 ms with no database and 110–190 ms against one, some 69 transactions. [9]'s 6 s is two 2.5 s probe timeouts (`LOCAL_PROBE_TIMEOUT_MS`).

The time is in the processes and the lock waits.

**Held.** A check fails when either leak comes back. Preflight's new assertion fails when the fixture goes back to the role (456/457). [12] fails when the count goes back to the cluster's (314/315), because of the lock it plants in `postgres`. Without that lock nothing would: in the pair preflight's captures end some 13 s before [12] counts, and the unscoped count passed 8 of 8 runs in CI's shape. Both leaks, reproduced beside test-upgrade in another database of the same cluster:

| Neighbour | Before | After |
| --- | --- | --- |
| An advisory lock held in another database | [12]'s and [13]'s "no advisory lock is held" fail | 315/315 |
| The same lock held in test-upgrade's own database (the scoped count's mutant) | — | the same two fail |
| Repeatable read on the role, cluster-wide (preflight's old fixture) | the suite stops at [20b], in 046's audit backfill: `could not serialize access due to concurrent update` (40001) | — |
| Repeatable read on the other database only (preflight's fixture now) | — | 315/315 (as a setting on the role in that database, too) |

Preflight's session gets the database's setting: with it dropped, the repeatable-read assertion is the one that fails. The remedy the warning names holds over it: after `ALTER ROLE … SET default_transaction_isolation = 'read committed'` a new session in that database is at read committed.

**Measured after.** The step, replayed from the workflow's own `run:` block, at 20725295 unless said:
- **Locally**, against a `with-postgres.sh` database: 40.6–47.2 s over ten runs, all green. The two suites took 76 s in series.
- **In CI's shape**, `oven/bun:1.4.0` at `--cpus 4` beside `pgvector:0.8.6-pg16` on a private network: 43.4–43.8 s over three runs, and 48.0–56.7 s over three more on a busier host (timed from the bun container's start, so a few seconds over the longer leg), all green. The same two suites in series took 108.7 s and 88.0 s. At 1e8978fd, three more took 43.0–45.8 s.

**Not taken.**
- **A lock-timeout knob on the migrator.** It would take [7]'s 30 s to about 3. But `LOCK_TIMEOUT_S` is "Ten, and only ten" (config.mjs): 023's hashed body sets 10 s transaction-local, so under `--reapply` a shorter value would be false for the run's tail. And it would buy little: the two legs are close, and which is longer depends on load. Locally and at `--cpus 2` test-upgrade was, about 41 s to preflight's 33; at `--cpus 4` on a busier host preflight was, 47–51 s to 42–47. Preflight is CPU-bound and grows with load, while test-upgrade mostly waits. The knob saves up to about 8 s when test-upgrade is the longer leg and nothing when preflight is.
- **Fewer preflight runs in [5].** Each case asserts a state's own output. Merging cases would change what each asserts.
- **Starting test-upgrade at the job's first step and joining it at the end.** That hides all of its time, not only preflight's share. But it needs a pid across steps and a join step. The pair in one step reuses SMD-2092's shape.
