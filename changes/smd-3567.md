## Changelog

The model-provider stub that Fork Checks' four stack jobs embed through gives each connection its own thread. A connection that never ends, left by a container stopped mid-request, can no longer hold the stub and fail every later embed (SMD-3567).

## FORK

The stack jobs' provider stub serves each connection on its own thread (SMD-3567)

**What changed.** `.github/ci/full-stack-up.sh`'s provider stub, the Python listener on the runner's :11434:
- **Threads:** it is a `ThreadingHTTPServer`, where it was an `HTTPServer` serving one connection at a time.
- **Its queue:** up to 128 connections wait to be taken (`request_queue_size`), where `socketserver`'s default is 5.
- **A read timeout:** the handler's `timeout = 10`. A read silent for 10 s, before the request line or partway through the body, drops the connection, and the stub's log says `Request timed out: TimeoutError('timed out')`. This doesn't keep the stub answering; the threads do that. It stops dead connections, and their threads, from piling up.
- **The reply:** it is written with no timeout. Python's `sendall` takes a socket timeout as the limit on the whole write, so `timeout = 10` would also cut any reply that takes longer than 10 s to read.
- The switches `provider-stub-wrong`, `provider-stub-slow` and `provider-stub-slow-embed` are as they were, and the script's comment says why the stub has threads, the queue and the timeout.

**Why.**
- **Seen:** on 2026-10-29, in Fork Checks run 40874482524's first attempt ([OB1 PR 569](https://github.com/MHarris-SgyMd/OB1/pull/569)), "Full stack, no Supabase":
  - the stub answered its last request at 12:38:12, as the GUI step's case of an 800 kB thought ended;
  - the plugin step after it recreated `api` at 12:38:13 and 12:38:20, the authorization-server step at 12:38:26, and the webhook step, two steps on, at 12:38:41;
  - each `api` start probes `GET /models`, and an answered probe is a line in the stub's log; none of these four has one;
  - the webhook step's `api` logged `provider: … does not answer (no HTTP answer in 2.5 s)`, and its delivery timed out (curl exit 28);
  - the stub's log has no line after 12:38:12, and no error.
- **Most likely cause:** the mechanism below, with `api` the client stopped mid-upload as the step recreated it. The run doesn't show that, or what kept the stub from reading at the time; it shows the stub silent from the first restart on.
- **The mechanism, reproduced** on podman machine, with the stub and its clients in containers on one network. The stub was held busy by its `provider-stub-slow-embed` switch, so it read nothing else. A client posting a large body was then removed with `podman rm -f`:
  - **The connection never ends:** the rest of the body, and the close behind it, waited in the client's socket for the stub to read, and went with the container's network. The stub's side of the connection got no EOF and no reset.
  - **Main's stub stayed stuck:** it kept waiting on that connection, and the next client's request got no answer in the client's 15 s.
  - **Size:** 1 MB and 20 MB bodies left it stuck; a 64 KB body did not, since it fits the socket buffers and the close arrives.
  - **Removal alone doesn't do it:** the same 20 MB body, not removed, was answered, and the next request with it.
  - **A Bun client too:** a Bun `fetch` removed the same way left the same never-ending connection.

**Held.** Through scripts that start the stub in a container and drive it from other containers, on Python 3.12 (the runners') and 3.14:
- a connection that sends nothing, held open, while another client embeds: the embed is answered at once (main's stub: no answer in 5 s);
- that connection is dropped by 11.5 s, the log saying so (main's: still held);
- a request line and a partial body, held: the other client is answered, and the partial one dropped at 10 s;
- a client removed mid-body, 1 MB or 20 MB: the next request is answered at once (main's: no answer in 15 s). The body's own thread had read it all before the removal;
- a reply of 10.3 MB, 2,000 inputs, read at 300 kB/s: all of it, in 34 s;
- 200 connections opened at once: each answered with its own text's vector, in each of 6 bursts;
- `provider-stub-slow`: GET /models in 2.0 s, and 0.002 s with the switch off;
- `provider-stub-slow-embed`: an embed in 6.0 s;
- `provider-stub-wrong`: dimension 0 at 0.954, against 0.0 for the right model;
- a non-embeddings POST: the 404 the observability job reads.
- **Mutants,** each failing the case that guards it:
  - with no timeout, the silent connection is never dropped;
  - with one thread, the embed beside it waits;
  - with the timeout left on the reply, the slow reader gets 6.1 MB of 10.3 and curl exit 18;
  - with the queue at 5, as main's stub has it, 3 of 7 bursts lost 1 to 5 connections to a reset.

**Not the product's.** The ticket asked whether the server's embedding client leaves a connection half-sent when it gives up. It doesn't. A Bun `fetch` aborted by its deadline partway through a 20 MB upload, as `embed.ts`'s `exchange` aborts at `OB1_LLM_TIMEOUT`, reset the connection: the server read 125 KB, then a reset. Only a container going away mid-body leaves one that never ends, and against a real provider that costs a connection, not the provider.

**Not taken.** `release.yml`'s rehearsal has a stub of its own. It answers only GET, so no request body is ever left waiting in a client's socket.
