## Changelog

Every read that returns a thought — `search`, `search_thoughts`, `search_thoughts_keyword`, `list_thoughts` and `fetch` — now says who holds a ticket it names: a `claimed` field with each unreleased work claim's ticket, piece, holder, label, times and state (live, or lapsed and never released), a `⚠ Claimed: …` line in the text, and a `[claimed: …]` title prefix on `search` and `fetch`. An answer to "what should we work on next" sees held and possibly paused work before it calls `list_claims` — a prompt, not the check AGENTS.md's rule still makes (SMD-3599).

## FORK

A result naming a claimed ticket says who holds it — `claimed` beside `moved` on every read of a thought (SMD-3599)

**What changed.**
- **The label.** `core/reads.ts`'s `claimedOf`, beside `movedOf`, reads the unreleased claims — live, and lapsed but never released, which AGENTS.md reads as work that may be paused — on the tickets each result names: one read per page of `work_claims`' unreleased rows (store `unreleasedClaimsOn`, 115's partial index), none when no result names a ticket.
  - A result's tickets: the ones its metadata files it under (a ticket row's `issue`, a note's `ticket`), then the first 50 others its text mentions (`keysInOrder`, `MOVED_READ`); `claimedNotRead` counts the rest. A read stopped at its reach of 1,000 counts every ticket it brought nothing back for, and the last it reached, as not read: an over-count rather than a miss, the database's order being its collation's.
  - Each claim: its id, ticket, piece, holder, `ownKey` when the holder is the caller's key, label, when it was claimed and runs out, and its `state`. The first ten per result, `claimedMore` the rest.
  - Unlike `moved`, a ticket's own row and a superseded thought are labelled: who holds a ticket is a fact about now, not about when the thought was written.
  - Best-effort, as `moved`: a brain before 115, or a read that fails, gives `claimed: null`, which every reply says, and the read goes on; a result naming no claimed ticket `[]`. It never moves a rank.
- **The surfaces.** `search_thoughts`, `search_thoughts_keyword` and `list_thoughts` carry `claimed`, `claimedMore` and `claimedNotRead` on every hit; `search` and `fetch`, the ChatGPT pair, carry them only beside a claimed ticket — the tickets not read with them, as `movedNotRead` rides beside a move — or as `claimed: null` on every result for a failed read, so every other result keeps the shape exactly (test-e2e-sql pins it). The claims read runs beside `supersededAmong`'s (`search_thoughts`, `list_thoughts`), `movedOf`'s (`search`) or the click-through's log (`fetch`).
- **The words.**
  - Over MCP the three text tools print `⚠ Claimed: SMD-3379 piece "PR 1b-2" by codex since …, until … (claim …, label "…")` — `lapsed at …, never released` for a lapsed one — as the claim tools say a claim in the way; the piece and label quoted, cut and on one line, being the holder's words. The line adds the claim tools' readings: a lapsed claim never released may be paused work, ask the user; one by the caller's own key may be this session's, another's or a lost token's. Tickets not read are said with no claim shown too, and a failed read once per reply.
  - `search`'s and `fetch`'s titles start `[claimed: SMD-3379 piece "PR 1b-2" by codex, since …]` (or `lapsed at …`) after any moved prefix — the first two claims, never the label, which every ChatGPT surface hands the model.
  - The three text tools' structured values keep each claim's id, ticket, holder, `ownKey`, state and times; the piece and label ride the text, as `WORK_CLAIMED`'s value keeps them out. `search`'s and `fetch`'s JSON, the ChatGPT shape and its own text, carries every field, label included, as the REST answer does.
- **The descriptions** of the five tools say what `claimed` is, that no `claimed` makes no claim, and that it is a prompt, not the check; the OpenAPI document and the rest-client are regenerated; server-portable/README.md has a section beside the moved label's.
- **The rule** (AGENTS.md and CLAUDE.md, step 1): a result's `claimed` label is a prompt, not the check — work it shows is held or may be paused, a result without it says nothing.

**Why.** On 2026-10-29 a "what next" answer recommended SMD-3379 PR 1b-2 while another session held it: the brain's search results named the ticket and gave no sign it was taken (SMD-3596). Claims now work end to end (SMD-3597, 3598, 3602, 3654), but a claim only behind `list_claims` is seen only by an agent that thinks to ask.

**Measured after.** On Postgres with 2,000 ended claims and 40 live ones, a page of 10 hits naming 50 tickets each, through the first version's `list_work_claims` read: `claimedOf` 1.06 ms median, `movedOf` 1.90 ms, the two in parallel 2.42 ms — about 0.5 ms on a search that takes around 10 ms (200 runs each, after 20 warm). Review pass 1's cold reader timed the read at the bound — `list_thoughts`' 1,000 rows of 50-odd keys, 52,000 keys — at 51 ms. Re-timed after pass 1 with the unreleased read the label now makes, same setup, a slower run of the machine: `claimedOf` 2.35 ms, `movedOf` 3.38 ms, the two in parallel 3.60 ms; `claimedOf` at the bound, 1,000 hits naming 52 tickets each, 81 ms (20 runs) — a 1,000-thought `list_thoughts` page, whose default is ten.

**Not taken.**
- **A migration or a new SQL function.** The server role already reads `work_claims`, and 115's partial index on its unreleased rows serves the read; `list_work_claims` gives live claims only, or every claim, released ones included, which would spend the read's reach on history.
- **The label in a title.** The maintainer chose the field and the text, quoted; a title is what every ChatGPT surface hands the model as the result's own words.
- **The operator GUI's search page.** It shows `moved` from the REST answer; showing `claimed` there belongs with SMD-3601's claims page, whose ticket now names it.
