## Changelog

A supersession proposal's review now names who wrote each side: `db/consolidate.ts --list` and the `list_supersession_proposals` tool show each side's writer and trust, whether its text is a capture-only key's, the ingested notice, and the later captures an accept would un-supersede (SMD-2707, and SMD-2480's proposals half).
A proposal whose side to stand is a capture-only key's text, or of a recorded trust below the other's, is flagged WRITER, and `--accept` refuses it without `--force`; an accept names each later capture it un-supersedes.
On a brain that has not applied migration 093 the listings say they cannot name the writers, and an accept goes ahead without the writer check, saying so.

## FORK

A proposal's review names who wrote each side, and flags a capture-only key's or lower-trust side proposed to stand (SMD-2707 / 2480)

**What changed** (migration 093).
- **`ob1_capture_only_text(id)`**: the first capture was under a capture-only key and 082's log has not taken it (no re-capture by a key that can read, no text move, no adoption). An accept's taking leaves it true: the text is still the key's.
- **`ob1_accept_takes(side, other)`**: 092's taken read with the side's pointer read as the other — whether an accept making the side stand takes it from its capturer.
- **`ob1_accept_lapses(side, other)`**: 092's lapse read and not written, as `[{id, onto}]`. None when the accept writes no event (the side already points somewhere; the pointer would loop, or run past update_thought's 1,001-row walk) or does not take the side; else its capturer's capture-scoped pointers onto it, up the chain, and on from a cleared row the log already took. Kept in step with 092's trigger by hand.
- **`list_proposal_writers(uuid[])`**: per proposal named (any status, 200 ids read), each side's `actor_name`, `actor_kind`, trust, `capture_only`, `superseded_by`, `lapses` (none on an accepted row), `takes`, and `same_capturer`. A companion of 070's listing, which is unchanged: recreating 070's form with more columns broke every `--reapply` after it (measured).
- **The readers.** The stores and `--list` read the listing, then the writers by the page's ids (a proposal gone in between is dropped); the accept guard reads the one row.
  - Under each side's ID line: its `By:` line, `· a capture-only key's text`, its superseder, and the pointers an accept making it stand would clear. An ingested side's notice before its text (SMD-1724's rule).
  - **WRITER** (`standingFlags`, one rule for the reply, `--list` and the guard): the side that stands (an accepted row's superseding_id, else `--direction`, else the verdict, each side an undirected one leaves open) is a capture-only key's text the accept would pass another's standing to — another capturer's on the other side, or one key's both and the accept takes the side — or carries a recorded trust below the other's. The line names the pointers the accept would clear; the accept line carries `--force` (per direction on an undirected verdict).
  - `--accept` on a flagged row (pending, stale or rejected) is refused without `--force`, naming both writers, the pointers it would clear, the `--list` status to read them under, and an edit since judged the `--force` would also waive. A lineage pair's refusal names a WRITER flag too. With no `--direction` on an undirected verdict, 029 asks for one first. The accept reply names each pointer it cleared, read off its own transaction's events (xmin).
  - The reply's value carries trusts, capture-only marks, superseders, lapses and flags — no writer's name.
- **Boyscout:** 070's lineage guard on `--accept` reads rejected rows too, which 029 accepts.

**Why.** A reviewer decided a proposal from both texts, cut at 160 or 200 characters, and nothing of their writers. SMD-1873's review pass 3 showed a capture-only key's near-copy accepted over a write key's thought. 092 (SMD-2708) stops the key passing that standing on; this shows the reviewer who wrote what. SMD-2708's review pass 1 added the comment's items: a side its capturer has since superseded, and an accept that cleared a pointer without saying so.

**Decisions** (the maintainer's).
- **Plan stage:** `--force` on a flagged accept, as on a lineage pair; SMD-2480's proposals half here; the trust always shown, on the read tools' `By:` line.
- **Review pass 1:** a side with no trust recorded is not flagged for it.
- **Review pass 5:** the lapse preview ships in this PR, with follow-ups for one definition of the lapse and a committed fuzz.

**Not here.**
- `review_supersession_proposal` is unchanged: the guard is the CLI's (070).
- Why a lapse happened, on its event: SMD-2933. `thought_changes`' trust: SMD-2480.
- A capture-scoped capture with no agent id is never taken by 082's log, so its text reads as a capture-only key's after an edit (082's gap).
- A bound on the listing's cost: a page costs with its chains — 20-30 ms for a realistic page of 200, about 7 s for 200 proposals under 1,000-link chains.
- One definition for the lapse, and the fuzz that held the read to it (about 106k comparisons over passes 3-5), committed.
- The guard reads the flags before the accept's transaction; a change landing between them is not seen.

**Tests.** test-schema [83] (the shape; each side's writer and marks; what an accept would lapse against the accept, run and rolled back, across chains, re-points, refusals, accepted rows and a planted pre-082 state; the takes read; the 200 cap; re-apply), test-upgrade [20an], test-live [16] (`--list`, the refusals, an equal-trust pair, a rejected, lineage and undirected row, the accept's lapse reply, a brain short of 093), test-store-sql and test-store-postgrest [10], test-e2e-sql [13i] (MCP and client modes, an equal-trust pair), test-server [16l] (the flag rule's cells and the words). Thirty-five mutants over four rounds, every one killed; the xmin key is held by review and the lapse cell, since only concurrent accepts tell it apart.
