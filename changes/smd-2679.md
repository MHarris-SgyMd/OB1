## Changelog

`db/rebuild.ts`, the operator's door to `rebuild_derived`, is now an importable engine: `run(opts)` runs it in-process and `sweepOrphans(sql, opts)` runs its orphan sweep on a caller's client. The CLI is unchanged (SMD-2679, the first of its PRs).

The sleep scheduler now runs each sleep under a budget (SMD-2679, its second PR). A sleep runs at most 60 minutes (`--budget`, `OB1_SLEEP_BUDGET`; 0 for no clock). Then its passes stop after the thought in hand, and the next sleep begins only after a live call. Each pass's connections open with `statement_timeout` 300 s, `work_mem` 4 MB and `maintenance_work_mem` 64 MB (`--statement-timeout`, `--work-mem`, `--maintenance-work-mem`, and their `OB1_SLEEP_*` settings). A statement past its timeout stops the sleep; before, it ended the scheduler. The heartbeat and preflight's `sleep` row say which stopped a sleep, the budget in force, and how many sleeps stopped on it. **Behaviour change:** a long quiet night now holds the model for an hour, not until the queues drain.

## FORK

rebuild.ts becomes an engine, and each sleep runs under a budget — per-pass session settings and a per-sleep clock, a stop counted and a statement past its timeout no longer the scheduler's end (SMD-2679)

### PR 2: the budget

**What changed.**
- **`db/connect.ts`:** `openSql(url, { max, session })`. `session` is Postgres settings every connection of the client opens with: Bun's `connection` startup parameters, so a connection the pool opens later carries them too (measured on Bun 1.4.3). It takes settings by name only: no `user`, `database`, `replication`, `options` or `password`, and no NUL. A transaction pooler (PgBouncer, a managed host's pooled endpoint) refuses startup parameters, so the scheduler connects to Postgres directly, as the compose stack does.
- **The engines** (`extract-entities.ts`, `consolidate.ts`): `run({ session })` applies them to the run's own client. Beside a caller's `sql`, which it would not reach, it is refused.
- **`db/sleep.ts`:**
  - **Four flags,** each run()'s option and each a compose setting:
    - `--budget MINUTES` (60; 0 none), `OB1_SLEEP_BUDGET`;
    - `--statement-timeout SECONDS` (300; 0 leaves the role's or the database's own), `OB1_SLEEP_STATEMENT_TIMEOUT`;
    - `--work-mem MB` (4), `OB1_SLEEP_WORK_MEM`;
    - `--maintenance-work-mem MB` (64), `OB1_SLEEP_MAINTENANCE_WORK_MEM`.

    The memory defaults are Postgres's own, so a server sized up for its awake index does not hand them to the background passes. These are stated defaults until SMD-1499 derives them.
  - **Where the settings apply.**
    - Every pass, and the start's check, run under them through `budgetSession()`.
    - A startup setting beats the role's and the database's own, so a timeout of 0 is left out.
    - The scheduler's own two connections take the memory settings and statement_timeout 0 (`schedulerSession()`), beating a role's or database's own. Its poll reads the pools, a scan of every thought, and a timeout there would end the scheduler. The clock is read between those reads, so a sleep can run one read past it.
  - **The clock.** Once spent, the passes stop after the thought in hand, every lease returned, and none starts. One still in hand 300 s later is stopped hard, a cut (101). A wake in that grace ends the sleep at once. The stop is counted and marked only when it cut work: either pool not drained as the drain reads it (consolidation's not yet enqueued included), or a pass waiting for its provider. An idle clock-out ends the sleep as quietly as a drained one, the next still after a live call, and one sleep's exits 0.
  - **A statement past statement_timeout (57014, in Postgres's "statement timeout" words; an operator's `pg_cancel_backend` is no budget's and ends the scheduler as before).** The pass ends, the others stop hard as a cut, and the sleep stops on its budget. Before, the pass's rejection ended the scheduler with 1.
  - **After a budget stop** the next sleep begins only after a live call and the quiet (`awaitWake`), as the maintainer decided: not after a rest. One sleep stopped on its budget exits 1.
- **The row** (`db/pass-stamp.ts`, `server-portable/brain-info.ts`):
  - `slept.budget` (`clock` or `statement`) says which stopped the last sleep, and `slept.limit` the bound it stopped at (minutes, or seconds);
  - `resting` says the scheduler waits for a live call before its next sleep;
  - `budget` carries the budget in force and the sleeps stopped on it since the process began;
  - each is left off when not of the shape;
  - a restarted scheduler carries the last sleep's word and bound, and sleeps after the quiet alone.
- **Preflight's `sleep` row:**
  - after a clock stop: "awake — the last sleep ended … stopped on its budget — its clock spent (60 min); the next begins after a live call and the quiet". This is ok, and says "after a live call" only while `resting`;
  - after a statement stop, a warning whose fix is the setting, in place of the generic "its last pass failed". It stays until the next sleep ends otherwise, or until the setting passes the bound it stopped at: raised past it, or set at all past the role's or the database's own (bound 0), which gets its own words and remedy;
  - a stopped scheduler whose last sleep a statement stopped is told to raise the setting before starting it again;
  - the stalled thought's fix names `OB1_SLEEP_BUDGET`, since no sleep now outlasts its clock;
  - the budget and its count are in the detail.
- **`db/config.mjs`'s restart command** lists all seven `OB1_SLEEP_*` settings.
- **Docs:** `db/README.md`'s sleep section has a budget table and the rule; sleep.ts's header has a Budget section; deploy/README.md and `deploy/.env.example` cover the settings.

**Why.** SMD-1794's step 2. A sleep ran until a wake, so a quiet night held the model and the passes' connections for as long as the queues lasted. Stable and the canary share one Ollama. A pass's statement timing out ended the scheduler.

**Upgrading.** The clock is on by default. Upgraded, stable and the canary hold the model for 60 minutes a night, and the rest of a backlog waits for the next live call and the quiet. `OB1_SLEEP_BUDGET=0` and a recreate restore the old behaviour; a larger value drains a backlog sooner.

**Not here.**
- A thought's own write that times out fails that thought, as any database error there does.
- A timeout is told apart by Postgres's English words, so under another `lc_messages` it ends the scheduler as before.
- A clock stop while the passes wait at their start for the provider clears that wait's word: the row reads the clock, not the provider, until the next live call.
- The clock also counts idle minutes, so a sleep whose pools drained still ends at it, and a sync write's capture after that waits for the next sleep.

### PR 1: rebuild.ts as an engine

**What changed.**
- **`db/rebuild.ts`** now has SMD-2304's engine shape:
  - **`run({ url, sql, input, reason, fingerprints, limit, gone, force, orphans, status, dryRun, writer })`** returns the code the CLI exits with.
    - Its usage refusals come first, in the CLI's words and order, before any connection.
    - Its lines go to the `Writer`.
    - A caller's client is used and never closed.
  - **`sweepOrphans(sql, { limit, dryRun, writer, onFound })`** is `--orphans` alone on the caller's client. The sleep scheduler's re-derive pass will call it (a later PR of this ticket).
    - It returns the thoughts it read, those it touched, the rows it deleted and the calls refused as a value.
    - It also returns `windowed` when the lineage table holds more than the 10,001 rows its read takes (in no order), so finding no orphans is then not a clean table. The count and the orphans are read in one statement, so they agree. The CLI's words are main's.
    - A `limit` that is not an integer is refused rather than rounded by Postgres.
  - **`hasRebuild(sql)`** says whether 063 is there.
  - **A dry run's call is a transaction of its own** (`sql.begin`, rolled back). A bare `BEGIN`/`ROLLBACK` held only on the CLI's one-connection client; through a pool, the call could land on another connection. A transaction's handle cannot hold that transaction (Bun refuses a begin inside one), so a dry run on one is refused before a query, by `run()` (exit 2) and by `sweepOrphans`.
  - The CLI is a thin `if (import.meta.main)` block over `run()`: the scanner, the URL, `--limit`'s digits, then `run()` through `closeThenExit`.
- **`db/test-engines.ts`:**
  - `rebuild.ts` joins the census: it imports with no side effect, and its code has no exit, no listener, no argv read and no console call.
  - The census's control runs `--status` for it, since its dry run needs a mode.

**Why.** SMD-1794's order puts the re-derive pass first in a sleep. `rebuild.ts` ran at top level with `process.exit` inline, so nothing could drive it in-process; the claim workers had the same shape before SMD-2304.

**Held.**
- **test-engines:**
  - `rebuild.ts` in the census;
  - **26 refusals**, each compared with the spawned CLI on both streams, exit 2, before connecting and without the password, and each held to main's own first line. The CLI goes through `run()` now, so the two agreeing alone pinned nothing: a refusal reworded, or two swapped, changed both sides. The cases:
    - no URL, an unparseable URL, no mode, two modes, `--input` with `--orphans`, a non-UUID `--input`;
    - `--gone`/`--force`/`--fingerprints` without `--input`, `--fingerprints` without `--gone` (and `--fingerprints ","`, refused as main refused it);
    - `--reason` on the sweep, `--limit` outside `--orphans`, `--dry-run` with `--status`, a blank `--reason`;
    - eleven that break two rules at once, where main's order decides: six neighbours (run-it, review pass 1) and five pairs further apart (cold read, review pass 2).
  - A caller's client is queried and never closed, and a bad URL beside it is refused.
  - A null url is read as absent; so are a null input, reason, fingerprint list or limit, beside a client, where `--status` runs on.
  - A dry run on a transaction's handle is refused by `run()` and by `sweepOrphans`, and so is a fractional or NaN `limit`, all before a query.
