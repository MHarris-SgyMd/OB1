## Changelog

A clear on a ticket now reaches the same key's statement on the ticket's own board-sync row (SMD-4127, migration 133). The priority writers take a ticket and its own rows as one item, as `effective_priority` already read them: a key's new statement on either replaces its statement on the other, and a clear naming either closes both. Before, `clear_priority` on a ticket left the key's older statement on the row standing, and the read showed it again. `priority_history` and `priority_of` list the ticket's and its rows' statements together, each naming its own item, and the replies' winner is theirs together. On upgrade, where one key already holds two open statements across a ticket and its rows, the older closes as replaced by the newest, the one the read already counted. The priority grant group gains `SELECT` on `thought_sources`, and the migration grants it to the roles holding the group.

## Record

A ticket and its own rows are one item to the priority writers — a key's statement on either replaces its statement on the other, a clear naming either closes both, and the history, `priority_of` and the winner read them together; migration 133 (SMD-4127)

**Why.** SMD-3667's `effective_priority` (122) reads a ticket and its own board-sync rows as one pool of statements, each maker's newest counting. 116's writers still keyed a statement by its exact `(kind, key)`, so one key could hold an open statement on ticket PRB-60 and another on PRB-60's row. `clear_priority('ticket', 'PRB-60')` closed only the first, and the read then showed the key's older statement on the row again: a stance the key had replaced and then meant to withdraw. The maintainer chose (SMD-3667's review pass 5) that the read takes each maker's newest across the pool, and that the writers follow here.

**What changed.** Migration 133:
- **The pool** is 122's: the ticket and each of its own rows (`ob1_ticket_rows`), whichever is named; any other item is a pool of one. `ob1_priority_pool_items` lists its items.
- **`assert_priority`** (117's body otherwise unchanged) closes, as replaced by the new statement, every open statement the maker holds across the pool. The same statement again is `unchanged` only when the maker's one open statement is on the item named; the same words on another item of the pool move it there. `replaced` is the newest it closed. A due reference into the item's own pool is refused as one naming the item itself.
- **`clear_priority`** closes the maker's open statements across the pool; `assertion` is the newest it closed.
- **`ob1_priority_lock`** takes the lock of the pool's key, `ob1_priority_lock_key` (the subject's, 122's `ob1_priority_subject`): a ticket's row locks as its ticket, so writers naming either wait on each other before they read. Any other item's lock is 116's, the same key; so is a thought key that is no uuid, which only a raw write leaves.
- **`ob1_priority_winner`** is 122's `ob1_priority_counted` with tier 4 counted, the rule's one copy: the pool, each maker's newest alone, then 116's rule. Both writers' replies, `priority_of` and the server's priority record (`GET /v1/priorities/{kind}/{key}`) name it.
- **`priority_history`** lists every statement across the pool, closed ones included, each naming its own item. **`priority_of`** lists the pool's open statements, the winner first, as in 116, and the rest in the rule's order.
- **`priority_current` stays keyed by the exact item**, as its drift check computes it: `ob1_priority_project` reads that item's own winner, and each writer re-projects every item whose statement it closed. No read joins it.
- **`ob1_rebuild_priority_projection`** takes the drifted items in their pools' order, each locked by the key it was sorted by, so two rebuilds take their locks in one order.
- **On upgrade**, where one maker holds two or more open statements across a ticket and its rows, each older one closes as replaced by the newest, and each item is re-projected. A maker is as the writers match one: its agent id, a statement made by name before the key had an id taking the id the registry gives that name now.
- **Grants:** every priority call on a thought or a ticket now asks which ticket's row a thought is, so the `priority` group (`db/config.mjs`) gains `SELECT` on `thought_sources`, and 133 grants it to each role holding the group's grants on `priority_assertions` and `SELECT` on `thoughts` already, as 132 carried its table. A role reading priorities without `thoughts` could make no call on a ticket or a thought before, and is given nothing. Preflight's priority row names its absence in a clause of its own.

The server's words: `priority_history`'s text and `set_priority`'s say where a statement stands when it is not on the item asked ("on thought …"). The three tools' descriptions, and so `rest-client/openapi.json`, say the pool. `docs/priority.md` decision 2, `server-portable/README.md` and `db/README.md` say it too.

**Not changed.** A thought that becomes a ticket's row after statements were made on it and on the ticket joins the pool with both standing: the read takes the maker's newest, and the maker's next write closes both. A write racing board-sync's recording of the row can leave the same, its lock read before the row committed, and so can a writer still running 116's body while 133 applies. A statement made before 133 whose due reference names its own pool stays open, and the read still finds no due time through it: closing it would withdraw a stance its maker took. 116's one-open index stays per exact item: membership is a read of `thought_sources` and `thoughts`, which no index can hold. The read still tells a key's by-name and by-id statements apart (122's `maker_key`); after the upgrade a key holds both only where a thought became a row late.
