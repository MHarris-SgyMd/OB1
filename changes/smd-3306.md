## Changelog

Migration 111 records a thought's own subject: a `subject` facet, one standing per thought, keyed `ticket:`, `pr:`, `version:` or `phrase:`, with its lineage at the text's fingerprint. `record_thought_subject` writes it, refusing a text that moved since it was read. The thoughts about one subject can then be read as one work thread, in capture order (SMD-3306).

The extraction pass now records each thought's own subject when a rule finds it: the ticket the thought is filed under, else the first ticket on its first line. `extract-entities.ts --subjects` finds the rest. It asks the judge model about a thought no rule keys, and takes up the thoughts extracted before migration 111 or edited since; `--subjects rules` asks no model (SMD-3306, its second PR).

## FORK

### PR 2: the subject found

**What changed.**
- **`server-portable/subject.ts`.**
  - The rules, in order: `metadata.ticket`, else `metadata.issue` (the fields node_state reads), when it is a ticket id; else the first ticket on the first non-blank line (077's pattern).
  - A ticket-shaped word counts as a ticket only under a prefix the brain files its tickets under (068's `ob1_ticket_head`, read once a run), so `UTF-8` or `SHA-256` on a first line leaves the thought to the model. A brain that files none counts any word but a short list of encodings, hashes, protocols, standards and licences. A ticket too long for 111's key is none.
  - The judge model's question: SMD-3299's round 4 own question word for word. That is step 1 of the mediated-pair test and version 2's rules 3 and 4, quoted from the pass's `MEDIATED_TEST`, then the thought as prompt 4 renders thought A. It is asked through `providerCall`, as decide mode's writer is.
  - `subjectKey` reads an answer whole into 111's shapes: the first ticket, else a PR's number (`PR-502` too), else a release's version, else a lower-case phrase of at most 200 code points. The recipe keeps the answer cut at 200. `subjectKeyValid` mirrors `subject_key_valid`.
  - The pass keys: `subject:rules@r1`, and `subject:<model>@p1` for the model's.
- **The pass.** After `record_thought_entities` succeeds, `processRow` records the rules' subject at the fingerprint the claim read, and asks no model. On a brain before 111, or under a role without the door's grants, the pass says so once and records none; any other error is said once. The thought's entities stand either way. The summary counts the subjects recorded when there are any.
- **`--subjects`, the sweep** (`db/subject-sweep.ts`, routed from `extract-entities.ts`).
  - It takes every thought with no standing subject whose lineage is at its current fingerprint, in id order, one at a time and without a lease: the door's lock and fingerprint check make it safe beside the pass.
  - The rules come first, then the judge model. An answer naming nothing records nothing.
  - An answer that is not JSON with a subject is counted, said and exits 1, as the pass fails a malformed answer. A timeout or a length refusal is counted unanswered, and a row the egress gate refuses is counted refused; both exit 1.
  - A rate limit, a server error or a lost connection is paused for and retried (`TRANSIENT_PAUSES_MS`), then ends the sweep, exit 1. Any other refusal ends it, exit 2.
  - A role without the door's grants is refused before any question, exit 2; a write the role is refused ends the sweep, exit 2.
  - `--subjects rules` asks no model. `--dry-run` counts and writes nothing, and reads on through a signal, as the pass's does. `--limit` caps the thoughts taken.
  - A policy that refuses every row refuses the sweep before its first question.
  - The first signal stops it after the thought in hand (130); the second aborts the call in hand.
  - It refuses a missing door by name, and a pass's flag beside `--subjects`.
- **`lineage.ts`'s `subjectRecipe`.** A rule's recipe is deterministic and names the rule. The model's names the model, the question's version and hash, the temperature and the subject it answered.

**Why.** The maintainer chose to find subjects inside extraction (2026-10-25): extraction already visits every thought on every brain, re-queues an edited one and runs under sleep. The rules and the model were checked against SMD-3299's 275 canary thoughts, where two blind raters agree on a ticket for 245. The two rules named a ticket for 232 of those and were right on 230. With the 27B asked about the other 13, 243 were right (99.2%). The model alone, asked this question (version 2 of the test's step 1), was right on 241; PR 1's 242 was version 3's. The raters agree with each other on 98.1%. So the pass records what the rules find, at no model's cost, and leaves about one thought in twenty to the model. The model step is the sweep's, not the pass's: a thought's extraction keeps its one call per window, and the judge model, unmeasured on the stable brain, can be left out there with `--subjects rules`.

### PR 1: the facet (migration 111)

**Why.** The consolidation pass decides "evolves" pair by pair: H2O's verdict, then, since decide version 4, a 27B link step that caps a pair linked only through a third subject (SMD-3203). Three rounds of asking that step another way did not meet their bars (SMD-3299). SMD-3116 defines evolves as a later segment of the same work. That is a property of each thought's own subject, not of the pair. Asked of one thought alone, the 27B names the ticket blind raters agree on for 241 to 242 of the 245 canary thoughts whose own subject is a ticket, under the test's versions 2 and 3, where the raters agree with each other on 98.1% (SMD-3299, comment ac4f9321). A work thread is one subject's thoughts in time order. This PR records the subject; the pass that finds it, the threads and the consolidation pass's use of them follow.

**What changed: migration 111.**
- **The `subject` facet kind.** `thought_facets_validate` is 102's body, with the kind check admitting `subject` and a new branch. The payload is:
  - `key`: `ticket:<ID>`, `pr:<number>`, `version:v<n>.<n>[…]` or `phrase:<lower-case words>`. `subject_key_valid` is the one rule for the validator and the writer.
  - `by`: `metadata`, `first_line` or `model`.
  - `judge_key`: the pass that found it.
  - `origin: derived`, the validator's word.

  The facet is written standing and only ever closed, as a relation is (084). One standing subject per thought is a partial unique index, and a second index probes the key.
- **`record_thought_subject(thought, key, by, judge_key, agent, fingerprint, recipe)`,** the pass's one write.
  - It is refused, answering `stale`, when the thought's fingerprint is not the one the pass read.
  - The same key, way and pass is kept, its lineage moved.
  - Anything else replaces it, the old facet closed as history.
  - A NULL key closes the standing subject.
  - A missing thought answers `gone`.
  - It takes an advisory lock per thought, and the thought FOR KEY SHARE first, as 084 does.
- **Lineage.** `subject` joins the artifact kinds: 084's CHECK and `ob1_record_derivation`, on 084's body. The facet's row names the thought at the fingerprint read, in the same transaction (`ob1:subject-lineage-with-its-artifact`). An AFTER DELETE trigger drops the row with its facet.
- **Preflight's lineage check** fails a standing subject without its row, naming it, with the remedy: close it and record it again. It counts no subject as stale: the subject pass reads the fingerprint itself until `rebuild_derived` gains an arm.
- **It refuses by name** without 016, 042, 061, 084 or 102, reading 102 by its door's sentinel. A re-apply is a no-op. No grant moves.
