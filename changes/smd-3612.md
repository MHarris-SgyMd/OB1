## Changelog

Two test-live checks that read state once, right after a log line printed before that state is written, now wait for it. One read a re-embed claim before the worker handed it back, the other the sleep's end before the heartbeat row carried it; each turned main's CI red once on 2026-10-30. A third read of the same shape, a claim read once its vector is written, waits too (SMD-3612, SMD-3627).

## FORK

Three test-live rows poll for the state they assert, where they read it once right after a line or a write that comes before it (SMD-3612, SMD-3627)

**What changed.** In `db/test-live.ts`:
- **`[9f]`'s "…is no suspect at the next outage"** (SMD-3612) polls the thought's claim until it is out of hand, bounded at 5 s, and asserts the value the poll saw: `pending` and, as the first outage case already asks, `last_error` null.
- **`[9f]`'s "outages that come back at once wait longer each time"** polls its claim the same way, once the thought is at the model, and asserts `succeeded`.
- **`[38]`'s "a live call during the sweep stops it between thoughts"** (SMD-3627) polls `heartbeat:sleep` until the sleep's mark has its end, bounded at 5 s, and asserts the value the poll saw: `to` later than `from`, and a `from` other than the mark an earlier case left on the row, read before the run starts. Asserting the polled value, not a re-read, follows the awake stamp's poll earlier in `[38]`, whose re-read could catch the next sleep's stamp.

**Why.**
- **SMD-3612:** Fork Checks push run 40990711143 (7930d64b), extract cell: `claim {"status":"claimed","last_error":null}`. The follower prints `<id> goes back to the pool unrecorded` in `sendBack` (`db/reembed.ts`) and returns; the claim goes back to `pending` only in the worker's `finally` (`release_claims_for_worker`), after it.
- **The throttled case** has not failed on CI. It reads its claim once the thought's vector is written, and the vector's `update_thought` commits before `release_thought`, a statement later.
- **SMD-3627:** push run 41078539185 (19caf3c8), sleep cell: `slept {"from":"2026-10-30T15:13:45.360Z","to":null}`. The sweep prints `awake: a live write … during the re-derive sweep — the sleep ends` as it returns (`db/sleep.ts`). The scheduler marks the sleep's end after `asleep()` returns, and `stamper.slept()` only sets a field the row's next write carries (`db/pass-stamp.ts`): the stamp the scheduler writes at the end of that sleep's turn of its loop, moments after the line. The failing read came within those moments, and its `from` was the woken sleep's own.

**Held.** Each mutant ran on main 19caf3c8 and on this change, with the shard cut to the section (`[9f]` alone, `[38]` alone):

- **The sleep's end stamped 500 ms late** (a sleep after a woken `asleep()`): main's row fails; with the fix `[38]` passes, 89 of its 89 assertions.

- **A mark whose `to` is its `from`**: the fix's row fails.
- Unmutated, `[9f]` passes 30 of its 30 assertions and `[38]` 89 of its 89.

**Not taken.**
- **A larger delay for the sleep's mutant.** 1.5 s fails the row with the fix too, and a second row besides. The woken mark is written then, but by then the brain has been quiet for the case's whole 3 s window, so `awaitQuiet` returns at its first read without stamping and the next sleep's open mark replaces the woken one within milliseconds, too soon for a 250 ms poll. The sweep's line comes about 1.5 s after the waking write, so a mark written within about 1.5 s of the line stays on the row until the next sleep begins; 500 ms is inside that.
- **The other single reads that follow a log line** in `[9f]` and the sleep section were checked. Most read after the run has exited; the backed-off retry's row is stamped `failed` before its line; and `[9f]`'s deadlock-at-the-write case reads its claim at once after its line too, but its check, `status !== "failed"`, passes on `claimed`, so it cannot flake.
