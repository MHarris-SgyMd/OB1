## Changelog

Six suites' server children now start at PORT 0, where each suite picked a free port and freed it for the child; the port a child bound is read from Bun's start line. Children started together no longer race for a port, so a case can't read another child's answers (SMD-3296).

## FORK

Server children bind their own ports (SMD-3296)

**What changed.** `db/test-support.ts` exports `startedPort`, which reads a child's port from Bun's start line. SMD-3263 gave `serveChild` that read, and now `serveChild` uses `startedPort` too. These sites started their children on a port picked by binding port 0 and freeing it, and now start them at PORT 0:
- **test-server [13d]:** its eight children start at once, and `start()` is async. The seven independent children start together, then the calling MCP server, which needs its REST core's port.
- **test-server [13c]:** its two REST cores.
- **extensions/test-auth.ts:** each vendored server on the SQL shim. Its comment allowed a rerun when another process took the port; that comment and `freePort` are gone.
- **mcp-server/test-mcp.ts:** its three entry children.
- **test-egress [10] and [11]:** the REST cores and the MCP server's entry, the entry started once its core has said its port.
- **test-e2e-sql and test-rest-sql:** their one `api.ts` child each.

A child's stdout is drained as it comes, and read to its end where a row reads it. The `up` checks in [13c], [13d] and test-e2e-sql [18] now read the child's own keyless `/health` body: the REST core's `{"status":"ok"}`, or the MCP server's plain `ok`. A 200 from any other server no longer passes. jev's suite still hands its child a random `JEV_PORT`, a child that never serves.

Three ports must refuse: test-egress [10]'s and [11]'s provider ports until their stubs bind them, and test-server [13c]'s refusing database. Each comes from `db/test-support.ts`'s new `quietPort`: a free port between 20000 and 30000, below every kernel's range for port 0 (Linux 32768–60999, macOS 49152–65535). So no child at PORT 0 can be handed it once it is freed.

**Why.** On Linux, bind(0) picks a random port. So a port picked and freed for a child can be picked again by the next pick before the child binds it, or taken by another server. SMD-3263 measured that at about 1% of runs with nine children at once, and this ticket's review measured main's picks alone at 12 in 5,000 rounds of eight returning one port twice. When a port is taken, the child exits ("Is port N in use?"). Or worse, the server that took it answers the child's `/health` and the case reads someone else's answers. On macOS ports are handed out in sequence, so no local run shows it.

A probe of test-server [13d]'s start pattern (eight `api.ts` children in one tick beside a silent listener) ran on Linux, `oven/bun:1.4.3` at 4 CPUs:
- main's pick: 1 round of 400 had two children on one port, the loser exiting with its port in use and no start line;
- PORT 0: none in 400.

The review gave [13d]'s cutter and graced children one port on main. graced won the bind both times, whichever spawned first. cutter's stalled call then went to graced, and its row waited with no timeout. That matches SMD-2576's first CI line: cutter exiting 1 with no cut line after a long wait. Its second line, graced exiting 0 in about 300 ms, was not reproduced.

**Review pass 1.** A cold read, about 25 mutants (each survivor run against main), and runs on a CI-like runner. The runs were 51 each of the branch and main: test-server, test-mcp and test-auth in 4-CPU Linux containers, plain, loaded and two at once, and the database suites on macOS. Every one passed, which says nothing about a race of about 1 in 400; the probes are the evidence.

- **Fixed:**
  - test-auth said a child that crashed before its start line was "alive after 30 s": its stdout ended a moment before its exit code was set. It waits for the exit now (caught: cold-read);
  - test-egress [11] held its three ports at once before; with the children at PORT 0, one could be handed the freed provider port and the suite crash later. Named at the first row, then made impossible in pass 2 (caught: cold-read);
  - the `up` checks in [13c], [13d] and test-e2e-sql [18] passed any server's 200, the very failure the ticket names; they read the child's own body (caught: mutant);
  - ctrlC's keyed fetch in [13d] was uncaught, so a dead child crashed the suite, and its comment said it built a store it has no connection string for (caught: mutant);
  - [13d]'s stalled calls had no timeout, so a call another child answered hung the suite rather than failing the row; bounded at 10 s, a timeout said as its own outcome (caught: run-it);
  - the record said every suite, where jev's still gives its child a random port, and described the race as a freed port taken by another server, where the commonest case is two picks returning one port (caught: run-it).
- **Seen, not this change's:**
  - test-egress [10] reads its second REST core's port only in its `up` check: the MCP entry pointed at either core passes (caught: mutant);
  - test-mcp's `call`, test-egress's `rpc` and test-rest-sql's scan read throw on a dead child and crash the suite, as they did when a child refused on its picked port (caught: mutant).

**Review pass 2.** Again a cold read, 22 mutant runs, and 108 runs, 54 each of the branch and main: test-server and test-auth in 4-CPU Linux containers with up to 16 burners and two at once, and the database suites on macOS with host burners. No row failed on the branch. [13d]'s stalled calls were cut in 505–614 ms (cutter) and 1,305–1,416 ms (graced), far inside their rows' bounds and the 10 s timeout.
