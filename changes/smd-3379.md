## Changelog

Background model calls are held — the sleep passes', board-sync's, the vector mender's, the provider reprobe's and a detached job's, among them — to a share of each model server's slots, across every process of the brain, by Postgres advisory locks, so a live call finds one free; a live call never takes a slot (SMD-3379 PR 2b-1). A semaphore whose database does not answer lets the call run without one, said once. Consolidation's per-pair `--timeout` no longer counts a wait for a slot. Across the brain's processes a share is a queue (SMD-3379 PR 2c-1): a call waiting for a slot waits in the share's queue, which Postgres grants in the order asked, one call at a time from each process, and the call at its head takes the first slot let go, told on the `ob1_slots` channel where another process let it go, not a poll later — two processes' backlogs keep a share busy between them and split it evenly, where a slot let go sat idle until a poller's next try; a process waiting holds one more connection, its subscription to the channel, closed 2 s after its last wait. Among the sleep scheduler's own calls waiting for a share, consolidation's go first, and one queued `OB1_SLEEP_MAX_WAIT` seconds (new, 60 unset; its `--max-wait`) goes first at the next turn, after older ones that go first — an order, not a timeout (SMD-3379 PR 2c-2).

Model calls are measured (SMD-3379 PR 2b-2a): its own time a point on `ob1.model.call.duration`, a background call's wait for a slot one on `ob1.model.slot.wait.duration` by how it ended, the decide tier's calls a span of their own and every provider point labelled live or background; the followers — the claim engines, the sleep scheduler, board-sync — which run no telemetry, carry the same as tallies on their pass stamps, which `brain_info`'s new Model calls row says. Preflight shows `OB1_BACKGROUND_SLOTS`' shares in force.

On by default for a local model server (SMD-3379 PR 2b-2b): `OB1_MODEL_SLOTS` (new, 4 unset) is its parallel calls per model, handed to the bundled Ollama as `OLLAMA_NUM_PARALLEL`, and its background chat and embedding calls are held to one fewer, the decide tier's to one; a remote provider's are not held. `OB1_BACKGROUND_SLOTS` (new) merges over the defaults — types with per-model overrides, e.g. `chat=2,chat:qwen3.8:27b=1`, holding every endpoint — and `off` holds none; a value that does not parse is said, and the defaults stay. Preflight warns where a share leaves a model server no slot for live calls, and a webhook's deferred capture renews its delivery's claim while it waits for a slot. A slot broker for the brains on one host (SMD-3379 PR 3a): `deploy/federated/`, a compose project of its own beside the edge proxy, run by `deploy/federated/federated.ts up`, hands the model servers the brains share their slots — a live call at once, a background call in turn across the brains within the room the live calls leave — each brain by a key of its own; no brain asks it yet.

### PR 2c-1: a share's queue across processes

**What changed.** `server-portable/slots.ts`: a process's call waiting for a slot — one at a time per share, its others queued behind it in the process, as before — waits in the share's queue: a lock of the share's, -1 less its size (slot -2 at N=1), taken with `pg_advisory_lock`, which Postgres grants in the order asked. Each share size is a queue of its own, so a process counting the share's slots otherwise — a CLI run without the stack's `OB1_MODEL_SLOTS` — does not wait behind a head that may not take the slot free to it (review pass 1). The call at the head tries the slots, takes the first free one and lets the queue go; with none free it waits for one let go. A release reads who heads the share's queue (`pg_locks`): this process's own call is woken directly, as 2b-1's local wake was; another process's is told on the `ob1_slots` channel (`pg_notify`, the share's key the payload); with no one at the head nothing is told. The release reads the heads in its own database alone: advisory locks are a database's. The head hears the channel on a subscription of the process's own (Bun's `listen`), opened at its first wait and closed 2 s after the process's last — a live one holds a process open, and a CLI never uninstalls the hook — and tries the slots again every 1 to 2 s untold, for a process that died or one from before this PR, at once when Bun re-subscribes after a dropped connection, and at once when its own session closes as it naps. A subscription that fails to open — Bun does not ask again after a first refusal — is said once, dropped, and asked again at a waiting head's round 2 s or more on (review pass 1); one gone deaf — Bun gives up re-subscribing a dropped one after some 3 minutes, and a path that swallows what it is sent never drops — is found by a ping of the process's own, sent at a nap no release ended and not heard back by a later one a second or more on — younger, another share's head may look while it is on its way — then dropped, said once, and subscribed again (review pass 2; pass 3: judged at once, twelve shares waiting dropped six healthy subscriptions in 30 s). A ping that fails is dropped, not failing the call open, and one lost while Bun re-subscribes is not judged. The subscription is asked for and not awaited, each head woken as it subscribes, so a LISTEN slow to answer holds no head's tries (review pass 3). 2b-1's waiting mark (slot -1, held shared) and the deference it drove — a call about to try sitting out a poll where another process's poller waited — go: the queue is the order. A poll (250 ms to twice it) stays only between sessions the database ends. A stop of a call waiting in the queue ends it at once, as before, and cancels the wait on the server with `pg_cancel_backend` from a connection of its own: Bun's query cancel leaves the server waiting (measured: still in the queue 500 ms on), and its close waits for the statement, so without it the queue would pass through the stopped call's backend. It cancels only a backend of the semaphore's still waiting on an advisory lock under that pid. The semaphore's sessions and its subscription start with `lock_timeout`, `statement_timeout` and `idle_session_timeout` none, beating a role's or a database's own — the wait in the queue is as long as the calls ahead of it, and a slot's session and the subscription sit idle while a call runs — and `client_connection_check_interval` 1 s, so a process killed while waiting leaves the queue within a second, not when the queue reaches it. Every other statement keeps its 5 s bound, and the connect too. **Tests:** test-local-provider [12]: a call behind another process's at the head waits in the queue though its slot is free, and runs 2 ms after the queue is let go; a role's `lock_timeout` and `statement_timeout` of 100 ms do not end a 400 ms wait, nor its `idle_session_timeout` the slot's session 300 ms into the call; another process at the head is told on the channel, 1 ms after the call ended, the key the payload, and no one is told with no one at the head; a process that waited ends on its own 2 s after its call ran; a call stopped in the queue ends at once and leaves it on the server; a process killed while waiting leaves it in about a second; two processes' backlogs of 20 ms calls at chat=1 make 138 calls in 3 s where 150 fit, 69 each; another process's ask gets the next slot but one, behind a backlog of one worker or three (0 and 1 of this process's calls started meanwhile, at most three allowed for a loaded runner's connect, where 2b allowed four and ten); the head that loses its connection as it naps takes the queue again on the new session, 11 ms on; a chat=3 process's call takes a slot free to it at once beside a chat=1 head; a subscription refused at a role's connection limit is said once and heard once the limit is lifted, each slot then told taken in 2 to 6 ms; a subscription gone deaf while open, its server's bytes swallowed, is found by the ping, said once, and subscribed again once the path passes, handoffs 4 ms before and 3 ms after; one closed as the process stops, its LISTEN unanswered, says nothing; three shares waiting in step in one process keep one subscription for 6 s; and a subscription dropped while a call heads the queue is re-subscribed, the head trying again then — a slot let go untold meanwhile taken 229 ms on, not at its next try; and a database that stalls under a slot leaves no session open, each ended a second on. 2b-1's rows for the mark — a slot let go not handed to this process's own poller while another waits, and a deferring call marking nothing — go with it.

**Why.** 2b-1 recorded that fairness across processes was a race, not a queue: a slot let go went to whichever poller asked first, and a call deferred a poll to another process's, so under two backlogs the share sat idle much of the time — two processes of 20 ms calls at chat=1 made 46 and 60 calls in 6 s where 300 fit, a process making calls back to back got about a third of the slot time, and the maintainer chose that fairness over the throughput for 2b-1 (2026-11-06), naming `pg_advisory_lock` as the way out with 2c. 2b-2b measured the same under the defaults, two re-embed followers keeping 1.54 of 3 embed slots busy, and the maintainer recorded it, 2c's FIFO the fix (2026-11-08).
