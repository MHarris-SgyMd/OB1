## Changelog

test-live's [33] gives its twenty thousand filler thoughts ids derived from their numbers, so the setup's pointer write needs no self-join. CI's planner had chosen a nested loop for that join, about 30 s of the section's 36. Fork Checks' service Postgres containers carry no health check: each job waits for Postgres over TCP in a step of its own, after its checkout and install, so those overlap Postgres's start where the runner held every step until the check first passed, about 6 s a job (SMD-3196).

## FORK

test-live [33]'s setup without a join; the service Postgres without a health gate (SMD-3196)

**What changed.**
- **[33]** (`db/test-live.ts`): the twenty thousand filler thoughts take `md5('[33] filler ' || n)::uuid` as their id, and the five thousand pointers are written as `supersedes = md5('[33] filler ' || (n + 10000))::uuid` with no join. The section's assertion also counts the filler's pointers, which must be 5000.
- **The service Postgres** (`.github/workflows/fork-checks.yml`): db-live, the data layer (both of its services) and the compat shim drop `--health-cmd pg_isready` and its options. A "Wait for the service Postgres" step runs after each job's install and before its first database step. It runs `pg_isready -h 127.0.0.1` inside the container, 0.2 s between tries, for up to 60 s. It stops at once if the container has exited, and on either failure it prints the container's last log lines. It checks over TCP, as `db/with-postgres.sh` does, because over the socket the entrypoint's initialising server reads as ready for a moment before the real one is up.

**Why.** The ticket's CI review, re-read on main's last six runs (to c960bdf0):
- **[33]:** 35.8 s median, of which the setup was 30.1 s on CI in one run read line by line. The planner estimated the self-join's two sides at tens of rows, against 20,000 and 5,000: the last ANALYZE saw some sixty rows (`reltuples` 61 when [33] begins), and `metadata->>'kind'` and `metadata->>'n'` have no statistics at any freshness, so their selectivities are defaults. On those estimates a nested loop sometimes won: on Linux (review pass 2), main's setup took it in 4 runs of 7, cost 1,464, for 14–36 s of UPDATE, with 100,445,000 rows removed by its join filter. On a Mac it took the hash join, 0.4 s. Which input tips it wasn't isolated, and an ANALYZE before the join wasn't tried. With the ids derived, the UPDATE is one scan of thoughts with no join to plan, 0.2–0.7 s, whatever the statistics say.
- **The health gate:** "Initialize containers" is a median 15 s per cell: the image pull, then the runner's wait for a healthy read: its polls back off from 2 s, so with Docker's 5 s probe the first healthy read is about 6 s in (actions/runner's `ContainerOperationProvider.cs` and `BackoffTimerHelper.cs`). Without a health check, the runner starts the steps when the container starts, and checkout, setup-bun and install run while Postgres initialises.

**Measured.**
- The rest shard passes locally (1010 assertions, 147 s). [33] didn't take the bad plan on the Mac before this change either, so its CI time is the PR run's to show.
- A mutant whose setup points nothing fails [33]'s assertion ("five thousand of them pointers (0)").
- The wait step was rehearsed under the workflow's shell (`bash -eo pipefail`) with podman standing in for docker. A fresh pgvector container was ready in about 1 s, and `ob1test` answered over TCP. A container with no Postgres in it failed the step at 60 s with its message. After pass 2, a container whose entrypoint exits (no `POSTGRES_PASSWORD`) failed it at once, with its log lines.
- actionlint 1.7.7 and its shellcheck pass on the workflow, and so does `scripts/check-fork-consistency.ts`.

**Not in this PR.** Items 3–6 of the ticket. The cells' sums no longer fit three: on main's last six runs the cells' suite steps read rest 202 s, long 223 s, sleep 191 s and extract 159 s. [38] has grown from about 100 s to 187 s and [10] to 109 s since the review. That's 745 s after [33]'s 30, about 248 s a cell in three, plus about 25 s of overhead. The run's longest job is 264 s (full stack, no Supabase). So the timer items, [38]'s outage schedule and late commit (item 4), [10] and [16]'s settle sleeps (item 6) and [38b]'s time scale (item 5), come first, and three cells (item 3) last, on the figures then.
