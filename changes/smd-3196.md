## Changelog

test-live's [33] gives its twenty thousand filler thoughts ids derived from their numbers, so the setup's pointer write needs no self-join. CI's planner had chosen a nested loop for that join, about 30 s of the section's 36. Fork Checks' service Postgres containers carry no health check: each job waits for Postgres over TCP in a step of its own, after its checkout and install, so those overlap Postgres's start where the runner held every step until the check first passed, about 6 s a job (SMD-3196).

test-live's [38] runs its retried refusal and its reset on an outage schedule from 2.5 s, and its late commit at a 5 s quiet. [10] and [16] wait for the events their fixed sleeps stood in for, and [10]'s stop during a probe runs at a 4 s call timeout (SMD-3196, its second PR).

### PR 2: [38]'s timers, and [10] and [16]'s settle sleeps

**What changed** (`db/test-live.ts`, items 4 and 6 of the ticket).
- **[38], the retried refusal and its reset:** an outage schedule from 2.5 s (`shortenOutage(2500)`), where they ran the shipped 5 s. The lines read 2.5 s, then 5 s, then 2.5 s again after a sleep that ran: the doubling and the reset, as before. The row is read 3 s into the 5 s wait and must be under 2 s old, where it was 4 s into a 10 s wait and under 3 s. `test-worker-bootstrap.ts` holds the shipped 5 s and 10 s. The section's last restore now puts the 2.5 s schedule back before shortening to 1 s, so the shipped one is what the section leaves.
- **[38], the late commit:** a quiet of 5 s, where it was 10. The sleep reads back as far as its quiet. The case now reads the transaction's start and, polled for, the row's mark of the sleep in progress, and asserts the transaction led the sleep by more than 0 s and less than the quiet. The sleep begins the quiet after the capture before it, and the transaction about half a second after that capture, so the lead is the quiet less that half second, 4.5 s. It fails if the scheduler's start outlasts the quiet, or if more than the quiet passes between that capture and the transaction.
- **[10] and [16], sleeps that stood in for an event,** now wait for the event, each bounded at 15 s:
  - a follower is polling once its first pass has stamped its heartbeat row: not ended, not running, with an outcome, since its start stamps the row running with none (1.5 s before, three followers);
  - a follower has met a database cut once it says "the database is not answering" (2.5 s before; [10]'s and [16]'s spawned followers, their output read as it comes with `watchStream`);
  - an in-process follower is asleep once its pass has stamped the row ok and not running, plus 300 ms to be inside the sleep (1.5 s before, two runs);
  - the alarm follower's tripped block once its row says so (2.5 s before).
- **[10], a stop during a probe after a timeout:** a 4 s call timeout and the stop 1 s after the probe arrives at the stub, where it was an 8 s timeout and the stop 10 s after the claim. The stub counts probes that arrive, answered or not, so the stop is timed from the probe itself: one during the hung call takes another path. A follower that ignored the stop would return about 3 s later, past the case's 2 s bound. The ticket's 3 s and 4.5 s would have left 1.5 s, under the bound, and the case without its tooth.
- **Kept:** the sleeps that are the mechanism under test: the cut held across a call (4 s, twice), the 4 s cut across the checks, the 3 s "three polls", the 1.5 s holds that show a word staying, the 1.2 s "nothing going on after", and the 0.5–1 s settles after a start's probe (an outage at the start is another path).

**Measured.**

  - a probe that ignores the stop (back 2,782 ms after it, timed from the probe's arrival).

**Review pass 1.** A cold read, and the three shards on Linux: twelve runs, eight of them under six or twelve busy loops, and main's three beside them.

### PR 1: [33]'s setup without a join; the service Postgres without a health gate

**What changed.**
- **[33]** (`db/test-live.ts`): the twenty thousand filler thoughts take `md5('[33] filler ' || n)::uuid` as their id, and the five thousand pointers are written as `supersedes = md5('[33] filler ' || (n + 10000))::uuid`, with no join. The assertion also counts the filler's pointers, which must be 5000.
- **The service Postgres** (`.github/workflows/fork-checks.yml`): db-live, the data layer (both its services) and the compat shim drop `--health-cmd pg_isready` and its options. A "Wait for the service Postgres" step runs after each job's install and before its first database step: `pg_isready -h 127.0.0.1` inside the container, 0.2 s between tries, for up to 60 s. It stops at once if the container has exited, and on either failure it prints the container's last log lines. It checks over TCP, as `db/with-postgres.sh` does, because over the socket the entrypoint's initialising server reads as ready for a moment before the real one is up.

**Why.**
- **[33]:** a 35.8 s median, of which the setup was 30.1 s.
  - The planner estimated the self-join's two sides at tens of rows, against 20,000 and 5,000. The last ANALYZE saw some sixty rows, and the jsonb predicates have no statistics at any freshness.
  - On Linux, main's setup took a nested loop in 4 runs of 7, for 14–36 s of UPDATE. On a Mac it took the hash join, 0.4 s.
  - Which input tips it wasn't isolated, and an ANALYZE first wasn't tried. With the ids derived, the UPDATE has no join to plan, 0.2–0.7 s.
- **The health gate:** "Initialize containers" was a median 15 s per cell: the image pull, then the runner's wait for a healthy read. The runner's polls back off from 2 s, so with Docker's 5 s probe the first healthy read lands about 6 s in.

**Measured.**
- **On PR 520's two CI runs:**
  - [33] took 10.2 s and 7.8 s;
  - "Initialize containers" 9–15 s per job;
  - the wait step 0–1 s.
- **Locally and on Linux:**
  - the rest shard passes;
  - a setup that points nothing fails [33]'s count;
  - the wait step, rehearsed under `bash -eo pipefail` with podman, passes a ready container, fails an exited one at once and fails one with no Postgres at 60 s.

**Review**, three passes and a tidy commit:
