## Changelog

Fork Checks' "Portable server" job starts `server-portable/test-server.ts` and `test-thoughts.ts` right after its first three installs, in the background, where they ran one after the other among its other steps; a step at the job's end waits on them and reports both. `test-server.ts` [17]'s keepalive ceiling case runs at 50 ms frames and a 500 ms ceiling, where it ran at 20 ms and 200 ms, and holds the ceiling by the time it was reached rather than by a frame count, and its frames' pace by the lower median of the gaps between their arrivals and the tick that stops it, so a stall of the event loop at its start under ~450 ms no longer fails it, where ~140 ms did; `mcp-server/test-mcp.ts`'s token cache row no longer fails when its token is minted and traded either side of a second (SMD-3470).

## FORK

Portable server runs its two timer-bound suites in the background (SMD-3470)

**What changed.**
- **fork-checks.yml, portable-server.** A step after the installs of `server-portable/`, `rest-client/` and `mcp-server/` starts `test-server.ts` and `test-thoughts.ts` in the background, each in a subshell with its own log, its exit code written by a rename, and a 300 s time limit. It is the data layer's pair (SMD-3195) in shape. The REST client's and MCP server's installs move up to before it, since `test-server.ts` drives an MCP server through both (SMD-2978); their comments go to the steps that run their suites. The two suites' own steps go. A last step, `if: ${{ !cancelled() }}`, waits on both up to the limit and the kill after it, and prints them as the data layer prints its pair: a passing log folded, a failing one open under an `::error` naming the suite, a hang as 124 (137 with TERM ignored), a start that never ran or a code never written each said, and both summary lines last.
- **`server-portable/test-server.ts` [17], two bounds a stall of the event loop broke.** Both are about timing under load, which the job's other suites beside this one now bring.
  - *The ceiling case:* a stream silent for 1250 ms with a 50 ms frame and a 500 ms ceiling, where it was 500 ms, 20 ms and 200 ms (nominally nine frames, about twenty-four uncapped). `withSseKeepalive` measures its ceiling from the stream's start, so a stall of the event loop there takes frames off the front, and a first tick past the ceiling sends none. The case held "stops at the ceiling" by a band of 4 to 10 frames, so at the old scale a stall of ~140 ms failed it on the floor (and ≥ ~190 ms gave `0 frames`, what the job's other suites beside it caused once, below). It now holds that by time: `onStall` records how long after the stream began the ceiling was reached, on `performance.now()`, the clock `sse.ts` reads, and a new row asserts at least 500 ms. The frames' count holds only that it pinged (at least one) and that it stopped (at most ten), so a stall at the start under ~450 ms passes (one later in the stream, under 500 ms). How often it pings is held by when the frames arrive (review passes 2 and 3): the client reads the stream, timing each frame, `onStall` times the tick that stops it, and the lower median of the gaps through that tick must be at most 75 ms, 1.5 times the frame. A stall at the start takes frames off the front and one later widens a gap, and neither moves it; a frame slowed at any multiple widens every gap. The section's other requests take 13 s, so the longer stream costs the suite nothing.
  - *"…at the moment the client left":* the abandoned line's time must be at least 1 s, where it was 1.4. The MCP server times the request from its arrival, and the client's 1.5 s from its send, in one process, so a stall between the two reads short: a 200 ms one read 1.3 s. The floor tells the abort's moment from a line written at once; the upper bound of 3 s, against the stream's 13 s, is unchanged.
- **`mcp-server/test-mcp.ts`, the token cache's "594 s on" row** (review pass 1). Its stub authorization server answered `expires_in` off the wall clock, while the exchanger it feeds runs on the suite's injected `clock`. When the token's mint and its trade fell either side of a wall-clock second, the answer was 599, the cache entry ended exactly at the row's 594 s, and the row traded again. The stub now answers the token's lifetime as issued, `exp − iat`, and `mint` reads the clock once for both. A real server answers the time left; the two agree when the mint and the trade fall in one wall-clock second, and the stub answers the lifetime so that a straddle can't move the entry. With the lifetime fixed, the row after it asks at exactly 595 s, the entry's `until` and the first instant it is gone, where it asked at 596 (review pass 2), so the cache's `until > now` is held at its edge. It ran beside the background suites now, and failed once in ~14 runs of the job beside the background suites.
- **`db/test-support.ts`:** the comment on the `N assertions:` summary line names Portable server's background pair among the steps that grep it.

**Why.** The ticket: the two suites were ~50 s and ~33 s of a job whose median was ~215–220 s, almost all of it waiting on real-clock timers (children's stop and flush bounds, [16]'s provider-state probes), one after the other on a runner whose cores sat mostly idle.

**Held.** The job's steps were run in order by a script generated from the workflow's own YAML, each under `bash -eo pipefail` in its working-directory, with GitHub's rule that a failure skips later steps except an `if: ${{ !cancelled() }}` one. Each run was in its own `oven/bun:1.4.3` container on 4 CPUs, as the image's non-root user, on a fresh copy of the tree, with the installs done by the job's own steps:
- **main's job: 189, 191 and 197 s.** The branch's, before the suite's two changes: 164 s, failed by the ceiling case alone (`0 frames in 500 ms at 20 ms with a 200 ms ceiling`; test-thoughts 845 of 845). The branch's after them: **96, 101, 110, 111, 119 and 135 s**, all green: test-server 1221 of 1221 (1223 since review passes 1 and 2 added a row each) and test-thoughts 845 of 845 every time, the last step's wait 0 s (both suites done before the job's last foreground step).
- **The wait step,** on stub suites in place of the two, under `bash -eo pipefail` on Linux:
  - both passing: exit 0, each log folded, both summary lines;
  - test-server failing with exit 3: the step fails, with `::error title=Portable server::test-server.ts failed (exit 3)` and test-thoughts' summary still printed;
  - test-thoughts hanging under a 3 s limit: `exit 124, over the 3 s limit` and "no summary line";
  - TERM ignored: `137, killed`;
  - the start step never run: "the two suites were never started";
  - a subshell that writes no code: "none, its subshell wrote no code by 1 s and 30 s".
- `bun scripts/check-fork-consistency.ts` passes: its reader reads both suites as run from `server-portable/`. Pinned actionlint 1.7.7 with shellcheck passes, and `tsc` in `server-portable/` is clean.

**Mutants** on `test-server.ts` [17], each the whole suite (`suite-mutant.ts`), as review pass 1 left it:
- the ceiling halved in `sse.ts`: KILLED by the new row (`254 ms after the stream began`). It passed the old band, on main too (4 frames);
- the ceiling dropped: KILLED (`24 frames`, onStall 0, `0 ms`, no stalled line);
- no keepalive frame sent: KILLED (8 rows);
- a busy stall at the ceiling stream's start of 200 ms and of 400 ms: both pass. 200 ms failed the build's version at the old scale (`0 frames`), the case the change is for;
- in `mcp-server/app.ts`, which writes the abandoned line: timed at once (`0.0 s`), 13 s late (`14.5 s`) and 0.6 s short (`0.9 s`) are each KILLED by the floor and bound.

On `test-mcp.ts`: with a sleep to the next wall-clock second between the token's mint and the ten asks, the row passes with the new stub and fails with the old one (`594 s on: still from the cache (2)`).

After review pass 3: the small intervals slowed ×1.6, ×2, ×3, ×4, ×5 and ×9 in `sse.ts` are each KILLED by the pace row (`80` to `451 ms`); ×3 to ×9 passed pass 1's floor of one frame, ×4 to ×9 pass 2's median, and ×2 main's band too. A 300 ms stall mid-stream passes, as do 200 and 400 ms at the start and 360 ms after the first frame (three left). In `mcp-server/exchange.ts`, the cache kept through its `until` (`>=`) is KILLED by the 595 s row; it passed on main and through pass 1.

**Not taken.**
- *Every suite in the background.* `test-rest.ts`, `test-mcp.ts` and the runner's self-check wait on timers too (~17, 15 and 12 s), but more suites beside the typecheck's seven `tsc`s and svelte-check is more load on timing assertions like the two above. The ticket's two take the job from the ~210–262 s cluster at no more risk than the two fixes cover.
- *test-thoughts [16]'s probes side by side* (the ticket's optional second item): in the background they cost the job nothing, and its rows passed in all 17 Linux runs of the branch's job with it there.
- *The suites' other short timing windows* (review pass 1, weighed, not changed): test-server [13d]'s `bothMs < 650` (nominal ~400 ms) and `duringMs < 400`, [13c]'s `head.took < 500`, [21]'s stalled line before a 600 ms answer, and test-thoughts' CPU-bound regex timings under 200 ms. None failed in 17 branch runs of the job (8 of them on 2 CPUs or beside four burners) or the 11 of main beside them. If one does, the [17] shape applies: hold the claim by a time the stall can't move.
- *Port reuse between the background pair and the foreground suites* (review pass 1): test-thoughts frees a port 0 and binds it again, and test-server's `quietPort()` must stay refusing, while other bun processes now take ports from the same range. No collision was seen. SMD-3296's fixes are the pattern if one is.
- *The abandoned line's floor back at 1.4 s:* it would need the client's 1.5 s measured on the server's own clock. At 1 s, a line timed up to ~0.45 s short passes, the trade the floor's comment states.

**Tidied while the files were open.** `server-portable/README.md`'s test-thoughts count (741, stale on main; 845) and `mcp-server/README.md`'s count for server-portable's test-server (1198; 1223); [17]'s comment reworded where "is the stall's own time" said what holds the ceiling's moment, and rewrapped; `readFrames` flushes its decoder at the stream's end (the text is ASCII, so nothing was lost).

## Review pass 1

A cold read and a run-it. The run-it ran the whole job 16 more times, branch and main alternating, on 2 CPUs and on 4 beside four `yes` burners, and mutants on the start/wait pair and on [17].

- **Fixed (cold read and run-it):** the ceiling case was held by a frame band whose floor, not the ceiling, failed under a stall (~140 ms at the old scale, ~340 ms at the build's new one), and the build's comment, Changelog and commit said "~120 ms … sent no frame", which was wrong. It is now held by the time the ceiling was reached. The halved-ceiling mutant, which passed on main and on the build, is now killed.
- **Fixed (run-it):** test-mcp's "594 s on" row failed once in the branch's runs: its stub answered `expires_in` off the wall clock beside an exchanger on an injected one. Proven by sleeping to a second's edge between mint and trade.
- **Fixed (cold read):** `db/test-support.ts`'s summary-line comment now lists the new step among its readers. "54 s … the job's median 215" became "~50 s … ~215 s" (ci-step-times over a week: test-server median 49 s, the job 220 s).
- **Seen, kept:** main failed the old ceiling case once with no background load at all (`2 frames in 500 ms`), so the case was fragile before this change. A failed REST client or MCP server install now also skips test-thoughts, which used to run before them.
- **Held:** an early foreground failure (the wait step still waited and printed both suites), a skipped start, a nonzero exit, a hang, TERM ignored and a missing code each reported; neither suite needs an install that now comes after it (both pass with only server-portable, rest-client and mcp-server installed); no shared ports or temp files with the foreground suites. After the fixes: the replay gate's job (37 s) and the portable job twice (127 and 125 s), green; the checker and `tsc` in `mcp-server/`, `server-portable/` and `db/` clean.

## Review pass 3

A cold read and a run-it (test-server 28 times on Linux, 12 of them as two concurrent streams pinned to 2 CPUs beside four burners each; the capped stream alone 200 times under the same load; test-mcp 20 times; the job twice; 15 `sse.ts` and 5 `exchange.ts` mutants).

- **Fixed (cold read and run-it, both):** pass 2's pace row judged frames only at three or more, so a keepalive slowed ×4 to ×9 for small intervals left one or two frames and passed every [17] row, which main's band of four caught. And with exactly three frames its "median" was the mean of two gaps, so one stall that left three could fail a correct keepalive (a 360 ms stall after the first frame, three in three in the run-it's harness). This was the third pass to find a seam in judging the frames under a stall, so it is now one rule rather than a patch: the series runs from the first frame to the tick that stops it (`onStall`'s moment, on the same clock in this process), judged by its lower median gap, at any frame count from one. Mutants slowing small intervals ×1.6, ×2, ×3, ×4, ×5 and ×9 are each KILLED (`80`, `101`, `151`, `201`, `251`, `451 ms`); stalls of 200 and 400 ms at the start, 300 ms mid-stream and 360 ms after the first frame (three left) each pass.
- **Fixed (cold read):** test-mcp's stub comment said a real server's answer and the stub's agree "within a second of its token's mint", which is false at a straddle, the case pass 1 fixed; it now says one wall-clock second. The 595 s row's comment called 595 s both the entry's last instant and when it is gone; it is its `until`, the first instant it is gone.
- **Seen, kept:** Bun's `setInterval` catches up after a stall with two ticks at once, a 0 ms gap, which pulls the median down; a mutant sending paired bursts at the right average rate would pass, as would frames alternating 25 and 75 ms (the right rate, no gap over 75). Neither is a slowed keepalive.
- **Held:** the pace row under real load (worst 54 ms in the suite, 59.8 ms alone, over pass 2's statistic); the 595 s row 20 of 20 on Linux, killing `>=`, the margin at 4 and at 6, and `> now() - 1`; the job 2 of 2 (114 and 121 s).
