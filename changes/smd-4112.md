## Changelog

The weekly digest takes what matters first (SMD-4112): `db/weekly-digest.ts` reads each thought's effective priority in the window, takes each day and writer's share what matters most first, and lists its picks for the synthesis model the same way, each with why it matters (its level and where it came from, its due time or overdue). A key that states Urgent on all its own captures still gets one row per day per turn. A brain before migration 122, or a priority read that fails, keeps the share by recency, and the run says why.

## Record

The weekly digest takes what matters first — each day and writer's share taken by effective priority and each pick saying why, a key's own Urgent captures held to their share (SMD-4112)

**What changed.** `db/weekly-digest.ts` reads the effective priority SMD-3667 added, as `docs/priority.md` decision 8 says it should:
- **The read.** `readPriorities` asks `effective_priority` for every thought the window read, in calls of its 1,000, under the brain's weights (the operator's setting over the built-in ones, since 132), and reads each row as the store does (`normaliseEffectivePriority`). A ticket's own row reads as its ticket. `readThoughts` reads up to 5,000 rows, from 400, so the whole window is ranked (a week on the canary is 554, two weeks 915), each row's text only as far as the prompt reads it (280 characters), and names each row's (day, writer) group. A window past 5,000 is said: each group's newest turns are read and ranked, the rest not.
- **The pick.** `pickForDigest` numbers each (day, writer) group's rows again, what matters most first, then newest. It takes the prompt's 80 a turn at a time as before, what matters most first within a turn, then lists them what matters most first, newest first among equals. With no priority stated, every row reads as unknown's 2, so the turns are the ones by recency and the pick and its order are the ones before this change.
- **Why each matters.** `priorityReason` says a pick's terms in its prompt row's `priority`: its level and where it came from (stated, from its open item, or inherited from a ticket or open item at that one's own level), its due time or overdue (said as its open item's when read from one), what it unblocks, and whether it is settled. A ticket's own row says so, so its ticket's statement does not read as the note's. Its level and due-time words are the MCP replies' (`render.ts`'s `LEVEL_WORDS` and `dueText`, now exported). A row whose priority is unknown alone carries no field, settled or not. The prompt's source line says the order when any row carries one; otherwise the prompt is the one before.
- **Not read.** A brain before migration 122 has no `effective_priority`, so the run says so and keeps the share by recency. A read that fails does the same, with a warning naming the database's words: the probe or a call failing, a role without the priority group's grants, an operator's setting 132 refuses, a row the store cannot read. The digest still goes out.

**Why.** Since SMD-2653 retired `importance`, the digest knew nothing of what matters: a week's Urgent ticket ranked with any note of its day. On the canary on 2026-11-11, the week's 554 thoughts were board-sync rows, one per ticket, and session-hook summaries, each read as unknown (2). The 80 picks keep 40 of each writer, as before; board-sync's 40 now hold 6 High tickets (3 before) and 10 Low ones (24 before).
