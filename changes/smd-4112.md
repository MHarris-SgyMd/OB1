## Record

The weekly digest takes what matters first — each pick ordered by the thought's effective priority and saying why, a share of each day and writer among equals (SMD-4112)

**What changed.** `db/weekly-digest.ts` reads the effective priority SMD-3667 added, as `docs/priority.md` decision 8 says it should:
- **One read.** `readPriorities` asks `effective_priority` for every thought the window read, in one call, under the brain's weights (the operator's setting over the built-in ones, since 132). A ticket's own row reads as its ticket. The rows read per run go from 400 to 1,000, the read's own limit, so every candidate's priority is read; a longer list is refused, never cut.
- **The pick.** `pickForDigest` takes the prompt's 80 by effective priority first, then by turn (the newest of each day and writer, then each one's second), then newest first. It says them most pressing first, newest first among equals. With no priority stated, every row reads as unknown's 2, so the pick and its order are the ones before this change.
- **Why each matters.** `priorityReason` puts a pick's terms in words in its prompt row's `priority`: its level and where it came from (stated, from its open item, or inherited from a ticket or open item), its due time or overdue, what it unblocks, and whether it is settled. A row whose priority is unknown alone carries no field. The prompt's source line says the order when any row carries one; otherwise the prompt is the one before.
- **Not read.** A brain before migration 122 has no `effective_priority`, so the run says so and keeps the share alone. A read that fails (a role without the priority group's grants, or an operator's setting 132 refuses) does the same, with a warning naming the database's words. The digest still goes out.

**Why.** Since SMD-2653 retired `importance`, the digest knew nothing of what matters: a week's Urgent ticket ranked with any note of its day. On the canary on 2026-11-11, the week's 548 thoughts were 306 board-sync rows and 242 session-hook summaries. Each summary reads as unknown (2); the ticket rows read from Low (1) to High (3). Ranked first by priority, the 80 picks keep 44 ticket rows and 36 summaries (40 and 40 before), and the Low tickets give way to the summaries. The read took 264 ms for 400 thoughts.

**Held.**
- **`test-weekly-digest.ts`:**
  - The ticket's Verify, pure: an Urgent ticket's row and an overdue item come before a newer unknown row and newer Low ones. Past the cap, the Urgent row at a late turn displaces an early turn's unknown one.
  - With every row unknown, the pick is the one before, on the busy-day fixture; among equal Highs the turns still hold.
  - Each term's words. The read over a stub: a brain before 122 is not read and says so, a failed read is said on one line, the read's flat terms are folded as the store folds them, and more than 1,000 ids are refused.
  - With no priority known the prompt is byte for byte the one before.
- **Eleven mutants,** each refused by its row: the priority order dropped from the pick, and from the said order; the turns among equals; "no level stated"; settled; the 122 probe; a failed read throwing; the 1,000 refusal; the field always present; the source line always ranked; a date-only due said as its stored instant.
- **`test-live.ts` [36],** through the CLI on a real Postgres: an operator states Urgent on a ticket whose own row is in the window, and Medium due yesterday on a thought, both two days old; Low on two thoughts a day old. The prompt leads with the ticket's row and then the overdue item, before every unknown row, with the newer Low ones left out. The two rows say `urgent, stated` and `medium, stated; overdue, due …`. Its first run caught the read passing the terms through unfolded (each said `level NaN`), which the stub had copied. (caught: run-it)

**Not taken.**
- **Holding each writer to a share of the priority picks.** A key the registry classifies can state a high level on its own captures and lead the digest with them. That is no longer SMD-2653's unattributed `importance`: a statement is ranked by decision 4's tiers, made by a classified key, and kept in its history, and the operator's statement on the same item wins. Per-key fairness now holds among equals, as the ticket asked.
- **Failing the run on a refused operator's setting,** as the agenda and a ranked search do (SMD-4193). The digest orders by no other weights: it falls back to the share, as a search's labels say "not read", and says so.
