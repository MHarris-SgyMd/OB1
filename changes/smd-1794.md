## Changelog

`db/sleep.ts` runs entity extraction and consolidation while the brain is quiet, and stops them within `--poll` seconds (5) of the first live call being recorded (SMD-1794). With `--follow` it sleeps whenever no write has been audited — sync writes aside — and, with the server's `OB1_QUERY_LOG=on`, no read logged, for `--quiet` seconds (300). It runs the extraction follower alone until its pool drains, then the consolidation follower beside it. A live event hard-stops both: every lease returned, the model call in hand aborted, the thoughts in hand moved to the back of the queue. Its heartbeat, `heartbeat:sleep`, is read by preflight's `workers` row, and its `consolidate pass` row reads the scheduler's work as running.

`db/extract-entities.ts`'s hard stop now aborts the model call in hand and sends no further window or retry, as `db/consolidate.ts`'s did. It is the second Ctrl-C, or the second call of the stop an in-process caller is handed. Before, run() waited for the call to answer — up to `--timeout` (300 s) per window, every remaining window of a long thought included — writing nothing for it.

## FORK

The sleep scheduler, `db/sleep.ts`: extraction and consolidation while the brain is quiet, yielding on the first live call — "dolphin sleep", its first cuts (SMD-1794)

**PR 1 — extract's hard stop aborts the model call in hand.**
- **Why first.** A yield is the pass's hard stop. Consolidate's aborted the judge's call (SMD-2304 PR 3). Extract's returned the leases but let the call in hand answer and the window loop go on to the thought's last window. On stable a thought takes p50 37 s, p90 121 s and p99 629 s (SMD-2424's claim durations).
- **`server-portable/entities.ts`:** `extractEntities` takes an optional `stop` signal after `windowing`. It joins each call's deadline (`AbortSignal.any`), the runaway retry's included, so a stop aborts the request and a streamed answer's read. No window or retry is sent once it has aborted (`throwIfAborted` before each call, so an uncalled window is not counted in `callsMade`).
- **`db/extract-entities.ts`:** an `onHardStop` controller, which the hard stop aborts and passes to `extractEntities`. SMD-2401 had removed it when nothing read it. A `--decide` decider call in hand is still waited for.

**The idle signal, measured (the ticket's step 1).** The stable brain's logs, 2026-09-23 to 10-07, read-only and replayed at each quiet — the scheduler did not run there:
- **A live event** is every `query_log` row (stable runs `OB1_QUERY_LOG=on`), plus every `thought_audit` row whose `actor_kind` is not `ingested` (board-sync's kind there).
- **Volume:** 3,379 events, bursty — the median gap is 0.3 s, the p90 19 s, the p99 2 h.

| quiet before sleep | sleeps in 14 days | share of time asleep | median sleep | p10 sleep |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 60 s | 303 | 98% | 12.6 min | 0.9 min |
| 300 s | 222 | 93% | 16.6 min | 2.4 min |
| 900 s | 143 | 84% | 25.5 min | 3.2 min |

Counting board-sync's writes as live halves the median sleep at 300 s (6.5 min, 337 sleeps). 300 s is the default. The poll, the reach-back and the passes' own calls are not in these numbers.

**PR 2 — `db/sleep.ts`.** An engine (`run(opts)`, SMD-2304's shape, in test-engines' census) and its CLI: `--follow`, one sleep, `--dry-run`, `--quiet`, `--poll` (at most 60), `--workers`.
- **Awake** — reads the logs for the newest live event, on the database's clock, again when the brain could first fall asleep (the event's age plus `--quiet`, at most a minute apart). The read is bounded below. A role that cannot read `query_log` (only its owner can) is told once; a log with no row in 24 h is said once too — with `OB1_QUERY_LOG` off, the default, a search never wakes it.
- **Asleep** — the extraction follower runs alone until its pool is drained (nothing pending, in flight or not yet pooled), then the consolidation follower joins it: the rule SMD-2424 left to an operator in `deploy/README.md`. Followers, not one-shot runs, so SMD-2599's outage waits apply mid-sleep. One sleep (no `--follow`) stops both once both pools drain, and exits 0.
- **Waking** — every `--poll` seconds while asleep, reaching up to 30 s before the sleep (an audit row is dated at its transaction's start). A live event hard-stops both passes through their own `signal` and `PassStop`; the claims they held are then moved to the back of the queue (their `enqueued_at` set to now), since the release keeps a claim's place and counts no attempt: a thought longer than the sleeps was every sleep's first, and the pool behind it never moved (review pass 1, reproduced). The passes' own lines about a "signal" are left out of a stop the scheduler made.
- **Refusals** — every sleep starts its passes afresh. A pass's first start refusing ends the scheduler with its code; a refusal by a pass that has run in this process before (a model re-pulled, a key rotated) is retried on SMD-2599's outage schedule, the heartbeat failed meanwhile (review pass 1).
- **The passes write no audit row** (extraction writes the entity tables, consolidation proposals), so the scheduler's own work cannot wake it. test-live holds that.
- **Heartbeat** — `heartbeat:sleep`, `--follow` only: running while asleep, at least every minute, `failed` while a pass's last word was, ended on a stop — a second signal's included, and stamped while a first stop waits for the thought in hand.
  - The engines gained a `stamper` option, so their followers stamp through the scheduler's row and a wake ends no follower row.
  - `pass-stamp.ts` gained `alive()`: a re-stamp of the row as it stands, running inside a pass, where `stamp()` says one ended.
  - `STAMPING_WORKERS`, `brain-info.ts`'s reader (no job, as board-sync) and `restartCommand` (from a checkout until SMD-2678's service) know `sleep`. Preflight's `consolidate pass` row reads a fresh `heartbeat:sleep` as the scheduler working the current judge's key, rather than asking for a second worker (review pass 1).
- **Followers outside it** (the `workers` profile) do not yield. The start and `--dry-run` name any whose heartbeat is fresh, with how to stop them and retire their rows.
