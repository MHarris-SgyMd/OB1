## Changelog

Extract's and consolidate's `--follow` followers no longer blame the wrong thought for a provider crash. After an outage, once the provider answers, the thoughts that were in hand at it are retried one at a time with one worker, and the follower says so; normal passes resume once each has settled, once the isolated pass reaches a thought that is none of them, or once the window passes. A thought retried alone that meets the error again is recorded failed only on a one-token call made at once: if it answers, the thought is its own; if not, the follower waits again, and the thought's third such outage alone makes it its own. Before, with several workers, a thought that crashed the provider could fail the innocent thought beside it, and a provider crashing every few minutes failed whichever re-claimed thought it met first. A thought that crashes the provider is now failed the fourth time it does, where it was the second; one the provider errs on while it answers is still failed the second time (SMD-2641).

Re-embed's `--follow` follower does the same since PR 2: after an outage its suspects are retried one at a time, and one that fails again alone is recorded failed only when a probe made at once answers, or at its third outage alone with none answering. Under the sleep scheduler re-embed is set down for the period at an outage its poll sees, its suspects forgotten, so a thought that crashes the provider is still never failed there, as before; a run by hand fails it (SMD-2641).

## Record

PR 1: an outage's suspects retried one at a time, and blamed only on a probe — extract's and consolidate's followers (SMD-2641)

The maintainer's rules (2026-11-10, on the ticket): the isolation reaches the suspects in the pool's order, with no migration; a suspect alone whose probe gets no answer is its own at its third outage alone (`ALONE_OUTAGES`, N = 3); two PRs, re-embed's follower in PR 2.

**What changes** (`ProviderOutage`, `db/worker-bootstrap.ts`, made `isolate` by extract and consolidate; re-embed and the subject sweep keep its rule unchanged):
- **Isolation.** Each suspect goes back at the pool's front (`setFront`, `db/lease.ts`: dated just before the oldest row pending or claimed, so no tie in a backlog pooled in one statement stands ahead of it). Once an outage that made suspects ends, `isolating()` holds the next passes to one worker and a batch of one, the pool's order, said ("retrying the last outage's N suspect thought(s) one at a time"), until each suspect has settled (finished, or recorded failed), the isolated pass claims a thought none of them (`claimed`: those left are behind the backlog — edited, set back by the sleep scheduler's abortInHand — or elsewhere: held by another process, deleted), or the window passes (15 minutes; consolidate's grows with its limit beside the subject sweep). The isolated pass ends as its last suspect settles, the next polled at once with every worker. An outage that begins while isolating keeps the suspects not yet retried, with their counts.
- **Blamed on a probe.** A suspect alone that errs again past the pauses (`blames`) is probed at once, or read off the probe just made (a deadline's, or consolidate's pair timeout's `ProviderDown`): answered, it is its own, recorded failed ("…alone in flight, and a one-token call right after answered…"), and the worker goes on; refused, the request's, every worker stopped, exit 2; no answer, an outage it was alone at, said "(n of 3)", and at the third its own ("…at 3 outages, the one-token call after each unanswered…"), the outage begun with no suspect and the worker stopping after the record. A suspect still until its record settles it; a blame whose probe ends past the window is an outage as any thought's. Before, a suspect that erred again within 15 minutes of a probe answering was its own, whatever else was in flight.
- **A halted thought joins.** A worker whose pauses another worker's outage cuts short makes its thought a suspect of that outage when its streak could be its own: which worker's pauses end first, and so whose thought began the outage, is a race. Before, only the thought whose worker began or joined the outage past its own pauses was one.

**Limits, kept.** Alone means alone in this follower: under the sleep scheduler extraction, consolidation and the subject sweep share the chat endpoint, each with its own outage, so another pass's poison can still crash the provider under a suspect alone, as before. Suspects and their counts are a run's own, as before. The blame probe, like the probe after a call (SMD-4166), does not hear the scheduler's abortInHand. The wait line after a third outage alone still says "recorded nothing", the failure said on the line before it, and the stored failure says "alone in flight" though alone means in this follower. When the scheduler's abortInHand sets back a poison whose call is still crashing the provider, the next suspect retried alone meets the outage and is charged one outage alone, failing nothing (three are needed), the poison moved behind the backlog and failed after more crashes (measured by review pass 2's run-it harness under the real sleep scheduler, two runs: six against four). The isolation also ends early, blaming nothing, when a suspect's hand-back to the front fails (the finally returns it where it stood), a `retry_failed` row older than the suspects is claimed first, or SKIP LOCKED hands rows out of order. A thought halted mid-pause whose streak is the provider holding calls back joins no outage, and no row holds that it does not: retried alone, it would cost only time; nor does any row hold that only a suspect is handed back at the front, that extract's blame probe asks the escalation model after its call, or the isolated batch of one (every row runs --batch 1).

**Tidied while the files were open.** The halted join's log line lost a review citation; two file headers and the ProviderOutage docblock came back inside their wrap; setFront's docblock reads the right way round; test-live lost a dead counter and an unused option.

PR 2: re-embed's follower onto the same rule — its suspects retried one at a time, blamed only on a probe (SMD-2641)

**What changes** (`db/reembed.ts`, its `ProviderOutage` made `isolate`, as extract's and consolidate's in PR 1):
- **Isolation.** A drain after an outage that made suspects runs one worker and a batch of one, said ("retrying the last outage's N suspect thought(s) one at a time"); each suspect goes back at the pool's front (`setFront`); the drain ends as the last suspect settles, the next poll at once with every worker, and the first thought claimed that is no suspect ends the isolation.
- **Blamed on a probe.** `sendBack` blames a suspect retried alone that errs again on a probe made at once — or on its own deadline's probe, unanswered, not made twice — as part of the call in hand: answered, its own, recorded failed ("…a probe's embedding call right after answered…"); refused, the request's, every worker stopped, exit 2; no answer, an outage it was alone at, "(n of 3 before it is recorded failed)", and at the third its own, the outage begun with no suspect; the call aborted meanwhile (abortInHand), set back.
- **No halted join.** Re-embed has no pauses, and `drainEnds` aborts no embedding call in flight (it wakes the admit gate and cuts a deadline's probe short, which then joins): each worker whose call fails at a crash reaches `sendBack` and joins the outage, a suspect, itself.
- **Moved rows behind.** While isolating, a succeeded row the poll moves back to the pool (its thought no longer at the target) is dated now, not where it was first pooled, so it stands behind the suspects.
- **At every outage, not only an isolated drain's**, a suspect goes back at the pool's front, its claim pending before the worker's finally returns the rest of its batch: the finally's "returned k unfinished row(s)" counts one fewer for each, and under the sleep scheduler (a batch of one) says nothing for it.

**Evidence.** `test-live` [9f]: two workers, a poison thought crashing the provider beside an innocent one in flight — the poison failed at its third outage alone, four crashes, the innocent re-embedded, each claim's attempt refunded; a crash at the next call after each recovery, three times, twice on a suspect alone — none failed, the drain after the isolated one with both workers at once. The hemlock row now fails on the probe made at once, and the deadline row whose probes meet an outage at its third outage alone. Rows pooled in one statement tie, so which suspect goes first is the hand-back's order, not the seeding's: one poison row, not both orders, as [10] and [16] hold.

**Tidied while the files were open.** [9f]'s two alone helpers kept once beside its others, where the deadline row had copied their regexes; the requeue comment names its ticket.

**Limits, kept.** Under the sleep scheduler re-embed is set down for the period once its poll sees it waiting on an outage (sleep.ts's setReembedDown), and its suspects go with the run: a thought that crashes the provider is retried at each period's first claim and never failed — on main as on this branch (measured by review pass 1's run-it harness under the real scheduler, ten runs at a 1 s poll and a 1 s first wait, the shipped ratio: no isolation, the poison pending, the period re-embedding nothing behind it; follow-up SMD-4254). The isolation starts there only when the provider answers before the scheduler's next poll sees the wait; then, as PR 1's limits say for extract and consolidate, the scheduler's abortInHand setting back a poison mid-crash charges the next suspect retried alone one outage, failing nothing. As PR 1's: the wait line after a third outage alone still says "recorded nothing", the failure said on the line before; the stored failure says "alone in flight" though the embedding endpoint is shared with the API's capture and search embeddings; and a blame probe that finds the model missing counts as unanswered. The isolated line's "the last outage's N suspect thought(s)" counts the suspects left from earlier outages too, as PR 1's lines do. Held by no row, each needing a harness [9f] has not: a future-dated succeeded row moved back keeping its date (only the scheduler's set-back-ahead makes one), the blame probe's slot wait counted inside the call's (the model-call seam), a setFront the database refuses, the alone branch's probe subject under a deny policy, and that only a suspect, not every thought sent back, goes to the front.

PR 3: re-embed's poison row in the other order too, so the isolated drain's mutant dies every run (SMD-2641)

**Why.** The ticket's Verify (2026-11-12, on main 46f9a8f8) ran each follower's drop-the-mechanism mutant. Re-embed's, every worker in the isolated drain, was killed in two of three runs. With two workers both suspects are in flight at each crash. When the poison's error is read first, its blame probe begins the outage and the innocent's error only joins it, so [9f]'s pair row passed. When the innocent's is read first, the innocent is blamed alone and failed at its third outage. The stub answered the poison's crash 400 ms late and the innocent's call in flight after 1000 ms, so the order was left to timing. [10] and [16] run both orders, and their mutants died each run.

**What changes** (`db/test-live.ts` only). [9f]'s stub answers the crashing call `crashAnswerMs` late (400, as before, unless a row sets it). A new pair row runs the other order: the innocent pooled first, its call in flight taking 500 ms and the poison's crash answered at 1250 ms, halfway between that and the 2 s call deadline. The row asserts that the innocent's error is the first read; that the poison is failed at its third outage alone, four crashes all its own; and that the innocent is re-embedded.

**Evidence.** [9f] alone, on main 46f9a8f8's re-embed with this row: 50 of 50 assertions pass. The isolated drain's mutant (every worker) is killed by the new row in each of five runs: three with the crash answered at 1600 ms, two at 1250 ms. The first pair row also failed in both runs at 1250 ms, and the timer row in one at 1600 ms. The late answer set back to 400 ms survives, and the reason was read off the follower's lines: a call made after the crash is answered 500 at once. So the innocent, its call made second in both runs, was still read first. An answer past the call in flight makes that order hold whichever call is made first, and the order assertion holds the premise.

**Tidied while the files were open.** The stub's crash branch says its answer comes `crashAnswerMs` late, not "a beat late", and the PR 2 block's header names the other order.

**Limits, kept.** The row sets calls in flight to 500 ms and puts them back to 1000 ms after it, for the timer row. No row depends on that: the timer row passes at 500 ms too (review pass 1's mutant).
