## Changelog

The supersession review listing (`consolidate.ts --list` and the `list_supersession_proposals` tool) no longer slows to seconds on long supersedes chains (SMD-3036, migration 105). Where a side's chain passes 25 rows, the listing says what an accept would take and lapse was not read; `--accept` still reads it in full.

## FORK

The listing caps the walks it runs — list_proposal_writers reads a side's takes and lapses only when the chain below the other side and the tree above it each hold 25 rows or fewer, and the replies name a side left unread (SMD-3036)

**What changed** (migration 105).
- **`ob1_listing_walk_fits(side, other)`**: whether the listing's walks for an accept making `side` stand over `other` stay within 25 rows each way. It counts `other` and the rows below it, and the rows whose chain reaches `side`, each a row at a time with each counted once (a cycle runs the count out) — to 26 at most each way. One equality read of the key or `supersedes` a row, as the walks themselves read, and no audit probes. Two shapes were dropped: a recursive query, whose plan on a brain with no statistics hash-joined a scan of `thoughts` into each of its 25 steps, and a read of a level's rows at once (`= ANY`), which took a scan under its generic plan. Each count holds what the walks can visit: the takes walk and the loop check go down from `other`; the lapse walk's clears, its stays-links and its walk-on rows are all above `side`.
- **`list_proposal_writers(ids)`**: 094's, with each side's `*_takes` and `*_lapses` read only where the count answers true; past it both are NULL and neither walk runs. An accepted row's lapses stay `[]`; names, kinds, trust, capture_only, superseded_by and same_capturer are read for every row. RETURNS TABLE unchanged, so 094 re-applied replaces it in place.
- **The readers.** `ProposalSide.walkCapped` (`server-portable/store.ts`: a NULL takes on a row that carries 094's columns, never on an accepted row, which takes no accept). The tool's reply and `--list` print `walkCappedLine()` under such a side; the reply's value carries `walkCapped: true`. `db/consolidate.ts`'s accept guard reads its one row's takes and lapses through `ob1_accept_takes` and `ob1_accept_lapses` itself, so its WRITER refusal and the pointers it names are never the cap's. A listing's WRITER flag reads an unread takes as false.

**Why.** 094's listing runs, for every listed proposal and each side, `ob1_accept_takes` and `ob1_accept_lapses`: walks of up to 1,000 rows with audit probes at every row. Measured 2026-10-21, every column read as the stores read them:
- the stable and canary brains (at 103): 78–135 ms for 200 proposals, 6–16 ms for 10. Their longest chain is 7 rows, and no side has more than one row above it;
- a throwaway Postgres at 104, ANALYZEd, 200 proposals each over a 1,000-row chain below the other side: 3.3–4.0 s (10: 0.18 s);
- one side with 1,000 re-captured capture-only pointers above it, proposed over 200 others: 17–19 s (10: 0.8–0.9 s);
- the same shapes at 100 rows: 0.35–0.43 s and 1.2–1.4 s for 200.

Two traps on the way, both in the ticket's comments. `SELECT count(*) FROM list_proposal_writers(...)` is inlined and never evaluates the walking columns (7–17 ms on canary, 13 ms over 1,000-row chains). And a fresh brain with no statistics plans the walks' reads as scans: the same shapes un-ANALYZEd took 5–7 s and 27–35 s.

**Measured after** (the same ANALYZEd brains, 200 proposals / 10):
- at 25 rows, within the cap: 0.14 s / 9 ms below and 0.30–0.36 s / 16 ms above, against 104's 0.11 s / 6 ms and 0.28–0.30 s / 15 ms;
- past it, at 100 and 1,000 rows: 36–74 ms / 3–5 ms, every capped side unread, against 104's 0.35 s to 19 s.

**Held.** test-schema [94]:
- the shape;
- 25 rows below the other side read as the direct reads give them, and 26 left NULL with the other side and every writer column read;
- 25 and 26 rows above, the stack's 25 lapses named;
- 25 rows directly onto a side fit and a 26th does not (rows counted, not depth);
- a raw cycle ends both counts;
- an accepted row past the cap keeps `[]`;
- 105 re-applied a no-op; 094 re-applied uncapped, then 105 restored.

[84]'s last definer is 105, and [84] and [87] restore the listing after their 094 re-applies. test-live [16]: past the cap `--list` prints the unread line and flags nothing, and the accept guard reads in full and refuses the taking. test-upgrade [20aw]: refused without 094; onto a populated brain at 104, a proposal within the cap lists the same and one over 26 rows has that side unread; no row or audit row moved; re-apply a no-op. test-server [16l]: the line, the value's mark, the store's reading.

**Not taken.**
- A cap inside 097's walk: an argument changes the signature every re-apply of 097 and 104 would stand beside, and a setting reaches the trigger in the same transaction, which would then clear fewer pointers than it must.
- Lapses only for the row an `--accept` names: the reviewer would stop seeing lapses while browsing.
- Skipping rows a reviewer cannot accept: 094 already does (029 accepts pending, stale and rejected rows; an accepted row's lapses are `[]`).
