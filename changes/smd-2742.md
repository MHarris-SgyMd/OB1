## Changelog

Two test checks that watch for a second session blocked on a lock no longer miss it when their first look comes too early. Inside an open transaction Postgres keeps `pg_stat_activity`'s list of sessions and their query text as first read, so each of the seven checks of that shape now clears it (`pg_stat_clear_snapshot()`) before every poll; the other five could not miss, and clear too (SMD-2742).

## FORK

The lock-wait polls in test-live and test-upgrade clear their connection's view of pg_stat_activity before each poll — a waiter whose query or session the poll's first read predates is no longer missed (SMD-2742)

**What changed.** Each of the seven polls runs `SELECT pg_stat_clear_snapshot()` on its connection before reading `pg_stat_activity`. Each runs on a connection that holds its own transaction open while a second session blocks on its locks. The sites:
- `db/test-live.ts`: `[25]`'s payload backfill (`held`), and the three `cA` races: `[30]`'s two extractions, and `[31]`'s reviewer against `rebuild_derived` and forget arm against a worker;
- `db/test-upgrade.ts`: 046's audit backfill (`passA`) and 050's actor backfill (`passB`, `passC`). passA's comment, which said its connection "can read pg_stat_activity inside its transaction", now says what it keeps.

**Why.**
- `[25]`'s "the second pass was seen waiting on the first's row locks" failed on CI five times since 09-25, most recently in runs 39690221206 and 40007030163, and locally under ci-parity and loaded runs.
- The reviewer-against-`rebuild_derived` race failed once locally on 10-23 with `waited 0`.
- The mechanism, measured on Postgres 16.15 with Bun's client:
  - inside an open transaction, Postgres keeps the sessions it lists, and their state and query text, as first read;
  - `wait_event_type` is read live.
  - So a poll that matches on query text, or on a session's presence, never sees a waiter whose query started, or whose session connected, after the first read.
- That is two of the seven sites:
  - `[25]` filters on the second pass's query text;
  - the reviewer race's waiter `cB` connects with its blocked call. On main with an early poll, connecting `cB` first makes that race pass: the missing session was the cause there.
- The other five match on the live wait column of a session already in the view, so they could not miss. Their clear keeps them from depending on that.

**Held.**
- Each site's own wait assertion (`waited`, `waitedOnB`, `waitedOnC`).
- An early poll on the polling connection, before the waiter is dispatched, fails main's `[25]` and reviewer race every time (frozen query text, and a session not yet there). With the fix all seven sites pass with the early poll: test-live's `rest` shard 1023 of 1023, test-upgrade 711 of 711.
- A waiter dispatched after the holder commits, or one that never meets a lock, still fails its site with the fix: six of the seven were run, all but the forget arm, the reviewer race's twin.

**Measured after.** test-live's `rest` shard and test-upgrade, unmutated, side by side on a loaded machine: 1023/1023 and 711/711, at 173 s and 148 s, the same as main's.

**Not taken.**
- **Polling from an autocommit connection,** the ticket's first option. test-upgrade's sections' pools hold one connection, and the waiter is running on it. test-live's pools could have polled, and a third connection would work anywhere, as a probe from one at `[25]` showed; one statement on the connection already there is simpler at all seven.
- **Lengthening the deadlines and connecting each waiter before its `BEGIN`,** the 10-25 rescope. It rested on a 10-23 measurement that found the view refreshing, which was very likely the live wait column at one of the five sites. The clear fixes both failing sites, the reviewer race's missing session included.
