## FORK

test-preflight's [9] moved to the rest shard: the first shard is [5] alone (SMD-3710)

**What changed.** In `server-portable/test-preflight.ts`, `SHARDS.first` is `["5"]`, where it was `["5", "9"]`; [9] now runs in the rest shard, in the preflight-rest job. The two shards still run every section once between them.

**Why.** Since SMD-3691 the rest shard runs in a job of its own, so the split no longer evens two legs. The first shard is the data layer's, beside `db/test-upgrade.ts` and the job's foreground suites; the job ends at the latest of the three. Over the 16 newest green main runs to 87240d21:
- **on an AMD EPYC 7763** (eight runs), the first shard ended 15.2–20.4 s after the later of test-upgrade and the foreground suites. [9] took 17.2–19.0 s of it: about 11 s are four cases that wait out preflight's 2.5 s probe timeout, and most of the rest some 40 preflight runs, a subprocess each;
- **on the EPYC 9V45 and 9V74** (five runs), the first shard already ended 2–15 s before test-upgrade, and on the Xeon 6975P-C (one run) 9 s before the foreground suites, so the move takes nothing off there. [9] took 15.1–16.4 s on the 9V45;
- **on the Xeon 8370C** (two runs) the first shard ended 16 and 11 s after the later of the two;
- **preflight-rest's job** ended 12–107 s before the data layer, 26–91 s on the 7763.

**What it should do.** Taking [9] out, the first shard on a 7763 ends near the later of the other two, the job 15.2–17.4 s sooner in those runs. preflight-rest gains [9] while the data layer loses up to as much, so the margin between them falls by up to twice [9]. In three of the 16 runs it would have gone below 0: a 7763 at 26 s (about −8), an EPYC 9V45 at 13 (about −3) and the 6975P-C at 12 (about −5). Neither job is the run's critical path: in all three the data layer ended 125 s or more before the run's last job, the sleep cell of the schema-against-Postgres job. So, as the ticket says, this buys the data layer headroom against the next creep, not run time.

**The Verify as written.** The first shard's median end on the 7763 has crept from SMD-3691's 170.0 s to 184.5 s in these runs, and test-upgrade's own median there is 164.2 s, so a median of 160 s (~10 s under 170.0) is out of reach whatever [9] does: the predicted median is about 166 s. Its second clause, preflight-rest ending before the data layer, would have failed one 7763 run in eight. Restating it is the maintainer's call.

**What [9] reads.** One case in [9] reads the database: a provider that is down, beside a reachable migrated brain, exits 0 with only a warning. It needs a brain that preflight passes. In the rest shard [9] now runs after [5b], as in a full run. [5b] ends with a brain preflight passes (`--json reports ok:true`). [6], [7], [7b] and [8] run between them and write nothing to the database; one case of [8]'s (`sourceTerm`) reads it through a read-only preflight run. [9] leaves nothing [10]–[12] read. [5]'s own end check stays, since it still shows that [5]'s fixtures clean up after themselves; only its words change.

**Comments.** The `SHARDS` docblock and preflight-rest's comment carry the figures. [5]'s and [5b]'s end comments and the data-layer start step's comment name the move. The README's two shard lines name `SHARDS`, not its sections, so they still read right and are unchanged.
