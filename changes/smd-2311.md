## Changelog

The learning path's six extensions are plugins, `family-calendar`, `home-maintenance`, `household-knowledge`, `meal-planning`, `professional-crm` and `job-hunt`: their tools, with lists of members and tasks, the shopping list's read and tick, a list and a change for opportunities, and lists of companies, postings, applications and interviews with a change of each, are operations of the brain itself behind its own keys, each plugin's tables in a schema of its own, the CRM reading a linked thought through the brain as its caller and the job hunt recording the CRM contact an agent found or added, and the six standalone servers, meal planning's shared household server, their key lists and `--grant`'s `extensions` group are gone; a one-off appointment is its one day, yearly dates come round on their next anniversary, and logging older maintenance no longer moves a task back (SMD-2311).
**Upgrading:** drop `extensions` from any `migrate.ts --grant … --groups` list, which now refuses it by name; turn on each extension's plugin (`OB1_PLUGINS`) in place of its server, and copy a brain's old `public` rows across with the block in its plugin's README.

## FORK

The family calendar, home maintenance, household knowledge, meal planning, the professional CRM and the job hunt become plugins — the six extensions leave their standalone servers (SMD-2311)

**What changed.** SMD-2311 ports the six curated extensions onto the plugin model, in four PRs (decided 2026-11-02), the fourth split in two (2026-11-03). PR 1 is family-calendar, and the suite the other ports join; PR 2 is home-maintenance and household-knowledge; PR 3 is meal-planning; PR 4a is professional-crm, and 4b job-hunt (below).
- **`plugins/family-calendar/`.** The six tools of `extensions/family-calendar/index.ts` are six operations under the same names, and a seventh, `list_family_members`, gives each member's id, which no reply gave an agent before:
  - `family_calendar_add_family_member`, `_add_activity` and `_add_important_date` need write;
  - `_list_family_members`, `_get_week_schedule`, `_search_activities` and `_get_upcoming_dates` need read.

  Each is a REST route under `/v1/plugins/family-calendar/` and an MCP tool on the brain's own connector. Every tool is closed-world, and an add is marked as destroying nothing.

  Inputs are checked before the database: a date is `YYYY-MM-DD` from the year 0001, a time is `HH:MM`, a day is a lower-case day name, and text holds no NUL (which Postgres will not store). Refusals:
  - an activity with neither a day nor a start date: `422 NO_WHEN`;
  - a member id that names no one, on an add or a filter: `404 NO_SUCH_MEMBER`, saying where the ids are;
  - an end before a start: `422 BAD_DATES`.

  Answers fold the member into `family_member`, `{ id, name, relationship }` or null.
- **Its tables.** `migrations/001_family_calendar.sql` makes `family_members`, `activities` and `important_dates` in `plugin_family_calendar`, as the plugin's role.
  - Two views, `activity_rows` and `important_date_rows`, build each answer as one jsonb column, so an operation selects named columns (check 41).
  - `important_date_rows` reckons each date's `next_date` from today in UTC.
  - The tables hold the operations' rules as checks: names and titles of 1 to 200 characters, a lower-case day, a day or a start date (named), an end on or after the start (named), a reminder of 0 to 365 days, and a member that exists.
  - Nothing is adopted from `public` (decided 2026-10-26). The plugin's README gives the copy for a brain that held the old tables, which neither dogfood brain did. It is one transaction, re-runnable after a refused row, and keeps `user_id` in the column the plugin ignores.
- **`user_id`** stays in each table, nullable, and nothing writes or reads it (decided 2026-11-02, Work 4). `DEFAULT_USER_ID` goes with the server.
- **What the port fixes, from the old server and from an agent's walkthrough of the tools:**
  - **A one-off is its one day.** A dated activity with no `end_date` is its `start_date` alone, as upstream's schema comment said. The old query, and this port's first draft, ran it on every week for ever, so a dentist appointment came back each week, with no delete to take it away. A weekly activity with no `end_date` still runs on.
  - **The week** is seven days in date order, each activity carrying `on_date`: the date it falls on that week, a span's first day in it.
    - The old query ran eight days (`start_date <= week_start + 7`), sorted by time of day across the week, and listed every weekly activity whatever its own dates.
    - Now a weekly one is listed only where its day in the week falls within its dates.
  - **A yearly date** comes round on its month and day, whatever year it was stored under. The old `get_upcoming_dates` compared the stored date, so a birthday stored with its birth year was never listed, and one stored under next year would skip this year.
    - Each listed date carries `next_date`, `days_until`, and `remind`: whether it falls within its `reminder_days_before`.
    - A date further off than the window whose reminder has begun is listed too.
    - `add_important_date` answers `next_date`, null for a one-time date already past, which no window lists.
  - **Today** is the date in UTC, not the database session's time zone, and is said to be.
  - **`search_activities`** also matches the member's name (as its description promised), the location and the notes, and `activity_type` in any case. It escapes `%`, `_` and the backslash, and takes a `limit`.
- **PR 2: `plugins/home-maintenance/`.** The four tools of `extensions/home-maintenance/index.ts` are four operations under the same names. A fifth, `list_maintenance_tasks`, gives every task's id: before, a one-time task with no date was in no list, so no log could name it.
  - **Scopes.** `add_maintenance_task` and `log_maintenance` need write; `list_maintenance_tasks`, `get_upcoming_maintenance` and `search_maintenance_history` need read.
  - **Its tables** are `maintenance_tasks` and `maintenance_logs` in `plugin_home_maintenance`.
    - Dates replace the old timestamps: `next_due`, `last_completed` and a log's `completed_on`.
    - Two views build the answers. A task answers `days_until`, negative when overdue, and `done`: a one-time task whose work has been logged.
    - The old schema's two triggers are `log_maintenance`'s own statements, in its one transaction, so no function lives in the schema.
  - **Logging** moves the task on: `last_completed` becomes the latest day logged, a recurring task falls due `frequency_days` after it, and a one-time task is done. The old trigger set `last_completed` to whatever day was logged, so a log of older work moved the task back.
  - **A recurring task added without `next_due`** falls due `frequency_days` from today. The old server left it undated, and so never upcoming.
  - **Upcoming** lists every overdue task and those due within `days_ahead`, by date and then by priority (urgent first); done tasks are left out.
  - **History** filters by task, by task name, by category and by dates, both edge days in. A `task_id` that names no task is `404 NO_SUCH_TASK` on a log or a history read, saying where the ids are. A `date_to` before `date_from` is `422 BAD_DATES`.
- **PR 2: `plugins/household-knowledge/`.** The five tools of `extensions/household-knowledge/index.ts` are five operations under the same names.
  - **Scopes.** `add_household_item` and `add_vendor` need write; `search_household_items`, `get_item_details` and `list_vendors` need read.
  - **Its tables** are `household_items` and `household_vendors` in `plugin_household_knowledge`.
    - `details` is a JSON object, checked by the table, at most 10,000 characters. The old tool took it as a JSON string.
    - Search reads the name, category, location, notes and every value in `details`, so "sea salt" finds the paint whose details say so. A comma no longer splits the query, which was the old server's PostgREST syntax.
  - **`get_item_details`** answers `404 NO_SUCH_ITEM`, saying where the ids are. A vendor's email and website (http or https) are checked, and its rating is 1 to 5.
- **PR 3: `plugins/meal-planning/`.** The six tools of `extensions/meal-planning/index.ts` are six operations under the same names; `get_shopping_list` and `mark_item_purchased` bring back, behind the brain's own keys, what the retired household server (`shared-server.ts`, SMD-2920) let a household read and tick off.

  - **Its tables** are `recipes`, `meal_plans`, `shopping_lists` and `shopping_list_items` in `plugin_meal_planning`: the old list's JSON `items` array is rows, so an item is marked by its id.
  - **What the port fixes:**
    - **Plans.** `create_meal_plan` sets each day and meal type given rather than appending, so a plan sent twice is planned once, two at once included. It answers what each slot held before (`replaced`), and keeps two meals of a slot in the order sent. A week is named by its Monday; any other day is a 400, and the tables hold the rule.
    - **The week's meals.** `get_meal_plan` comes by day and meal, with each meal's `on_date`. The old one sorted both alphabetically.
    - **The shopping list.** A regenerated list keeps what was marked bought while its quantity is no more than was bought (`bought_quantity`); the old one wiped every mark. `get_shopping_list` says it is `stale` when a list made now would say otherwise.
    - **Quantities.** Numeric ones are summed ("1/2", "½", ".5", or "1 lb" with its unit in the quantity) and shown as fractions where simple. Others follow the sum with their count ("2-3 ×2"): the old list counted those once however often planned. Units are put in one spelling ("lbs" is "lb").

A missing recipe is `NO_SUCH_RECIPE`, naming each missing id. The table checks each ingredient's and step's shape.
