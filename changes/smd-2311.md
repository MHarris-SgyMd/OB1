## Changelog

The learning path's family calendar is a plugin, `family-calendar`: its six tools, and a seventh that lists the members, are operations of the brain itself behind its own keys, its tables in a schema of its own, and the standalone server and its key list are gone; a one-off appointment is its one day, and yearly dates come round on their next anniversary (SMD-2311).

## FORK

The family calendar becomes a plugin — the first of the six extensions to leave its standalone server (SMD-2311)

**What changed.** SMD-2311 ports the six curated extensions onto the plugin model, in four PRs (decided 2026-11-02). This is PR 1: family-calendar, and the suite the other ports join.
- **`plugins/family-calendar/`.** The six tools of `extensions/family-calendar/index.ts` are six operations under the same names, and a seventh, `list_family_members`, gives each member's id, which no reply gave an agent before:
  - `family_calendar_add_family_member`, `_add_activity` and `_add_important_date` need write;
  - `_list_family_members`, `_get_week_schedule`, `_search_activities` and `_get_upcoming_dates` need read.

  Each is a REST route under `/v1/plugins/family-calendar/` and an MCP tool on the brain's own connector. Every tool is closed-world, and an add is marked as destroying nothing.

  Inputs are checked before the database: a date is `YYYY-MM-DD` from the year 0001, a time is `HH:MM`, a day is a lower-case day name, and text holds no NUL (which Postgres will not store). Refusals:
  - an activity with neither a day nor a start date: `422 NO_WHEN`;
  - a member id that names no one, on an add or a filter: `404 NO_SUCH_MEMBER`, saying where the ids are;
  - an end before a start: `422 BAD_DATES`.

  Answers fold the member into `family_member`, `{ id, name, relationship }` or null.
- **Its tables.** `migrations/001_family_calendar.sql` makes `family_members`, `activities` and `important_dates` in `plugin_family_calendar`, as the plugin's role.
  - Two views, `activity_rows` and `important_date_rows`, build each answer as one jsonb column, so an operation selects named columns (check 41).
  - `important_date_rows` reckons each date's `next_date` from today in UTC.
  - The tables hold the operations' rules as checks: names and titles of 1 to 200 characters, a lower-case day, a day or a start date (named), an end on or after the start (named), a reminder of 0 to 365 days, and a member that exists.
  - Nothing is adopted from `public` (decided 2026-10-26). The plugin's README gives the copy for a brain that held the old tables, which neither dogfood brain did. It is one transaction, re-runnable after a refused row, and keeps `user_id` in the column the plugin ignores.
- **`user_id`** stays in each table, nullable, and nothing writes or reads it (decided 2026-11-02, Work 4). `DEFAULT_USER_ID` goes with the server.
- **What the port fixes, from the old server and from an agent's walkthrough of the tools:**
  - **A one-off is its one day.** A dated activity with no `end_date` is its `start_date` alone, as upstream's schema comment said. The old query, and this port's first draft, ran it on every week for ever, so a dentist appointment came back each week, with no delete to take it away. A weekly activity with no `end_date` still runs on.
  - **The week** is seven days in date order, each activity carrying `on_date`: the date it falls on that week, a span's first day in it.
    - The old query ran eight days (`start_date <= week_start + 7`), sorted by time of day across the week, and listed every weekly activity whatever its own dates.
    - Now a weekly one is listed only where its day in the week falls within its dates.
  - **A yearly date** comes round on its month and day, whatever year it was stored under. The old `get_upcoming_dates` compared the stored date, so a birthday stored with its birth year was never listed, and one stored under next year would skip this year.
    - Each listed date carries `next_date`, `days_until`, and `remind`: whether it falls within its `reminder_days_before`.
    - A date further off than the window whose reminder has begun is listed too.
    - `add_important_date` answers `next_date`, null for a one-time date already past, which no window lists.
  - **Today** is the date in UTC, not the database session's time zone, and is said to be.
  - **`search_activities`** also matches the member's name (as its description promised), the location and the notes, and `activity_type` in any case. It escapes `%`, `_` and the backslash, and takes a `limit`.
- **Retired.** `extensions/family-calendar/index.ts`, `schema.sql` and `.env.example` are removed. The README there is now Extension 3's "turn on the plugin", and its metadata needs no Bun or primitives. Also:
  - `extensions/test-auth.ts` loses the server's row; its mid-upload case runs at job-hunt's route, which takes the `/` health probe.
  - `extensions/test-tools.ts` loses its section: thirty-nine tools on six servers.
  - `ROLE_GRANTS.extensions` loses the three tables, with `db/README.md`'s row and `CONTRIB_SCHEMA_FILES`' entry. `test-schema` [50] and `test-live` [18] count five extension schemas and fifteen extension tables. `--groups extensions` still grants the other five extensions' tables, until PR 4 empties it.
  - Three primitives' lists of the extensions that use them drop it.
  - `deploy-remote-mcp`'s re-run line is corrected: every extension schema re-runs cleanly.
- **`server-portable/test-plugin-ports.ts`**, new, in CI after the plugin tables step and in `ci-parity.sh`. It runs every ported plugin against Postgres through both servers:
  - the migrator makes its tables and views in its own schema, each its role's, and nothing in `public`;
  - each key's tool list is what the manifest's scopes give it, and only a read is annotated read-only;
  - an operation a key's scope does not reach is `403` naming its scope over REST, and no such tool over MCP;
  - every read's MCP structured content is its REST body;
  - its drift guard fails a tool no section drives.

  Those checks read each scope from the manifest, so a plugin's own section pins its operations' scopes. Each later PR adds its plugins to `PORTED` and a section.
- **`server-portable/test-plugins.ts`** reads the known plugins in its unknown-name message from the registry, not a pinned `example`.

**Why.** Each extension was a standalone MCP server with a key list of its own. It reached Postgres through `compat/supabase-sql`, outside the REST core's keys and audit (SMD-2308, SMD-3216). As a plugin, it runs behind the brain's keys and scopes, and its SQL runs as a role holding nothing but its own schema (SMD-2756). The operator has one connector, not one per extension.

**Version.** `minor`. The brain gains seven operations while the plugin is enabled, and none otherwise. The standalone server goes, and an operator who ran it turns the plugin on instead. Its tool names gain the plugin's prefix, and no dogfood client held the old ones (decided 2026-10-26). No core route, setting or table changes.

**Held.**
- **`test-plugin-ports.ts`** (102 assertions, Postgres 16). The family calendar's section:
  - **Members:**
    - its seven scopes pinned, and all seven tools' hints;
    - members listed by name with their ids;
    - the name trimmed, and `user_id` left null;
    - an impossible date, one in the year 0000, and a NUL in a body or a query refused.
  - **The week of 2026-09-21, around which activities are added:**
    - it lists the weekly one, two spans that reach it, a one-off inside it and a one-day span;
    - it leaves out the span ended before it, the one-off on the day after it, and a weekly one whose dates ended;
    - in date order with each `on_date`, and by time within a day;
    - members folded in, and the end time and `created_at` in their formats.
  - **Weekly activities' own dates:** a Monday class starting on that week's Wednesday is left out and listed the week after. A Monday club ending the Sunday before the week's Monday is left out of a week starting on a Tuesday, and listed in its last week.
  - **Edges:** in a week of 2027, each edge day is in and the day past it out, for a one-off, a span and a weekly activity's own dates. The untimed one comes after the timed one on the same day.
  - **Search:**
    - by title in any case, the member's name, the location, and type in any case;
    - filters together, all nine with the latest start first and the two with none newest first, and the limit;
    - `%`, `_` and a backslash as themselves.
  - **Refusals:**
    - `NO_SUCH_MEMBER` on an add over both doors and on a week or search filter, saying where the ids are;
    - `NO_WHEN` and `BAD_DATES`, and fourteen inputs out of their bounds, each a 400;
    - none of them leaves an activity or a date;
    - seven table checks refuse a row written around the operations, each by name.
  - **Upcoming dates** against today in UTC:
    - each add answers its `next_date`, and a past one-time date's is null;
    - a yearly birthday stored eight years back is listed on its anniversary ten days on, with `remind` false, and today's date is zero days off, reminded;
    - the past one and the one beyond are left out, and the window widens;
    - with `days_ahead` 0, today's are listed, a yearly anniversary among them, and beyond today only a date whose 45 days' reminder has begun;
    - the default takes the thirtieth day and not the thirty-first;
    - a yearly date stored under next year comes round this year;
    - a yearly 29 February comes round on the date a JavaScript reckoning expects;
    - the past one-time date is in no window, a year's included.
- **Mutants**, each run against the suite and each failing it, 30 in all. One whole-diff pass ran 103, and every survivor that mattered got an assertion.
  - **The week:**
    - its end a day late;
    - weekly activities ignoring their end, by overlap alone, or with their own start day out;
    - a one-off running on;
    - a dated one's last week day out;
    - the week by time alone.
  - **Dates:**
    - yearly dates on their stored day, or reckoned from their stored year;
    - today's anniversary skipped;
    - `remind` strictly inside, and no reminder window;
    - a default of 31 days;
    - the added date's `next_date` null.
  - **Inputs:**
    - year 0000 let through, and NUL let through;
    - no `NO_WHEN`, `BAD_DATES` on a one-day span, and no member check;
    - the week taking any member id.
  - **Search:** no ILIKE escaping; no member-name search; no location search; type in its case.
  - **Elsewhere:**
    - the table's day-or-date rule dropped;
    - members unordered by name;
    - the no-start-date tiebreak oldest first;
    - an add with no hints;
    - `add_family_member` declared read, and `add_important_date` declared capture.
- **The README's copy,** run against the old `schema.sql`, seeded with a NULL `created_at`, a capitalised day, a time with seconds, a NULL `recurring_yearly`, two users and an activity with no day or date:
  - the first run is refused whole, and nothing is copied;
  - with the row fixed, the block run again copies every row, each `user_id` kept, the day lower-cased, and the time answered to the minute.
- **Unchanged suites:** `extensions/test-auth.ts` (508), `extensions/test-tools.ts` (147), `db/test-schema.ts` (3127), `server-portable/test-plugins.ts` (284). The checker and the typechecks pass.
