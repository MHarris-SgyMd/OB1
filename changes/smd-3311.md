## Changelog

A `--follow` extraction or consolidation worker reads a 408 from its provider as an outage it waits out, where it stopped every worker as a refused request, and a 429, a 503 or a model being pulled no longer makes the thought in hand a suspect, so a provider holding calls back never gets a thought recorded failed as its own. Their error reading is now the re-embed follower's (SMD-3311).

## Record

The extract and consolidate followers read a provider's errors through `followClassify`'s split — a 408 waited out, backpressure no suspect (SMD-3311)

**The decision.** SMD-3311's Work 1 had two ways: read extract's and consolidate's provider faults through `followClassify`'s split (backpressure / maybe-poison / the thought's / probe-decides), keeping their pauses, or fix the two cells in `classifyError`, which both share. The maintainer chose the first (2026-11-05). SMD-2636's move builds the one follower helper on `followClassify`, so this is that rule first, in its own PR.

**What changed**, all in `db/`:
- **`followerFault(e)`** in `worker-bootstrap.ts`: the pausing followers' reading. `kind` is `classifyError`'s, so the pauses and the one-shot rules stand, but an error `followClassify` reads as the provider's outage is `transient`, paused for, then an outage: a 408, and since review pass 1 any answer with a status whose words say "timed out" (a gateway's 408 "Request timed out", a 504 "upstream request timed out"), which `classifyError` read as a call's deadline. A deadline with no status stays the thought's, for the probe to decide. `suspect` is false for `followClassify`'s backpressure (a 429, a 503, the model not served) and true for anything else, as before.
- **`extract-entities.ts` and `consolidate.ts`** take it under `--follow`, and begin an outage over a backpressure error with no suspect (`outage.begin(msg, undefined)`). Without `--follow` they read `classifyError` as before.
- **`followClassify`** reads the extraction and judge calls' own errors, an `Error` carrying the answer's `status` (`entities.ts`, `consolidate.ts`), as an `embed.ts` `http` error of that status, and a call's deadline (`timedOut`, consolidate's `ProviderDown` included) as a timeout. Before, both were `probe-decides`. Re-embed and the subject sweep throw `ProviderError`s, which it reads as before; only a non-`ProviderError` whose message says "timed out" moves, from `probe-decides` to `maybe-poison`.
- **`probeChat` and `probeTier`** read a 408 as out (`probeTier` since review pass 1). Read as up or refused, a follower's outage over a 408 ended at once, and its next call met the 408 again right after the probe "answered", failing the suspect as its own. `probeChat` is every follower's: a 408 at a follower's start is now waited for, and in the subject sweep a 408 to the probe after a timeout is the provider out, an outage with a suspect, where it was an answer and the timeout the thought's.

**Not changed:**
- A one-shot run (no `--follow`) still reads a 408 as `classifyError`'s fatal, and the subject sweep's and weekly digest's own errors keep `classifyError`. The ticket is the followers' rule; a 408 there is SMD-2461's, one fault classifier across the workers.
- **A 429 that is always the thought's** — a request too large for the account's per-minute limit — makes no suspect either, so a follower goes back to it after every outage, before the rest of the pool, and never records it failed: one worker would spin on it. Re-embed's follower has had the same since SMD-2679; the subject sweep sets such a thought behind the rest. Extraction windows and judge pairs are bounded well under any such limit, so this was left as decided (a 429 makes no suspect).
