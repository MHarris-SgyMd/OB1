## Changelog

test-live's [38], the sleep scheduler's section, runs its pause cases at a section clock of 0.6, and a refusal case no longer waits 10 s for a wake that is now a pause, so db-live's sleep cell takes about 30 s less (SMD-3714).

## FORK

test-live [38]'s pause cases on a scaled clock — the sleep cell about 30 s shorter (SMD-3714)

**What changed** in `db/test-live.ts`'s [38]:
- **A section clock, as [38b]'s:** `SCALE` (0.6) and `scaled(ms)`.
  - A scaled case passes its quiet and poll as `quietMs` and `pollMs`, since `sleepRun`'s `quiet` and `poll` are whole seconds.
  - Its capture, clock, the stub's hold and the waits it makes around them go through `scaled()` too, so their ratios hold.
  - `capS` writes a scaled capture as the scheduler prints it ("3.6") into a line's regex.
  - The comments keep the seconds the cases were written in. A message prints a scaled figure where its case reads one.
- **Scaled:**
  - the follow run's search during a pass, its pause, resume and extended pause, and the sync write and mend.
    - Their call in hand runs 3.6 s, scaled less than the rest, since the write's lag before the pause doesn't scale.
    - The case now asserts the call is still in hand at the pause, and that only that call's extraction finishes while paused;
  - the one-shot drain with `--abort-after`;
  - the live write on a thought in hand, the drained sleep a capture ends, and the earlier claim that pauses.
- **Kept at their seconds:** each margin rests on a pass's start, which doesn't scale on a slow runner, or on the 1 s poll itself.
  - paused time off the clock. Scaled, its bound sat within about 0.2 s of the right code at its worst, and the mutant that counts paused time could pass it, since its two reads' 250 ms poll lags don't scale (review passes 1 and 2);
  - the wake in the clock's grace;
  - the late commit;
  - the sweep's wake cases;
  - the clock and grace cases;
  - the judgement in hand aborted past N12's bound, and the run it shares with the outage retry and its reset, whose teeth are a row re-stamped 3 s into a 5 s wait;
  - the model a loaded check outlasts.
- **The refusal at the start.** Since SMD-3379 PR 1c-3b the capture that woke it pauses it, its own extraction queued. So the case waited 10 s for an `awake:` line that no longer comes, then for the outage schedule's next probe, and asserted nothing about the wake.
  - It now waits for the pause or the wake and asserts it saw one: 15 s to 3 s.
  - Either line will do: the case is about the refusal at a start the pass never got through, not about what the write does.

**Why.** [38] grew from 179 s to 296 s with SMD-3379 PR 1c-3b (OB1 PR 617), the median of two runs. That took db-live's sleep cell from ~240 s to 313–325 s. Its time is cases waiting out real timers: in run 41573992916 the 14 largest gaps between lines were 141 of [38]'s 300 s span. Locally [38] took 318 s.

**Held.**
- **SMD-3379 1c-3b's mutants of `db/sleep.ts`.** 1c-3b killed twenty-one, and its originals weren't kept. This change rebuilt the twelve that reach [38]'s scaled cases and its idle read, plus one review finding and one survivor, each run against the sleep shard. Pass 1's four ran against a draft whose own clean run failed the follow case; each was killed by another row of its own.
  - **Eleven of the twelve are killed,** each by its own rows:
    - the gate never closing;
    - the gate never opening;
    - the resume leaving `paused` on the row;
    - the resume not stamping the row;
    - paused time on the clock, against the case at its seconds (4063 ms against its 4800 bound, the right code 6067);
    - a closed poll reading the pools;
    - the idle read while the gate is closed;
    - the idle read counting the write's own work;
    - the idle read setting aside a claim queued before the write;
    - no idle end under `--follow`;
    - the capture not held to the quiet.
  - **The review finding is killed too:** the idle read setting aside every claim of a touched thought.
  - **The twelfth survives the unscaled test as well:** the clock's rest taken from the sleep's start. 1c-3b's pass 1 killed it, so the test or this rebuild has drifted since. Noted on SMD-3379 for 1c-4b, which rewrites the clock.
  - **The survivor survives,** as 1c-3b recorded: a claim in hand set aside.
  - **The nine not rebuilt:**
    - Seven are on cases kept at their seconds, so this change doesn't reach them: no N12, no cap on consolidation's aborts, the cap's line every poll, no move behind newer work, the late commit timed from its own date, no move to the tail on a grace wake, and the statement stop's rest.
    - Two are on the live read's bookkeeping, which the scaled cases share: a write marked seen before the poll acts on it, and the read's window moved before the poll acts.
  - **A gate that lets workers through while closed** (`db/lease.ts`), a mutant of review pass 2: killed by the new count alone, 3 → 6 extractions while paused against the clean run's 3 → 4.
- **Every [38] case still runs:** 110 assertions, as before.
- **Under load:** two runs of the final test with every CPU busy passed, [38] at 266 and 270 s. Under load the margins barely move: these cases wait on timers, not CPU. A run at 0.5 of pass 1's test passed too.
- **Two runs of a pass-1 draft failed under heavier load,** with another session's nine Postgres containers running beside the burners.
  - One failure was the refusal case, whose capture landed before the first sleep; it now waits for `asleep:`.
  - The others were one each in two cases this change leaves alone: the stop during a refusal's retry wait, and the wake in the clock's grace.

**Measured after.**
- **Locally,** [38] went from 318 s to 266–270 s over three runs of the final test, one of them unloaded.
- **On CI, one run** (41591590383, OB1 PR 627's first head, with the paused clock still scaled) against run 41573992916:
  - [38] spanned 261 s, from 300;
  - the sleep cell took 282 s, from 317;
  - five scaled cases shrank 3.5–5.8 s each, three 1.0–1.3 s, and the refusal case 12 s net;
  - every case kept at its seconds held to within 0.2 s.
  - Review pass 2 put the paused clock back at its seconds, about 5 s of [38] on CI, so expect the cell about 30 s under its old time.
- **The other cells** read extract 322 s (SMD-3713's [10h]), long 250 s and rest 249 s. So the sleep cell is no longer the run's longest, but is still over the ticket's ~260 s.

**Not taken.**
- **Running [38]'s cases side by side** (the ticket's step 3). They share one database, and a live write pauses every sleep on it.
- **A blanket scale in `sleepScaffold`,** prototyped at 0.6:
  - `abortAfterMs` has a 60 s floor and takes whole milliseconds;
  - the `--poll` refusal case was bypassed;
  - every line regex naming seconds failed.
- **`endSleep` waiting less per try.**
  - At 3 s its capturing writes outran a 4 s capture and the sleep never drained: one run went the full 60 s.
  - Waiting for drained pools before each write was worse, since the idle read counts differently: seven 6 s misses in one run.
