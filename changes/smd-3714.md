## Changelog

test-live's [38], the sleep scheduler's section, runs its pause cases at a section clock of 0.6, and a refusal case no longer waits 10 s for a wake that is now a pause, so db-live's sleep cell takes about a minute less (SMD-3714).

## FORK

test-live [38]'s pause cases on a scaled clock — the sleep cell about a minute shorter (SMD-3714)

**What changed** in `db/test-live.ts`'s [38]:
- **A section clock, as [38b]'s.** `SCALE` (0.6) and `scaled(ms)`. A scaled case passes its quiet and poll as `quietMs` and `pollMs`, since `sleepRun`'s `quiet` and `poll` are whole seconds. Its capture, clock, the stub's hold and the waits it makes around them go through `scaled()` too, so their ratios hold. `capS` writes a scaled capture as the scheduler prints it ("3.6") into a line's regex.
- **Scaled:** the follow run's cases (a search during a pass, a live write pausing a 5 s call, the resume, a second write extending the pause, the sync write and the mend); the live write on a thought in hand, with the drained sleep a capture ends and the earlier claim that pauses; and paused time off the clock. The clock case's slack against the pause, rounded to a whole second, stays 1.2 s unscaled.
- **Kept at their seconds,** each because its margin rests on a pass's start, which doesn't scale on a slow runner, or on the 1 s poll itself: the wake in the clock's grace, the late commit, the sweep's wake cases, the clock and grace cases, the outage retry and its reset (their teeth are a row re-stamped 3 s into a 5 s wait), the N12 run, and the model a loaded check outlasts.
- **The refusal at the start.** Since SMD-3379 PR 1c-3b the capture that wakes it pauses it, its own extraction queued: the case waited 10 s for an `awake:` line that no longer comes, then for the outage schedule's next probe, and asserted nothing of the wake. It now waits for the pause or the wake, and asserts it saw one; 15 s to 3 s.

**Why.** [38] grew from 179 s to 296 s with SMD-3379 PR 1c-3b (OB1 PR 617), taking db-live's sleep cell from ~240 s to 313–325 s. Its time is cases waiting out real timers: in run 41573992916 the 14 largest gaps between lines were 141 of its 300 s. Locally it took 318 s, longer than on CI, though this Mac's CPU is faster.

**Departures from the ticket.**
- **No cases run concurrently** (step 3). [38]'s cases share one database, and a live write pauses every sleep on it.
- **No rebalance yet** (step 4). The ticket ties it to SMD-3713's [10h] result.

**Held.**
- **SMD-3379 1c-3b's mutants of `db/sleep.ts`,** rebuilt as ten and each run against the sleep shard with the scaled cases. Eight are killed:
  - the gate never closing;
  - the gate never opening;
  - the resume leaving `paused` on the row;
  - paused time on the clock;
  - a closed poll reading the pools;
  - the idle read counting the write's own work;
  - the idle read setting aside a claim queued before the write;
  - the idle read setting aside every claim of a touched thought.

  The other two survive:
  - The idle read setting aside a claim in hand, as written, is equivalent: the write's bound still excludes it.
  - The clock's rest taken from the sleep's start survives against the unscaled test too, so it is not this change's.
- **Every [38] case still runs:** 110 assertions, as before.
- **Under load:** three sleep-shard runs with every CPU busy (18 burners) all passed, [38] at 262 s each.

**Measured after.** Locally, [38] took 318 s before, and 262–272 s after over six runs. The spread is `endSleep`: a try whose write finds work pauses the sleep and waits its 6 s, two to four times a run. CI: CIMEASURED.

**Not taken.**
- **A blanket scale in `sleepScaffold`,** prototyped at 0.6: `abortAfterMs` has a 60 s floor and whole-millisecond values, the `--poll` refusal case was bypassed, and every line regex naming seconds failed.
- **`endSleep` waiting less per try.** At 3 s its capturing writes outran a 4 s capture and the sleep never drained (one run went the full 60 s). Waiting for drained pools before each write was worse, since the idle read counts differently: seven 6 s misses in one run.
