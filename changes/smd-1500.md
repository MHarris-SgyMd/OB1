## FORK

bench-hnsw.ts section F: `match_thoughts` under concurrency — ten connections at once, QPS and p99, no answer changed, and the walk's memory not where 014 priced it (SMD-1500)

**What changed.**
- **`db/bench-load.ts` (new): the closed loop and its pure parts.**
  - N connections, each its own backend (asserted: calls queued on one backend are not N at once). Each makes its next call the moment its last returns, for `OB1_BENCH_LOAD_S` seconds (60). No call starts after the deadline; the calls in flight at it are awaited and counted. The first error stops every connection from starting another call and is thrown.
  - Each connection takes its mix's slots in turn, one call each, and walks every query; the connections start on different slots and different queries.
  - p50 and p99 are nearest-rank. Recall is averaged per query, then over the queries reached, beside one call's recall over the same queries. "Changed" counts calls whose rows, as a set, differ from one call alone's for the same query.
  - Through `pg_read_file` on a connection outside the pool: the container's memory (cgroup v2 `memory.current`, `memory.stat`) every 100 ms, and its CPU time (`cpu.stat`) and the VM's (`/proc/stat`) across the run, so a row says how many CPUs other containers used meanwhile.
- **`db/bench-hnsw.ts`: section F**, after D, last for its scale, so it cannot perturb A–E; a failure in it is reported without discarding them, and its sessions carry a statement timeout.
  - Three mixes, in this order: the unfiltered default path; 50% alone, the call 014's header prices; and 50%, 1% and 900 rows in turn (at a million rows and up the HNSW walk, the tier whose plan flips between GIN and the walk, the exact branch). The turn weights the slots equally by calls, not by time: the slow 1% call holds a connection about 93% of the filtered mix's time.
  - Each backend first makes one untimed call per slot. Then every pair is asked alone on one connection, for the answers the loaded calls are compared with. Then every mix runs at every count.
  - Off unless `OB1_BENCH_LOAD` is set, so the documented commands measure what they did.
- **The report** belongs beside change 28's single-call tables. Change 28 is at its line ceiling (707 of 710; check 15's `OVERSIZE_AT_SPLIT`), so it is here.

**Why.** Every latency the bench printed was a median of one call at a time on one connection. The server's default pool is ten (`OB1_PG_POOL`), and the figures the fork compares itself to are throughput and p99 under load. 014's header priced ten concurrent broad filters at 430–640 MB of walk memory on top of `shared_buffers`, and nothing had measured it.

**Held.**

- test-live [39] (+8): four connections make at least three times one connection's calls (80 against 20 at 50 ms a call); one backend under two connections is refused; the last call starts before the deadline and the run ends after it; every pair is reached; a sort's memory that comes and goes inside a run reads as the peak; the first error stops the run.

The VM is shared with other sessions' containers; they used 0–1.6 CPUs during the runs. "Alone" is section A's or B's median, taken earlier in the run on a heap and cache in another state; the one-connection rows are the same-state baseline.

**What the numbers say.**
- **Concurrency changed no answer.** Not one call in any mix, at either scale; recall under load equals one call's. The recall floor is the index's own (change 28), not the load's.
- **Ten connections on eight vCPUs give about four times one connection's throughput** on the unfiltered and broad paths (4.2–4.3×), with their p99 at 18–53 ms.
- **The memory 014 priced is not where it goes.** Ten concurrent broad filters grew the backends' memory by 4–5 MiB against the 640 priced: the broad walk fills its candidates early and stops. The growth is the filtered mix's, and by elimination the 1% tier's (the broad slot alone grows 4–5 MiB): one band walk at a time peaked at 30–38 MiB, inside 014's 43–64 MB a backend, and about nine at once at 201–224 MiB.
- **The band tier is the slow one, and it is CPU-bound under load.** The planner walked HNSW for 1% at both scales (9.0 and 5.4 of 10), as in change 28's passes 1–2 at a million rows and pass 2 at ten million. Under ten connections its p99 is 0.8 s at a million rows and 1.7 s at ten million, with the database on 5.8–6.7 of the 8 CPUs. That is SMD-1464's band.
- **037's gate shows here as the ticket expected.** The 50% tier is 15 ms alone at ten million rows, where change 28 measured 268 ms before the routing count was gated (SMD-1463); ten at once are 33 ms at p50.
- Between the first measured run, before review pass 1's fixes, and this one, the unfiltered and broad throughputs moved 12–28% with what else shared the VM; the zero in "changed" did not move, and the memory figures moved by under 20 MiB.
