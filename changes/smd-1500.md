## FORK

bench-hnsw.ts section F: `match_thoughts` under concurrency — ten connections at once, QPS and p99, no answer changed, and the walk's memory not where 014 priced it (SMD-1500)

**What changed.**
- **`db/bench-load.ts` (new): the closed loop and its pure parts.**
  - N connections, each its own backend (asserted: calls queued on one backend are not N at once). Each makes its next call the moment its last returns, for `OB1_BENCH_LOAD_S` seconds (60). No call starts after the deadline; the calls in flight at it are awaited and counted. The first error stops every connection from starting another call and is thrown.
  - Each connection takes its mix's slots in turn, one call each, and walks every query; the connections start on different slots and different queries.
  - p50 and p99 are nearest-rank. Recall (of the exact top 10) is averaged per query, then over the queries reached, beside the reference pass's recall over the same queries. "Changed" counts calls whose rows, as a set, differ from the reference pass's for the same query.
  - Through `pg_read_file` on a connection outside the pool: the container's memory (cgroup v2 `memory.current`, `memory.stat`) every 100 ms, and its CPU time (`cpu.stat`) and the VM's (`/proc/stat`) from the run's start to its last return, so a row says how busy everything else on the machine was meanwhile (other containers, the kernel, the machine's side of the network path; the client and podman's host-side port forwarder run outside the VM).
- **`db/bench-hnsw.ts`: section F**, after D, last for its scale, so it cannot perturb A–E; a failure in it is reported without discarding them and the bench exits 1, and its sessions carry a statement timeout.
  - Three mixes, in this order: the unfiltered default path; 50% alone, the call 014's header prices; and 50%, 1% and 900 rows in turn (at a million rows and up the HNSW walk, the tier whose plan flips between GIN and the walk, the exact branch). The turn weights the slots equally by calls, not by time: by the p50s, the slow 1% call holds a connection 91–95% of the filtered mix's time.
  - Each backend first makes one untimed call per slot. Then the reference pass: every pair asked once, one call at a time, on one connection. Then every mix runs at every count.
  - Off unless `OB1_BENCH_LOAD` is set, so the documented commands measure what they did.
- **The report** belongs beside change 28's single-call tables. Change 28 is at its line ceiling (707 of 710; check 15's `OVERSIZE_AT_SPLIT`), so it is here.

**Why.** Every latency the bench printed was a median of one call at a time on one connection. The server's default pool is ten (`OB1_PG_POOL`), and the figures the fork compares itself to are throughput and p99 under load. 014's header priced ten concurrent broad filters at 430–640 MB of walk memory on top of `shared_buffers`, and nothing had measured it.

Read together:
- **Concurrency changed no answer.** Not one call in any mix, at either scale; recall under load equals the reference pass's. The recall floor is the index's own (change 28), not the load's.
- **Ten connections give about four times one connection's throughput** on the unfiltered and broad paths (4.2–4.3×), with their p99 at 18–53 ms. The ceiling is not the database's CPU: it used 3.6–3.8 of the 8 at ten connections, its CPU per call flat (1M unfiltered: 4.9 ms at one connection, 4.5 at ten). What bounds it — the client on the host, the port forward into the VM, or waits inside the server that use no CPU (the 1M corpus is about six times `shared_buffers`) — is not identified here.
- **The memory 014 priced is not where it goes.** Ten concurrent broad filters grew the backends' memory by 4–5 MiB against the 640 priced: the broad walk fills its candidates early and stops. The growth is the filtered mix's, and by inference the 1% tier's (the broad slot alone grows 4–5 MiB; the 900-row exact branch scores and sorts under a thousand rows): one band walk at a time peaked at 30–38 MiB, under 014's 43–64 MiB a backend, and about nine at once at 201–224 MiB.
- **The band tier is the slow one, and CPU-heavy under load.** The planner walked HNSW for 1% at both scales (9.0 and 5.4 of 10), as in change 28's passes 1–2 at a million rows and pass 2 at ten million. Under ten connections its p99 is 0.8 s at a million rows and 1.7 s at ten million, with the database on 5.8–6.7 of the 8 CPUs and at least 1.3–1.6 not busy (8 less both CPU columns); at a million rows the filtered mix's CPU per call doubles under ten connections (75 to 151 ms), by inference the band's (the broad slot's stays flat). That is SMD-1464's band.
- **037's gate shows here as the ticket expected.** The 50% tier is 15 ms alone at ten million rows, where change 28 measured 268 ms before the routing count was gated (SMD-1463); ten at once are 33 ms at p50.
- Between the first measured run, before review pass 1's fixes, and this one, the unfiltered and broad throughputs moved 8–34%, every one up. The runs differ in mix order, schedule, cache state and what else shared the VM, so the cause is not isolated; the zero in "changed" did not move, and the anon growth figures moved by under 20 MiB.
