## Changelog

`db/bench-hnsw.ts` section F measures `match_thoughts` under concurrency when `OB1_BENCH_LOAD` names connection counts, such as `1,10` (SMD-1500). N connections run closed-loop over three mixes. It reports QPS, p50 and p99 beside the single-call medians, recall@10 against the exact oracle, the answers that differed from one call alone, and the database container's memory against migration 014's price.

## FORK

bench-hnsw.ts section F: `match_thoughts` under concurrency — ten connections at once, QPS and p99, no answer changed, and the walk's memory not where 014 priced it (SMD-1500)

**What changed.**
- **`db/bench-load.ts` (new): the closed loop and its pure parts.**
  - N connections, each its own backend (asserted: calls queued on one backend are not N at once). Each makes its next call the moment its last returns, for `OB1_BENCH_LOAD_S` seconds (60). No call starts after the deadline; the first error stops every connection and is thrown.
  - Each connection walks every (slot, query) pair of its mix, slot by slot, and the connections' cycles start spread apart.
  - p50 and p99 are nearest-rank. Recall is averaged per query, then over the queries reached, beside one call's recall over the same queries. "Changed" counts calls whose rows, as a set, differ from one call alone's for the same query.
  - The container's memory (cgroup v2 `memory.current` and `memory.stat`) is sampled every 100 ms through `pg_read_file` on a connection outside the pool, and the VM's load average is read before each run.
- **`db/bench-hnsw.ts`: section F**, after D, last for its scale, so it cannot perturb A–E.
  - Three mixes: the unfiltered default path; 50%, 1% and 900 rows in turn (the HNSW walk, the tier whose plan flips between GIN and the walk, the exact branch); and 50% alone, the call 014's header prices.
  - Each backend first makes one untimed call per slot. Then every pair is asked alone on one connection, for the answers the loaded calls are compared with. Then every mix runs at every count.
  - Section F does not run unless `OB1_BENCH_LOAD` is set, so the documented commands measure what they did.
- **The report** belongs beside change 28's single-call tables. Change 28 is over the line cap and may only shrink (check 15's `OVERSIZE_AT_SPLIT`), so it is here.

**Why.** Every latency the bench printed was a median of one call at a time on one connection. The server's default pool is ten (`OB1_PG_POOL`), and the figures the fork compares itself to are throughput and p99 under load. 014's header priced ten concurrent broad filters at 430–640 MB of walk memory on top of `shared_buffers`, and nothing had measured it.

**Held.**
- test-schema [73] (+17): the schedule covers every pair once per cycle and spreads the connections, the percentiles, the per-query recall weighting, "changed" by set, the cgroup and load-average parses.
- test-live [39] (+7): four connections make about four times one connection's calls (20 against 80 at 50 ms a call), one backend under two connections is refused, the run ends within a call of its deadline, every pair is reached, the first error stops the run, and memory is read or the reason given.

**Measured.** Same machine as change 28's scale tables: Apple M5 Pro host, podman libkrun VM with 8 vCPUs and 14.8 GB, `pgvector/pgvector:0.8.6-pg16` at image defaults (`shared_buffers` 128 MB, `work_mem` 4 MB), 50 queries, 60 s a run. The VM was shared with other sessions' containers: its one-minute load average before a run read 1.7–9.1 at a million rows, so the latencies carry that noise and the recall columns do not.

**What the numbers say.**
- **Concurrency changed no answer.** Not one call in any mix, at either scale; recall under load equals one call's. The recall floor is the index's own (change 28), not the load's.
- **Ten connections on eight vCPUs give four to five times one connection's throughput** on the unfiltered and broad paths: 683 and 410 QPS at a million rows, 521 and 233 at ten million. p99 stays under 90 ms.
- **The memory 014 priced is not where it goes.** Ten concurrent broad filters grew the backends' memory by 4 and 11 MiB against the 640 priced: the broad walk fills its candidates within a few hundred tuples and stops. The growth is the 1% tier's. In the filtered mix's closed loop a 1% call is 93% of a connection's time, so about nine band walks are in flight at once: 201 and 243 MiB, 22–26 MiB a walk, under the 64 MiB a backend 014 priced.
- **The band tier is the slow one, and more so under load.** The planner walked HNSW for 1% at both scales this run (444 and 365 ms alone; 9.0 and 5.4 of 10), as in change 28's passes 1–2. Under ten connections its p50 is 839 and 947 ms and its p99 1.3 s. That is SMD-1464's band.
- **037's gate shows here as the ticket expected.** The 50% tier is 11.9 ms alone at ten million rows, where change 28 measured 268 ms before the routing count was gated (SMD-1463); ten of them at once are 40 ms at p50.

**Not taken.**
- An open-loop arrival rate: closed-loop is what a pool of ten callers does, and what the ticket asked.
- A sleep pass in progress during the run, which SMD-1794's Verify needs. The bench corpus has no extraction queue and no entities, so a sleep over it would measure a pass with nothing to do. That arm needs a corpus a pass can work on, and stays with SMD-1794.
