## Changelog

Fork Checks' observability job runs the collector's scrape of pg-exporter and the alert rules' evaluation every 10 s, through an overlay derived from compose's own render, where the shipped files say 30 s. It pulls lgtm's and pg-exporter's images in the background from the job's start, and no longer restores a stack that no step reads after it (SMD-3199).

## FORK

The observability job: CI's clocks at 10 s from compose's render, the images pulled in the background, no restore (SMD-3199)

**What changed** (`.github/workflows/fork-checks.yml`, the "Full stack: the observability profile" job).
- **The clocks.** After the render-and-cmp checks, the step writes an overlay under `$RUNNER_TEMP` from compose's own render, substituting 30 s for 10 s in two places:
  - `"scrape_interval":"30s"` in lgtm's OTELCOL_EXTRA_ARGS;
  - the rule group's `interval: 30s`.

  Only the profile's `up` takes it. The step asserts that each substitution changed its value, and that the overlay's render differs from the shipped one in those two values alone. Checks 37 and 38 hold the files' text.
- **The pull.** A step before the bring-up reads lgtm's and pg-exporter's refs from compose.yaml with Bun's YAML parser, refusing a ref not pinned by digest. It pulls them in the background, writing the log and then the exit code by a rename. The observability step waits up to 10 minutes for a 0 before it reads the disk, and on anything else prints the pull's log.
- **No restore.** The step's tail is gone: `.env` copied back, the profile's containers removed, the servers recreated, and the check that they no longer send to the collector. The step is the job's last before the teardown, and its comment says a step added after it brings the restore back.

**Why.** The step took a median of 143.5 s over 34 runs:
- the series wait was on the collector's 30 s scrape;
- the Postgres rule waited for the 30 s evaluation after pg-exporter stopped;
- the pull took 21–28 s in the foreground;
- the restore took about 12 s.

**Held.** The overlay's own check fails the step if a substitution misses, or if the overlay changes anything else.

**Measured after.**
- **On a scratch stack** (the scratch kit, the job's own lines, review pass 1), the step passes end to end in 106.6 s:
  - every one of the dashboard's 33 queries returned a series in 43 s and 32 s on two runs, where CI's 30 s scrape took about 63 s;
  - the Postgres rule went pending 10.4 s after pg-exporter's stop, where CI took about 31 s;
  - Grafana reports the rule group at interval 10, and lgtm's collector at a 10 s scrape.
- **The ticket's two mutants** each fail the step at its own line:
  - a dashboard query's metric renamed, in dashboard.json and compose.yaml's copy, fails the series wait: "Database size [A]: 0";
  - the Postgres rule's two factors pointed at a metric that stays 1 fails its wait 186 s after the stop. A plain rename of `up` doesn't reach that wait: the rule's NoData state is Alerting, so it reads pending at the start and the rules' judgement fails first.
- **The overlay's lines**, rehearsed against a local render under `bash -eo pipefail`, write 10 s in both places and pass the check. With the scrape substitution dropped, the check refuses the overlay.
- **The pull step and its wait**, rehearsed with docker stubbed, pass a clean pull. With one image's pull failing, the wait stops the step with the exit code and the log. On the scratch stack, a real 2.6 GB pull outlived its step and wrote 0.
- **actionlint 1.7.7 and its shellcheck** pass, and so does the consistency checker. CI's job time is the PR run's to show.

**Review pass 1.** A cold read, and the step on a scratch stack with the ticket's two mutants.
