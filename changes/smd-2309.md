## Changelog

The authorization server limits abuse before its pages go public: password checks across every client (ten at once, then one a second; a right password costs none) and five wrong passwords per sign-in, always; and with `OB1_AUTH_TRUSTED_PROXY` naming the proxy in front, per client address, five wrong passwords free and then a lockout of a minute doubling to fifteen, twenty failed authentications of one client in fifteen minutes, and `OB1_AUTH_REGISTRATIONS_PER_HOUR` registrations an hour (30 unless set). Each refusal is a 429, with `Retry-After` where waiting helps (SMD-2309).

## FORK

The authorization server limits abuse itself — `/auth` can go public with SMD-1846's PR 2 (SMD-2309)

SMD-2285's third cut capped registration across all addresses. Nothing limited sign-in: once the proxy routes `/auth`, the operator's password page is public, and each guess costs one argon2id verify, 52 to 63 ms on the host.

A per-address limit is only as good as the address. Behind a host tunnel the proxy sees one peer for the whole internet, and compose's Traefik trusts no forwarded header, so it forwards that one address (measured): a per-address lockout there locks out everyone. So the limits come in two layers.

- **`deploy/auth/limits.ts`**, dependency-free, kept in memory, every map bounded at 50,000 entries (the oldest go first):
  - Always on: `Bucket`, one for password checks across every client (`SIGN_IN_RATE`, ten at once, then one a second). A right password gives its check back. Past it the page asks for a moment. It does not keep the operator in: under a flood the right password is refused while the flood lasts, and nothing is left when it stops. `Tries`: five wrong passwords per sign-in flow, then start again.
  - With a trusted proxy: `SignInBackoff` (five free per address, then a minute doubling to fifteen, cleared by the right password, forgotten fifteen minutes past both the last failure and the lockout's end). `WindowLimit` counts failed client authentications (20 in 15 minutes, per address and client: `clientIdOf`, `clientKey`) and registrations an hour, keeping only the last `limit` events.
  - `clientAddress`: a peer that is not the trusted proxy is its own address, whatever its headers say. From the proxy, it is the `X-Forwarded-For` entry `OB1_AUTH_FORWARDED_HOPS` from the right. With that entry missing it is none, and only the first layer applies. Every address, the proxy's included, is put in one form (no port, brackets or zone; IPv6 as a URL writes it; v4-mapped as IPv4), and `addressKey` counts an IPv6 address by its /64.
  - `trustedProxy` resolves the name at start and every five minutes. While it does not resolve, the set is empty and the per-address layer is off.
  - `countsAgainstClient`: `invalid_client` counts only when the request presented a Basic header, a `client_secret` or a `client_assertion`, so a public client with a purged `client_id` never does. `server.ts` also counts only a client the library found that holds a secret, so neither made-up ids nor public or metadata-document clients can push an address's own entry out of the map. `clientIdOf` keys an assertion with no `client_id` by its `sub`.
- **`deploy/auth/server.ts`:** a sign-in checks every limit, then reserves every one, before its first await (the body read, the verify), so a concurrent burst is held as the same sign-ins one at a time. A token or revocation request's form body is read before the library sees it (the library takes a body read upstream), so the client it names is known and a refused one never reaches the secret check. A registration takes its per-address slot at admission. It gives the slot back once an answer other than 201 is sent, or when its body stalls past the 30 s timeout; a request dropped otherwise keeps it. A spent sign-in's page has no form. Failed client authentications are counted from the library's `grant.error` and `revocation.error` events, after it answers, so a concurrent burst can pass twenty by the requests in flight. Refusals are the sign-in page or OAuth's JSON shape; the token paths match as the library routes them.
- **Three settings**, `OB1_AUTH_TRUSTED_PROXY`, `OB1_AUTH_FORWARDED_HOPS` and `OB1_AUTH_REGISTRATIONS_PER_HOUR`, checked at start, passed by compose, and documented in `.env.example` and the README's "Abuse limits", which says when to leave the proxy unset. The proof of concept sets none of them. Its relay is one address, and the always-on layer passes it.
- **Left, with the reason:**
  - No lockout across addresses. A shared one would let anyone lock the operator out, and the break-glass sign-in that would answer it (and a flood) is SMD-2286's.
  - Registrations an hour stay per address, not per client, since each registration is a new client: a platform's shared egress shares its thirty (README).
  - No per-address bucket. Measured on the host against twenty right passwords, one a second: a flood from four loops let none in with no proxy (the pass-2 reviewer's own run let one in). With the proxy and four attacking addresses, each was locked after six checks and six right passwords got in. A hundred addresses let none in. The backoff already bounds one address, and a per-address bucket would not bound many.
  - Bad client secrets in a concurrent burst are each checked before any counts. Reserving needs a refund on success, and the secrets are long: SMD-2498.
  - Naming `proxy` as trusted waits for SMD-1846's PR 2. The rest of SMD-2309 (`/mcp`, `/dashboard`, `/api` and the proxy's own limits) is still open.
- **Tidied after the review passes, no behaviour changed:** the failure listener beside its handler again, and the token body reader beside its one caller; `loginPage` takes `{ wrong, note, form }` by name, not three positional values.
- **Held by:**
  - `limits.ts --self-check`: 40 probes on a clock of their own, among them a burst of eight from one address (five free, the sixth reserved and locking, the rest refused), the forgetting edge to the millisecond, the address rule with and without trust, a missing entry, ports, brackets, zones, the /64 and a proxy written in another form, the client a request names, a failed resolution, and the bounded map.
  - `provision.ts --self-check`: the three settings and compose passing them.
  - The full-stack CI step: with the trusted proxy set to the container's loopback, 21 bad secrets for one client from one forwarded address give 20 401s, then a 429; that client from another address, or another client, still gets 401; and 21 bad secrets each for a client that does not exist and for a public one all get 401.
  - Measured once on the host, not in CI. With no proxy: a sixth wrong password in one flow is refused, and the right one on a new flow signs in. Twenty concurrent wrong passwords get ten checks and ten 429s. The per-address limits stay off whatever `X-Forwarded-For` says.
  - Measured once with the proxy: the sixth wrong password from an address locks it (the right one too) and leaves another address alone. Eight concurrent attempts verify six. Forty concurrent registrations create thirty, and 31 from new addresses in one /64 create thirty too. Failed registrations give their slots back. Bad secrets with no forwarded entry are never charged to the proxy. Past twenty failures for `gui` from one address, `mcp` from it still gets 401, and `gui`'s right secret from another gets 200. A body-borne bad secret counts the same, and bad secrets for a made-up or a public client never count. A registration stalled past 30 s gives its slot back.
  - Run once, scratch (not committed): 58 drop-the-mechanism mutants, all killed, 41 by the two self-checks and 17 in `server.ts` by the host runs. Without the right password's check given back, the proof of concept fails A4, A8 and X3; with it, 32/32 after each pass.
