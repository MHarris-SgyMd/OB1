## Changelog

`db/test-live.ts`'s [10h] now fails a subject sweep whose end-of-run hand-back of its claims gives up at a scheduler's statement budget instead of waiting up to `RELEASE_WAIT`: a new case stops the run so that this hand-back alone returns the claim (SMD-3737).

## FORK

test-live [10h] holds the end-of-run hand-back to its wait of `RELEASE_WAIT` — one that gives up at the budget no longer passes (SMD-3737)

**What changed.** A [10h] case beside the lost poll's stop cases, note 18 in `db/test-live.ts`:
- **The run.** Following, under `BUDGET_SESSION` (750 ms), `RELEASE_WAIT` at its default 30 s.
- **The hold.** The case takes the claim's row as the judge is asked (`holdClaimRow`), and sends a first stop once it holds it. The thought's release then waits on the row past the budget with the stop already in, so `lostPoll`'s first check ends the run at the stop and passes its own hand-back over. That leaves the claim to the end-of-run hand-back, in the loop's `finally`, by itself.
- **Whose hand-back waits.** The case reads the run's lines as they are said. The release's lost-poll line, "…its stop ends it there", must be said before a hand-back is seen waiting on the row. A poll that handed back before its stop check would say it only once its own hand-back ended.
- **The let-go.** The row goes `BUDGET_MS + 500` after the end-of-run hand-back is seen waiting, or as the run ends, or as the 20 s poll for that wait runs out. The 500 ms is the margin `within`'s landing keeps, and the hold is what fails a hand-back that gives up at the budget.
- **The asserts.** The claim ends `pending`. The release's lost-poll line is said, before the wait. "The claims this run holds could not go back to the pool" is not said. The stop line says what the run held is back in the pool, and the run exits 0.

**Why.** `releaseHeld` (`db/subject-sweep.ts`) waits up to `RELEASE_WAIT` under a scheduler's session, whatever the budget. It runs as the second stop's hand-back, as a lost poll's and as the end-of-run hand-back. During SMD-3713's review pass 2, a mutant bounded only the end-of-run hand-back, at `min(RELEASE_WAIT, budget)`, and it passed every [10h] row:
- `within`: the stop's hand-back waits on the row too and covers for it;
- `past`: `RELEASE_WAIT` there is `BUDGET_MS + 100`, so both bounds fail alike;
- the every-kind case: `RELEASE_WAIT` there is 500 ms, under the budget.

The run it breaks is a scheduler's that ends without a second stop, with a claim's row held past the budget but within `RELEASE_WAIT`. That run would leave the claim leased until its lease runs out.

**Held.** [10h] by itself, against the unchanged engine and mutants of the hand-backs and of the case:
- **The ticket's mutant,** `min(RELEASE_WAIT, budget)`: 222 of 223 pass, only the new case failing, the claim left `claimed`. A hold of `BUDGET_MS + 250` still fails it, three of three; with no hold after the wait is seen, it lives.
- **A lost poll that hands back before its stop check**, with or without the ticket's mutant: only the new case fails, the release's line not said before the wait. With the poll's own hand-back bounded at the budget too, the line read after the hold would pass; read as the wait is seen, it fails.
- **The budget as the end-of-run hand-back's only bound:** the new case fails, as does the hard stop's race case (SMD-3306 PR 6a's review pass 7). Its `RELEASE_WAIT` is 1 s, under its 30 s budget, so its end-of-run hand-back is still waiting on the claims' table, taken again in SHARE mode, at the case's 20 s bound.
- **No first stop sent:** the new case fails, the claim `succeeded` and the run still following at 20 s.
- **The unchanged engine:** 223 of 223 pass.

**Measured after.** By its lines' timestamps, the case takes 2.26–2.28 s, review pass 2 changing no timing:
- **On the Mac:** alone, three side by side, and in eight runs of [10h] beside eight CPU burners (load ~16 and other sessions' suites), all passing. The first commit's case, with its own holder, took 2.3–2.7 s in six runs beside the burners at load ~40–56.
- **On Linux at 4 CPUs,** Postgres and Bun 1.4.3 in one pod: 2.26–2.28 s in six runs, two beside four burners, all passing; both mutants above fail only the new case there too.
- **By design** it is the release's wait to the budget (750 ms), up to 250 ms of the poll, and the hold's 1,250 ms: 2.0–2.25 s and the run's own steps.

| 2 | The line's read was placed before the hold for no stated reason; read after it, a poll's own hand-back bounded at the budget gives up and says the line, and passes | The reason in the comment | mutant |

**Not taken.**
- **A hold of `BUDGET_MS + 250`** would take 250 ms off the case. It would also leave a 250 ms margin over the mutant's bound, half of `within`'s, for a case a mutant must fail on every run.
- **Accepting another lost poll's line in place of the release's.** The line is said once a run, so a statement before the release past the budget on a loaded host would say its line first and leave the release's unsaid. Notes 10 and 17 assert the same once-said line, and in SMD-3713's Linux runs, burners beside some, Postgres logged no unheld statement past 500 ms, so the case keeps their shape.
- **Reading the line in the same instant the wait is seen.** It is read after the poll's query returns. A poll-first engine whose own hand-back gives up at the budget could say the line in that gap only on a host stalled ~500 ms; a read before the query could fail a correct engine whose hand-back starts during it.
