## FORK

test-live [10h] holds the run's end hand-back to `RELEASE_WAIT` alone — a hand-back bounded at the budget no longer passes (SMD-3737)

**What changed.** A [10h] case beside the lost poll's stop cases, note 18 in `db/test-live.ts`:
- **The run.** Following, under `BUDGET_SESSION` (750 ms), `RELEASE_WAIT` at its default 30 s.
- **The hold.** The case takes the claim's row as the judge is asked (`holdClaimRow`), and sends a first stop once it holds it. The thought's release then waits on the row past the budget with the stop already in, so `lostPoll`'s first check ends the run at the stop and passes its own hand-back over. That leaves the claim to the run's end hand-back, in the loop's `finally`, alone.
- **Whose hand-back waits.** The case reads the run's lines as they are said. The lost poll's "its stop ends it there" must be said before a hand-back is seen waiting on the row. A poll that handed back before its stop check would say it only once its own hand-back landed.
- **The let-go.** The row goes `BUDGET_MS + 500` after the end's hand-back is seen waiting, or as the run ends, or as the 20 s poll for that wait runs out. The 500 ms is the margin `within`'s landing keeps, and the hold is what fails a hand-back bounded at the budget.
- **The asserts.** The claim ends `pending`. The release's lost poll is said as ended by the stop, before the wait. "The claims this run holds could not go back to the pool" is not said. The stop line says what the run held is back in the pool, and the run exits 0.

**Why.** `releaseHeld` (`db/subject-sweep.ts`) waits up to `RELEASE_WAIT` under a scheduler's session, whatever the budget. It runs as the second stop's hand-back, as a lost poll's and as the run's end hand-back. During SMD-3713's review pass 2, a mutant bounded only the run's end hand-back, at `min(RELEASE_WAIT, budget)`, and it passed every [10h] row:
- `within`: the stop's hand-back waits on the row too and covers for it;
- `past`: `RELEASE_WAIT` there is `BUDGET_MS + 100`, so both bounds fail alike;
- the every-kind case: `RELEASE_WAIT` there is 500 ms, under the budget.

The run it breaks is a scheduler's that ends without a second stop, with a claim's row held a little past the budget. That run would leave the claim leased to its end.

**Held.** [10h] alone, against the unchanged engine and mutants of the hand-backs:
- **The ticket's mutant,** `min(RELEASE_WAIT, budget)`: 222 of 223 pass, and the new case alone fails, the claim left `claimed`. A hold of `BUDGET_MS + 250` still fails it, three of three; with no hold after the wait is seen, it lives.
- **A lost poll that hands back before its stop check**, beside the ticket's mutant: the new case alone fails, the stop's line not said before the wait.
- **The budget alone as the bound:** the new case fails, as does the hard stop's race case (review pass 7). Its `RELEASE_WAIT` is 1 s, under its 30 s budget, so the run's end hand-back is still waiting on the claims' table, taken again in SHARE mode, at the case's 20 s bound.
- **No first stop sent:** the new case fails, the run still following at 20 s.
- **The unchanged engine:** 223 of 223 pass.

**Measured after.** On the Mac, by its lines' timestamps, the case takes 2.27–2.28 s in single runs and runs three side by side. It takes 2.3–2.7 s in six runs of [10h], three side by side beside eight CPU burners, all six passing. By design it is the release's wait to the budget (750 ms), up to 250 ms of the poll, and the hold's 1,250 ms: 2.0–2.25 s and the run's own steps.
