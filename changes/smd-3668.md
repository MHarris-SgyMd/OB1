## Changelog

`search_thoughts` can weigh what matters (SMD-3668), with two opt-in inputs. `priority_weight` (0–1, default 0) orders the top matches by meaning and effective priority together, so of four High tickets the one due tomorrow comes first even when it matches the query least. `min_priority` (0 none to 5 critical) keeps only matches whose priority is known and at least that level, urgency included.
A match's priority is its own effective priority: a ticket's row reads as its ticket, and a note inherits from the ticket it is about at one level less. The header says what was ranked and kept, and with neither input set, search ranks exactly as before.
Every search, list and fetch result now says what matters among what it names: the known priority of itself and of the tickets it is filed under or names, with the terms behind each (a `Priority:` line, a `[priority: …]` title prefix, or fields over REST).

## Record

`search_thoughts` takes `priority_weight` and `min_priority`, ranking and filtering a window of its matches by effective priority, and every result carries its priority label, PRs 1 and 2 of 3, and PR 3 measures `priority_weight` on SMD-3662's eval (SMD-3668)

**What changed.**
- **Two inputs on `search_thoughts`**, so on REST `/v1/search` too:
  - `priority_weight`, clamped to 0–1 as `recency_weight` is;
  - `min_priority`, an integer 0–5 as `set_priority`'s level is.
- **A window re-rank in the core** (`core/reads.ts`), not a migration:
  - With either set, the hybrid (or `search_thoughts_current` under `prefer_current`) is asked for min(100, 4 × limit) matches, 077's window.
  - **One `effective_priority` read (122)** covers the whole window, each match asked as its own thought (`prioritiesOf`). Known means a stated level, or an inherited one that 122 counted. With neither, the match is unknown.
  - **The order** is `(1 − w) × relevance + w × priority` (`rankByPriority`). Relevance is the match's place in the window, (n − i) / n, so the search's own order, ties and all, is what weighs. Priority is effective over 9, the default weights' largest (level 5, overdue 3, unblocks 1). Ties keep the window's order.
  - **The floor** keeps known priorities at or above `min_priority`, urgency included, per decision 5's composition. An unknown never passes.
- **With neither set:** no window, so the same SQL call and the same order; PR 2's label read is the only read, and the label the only change to the reply.
- **The reply:**
  - The header says how the window was ranked, how many matches carry a known priority, what a floor kept, and whether a match past the window went unread.
  - Under `prefer_current`, the current-first note says what the demotion did and leaves the order to the priority note.
  - A literal cut off by a floor says to lower it.
  - Under a weight, the notes on the order (a text match's newest, a literal-only query's similarity) say priority ordered the rows.
  - A ranked search on a brain short of 122, or under a role granted before 116, fails with a hint naming the fix (`priorityHint`).
  - The structured value carries `priority`: weight, floor, window, read, kept and known.
- **The query log:** a search with either set is not logged, as one with `min_trust` isn't. The log has no column for them, so a replay would run it without them.
- **Docs:** `server-portable/README.md` has a section on it, and `docs/priority.md`'s "A read" names the window.

**A match's priority is 122's, by the ADR.** The ticket says "a thought about a ticket reads the ticket's, as moved does". `docs/priority.md`'s weights table, decided again by the maintainer on 2026-11-06 in SMD-3667's review, says otherwise:
- inheritance costs one level a hop, and counts only above unknown's 2;
- a stated level beats it;
- a settled item passes nothing down.

`effective_priority` implements that table, and this read takes its answer for each match's own thought. So:
- a ticket's row reads as its ticket;
- a note filed under an Urgent ticket reads High (4 − 1);
- a note under a High ticket stays unknown (3 − 1 is not above 2), so `min_priority: 3` doesn't keep it;
- a note under a finished ticket reads nothing from it, while the finished ticket's own row keeps its stated level (122 drops only its urgency and what it unblocks), so `min_priority` and `priority_weight` treat a Done High as High; `prefer_current` is the way to rank finished work lower (the maintainer's call in review pass 2, against folding finished-ness into the floor);
- a thought stated None stays None, whatever its ticket says.

Review pass 1 found the first reading, the highest of a thought's and its filed tickets', breaking all three rules.

**Why not in SQL.** `recency_weight` blends inside `match_thoughts`' four branches (074), and the hybrid's score is its fusion score (075). A 0–1 blend there needs a migration through those branches and 059's frozen callers. `prefer_current` already re-ranks a window (077), and so does this, from one read per search.

**Narrowed.**
- **The compat `search`.** The ticket names `priority_weight` on it too. That tool takes only its query, which is ChatGPT's shape, and already pins `recency_weight` and `prefer_current` off. A client never sends it more, so it doesn't rank by priority. PR 2's label reaches it.
- **Mentions.** "As `moved` does" could read as counting the tickets a hit's text mentions, as `moved` reads them. Inheritance through mentions is one of `docs/priority.md`'s "Not decided here", waiting for SMD-3662's numbers, so a mention counts for nothing.
- **Open items.** The ticket's "or open item it names" is not read. A thought names no open item in its metadata. An open item serves its origin thought, not the reverse (122), so a thought takes nothing from one.

**Calibration.** Relevance by place moves 1/n a rank, and priority 1/9 a level. In a window of 40 at weight 0.2, one level is worth about one rank. Review pass 2 replaced relevance by score: the fusion scores bunch (the 40th about 0.6 of the first), and several kinds of row all score 0. This is not the same blend as `recency_weight`'s, and SMD-3662's eval (PR 3) measures it before any default changes.

**Measured.** One `effective_priority` read for a full window (100 thoughts over 5,000, no priorities stored) takes 6.5 ms median, 6.9 ms p90. The cost applies only with either input set.

**PR 2, the label.** Every result of `search`, `search_thoughts`, `search_thoughts_keyword`, `list_thoughts` and `fetch` carries `priority`:
- **What it names:** the known priorities of the result itself (a ticket's row as its ticket, once) and of the tickets it is filed under or names, its first 50 (`priorityNotRead` the rest). An unknown is no label. Each item has its kind and key, `self`, effective, level or `inheritedFrom`, due time, urgency, `overdue`, `unblocks`, `settled`, `waiting`, and over REST its terms. Itself first, then the most pressing; ten named, `priorityMore` the rest.
- **Over MCP:** a `Priority:` line on the three text tools, each term as the agenda says it; their values without the terms, which name the stating key, and compact: no null, false or zero field, and no key on a result's own thought (review pass 4: a 100-row list's value went from 35 KB to 103 KB). `search` and `fetch` start the title `[priority: …]` (two named) after any claimed prefix, the field only where an item is prioritised, so every other result keeps ChatGPT's shape.
- **One read per call** (`readPriorities`): the page's thoughts, then its tickets, 1000 items at most, a key 115 refuses never sent. Best-effort as the claims read is (`priorityOf`): a failed read is `priority: null`, said once a reply, `[priority not read]` in the compat titles. A ranked search makes two: its window read asks the window's own thoughts alone, which rank it and fail it as in PR 1, and its label read only the kept page's tickets, reusing the window's answers, best-effort. An empty page asks nothing. A ticket's own row is asked by its key too: whether 122 reads its thought as the ticket rests on what this side can't see.
- **A mention is labelled though it never ranks:** the label shows what a result names, the rank what it is. The ticket's "open item it names" is narrowed as in PR 1.
- **Measured,** no priorities stored, 5,000 thoughts: 2.9 ms for a 10-hit page (10 tickets), 14 ms for a 100-row list page (40 tickets), 88 ms (p90 107 ms) for `list_thoughts`' largest page of 1,000 rows, every row's own priority labelled and the tickets they name past the reach (the maintainer's call in review pass 2, over labelling the first 100); a `SELECT 1` is 0.2 ms. On a seeded board (review pass 4: 20,400 thoughts, 400 linked tickets, 500 statements) it is 20–30 ms a 10-hit page and 135–167 ms a 100-row page, an unstated ticket walking its parents; the maintainer took it documented, with 122's projection of unblocks and inheritance as the follow-up (SMD-4150). A ranked search's window read stays PR 1's. Every search, list and fetch now pays it, beside the claims read.

**PR 3, the measurement.** SMD-3662's eval measures `priority_weight` as `PRIORITY_WEIGHT_READ` in `evals/priority.ts`: `search_thoughts` with `priority_weight` 0.5 and every other argument at its default.
- **The weight was pre-registered** on SMD-3668 on 2026-11-08, before any run, as the value it would ship with (the bar's rule for a read with a weight). The self-check pins the call, so a changed weight is a new pre-registration, never a re-run.
- **It waited for SMD-3669 PR 2** (OB1 PR 714), whose `plantFacts` plants the levels, due times, links and claims. Before that, the seeded brain stored no priority, and the read would have scored as plain search.
- **The verdict: it doesn't clear the bar** (top-1 0.143, top-3 0.286, mean tau 0.212; plain search 0.143, 0.286, 0.252). It fails (a), (b) and (c), and keeps (d). `evals/README.md` has the table and the reasons.
  - Every note reads unknown 2, since its facts sit on its open item (SMD-4149). The blend keeps rows of equal priority in the window's order at any weight, and on three prompts the gold's first item is a note behind another note. So no weight reaches 5 of 7 here.
  - At 0.5 the Urgent ticket rises from eighth to fifth on two prompts, but not first.
- **So the default stays 0.** SMD-4149 re-measures this read at the same 0.5 once `effective_priority` reads a note's open items.
- **`--priority-weight w,...`** measures the read at other weights too, each an exploratory read (`priority_weight@<w>`) that is scored and set against the bar but says it is no verdict. A weight outside 0 to 1, one listed twice, the pre-registered one, or another option is refused.
- **At 0.7 and 0.9** (the maintainer asked for them after the verdict, exploratory): top-1 3 and 4 of 7, mean tau 0.520 and 0.559. Each would pass (b) and (c), and fail (a) and (d). The Urgent ticket rises to first, and the notes fall: on `due-soon` the gutters, the gold's first, drop out of the top 3. 0.9 reaches the 4-of-7 ceiling.
- **On the canary's real board** (the maintainer asked for it, reads only, not a verdict): the seven prompts at weights 0, 0.5, 0.7 and 0.9. Only 2 to 14 of the 20 rows re-ranked carry a known priority, so the weight moves little. At 0.9, "which ticket next" puts three Done tickets first, each keeping its stated level, and `prefer_current` beside the weight puts an open one first. The 11-02 filter case is in priority order already at 0. `evals/README.md` has the detail.
- **Held:** the self-check (91 rows), two seeded runs with the same hits for this read, and the sweep through the flag giving the same hits as two runs with the weight edited in. Mutants are killed: another weight, another tool, an extra argument, no weight, the hits reordered, an exploratory read ignoring its weight, and the flag taking a weight outside 0 to 1, one listed twice, or the pre-registered one.
