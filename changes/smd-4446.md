## Changelog

Migration 148 adds the store for settled parts (SMD-4446): a statement in one thought that the other thought of a pair shows is no longer true or no longer open, kept with a quote from each, a reason and the word, as a proposal a reviewer decides. An accepted part writes no thought, so nothing is superseded or demoted. Nothing writes or shows a part yet, and an upgrade asks nothing of its operator: the pass and the review surfaces are the ticket's later pull requests.

## Record

Settled parts get a store — a statement in one thought that another settles is recorded with both quotes and decided by a reviewer, both thoughts left standing (SMD-4446)

**What changed.** PR 1 of four: the store. `docs/subject-anchored-claims.md`, rows 8 to 10, decides what it holds.

- **`settled_parts`** (migration 148), one row a part: the pair as a proposal names it, which thought holds the statement (`holder`, with `holder_id`), the statement and the words of the other thought that show it, the fingerprint of each text as judged, the word (one of SMD-3116's eight settling labels) and its group (`word_group`: `no_longer_holds` or `done_or_settled`; a word in neither is refused), the reason, the judge's key, a status (pending, accepted, rejected, stale) and who decided it.
  - A pair may hold several parts, and a whole-thought proposal beside them. One statement of one thought stands once a pair (`settled_parts_one_statement`), read as a thought's text is (`content_fingerprint_of`: case and runs of white space folded). So a statement a reviewer rejected is not proposed there again in other capitals, while another statement of the pair may be. The fold is that much and no more.
  - **A row is one judgement, and none is written over another** (`settled_parts_keep_judgement`): no UPDATE moves its pair, holder, quotes, word, reason, fingerprints or judge, whatever its status. When the pass judges a statement again, the judgement is another row with another id, or is not recorded. So, through the two functions and against any UPDATE, an id names the words a reviewer read, or nothing.
  - Both thoughts are foreign keys that cascade, and a part's lineage row goes with it (`settled_parts_drop_derivation`): a deleted or forgotten thought leaves neither its parts' quotes nor their recipes. The planner keeps no statistics on a part's words (`SET STATISTICS 0` on the four columns that hold them and on the identity index's fingerprint), so `ANALYZE` copies none into `pg_statistic`.
- **`record_settled_part`**, the pass's one write. It refuses a part of the wrong shape with a RAISE that echoes no argument, and a call above READ COMMITTED. It takes the supersession advisory lock, which a decision on a proposal or a part, a rebuild, a delete and a forget each take first, so that no rebuild at READ COMMITTED runs between its read of the two texts and its commit. It reads both texts and answers one of three ways:
  - `{ok: false, error: "EDITED_SINCE", older_edited, newer_edited}`, nothing written, when a fingerprint given is not its text's: its caller puts the thought back for its new text.
  - `stands`, with the id and status of the row that holds the statement, when that row is pending at these texts, or decided, whatever its texts have done since. The judgement given is not recorded, and the row is locked until the write's transaction ends.
  - `recorded`: a new pending row and its one lineage row (kind `part`). `replaced` names the row it took the place of: an undecided row holding the statement that was no longer a judgement of these texts (stale, or pending at other fingerprints), deleted with its lineage row.
  - The lock is held to the end of its transaction, and no lock an edit waits for is taken. A caller makes the write as a statement of its own: no thought's or claim's row held when it is called, nothing awaited before the commit.
- **`review_settled_part`**, the reviewer's decision. `EDITED_SINCE` refuses an accept when either text has moved since it was judged, unless forced. `ALREADY_ACCEPTED` and `ALREADY_REJECTED` refuse a decision that repeats the row's status, and a reviewer may reverse one: the row keeps the last decision alone. A caller that says the status it read (`p_was`) is refused `STALE_READ`, with the row's, when that is another. The id of a part since judged again is `NOT_FOUND`. A stale row may be decided as a stale proposal may. A decision asks no actor, as a proposal's asks none. READ COMMITTED alone. The reviewer columns are stamped by 114's trigger function. An acceptance updates the part's own row and writes no thought.
- **Lineage:** `part` joins `derivations`' kinds. `ob1_record_derivation` is 111's body with the kind. `derivation_descendants` is 063's with a part beside the proposal in the walk's order. `rebuild_derived` is 067's with the part beside the proposal: a moved text sets a pending part stale and queues its newer thought again under the part's key; a decided part stands; a lineage row whose part is gone is an orphan. Its result gains `stale_parts` and `cascading.parts`, which `db/rebuild.ts` prints. The two censuses of stale lineage rows (preflight's `lineage` row and `db/rebuild.ts --status`) leave a part's row out, as they do a proposal's.
- **Grants:** the worker and server groups gain `settled_parts` (`db/config.mjs`), the worker's with `DELETE`, which the write asks on every call that gets past its refusals. 148 carries `SELECT, UPDATE` to every role holding `UPDATE` on `supersession_proposals` and `INSERT, DELETE` to every role holding `INSERT` there; a role that only reads the proposal queue gains nothing. `rebuild_derived` reads the table once a thought's walk holds a part's lineage row, so a role outside those groups that ran it before is refused on such a thought.

**Why.** The maintainer marked every proposal decide version 5 had made on the canary by 2026-11-18 (SMD-3340): of 41, 2 were right as a whole, 32 right about a part and 7 neither. Accepted as built, the 41 would mark 39 thoughts superseded that still stand. Nothing stored a part.

**Not taken.**

- **A rule that a part's proposer may not accept it, that a decision names its maker, or that it says the status it read.** 117 has the first two for a proposed close. The proposals' review has none of the three, and each would be enforcement nobody asked for. They are put to the maintainer on the ticket.
- **A guard in `rebuild_derived` against REPEATABLE READ.** The write and the decision refuse above READ COMMITTED; the rebuild, a carried body, does not. One whose transaction is REPEATABLE READ or SERIALIZABLE, having waited on the lock behind a write, reads the store as it first saw it, misses the part and queues nothing: 20 of 20. Every caller in the repository runs it at READ COMMITTED (0 of 20). It is one condition, in a function every rebuild and forget goes through: put to the maintainer on the ticket.
- **A raw write held to the functions' rules.** A role holding `UPDATE` can decide a part by hand, or take a stale one back to pending, as it can a proposal; no UPDATE changes the row's words. A role holding `DELETE` and `INSERT`, as the pass's now does, can remove a row and write another under its id (4 of 4 statuses), and a decision by that id then lands on the new words (20 of 20). The table could give every id itself; not built.
- **The rewrite in place, kept and fenced.** Fixed where it stood, a pending row at a moved text rewritten too, a decision that gave the id alone landed on unread words 60 of 80: it is safe only while every caller proves what it read. A row that is never written over asks no proof.
- **A lock of the write's own, or one a pair.** The rebuild that must not run under the write takes the supersession lock, and so does every decision and delete: the write takes that one, and is single file with them.
- **An index for a listing.** Nothing lists parts yet, and the partial index the first cut made for the queue would not have served a listing that takes the status as a parameter, as 110's does: 4.4 ms for 20 of 30,000 parts under a generic plan, 0.3 ms with `(status, judged_at, id)`. PR 3 brings the index its listing is measured to need.
- **The quote check in SQL.** Whether a part's words are in its two texts stays the pass's check, whose rule for a part SMD-4442 decides. Nor is the pair's capture order checked: the caller names it, as a proposal's does.
- **A settle for parts the pass no longer names, and a requeue for a pending part whose text moved with no rebuild.** Both are the pass's, in PR 2, with the reopening of a rejection the pass made.
- **One part for a statement quoted two ways.** The fingerprint folds case and spacing. Of 20 spellings of one statement a person would call the same, 9 were recorded beside the rejected one; of 10 the pass's own quote check finds in a text, 9 were another part. Which characters the pass records for a span is PR 2's.
