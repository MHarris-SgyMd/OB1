## Changelog

Migration 151 adds the store for settled parts (SMD-4446): a statement in one thought that the other thought of a pair shows is no longer true or no longer open, kept with a quote from each, a reason and the word, as a proposal a reviewer decides. An accepted part writes no thought, so nothing is superseded or demoted. Nothing writes or shows a part yet, and an upgrade asks nothing of its operator: the pass and the review surfaces are the ticket's later pull requests.

## Record

Settled parts get a store — a statement in one thought that another settles is recorded with both quotes and decided by a reviewer, both thoughts left standing (SMD-4446)

**What changed.** PR 1 of four: the store. `docs/subject-anchored-claims.md`, rows 8 to 10, decides what it holds.

- **`settled_parts`** (migration 151), one row a part: the pair as a proposal names it, which thought holds the statement (`holder`, with `holder_id`), the statement and the words of the other thought that show it, the fingerprint of each text as judged, the word (one of SMD-3116's eight settling labels) and its group (`word_group`: `no_longer_holds` or `done_or_settled`; a word in neither is refused), the reason, the judge's key, a status (pending, accepted, rejected, stale) and who decided it.
  - A pair may hold several parts, and a whole-thought proposal beside them. One statement of one thought stands once a pair (`settled_parts_one_statement`), read as a thought's text is (`content_fingerprint_of`: case, runs of white space and white space at either end folded). So a statement a reviewer rejected is not proposed there again in other capitals, while another statement of the pair may be. The fold is that much and no more. The index holds what that function read when each row was written: a later file that changes the fold rebuilds it.
  - **A row is one judgement, and none is written over another** (`settled_parts_keep_judgement`): no UPDATE moves its pair, holder, quotes, word, reason, fingerprints or judge, whatever its status. When the pass judges a statement again, the judgement is another row with another id, or is not recorded. So, through the two functions and against any UPDATE, an id names the words a reviewer read, or nothing. An undecided part's id ends when a later judgement replaces it: a foreign key to it that refused the delete would refuse the write.
  - Both thoughts are foreign keys that cascade, and a part's lineage row goes with it (`settled_parts_drop_derivation`): a deleted or forgotten thought leaves neither its parts' quotes nor their recipes. The planner keeps no statistics on a part's words (`SET STATISTICS 0` on the four columns that hold them and on the identity index's fingerprint), so `ANALYZE` copies none into `pg_statistic`.
- **`record_settled_part`**, the pass's one write. It refuses a part of the wrong shape with a RAISE that echoes no argument, and a call above READ COMMITTED. It takes the supersession advisory lock, which a decision on a proposal or a part, a rebuild, `delete_thought` and `forget_thought` each take first, so that no rebuild at READ COMMITTED runs between its read of the two texts and its commit. It reads both texts and answers one of four ways:
  - `{ok: false, error: "NOT_FOUND", older_gone, newer_gone}`, nothing written, when a thought is not there: deleted or forgotten while the judge was out. Said before a fingerprint is compared.
  - `{ok: false, error: "EDITED_SINCE", older_edited, newer_edited}`, nothing written, when a fingerprint given is not its text's: its caller puts the thought back for its new text.
  - `stands`, with the id and status of the row that holds the statement, when that row is pending at these texts, or decided, whatever its texts have done since. The judgement given is not recorded, and the row is locked until the write's transaction ends. (The status is `stale` only where one was moved by hand under the write.)
  - `recorded`: a new pending row and its one lineage row (kind `part`). `replaced` names the row it took the place of: an undecided row holding the statement that was no longer a judgement of these texts (stale, or pending at other fingerprints), deleted with its lineage row.
  - The lock is held to the end of its transaction, and no lock an edit waits for is taken. A caller makes the write as a statement of its own: no thought's or claim's row held when it is called, nothing awaited before the commit.
- **`review_settled_part`**, the reviewer's decision. `EDITED_SINCE` refuses an accept when either text has moved since it was judged, unless forced. `ALREADY_ACCEPTED` and `ALREADY_REJECTED` refuse a decision that repeats the row's status, and a reviewer may reverse one: the row keeps the last decision alone. A caller that says the status it read (`p_was`) is refused `STALE_READ`, with the row's, when that is another. The id of a part a later judgement has replaced is `NOT_FOUND`. A stale row may be decided as a stale proposal may. A decision asks no actor, as a proposal's asks none. A forced accept is recorded as any accept is. READ COMMITTED alone. The reviewer columns are stamped by 114's trigger function. An acceptance updates the part's own row and writes no thought.
- **Lineage:** `part` joins `derivations`' kinds. `ob1_record_derivation` is 111's body with the kind. `derivation_descendants` is 063's with a part beside the proposal in the walk's order. `rebuild_derived` is 150's, which is 067's with the tags' arm feeding the re-tag pool (SMD-2298), with the part beside the proposal: a moved text sets a pending part stale and queues its newer thought again under the part's key; a part already stale is queued again and counted once; a decided part stands; a lineage row whose part is gone is an orphan, tested in a statement of its own. Its result gains `stale_parts` and `cascading.parts`, which `db/rebuild.ts` prints. The two censuses of stale lineage rows (preflight's `lineage` row and `db/rebuild.ts --status`) leave a part's row out, as they do a proposal's.
- **Grants:** the worker and server groups gain `settled_parts` (`db/config.mjs`), the worker's with `DELETE`, which the write asks on every call that gets past its refusals. 151 carries `SELECT, UPDATE` to every role holding both on `supersession_proposals`, and `INSERT, DELETE` to one holding `INSERT` there besides; a role holding less gains nothing. `rebuild_derived` reads the table once a thought's walk holds a part's lineage row, and updates a pending part whose text moved: a role that ran it before and holds neither privilege is refused on such a thought, and on no other.

**Not taken.**

- **A rule that a part's proposer may not accept it, that a decision names its maker, or that it proves what it read.** 117 has the first two for a proposed close; the proposals' review has none. `p_was` is the status alone: two decisions in between that leave the status as read are not seen (10 of 10), and a forced accept leaves no mark that it was forced or at which texts. What a decision records is cheap to change until the first is made, and costly after: put to the maintainer, with the review surfaces (PR 3).
- **The kind's CHECK in a file of its own.** Applied alone it cost no writer a deadlock (0 of 20 with forgets looping, 0 of 12 with page writes); ahead of the table in this file it deadlocked captures and edits instead. 064 and 084 took the two tables in one file as this does. Put to the maintainer.
- **A guard in `rebuild_derived` against REPEATABLE READ.** The write and the decision refuse above READ COMMITTED; the rebuild, a carried body, does not. One whose transaction is REPEATABLE READ or SERIALIZABLE, having waited on the lock behind a write, reads the store as it first saw it, misses the part and queues nothing: 20 of 20. A forget refuses there; `db/rebuild.ts` and the sleep scheduler's sweep run at their session's level, READ COMMITTED unless a default is raised (0 of 20). Put to the maintainer.
- **A raw write held to the functions' rules.** A role holding `UPDATE` can decide a part by hand, or take a stale one back to pending, as it can a proposal; no UPDATE changes the row's words. A role holding `DELETE` and `INSERT`, as the pass's does, can remove a row and write another under its id (4 of 4 statuses), and a decision by that id then lands on the new words (20 of 20). The table could give every id itself; not built.
- **Counts of parts where an operator looks.** `db/rebuild.ts --status` prints the stale proposals and no stale part; preflight, `brain_info` and the dashboard say nothing of parts; a pending part at a moved text is counted nowhere until a rebuild or the next judgement; the sweep finds four kinds' orphaned lineage rows and neither a proposal's nor a part's; preflight's lineage coverage reads no `settled_parts`. No part exists before the pass writes one: these go with PR 2 and PR 3.
- **The rewrite in place, kept and fenced.** Fixed where it stood, a pending row at a moved text rewritten too, a decision that gave the id alone landed on unread words 60 of 80. A row that is never written over asks no proof.
- **An index for a listing, and commit order.** Nothing lists parts yet. A listing that takes the status as a parameter read 20 of 30,000 parts in 5.2 ms under a generic plan, which cannot use the first cut's partial index, and in 0.3 ms with `(status, judged_at, id)`: PR 3 brings the index its listing is measured to need. `judged_at` is the write's transaction start, so a listing that pages by it can pass over a row committed late.
- **The quote check in SQL, and one part for a statement quoted two ways.** Whether a part's words are in its two texts stays the pass's check (SMD-4442), as does the pair's capture order. The fingerprint folds case and spacing: of 20 spellings of one statement a person would call the same, 9 were recorded beside the rejected one; of 10 the pass's own quote check finds in a text, 9 were another part. Which characters the pass records for a span is PR 2's.
- **A settle for parts the pass no longer names, and a requeue for a pending part whose text moved with no rebuild.** Both are the pass's, in PR 2, with the reopening of a rejection the pass made.
- **Preflight reading this file's own sentinel.** It reads 067's in `rebuild_derived`, so 067 alone re-applied by hand over this file is not seen, and leaves no arm for a part. `migrate.ts --reapply` runs every file in order.
