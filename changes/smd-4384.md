## Changelog

Under the sleep scheduler, re-embed now runs alone first for at most its share of a period — `OB1_SLEEP_REEMBED_SHARE` (`--reembed-share`), 120 s by default, a live write's pause and its isolation of an outage not counted — then gives way to extraction and consolidation while they have work; they run as long in turn, and with re-embed's work waiting the period then ends and the next re-embeds first (SMD-4384). Under a steady inflow of thoughts with no vector neither pool drained, so re-embed held the claim passes the whole period and nothing was extracted or consolidated. A model switch's backlog is shared the same way. No migration. **Upgrading:** a re-embed backlog longer than 120 s — a model switch's, a brain's from before migration 021 — is no longer re-embedded whole before extraction while extraction or consolidation has work; consolidation may judge some neighbours on the old vectors meanwhile, and a one-shot `db/sleep.ts` run exits 1 with the backlog left. `OB1_SLEEP_REEMBED_SHARE=0` (`--reembed-share 0`) keeps the old order.

## Record

Re-embed and the claim passes take turns in the sleep scheduler's periods — a steady inflow of thoughts with no vector no longer holds extraction and consolidation for good, nor, once re-embed gives way, its vectors (SMD-4384)

**Why.** `db/sleep.ts` runs re-embed alone at a period's start, and the claim passes (extraction, consolidation) start only once it ends: its pool drained, or its provider not answering. Its follower pools every 15 s, so while thoughts with no vector arrive faster than that, one not yet pooled is always counted (`unpooled` at least 1) and `drained()` never holds. SMD-4254's review pass 1 measured it with the real scheduler at its shipped settings: on main, one vectorless thought every 4 s, extraction ran 0 of 64 thoughts in 240 s; on SMD-4254's branch, its crashing thought beside the same inflow, 0 of 80 in 300 s, where main's old set-down at the crash's outage had freed extraction by accident. Such an inflow comes from an egress policy keeping captures from a remote embedding provider, a provider too slow for captures while the vector mender queues them, or a bulk import of rows with no vector.

**Decided.** The maintainer's choices (2026-11-19), of the ticket's three options: a share of the period (not "drained enough", not re-embed beside the claim passes); by time, alone first, then stopping for the period (not a fraction of the period's time with the claim passes paused for it, not continuing beside them); applied to a model switch's backlog too (not exempting the backlog a period starts with), so consolidation may judge some pairs on the old model's vectors while a switch is re-embedded across periods; 120 s a period by default (SMD-4254's per-wait bound, about 400 vectors at 0.3 s each).

**What changed.**

- `db/sleep.ts`: `REEMBED_SHARE_S` (120, exported) and `--reembed-share SECONDS` (0–86,400; `SleepOptions.reembedShare`, `reembedShareMs` the tests' override). Re-embed's start in a period is taken on its clock (`reembedClock`, which a live write's pause stops); each poll while it runs, its time since less the period's isolation (`isolatedMs`) at or past the share — no wait in hand, no isolation in hand, the pool not drained — ends it after the thought in hand, said with what it leaves ("re-embed has had its share of the period — 120 s alone — N thought(s) left for the next period, which begins once the claim passes drain — extracting"), and the claim passes start. Nothing is set down (no `outage`, no row `aside`), nothing backed off, no suspect moved. `reembedLeft` is set, so a one-shot run exits 1, its pool left, as at a set-down; and the next idle poll reads the pools whole (`lastWhole` cleared; `wholeNext` since review pass 1) — no poll between reads re-embed's claims (`workClaimed` skips it) — and the next period begins as soon as the claim passes drain, re-embed first again, where it waited up to the hourly rescan. The start says the share (`share:`), and the "re-embedding N thought(s)" line names it. 0 is no share, as before.
