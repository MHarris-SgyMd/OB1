## Changelog

A listen stream's end is recorded: a line of its own on the MCP server's stdout, `listen_end`, with the caller's name, how the stream ended (`client`, `stop` or `server`) and how long it was held, and, where telemetry is sent, a log record and a point on `ob1.mcp.listen_stream.duration` (SMD-4459). No server holds a stream yet, so nothing the brain serves or sends changes. `deploy/README.md`'s filter for requests that did not end `ok` now asks that a line have an `outcome`, which an end's line has not.

## Record

The end of a listen stream is recorded: a line, a log record and a histogram of the time held, by how it ended (SMD-4459)

**What changed.**

- `server-portable/telemetry.ts`: `endListenStream` records one end, at once and whole. Its line goes where request lines go: `ts`, `door`, `event` (`listen_end`), `agent`, `end`, `held_ms` and `trace`, each held to its rule as a request's fields are. One point goes on `ob1.mcp.listen_stream.duration`, in seconds from one to a day, labelled `ob1.end`. Where log records are sent, one named `ob1.listen_end` goes at `INFO`, its body the line's fields, under no span. It throws nothing: a sink or an SDK that throws loses the record and not the stream's end.
- `server-portable/otel-setup.ts`: a log record may carry a name of its own; a request's is `ob1.request`, as it was.
- `mcp-server/app.ts`: a stream held open has its ends made by `holdStream`, outside the route. The request's abort and the reader's leaving are `client`; the stream's own end is `server`, or `stop` once the stop's drain has ended. Each gives the stream's place back and records the end once: a stream is in a set until its end is said, and an end is said only by taking it out.
- `mcp-server/listen-streams.ts` and `mcp-server/index.ts`: the entry's stop tells the app as its drain ends (`stopped`), and the app records each stream still open as the stop's, keeping its place until the stream is closed.
- `deploy/README.md` has the operator's account: the line, its three kinds of end, what a dropping stream, a stop and a kill each leave, how an end is paired with its opening, and what a collector gets. Its filter for requests that did not end `ok` asks for an `outcome`, and filters for the ends, their counts and their openings stand beside it. `mcp-server/README.md` names the line. `docs/mcp-listen-streams.md` has the rule as amended and a section for this change.

**Why.** SMD-4347's PR 2a wrote a stream's line as the stream opens and nothing at its end, so that no record is open across the stop's flush. The cost was that a stream which keeps dropping read as healthy traffic: one `ok` line of a millisecond for each reopening. The maintainer set this ticket to come before that capability is turned on, and decided on 2026-11-21: a line and a metric; one kind of end for a client's close and a dropped connection; the stop's ends recorded as its drain ends; no dashboard panel yet.

**Held.**

- Mutants, from a scratch spec outside the tree: thirty-seven of the route, the stop's word and the record, five of them from what the first review's mutants passed, each killed by one suite or the other. Two reviewers' own are under Review passes.

**Measured after.** Before the build on macOS, and in it in the Linux image CI's Bun pin has, each once, on loopback, by a probe outside the tree: what a Bun 1.4.3 server is told when a held stream's client goes. a request aborted, its reader's cancel, the client's exit, its SIGKILL, and a raw socket half-closed, destroyed and reset each reach the request as an abort, "The connection was closed", and then the stream's cancel. The server's own close alone is the stream's end. A client frozen for 2 s is seen only when it is killed. So the ticket's Verify item that a drop be told from a close was amended by the maintainer.

On the real entry, by the first review's adversary, on macOS: every opening had one end and, with a collector, one record and one point, with 200 streams stopped together, all leaving first, leaving as the signal came, and through 3,000 cycles of clients going at random moments; a stream's 3,202 ms on its line was 3,201 to 3,203 by its client's clock; and a stop with 250 streams took 28.8 ms where main's took 25.4 (the mean of eight, a collector answering), and with a silent collector 1,814 ms to main's 1,811.

**Review passes.** One, of two independent readers, each with a copy of the commit: an adversary on a real process with twenty-two mutants of its own, and one on every claim the change writes, as the operator and as each reader of the log. Neither found the mechanism wrong.

**Not taken.**

- No panel reads the ends: the maintainer left it until SMD-4347's PR 2b has real streams. The histogram's name in the profile's Prometheus was not measured, and check 37's table has no label for it yet, so a panel on `ob1_end` is refused until one is added.
- Nothing was measured through the stack's proxy, where the server's peer is the proxy: that a client's leaving reaches the server there, and how soon, is on the page's list for PR 2b.
- A stream open as the drain ends is `stop` though its client may leave before it is closed: 200 clients leaving as the signal came were 200 `stop` lines.
- A server that is killed records no end for the streams it drops, and a stop whose flush does not get out has its ends on stdout alone.
- The stop's `held_ms` is to its drain's end, short of the real close by the flush, 5 s at the most.
- The log record carries the opening's trace id in its body and is under no span: the stream's request ended its span as the stream opened.
- No row holds the route's own catch once a stream is held (a throw between the hold and the answer, which nothing there makes): it would record `server`. Nor that an SDK that throws loses the record alone, which a sink that throws is held to; nor that a client's leaving after the drain's end is not `client`, which is the same on the entry, the stop's word being said in the turn its flag is set.
