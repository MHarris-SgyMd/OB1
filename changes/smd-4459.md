## Changelog

A listen stream's end is recorded: a line of its own on the MCP server's stdout, `listen_end`, with the caller's name, how the stream ended (`client`, `stop` or `server`) and how long it was held, and, where telemetry is sent, a log record and a point on `ob1.mcp.listen_stream.duration` (SMD-4459). No server holds a stream yet, so nothing the brain serves or sends changes. `deploy/README.md`'s filter for requests that did not end `ok` now asks that a line have an `outcome`, which an end's line has not.

## Record

The end of a listen stream is recorded: a line, a log record and a histogram of the time held, by how it ended (SMD-4459)

**What changed.**

- `server-portable/telemetry.ts`: `endListenStream` records one end, at once and whole. Its line goes where request lines go: `ts`, `door`, `event` (`listen_end`), `agent`, `end`, `held_ms` and `trace`, each held to its rule as a request's fields are. One point goes on `ob1.mcp.listen_stream.duration`, in seconds from one to a day, labelled `ob1.end`. Where log records are sent, one named `ob1.listen_end` goes at `INFO`, its body the line's fields, under no span. It throws nothing: a sink or an SDK that throws loses the record and not the stream's end.
- `server-portable/otel-setup.ts`: a log record may carry a name of its own; a request's is `ob1.request`, as it was.
- `mcp-server/app.ts`: a stream held open has its ends made by `holdStream`, outside the route. The request's abort and the reader's leaving are `client`; the stream's own end is `server`, or `stop` once the stop's drain has ended. Each gives the stream's place back and records the end once.
- `mcp-server/listen-streams.ts` and `mcp-server/index.ts`: the entry's stop tells the app as its drain ends (`stopped`), and the app records each stream still open as the stop's, keeping its place until the stream is closed.
- `deploy/README.md` has the operator's account: the line, its three kinds of end, what a dropping stream looks like, and what a collector gets. Its filter for requests that did not end `ok` asks for an `outcome`. `mcp-server/README.md` names the line. `docs/mcp-listen-streams.md` has the rule as amended and a section for this change.

**Why.** SMD-4347's PR 2a wrote a stream's line as the stream opens and nothing at its end, so that no record is open across the stop's flush. The cost was that a stream which keeps dropping read as healthy traffic: one `ok` line of a millisecond for each reopening. The maintainer set this ticket to come before that capability is turned on, and decided on 2026-11-21: a line and a metric; one kind of end for a client's close and a dropped connection; the stop's ends recorded as its drain ends; no dashboard panel yet.

**Held.**

- `mcp-server/test-mcp.ts`, 307 rows from 297. [9c], in process: a stream its client closes leaves one line, `client`, with the time it was held; one whose request aborts the same, once; a listen that asks for nothing is `server`; a listen refused was never held and has no end; at the stop's drain's end each open stream is `stop` and keeps its place, and its close records nothing more; a close after the drain's end with no word first is `stop` too. [8h], the real entry: with no collector, the stop's line between the stop's two; with one, a stream closed after 400 ms and one held to the stop each a line, a log record and a point, the stop's sent by its flush, and the request-duration histogram holding the two openings alone; today's entry says nothing of an end.
- `server-portable/test-server.ts`, 1,800 rows from 1,797: the line's fields and their rules; the point, its label and bounds, and none on the request-duration histogram; the log record; a sink that throws.
- Mutants, from a scratch spec outside the tree: thirty-two of the route, the stop's word and the record, thirty-one killed by one suite or the other. One stands and is equivalent in what is recorded: an ended stream left in the set the stop reads is said once all the same, its end being said already, and the line it removes keeps that set from growing. One more stood at first, the request's abort saying no end, since the row's own close of the reader said it; the row now reads the end before it lets go.

**Measured before the build, and again in it.** What a Bun 1.4.3 server is told when a held stream's client goes, by a probe outside the tree, on macOS and in the Linux image CI's Bun pin has, on loopback: a request aborted, its reader's cancel, the client's exit, its SIGKILL, and a raw socket half-closed, destroyed and reset each reach the request as an abort, "The connection was closed", and then the stream's cancel. The server's own close alone is the stream's end. A client frozen for 2 s is seen only when it is killed. So the ticket's Verify item that a drop be told from a close was amended by the maintainer.

**Not taken.**

- No panel reads the ends: the maintainer left it until SMD-4347's PR 2b has real streams. The histogram's name in the profile's Prometheus was not measured.
- A server that is killed records no end for the streams it drops, and a stop whose flush does not get out has its ends on stdout alone.
- The stop's `held_ms` is to its drain's end, short of the real close by the flush, 5 s at the most.
- The log record carries the opening's trace id in its body and is under no span: the stream's request ended its span as the stream opened.
- No row holds the route's own catch once a stream is held (a throw between the hold and the answer, which nothing there makes): it would record `server`.
