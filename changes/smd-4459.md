## Changelog

A listen stream's end is recorded: a line of its own on the MCP server's stdout, `listen_end`, with the caller's name, how the stream ended (`client`, `stop` or `server`) and how long it was held, and, where telemetry is sent, a log record and a point on `ob1.mcp.listen_stream.duration` (SMD-4459). No server holds a stream yet, so nothing the brain serves or sends changes. `deploy/README.md`'s filter for requests that did not end `ok` now asks that a line have an `outcome`, which an end's line has not.

## Record

The end of a listen stream is recorded: a line, a log record and a histogram of the time held, by how it ended (SMD-4459)

**What changed.**

- `server-portable/telemetry.ts`: `endListenStream` records one end, at once and whole. Its line goes where request lines go: `ts`, `door`, `event` (`listen_end`), `agent`, `end`, `held_ms` and `trace`, each held to its rule: a name that is no string and an end it does not know are `?`, a time held that is no count is 0, a trace id that fails is left out. One point goes on `ob1.mcp.listen_stream.duration`, in seconds from one to a day, labelled `ob1.end`. Where log records are sent, one named `ob1.listen_end` goes at `INFO`, its body the line's fields, under no span. It throws nothing: a sink or an SDK that throws loses the record and not the stream's end, and a record that is null or undefined is left unsaid.
- `server-portable/otel-setup.ts`: a log record may carry a name of its own; a request's is `ob1.request`, as it was.
- `mcp-server/app.ts`: a stream held open has its ends made by `holdStream`, outside the route. The request's abort and the reader's leaving are `client`; the stream's own end is `server`, or `stop` once the stop's drain has ended. Each gives the stream's place back and records the end once: a stream is in a set until its end is said, and an end is said only by taking it out. The app says the set's size (`unsaid`).
- `mcp-server/listen-streams.ts` and `mcp-server/index.ts`: the entry's stop tells the app as its drain ends (`stopped`), and the app records each stream still open as the stop's, keeping its place until the stream is closed.
- `deploy/README.md` has the operator's account: the line, its three kinds of end, how an end is paired with its opening, what a dropping stream, sessions coming and going, a client that gave up, a silent connection, a stop and a kill each leave in the log, and what a collector gets. Its filter for requests that did not end `ok` asks for an `outcome`, and filters for the ends, their counts and holds by caller, the listen requests, and the server's starts and stops stand beside it. `mcp-server/README.md` names the line. `docs/mcp-listen-streams.md` has the rule as amended and a section for this change.

**Why.** SMD-4347's PR 2a wrote a stream's line as the stream opens and nothing at its end, so that no record is open across the stop's flush. The cost was that a stream which keeps dropping read as healthy traffic: one `ok` line of a millisecond for each reopening. The maintainer set this ticket to come before that capability is turned on, and decided on 2026-11-21: a line and a metric; one kind of end for a client's close and a dropped connection; the stop's ends recorded as its drain ends; no dashboard panel yet.

**Not taken.**

- No panel reads the ends: the maintainer left it until SMD-4347's PR 2b has real streams. The histogram's name in the profile's Prometheus was not measured, and check 37's table has no label for it yet, so a panel on `ob1_end` is refused until one is added.
- Nothing was measured through the stack's proxy, where the server's peer is the proxy: that a client's leaving reaches the server there, and how soon, is on the page's list for PR 2b.
- A stream open as the drain ends is `stop` though its client may leave before it is closed: in the first adversary's run, 200 clients leaving as the signal came were 200 `stop` lines.
- A server that is killed records no end for the streams it drops, and a stop whose flush does not get out has its ends on stdout alone.
- The stop's `held_ms` is to its drain's end, short of the real close by the flush, 5 s at the most.
- The log record carries the opening's trace id in its body and is under no span: the stream's request ended its span as the stream opened.
- An `ok` listen line does not say that a stream was held: one the SDK refused for its filter reads as an opening does, so a killed server's dropped streams are counted to a ceiling. A mark on the opening's line would settle it, and is a change to the request line's keys (SMD-4530).
- No row holds the route's own catch once a stream is held (a throw between the hold and the answer, which nothing there makes): it would record `server`. Nor that an SDK that throws loses the record alone, which a sink that throws is held to; nor that a client's leaving after the drain's end is not `client`, which is the same on the entry, the stop's word being said in the turn its flag is set.

**Follow-ups.** SMD-4530: a listen's request line reads `ok` whether a stream was held or the SDK refused it.
