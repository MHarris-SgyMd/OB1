# 322. A capture-only key's thought is taken while its supersedes chain reaches another's (SMD-2708)

**What changed** (migration 092).
- **The taken read** (`ob1_thought_taken`). 082's log read — now by name, `ob1_capture_taken_by_log`, over 082's unchanged rule `ob1_takes_thought` — or `ob1_chain_reaches_outside`: the thought's current `supersedes` pointers walked down reach a thought its capturer did not capture (its first capture row's agent, none included) or one 082's log took. The walk is cycle-safe and bounded at 1,000 steps; past the bound the taken read says taken (a refused pointer is safe). Read from the current pointers: an accept or another key's link onto another's thought takes the row, through any number of links of the key's own; a pointer between two of the key's own thoughts takes neither; a reject clearing the accept's pointer gives the row back; an accept made before 092 counts from 092 on.
- **The lapse** (082's trigger function, redefined). On an update event that takes its row by 082's rule or writes its `supersedes`, when that row is taken now — the chain read with the event's new pointer, which is not yet projected; past the bound, not taken (a lapse is not fail-safe) — it and every row above it through the pointers that stay (up to 1,000), each taken through it or captured by no one, has the capture-scoped pointers its capturer wrote onto it cleared (082's predicate, each an event under the actor in force) — never the event's own row, which a raw write's cycle could bring the walk back to. One walk down, for the event's row only: linear in the rows above. So an accept below a write key's link onto the key's thought lapses the key's pointer above the link. The trigger's WHEN gains `supersedes`.
- **The check at the write** (082's, redefined) reads the target's chain, locks it FOR SHARE from its deepest row up to the target, and reads it again: a chain that moved onto a row the first read did not lock is refused (OB004; the server drops the pointer and writes again), one only shortened — a taker's lapse cut it — is all locked and holds. A taker re-pointing a row of the chain holds that row and then the rows its lapse clears above it, as a delete's cascade does, so the capture waits behind it rather than crossing it, and the second of the two sees the first. Refused, not retried: a retry would lock the moved chain's new rows while holding the first pass's.
- **The re-capture note** (`ob1_note_recapture`, 082's, redefined): its once-only guard reads 082's log, not the chain, so a write key re-capturing a thought taken only through its chain is recorded and the thought stays taken when the chain is cleared.
- **Unchanged:** `ob1_takes_thought`, and the server's pre-check, which reads `ob1_thought_taken`. A capture naming a taken thought lands without the pointer, its reply as for any target the key cannot point at (SMD-2473: the reply says nothing of it).
- **Docs:** capture_thought's `supersedes` description (and the regenerated REST client), the store's docblock, and the session-capture recipe (the takings, the troubleshooting's audit query, and the repair's limit) say so.

**Why.** 082 (SMD-2638) stops a capture-only key's thought being its own once another key takes it, and counted no pointer a taking. SMD-1873's review pass 3 proved the chain on a scratch server: a capture key's copy was accepted over a write key's thought, `ob1_thought_taken(copy)` stayed false, the key captured new text with `supersedes: <copy>`, and search showed its unreviewed text current with both the copy and the write key's original superseded. The harm is the key's text current over another key's; a capture-only key's own pointers can only reach its own untaken thoughts, so only another key's pointer can put it there.

**Decisions** (the maintainer's).
- **At the plan stage: another key's pointer, not accepts alone** by the consolidator's door — a content rule, with no trust in a door string.
- **At the plan stage: no sweep of old accepts.** A pointer written before 092 onto a thought an earlier accept took stays until an event below it fires the lapse (SMD-2934 counts them); migrations here write no thought events with no actor.
- **At review pass 1: not the recipe's repair.** "Any other key's pointer takes the row" made the session-capture recipe's repair (a write key linking two of the hook's summaries) take the newer and lapse the hook's next pointer.
- **At review pass 2: the chain.** "A pointer onto a thought the capturer did not capture" let the standing through one link more — a second accept between two of the hook's thoughts, a write key's link onto an accepted one, an accept arriving after the hook's pointer above a link. Two seams in one mechanism: the rule now reads the harm itself.

**Not here.**
- **A pointer a lapse cleared** is not put back when the taking is undone (a reject of the accept), and `thought_changes` does not say why it lapsed: SMD-2933.
- **A taking of a row the capturer had moved on from** — a hook summary its later summary replaced, accepted or ranked over another's after — lapses the later summary's pointer, so the older reads current beside it (082's lapse, by design). The listing side is a comment on SMD-2707.
- **A thought the key wrote with no agent id** (the registry could not attribute it) counts as another's, as 082 counts it not the key's: a write key linking a summary to one written during such an outage takes the newer. 082 refuses ownership by name. So does a link onto one of the key's own thoughts that is, or later becomes, taken: the newer is taken through it, and the key's pointer onto the newer lapses.
- **The bound.** A chain of more than 1,000 of the key's own links reads as taken, and its next pointer is refused — far past a session hook's episode. The bound's two branches are held by review (a 1,001-link chain on a real Postgres), not by a suite.
- **Board-sync's chain** re-pointing a capture key's pasted-header row onto another's row takes it and lapses the key's pointer onto it until the next pass re-chains; the key's own pointer that board-sync carries is SMD-2670's.
- **Re-applying 082** restores its taken read, lapse, check and three-key WHEN; 092 after it puts them back.

**Tests.** test-schema [72] (082's rule cells as they were; a write key's pointer onto a thought another key took is a taking through the chain; [72]'s re-apply of 082 is followed by the shipped read) and [82] (the accept takes the thought and lapses the capturer's earlier pointer under the reviewer, and a capture naming it is refused; a write key's link onto another's takes; the recipe's repair keeps the hook's chain; an accept between two of the hook's thoughts takes nothing; a re-point onto another's takes and onto the hook's own does not; two accepts, a link onto an accepted copy, and an accept arriving after the hook's pointer above a link each take and refuse or lapse; a reject takes nothing; an accept then a reject gives the thought back with the lapsed pointer not restored; a thought with no agent id counts as another's; the walk stops at a cycle; a re-apply moves nothing), test-upgrade [20am], test-store-sql and test-store-postgrest [8c], test-store-sql [8f] (two connections: a capture above an accept in flight waits and is refused; one behind a taker's row lock does not deadlock with its lapse and lands on the chain the lapse shortened; one whose chain a write key re-points between its two reads is refused), test-e2e-sql [13e] (SMD-1873's chain through the real server). Ten mutants of the chain rule (the outside test or 082's log dropped from the walk, a cycle read as outside, the walk stopping at the first link, the taken read without the chain, the lapse without the event's new pointer, not walking up, or ignoring pointer events, the WHEN without `supersedes`, the check without the taken read), every one killed; the chain locks are held by [8f]'s two-connection cells (a reviewer's race found 43 of 60 runs breaking without them). Five more at pass 3: the note reading the chain, the locks taken top-down, no chain locks, and the lapse not walking up — four killed; the chain read again after the locks, which [8f]'s third cell (pass 4) now holds. Four at pass 4 — no re-read, refusing any change rather than a move onto an unlocked row, the lapse clearing the event's own row, the locks top-down again — each killed; [8f]'s third cell bounds its re-point, so a top-down regression fails rather than hangs the suite.
