## Changelog

The incremental-replay spike, measured on the dogfood brain: a projection rebuild reuses every stored vector by the `(content_fingerprint, embedding_model)` key the schema records, N edits recompute exactly N, a fresh vector equals its cached one to the last bit, and a full re-embed costs minutes where a full re-extract costs hours — the event-sourcing ADR's gate opens, with the graph named as the projection whose key and cost want the discipline first (SMD-1998).

## FORK

The incremental replay measured — every vector reused by the key the schema records, a full re-embed of the dogfood brain in minutes against a four-hour re-extract; the event-sourcing ADR's first gate opens and the graph is named the expensive projection (SMD-1998)

**What changed.** `evals/eval-projection-replay.ts` reads a live brain through `DATABASE_URL` on one connection set read-only before its first statement (nothing is written) and answers the ADR's gating question with arithmetic over every live thought: for each, the key its payload derives — `content_fingerprint_of(content)` by the SQL rule's owner (016), the target model from `ob1_config` — against the key its row records (`content_fingerprint`, `embedding_model`, a vector present or not), reuse on a hit, recompute on a miss with the reason the key states (`no-fingerprint`, `content`, `no-vector`, `unlabelled`, `model`). Five scenarios, the ticket's: a no-op rebuild (each row's key against its own text — the log as payload is out of scope, and the report says for how many rows the log holds text), N edits (in the arithmetic; the brain is untouched), a model bump, a comprehension-only change (derived from the contract, marked so), a window-recipe change. Unless `--no-provider`, a stratified sample of rows (by length, deterministic, the shortest and the longest always in it) is embedded through the REAL `createEmbedder` — the same windows, template and fallback a capture takes, `rememberRefusal` off as `reembed.ts` runs it — against the configured provider, one row at a time, for the cost per row (mean, median, and the mean without the two extreme ranks the spread over-weights) and per thousand characters, and for the cosine of the fresh vector to the stored one over the rows the replay would REUSE, parents and window vectors both. The graph's own cost is read from `thought_work_claims` (`finished_at − claimed_at` over succeeded rows, as the pass ran them under its own workers) per pass key, not re-run; the stale graph is counted from the audit (rows extracted before the fingerprint last moved) and classified by the pool under the RECORDED extraction key. `evals/projection-replay.ts` holds every rule pure: the contract (`PROJECTIONS`, the snapshot key per projection and what the row records of it), the replay rule, the pool parser, the scenarios, the sample, the cost model, the verdict against the pre-registered bar, the report. `--self-check` (96 probes) runs in the portable-server job; `--fixture-check` (25, a throwaway brain seeded with one row per branch) in the data-layer job. `evals/lib.ts` gains `median`.

**Why.** SMD-1997 proposes CQRS-lite — `thought_audit` as the write-side truth, the thoughts row, the chunk rows, the entity graph, the proposals and the capture-time metadata as projections that can be thrown away and replayed — and rests on one assumption it named as its first risk: that a rebuild does not re-embed the world, because re-embedding is the single most expensive operation in the system (015/018/021/034 exist to make it survivable). Nothing had measured whether the key 021 records is sufficient, whether a cached vector is what a fresh one would be, or what a recompute costs on the hardware the brain runs on. The contract was pre-registered on the ticket before the first run (the key per projection, the replay rule, the five scenarios and their expectations, the go/no-go bar: A reuses ≥ 99% with every miss stated by the key, B recomputes exactly N, D recomputes 0, C priced in wall-clock); one bar was added between the comment and the first run and is said here — a reused row whose fresh vector sits under cosine 0.99 to its stored one is a vector that is not a function of its key, the one defect the key cannot express, and fails the verdict.

**Held.** `--self-check` (portable-server job; no database, no model): the contract names five projections with a verdict from the list; the replay rule branch by branch and in order (no key, then a moved key, then the vector, then the label; a NULL label is unknown, unknown is not the target — 021); the pool parser and its classification under the recorded key; the five scenarios on a hand-known corpus; the stale-graph breakdown (queued, failed, finished before, never asked, pending elsewhere); the sample deterministic under any input order with the extremes always in it; the cost model's arithmetic (the trimmed mean, the cosine over reused rows only, the window cosine, the gap beside the four-decimal print); the verdict — GO on the ideal with a line per scenario and D marked derived, provisional with no provider or no reused row in the sample, NO-GO when reuse falls under the bar, when N edits recompute more or fewer than N beyond the no-op's or the wrong rows, when a model bump reuses a vector under another label, when a comprehension-only change touches a vector, when a reused row does not reproduce, when a cosine is not a number, on an empty brain; the report's lines; the argument rules, every shape. `--fixture-check` (data-layer job, a throwaway Postgres): the schema reset and seeded through raw SQL with one row per branch — a NULL label, no vector, no fingerprint, a key left stale by a raw content update, another model's label, a whitespace-only edit that moved content and not the key, three graph thoughts whose text moved after extraction with the pool under the recorded key queued / failed / finished before and one pending under another key, a windowed thought — read back through the queries the live measurement runs and held to the contract: every decision in its branch, 5/10 reused and NO-GO by the reuse share, two edits with one already a miss counted once, the audit's content and key counts apart, the stale graph and its pool as seeded, the claim log's span.

**Measured after** (the dogfood brain as of 2026-09-24 01:06 UTC, 421 thoughts, 1.76 M characters, every vector `qwen3-embedding:4b@1024`, host Ollama warm and otherwise idle; the full report is in `evals/README.md`, verbatim). A: the no-op rebuild reuses 421/421 — `content_fingerprint = content_fingerprint_of(content)` on every row, none NULL, every label the target. B: 10 edits recompute exactly 10, 411 reused. C: a model bump recomputes 421/421 at 7.9 min by rows (mean 1.13 s a row over 21 sampled), 4.7 min without the two extreme ranks (0.66 s), 7.2 min by characters (0.243 s per 1k) — the worst case is minutes. D (derived): a comprehension-only change recomputes no vector; the graph re-reads 421 thoughts and replaces 5,922 mention rows, and the claim log prices that at a median 36.9 s a thought under `extract:qwen2.5:7b@p2` (41.9 s under the 27B; 17.6 s a thought judged) — 4.3 h one row after another, thirty-three times the re-embed, an order of magnitude rather than a digit since the log recorded its seconds under two workers and the sample ran alone. E: a window-recipe change replaces 20 chunk rows under 3 thoughts (the same 3 the current recipe windows) and reuses every parent. The cached value reproduces: fresh against stored over the 21 reused rows, 1 − cosine at most 2.2e-16, the 21,345-character thought included (7 windows, 8 calls, 11.05 s) and its 7 window vectors at cosine 1.0000. Verdict GO, every pre-registered line met. Beside it, what the schema states and what it cannot: the capture row in `thought_audit` carries `metadata` and not `content` (008), so the log alone holds the text of 210/421 live thoughts (those with a content-moving update) and today the `thoughts` row is the payload store; the graph rows carry no fingerprint (016 checks one at write time and keeps none) — as of the run 11 thoughts held 205 mention rows extracted before their fingerprint last moved, under the recorded key 5 queued, 5 failed on a retry, 1 finished before the move, 6 of the unqueued pending under another key that refreshes nothing here; the chunk rows carry no recipe (022); the prompt template and the requested width ride on the model name by convention (code, not data), which the cosine bar checks over the rows sampled; and a long thought whose whole-content call was refused holds its head window as its vector with nothing on the row to say so (034 records it on a re-embed claim row; none has run here) — 0 of 21 fell back in the sample, and the count over the corpus is unknowable from the schema.

**What it says for the ADR.** The premise inverts on this hardware at this corpus: the embedding is the CHEAP projection to rebuild and the best-keyed, the comprehension projections are the expensive ones and the worst-keyed — the graph half recorded, the capture-time metadata unkeyed. Snapshot discipline (a key on the row that a replay can derive from the payload and the configuration) matters most where it is weakest, and the ADR's snapshot strategy should be written for the graph and the metadata first, with the embedding as the worked example that already holds. And a "rebuild from the log" needs the capture event to carry the content before the log is the truth — 1.76 M characters of text against a 4.1 MB audit table (table and TOAST).

**Not taken.** A gate in the data-layer job on the live numbers (the run reads a live brain; the fixture check holds the SQL, and the counts on a throwaway would be its own). A `baselines.json` section (the numbers describe one brain on one day; the ADR consumes them once). A projector, a schema change, a recipe column, a fingerprint on the graph rows, content in the capture event — each an input to SMD-1997 and SMD-1999, not this spike's. A random sample (a fixed rank sample reproduces on an unchanged brain). A parallel embed (the per-row figure is what one worker sees; `reembed.ts --workers` divides it). A cosine bar tighter than 0.99 (the measured floor is 1 − 2.2e-16; the bar exists to fail a vector that is not a function of its key, not to measure drift). Modelling the recipe a change would move to (the rows a change replaces are every chunk row, whatever the target). `--sample 07` read as 7.
