## Changelog

The incremental-replay spike, measured on the dogfood brain: a projection rebuild reuses every stored vector by the `(content_fingerprint, embedding_model)` key the schema records, N edits recompute exactly N, a comprehension-only change recomputes none, a fresh vector equals its cached one to 1e-16, and a full re-embed costs eight minutes where a full re-extract costs four hours — the event-sourcing ADR's gate opens, with the graph named as the projection whose key and cost want the discipline first (SMD-1998).

## FORK

The incremental replay measured — every vector reused by the key the schema records, a full re-embed of the dogfood brain in eight minutes against a four-hour re-extract; the event-sourcing ADR's first gate opens and the graph is named the expensive projection (SMD-1998)

**What changed.** `evals/eval-projection-replay.ts` reads a live brain through `DATABASE_URL` (the session set read-only before its first statement; nothing is written) and answers the ADR's gating question with arithmetic over every live thought: for each, the key its payload derives — `content_fingerprint_of(content)` by the SQL rule's owner (016), the target model from `ob1_config` — against the key its row records (`content_fingerprint`, `embedding_model`, a vector present or not), reuse on a hit, recompute on a miss with the reason the key states (`content`, `no-vector`, `unlabelled`, `model`). Five scenarios, the ticket's: a no-op rebuild, N edits (in the arithmetic; the brain is untouched), a model bump, a comprehension-only change, a window-recipe change. Unless `--no-provider`, a stratified sample of rows (by length, deterministic, the shortest and the longest always in it) is embedded through the REAL `createEmbedder` — the same windows, template and fallback a capture takes, `rememberRefusal` off as `reembed.ts` runs it — against the configured provider, one row at a time, for the cost per row and per thousand characters and for the cosine of the fresh vector to the stored one. The graph's own cost is read from `thought_work_claims` (`finished_at − claimed_at` over succeeded rows) per pass key, not re-run. `evals/projection-replay.ts` holds every rule pure: the contract (`PROJECTIONS`, the snapshot key per projection and what the row records of it), the replay rule, the scenarios, the sample, the cost model, the verdict against the pre-registered bar, the report. `--self-check` (66 probes) runs in the portable-server job.

**Why.** SMD-1997 proposes CQRS-lite — `thought_audit` as the write-side truth, the thoughts row, the chunk rows, the entity graph, the proposals and the capture-time metadata as projections that can be thrown away and replayed — and rests on one assumption it named as its first risk: that a rebuild does not re-embed the world, because re-embedding is the single most expensive operation in the system (015/018/021/034 exist to make it survivable). Nothing had measured whether the key 021 records is sufficient, whether a cached vector is what a fresh one would be, or what a recompute costs on the hardware the brain runs on. The contract was pre-registered on the ticket before the first run (the key per projection, the replay rule, the five scenarios and their expectations, the go/no-go bar: A reuses ≥ 99% with every miss stated by the key, B recomputes exactly N, D recomputes 0, C priced in wall-clock); one bar was added between the comment and the first run and is said here — a reused row whose fresh vector sits under cosine 0.99 to its stored one is a vector that is not a function of its key, the one defect the key cannot express, and fails the verdict.

**Held.** `--self-check` (portable-server job; no database, no model): the contract names five projections with a verdict from the list; the replay rule branch by branch and in order (the fingerprint before the vector before the label; a NULL label is unknown, unknown is not the target — 021); the five scenarios on a hand-known corpus (a stale key is one recompute for content, named; two edits recompute exactly two by id; a model bump recomputes every row and reuses a row already at the new model, so a switch back re-embeds nothing twice; a comprehension-only change recomputes no vector and re-reads every thought; a recipe change replaces the chunked thought's rows and keeps every parent); the sample deterministic under any input order with the extremes always in it and equal lengths falling to the id; the cost model's arithmetic (median odd and even, the two extrapolations, the cosine over rows that had a vector, the fallbacks counted, no sample → no numbers); the verdict — GO on the ideal with a line per scenario, provisional with no provider, NO-GO when reuse falls under the bar (the misses named), when N edits do not recompute exactly N, when a model bump reuses a vector under another label, when a comprehension-only change touches a vector, when a cached vector does not reproduce, on an empty brain; one stale key in two hundred is over the bar and stated; the report carries the contract, the scenario rows, the log's completeness, the stale graph, the cost lines and the graph's own cost; the argument rules, every shape (a flag given twice is refused, not read once — SMD-1713's lesson).

**Measured after** (the dogfood brain, 2026-09-24 00:27 UTC, 419 thoughts, 1.75 M characters, every vector `qwen3-embedding:4b@1024`, host Ollama warm; the full report is in `evals/README.md`, verbatim). A: the no-op rebuild reuses 419/419 — `content_fingerprint = content_fingerprint_of(content)` on every row, every label the target. B: 10 edits recompute exactly 10, 409 reused. C: a model bump recomputes 419/419 at 7.7 min by rows (mean 1.10 s a row over 21 sampled) or 6.9 min by characters (0.238 s per 1k) — the worst case is minutes. D: a comprehension-only change recomputes 0 vectors; the graph re-reads 419 thoughts and replaces 5,922 mention rows, and the claim log prices that at a median 37.0 s a row under `extract:qwen2.5:7b@p2` (41.9 s under the 27B; 17.6 s a pair for the judge) — 4.3 h sequential for the corpus, thirty-three times the re-embed. E: a window-recipe change replaces 20 chunk rows under 3 thoughts (the same 3 the current recipe would window) and reuses every parent. The cached value reproduces: the fresh vector against the stored one, cosine within 1e-16 of 1 on all 21 rows, the 21,345-character thought included (7 windows, 8 calls, 10.9 s). Verdict GO, every pre-registered line met. Beside it, three facts the schema states and one it cannot: the capture row in `thought_audit` carries `metadata` and not `content` (008), so the log alone holds the text of 209/419 live thoughts (those with a content-moving update) and today the `thoughts` row is the payload store; the graph rows carry no fingerprint (016 checks one at write time and keeps none), and 8 thoughts hold 151 mention rows extracted before their fingerprint last moved, every one queued for a re-read (five after a first retry failed under the recorded key, pending under another); the chunk rows carry no recipe (022 has the parent's label vouch for them); and a long thought whose whole-content call was refused holds its head window as its vector with nothing on the row to say so (034 records it on a re-embed claim row; none has run here) — 0 of 21 fell back in the sample, and the count over the corpus is unknowable from the schema.

**What it says for the ADR.** The premise inverts on this hardware at this corpus: the embedding is the CHEAP projection to rebuild and the best-keyed, the comprehension projections are the expensive ones and the worst-keyed — the graph half recorded, the capture-time metadata unkeyed. Snapshot discipline (a key on the row that a replay can derive from the payload and the configuration) matters most where it is weakest, and the ADR's snapshot strategy should be written for the graph and the metadata first, with the embedding as the worked example that already holds. And a "rebuild from the log" needs the capture event to carry the content before the log is the truth — 1.75 MB of text against a 4.4 MB audit table.

**Not taken.** A gate in the data-layer job (the run reads a live brain; a throwaway Postgres has no corpus to replay, and the counts on one would be its own). A `baselines.json` section (the numbers describe one brain on one day; the ADR consumes them once). A projector, a schema change, a recipe column, a fingerprint on the graph rows, content in the capture event — each an input to SMD-1997 and SMD-1999, not this spike's. A random sample (a fixed rank sample reproduces on an unchanged brain). A parallel embed (the per-row figure is what one worker sees; `reembed.ts --workers` divides it). A cosine bar tighter than 0.99 (the measured floor is 1 − 1e-16; the bar exists to fail a vector that is not a function of its key, not to measure drift).

**Follow-ups.** None filed here: the four gaps above are recorded on SMD-1997 as design inputs and SMD-1999 is unblocked by this verdict. SMD-1868 and SMD-1873 (the judge's candidate rule and its verdicts) are unchanged by this.

**Upstream status.** Not upstream: upstream has no audit log of record, no per-row embedding model, no claim table and no eval directory of this shape.
