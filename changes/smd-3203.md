## Changelog

`evals/eval-mediated-pairs.ts` measures whether the consolidation pass can ask the subject-anchored ADR's mediated-pair test: the typed-decision tier and the pass's chat model, against two blind raters' labels of 100 canary pairs. Neither meets its bar. The chat model's first step finds mediated pairs (κ 0.64), but no model's state step meets its bar, and the ADR's test is now version 2. So decide mode caps a mediated pair (decide version 4). The judge's model is asked the test's link step about a pair the decider calls outdates at the pass's floor, or evolves the pass would store. A pair linked only through a third subject, or by one thought naming the other's subject in passing, is stored as related at most, with no supersession proposed. The pass key moves to `@p4d4`, so a brain running decide mode judges its pool again (SMD-3203). The sleep scheduler can write each consolidation pass's verdicts, pair by pair: `OB1_SLEEP_DUMP_DIR` (or `db/sleep.ts --dump-dir`) names a directory (in compose, where the `sleep` service mounts its `sleep-dumps` volume), and each pass appends every verdict there, decide mode's link step included, to one file per pass key.

## FORK

Can the pass ask the mediated-pair test? Measured in two rounds, then decide version 4 caps a mediated pair, and sleep can write a pass's verdicts (SMD-3203)

**What changed: sleep's dump (PR 3a).** PR 3, the canary's re-judge under `@p4d4`, samples capped pairs for two blind raters. A capped outdates rarely stores a relation (its relation words' mass is under the floor), and closing an edge writes no lineage, so such a pair left no record to draw from. The maintainer chose to let sleep write a pass's verdicts (2026-10-23).
- **`db/sleep.ts --dump-dir DIR`:** each consolidation pass appends every verdict to `DIR/<its pass key>.jsonl`, as `db/consolidate.ts --dump` writes them, the link step's `link`, `asked` (first or after, new here) and `capped` included. One file per pass key (`passDumpFile`), so a new judge or decide version starts a file of its own. The directory must be one the scheduler can write, checked before anything runs (`dumpDirProblem`, exit 2); the start says where the verdicts go. `db/consolidate.ts` creates a dump file 0600, since a verdict's reason and quotes are the brain's text.
- **`deploy/compose.yaml`:** the `sleep` service mounts a named volume, `sleep-dumps`, at `/var/lib/ob1/sleep-dumps`. `OB1_SLEEP_DUMP_DIR`, empty by default, hands the scheduler `--dump-dir` only when set. Check 28's tables hold the volume and the mount; the fork-checks step that holds the service's shape holds the setting, the three mounts and the `--run` string.
- **`deploy/canary.sh down`** names the sleep profile. `canary_compose` clears `COMPOSE_PROFILES`, and a down without the profile counted neither the sleep worker nor its volume. So `down --volumes` deleted the database and left the dumps, and the canary's CI step found a volume of the canary's after its last down. `--volumes` now deletes the canary's sleep dumps with its database.
- **Docs:** `deploy/.env.example`, `deploy/README.md` (reading a pass pair by pair) and `db/README.md`.
- **Tests:** `db/test-live.ts` [38]: a `--dump-dir` naming no directory is refused with 2 before the brain is read; the one-sleep run writes its three verdicts to the consolidation key's file, created 0600, which its start names. The live suite counts 1,514 assertions, the sleep shard 71. Fork Checks' sleep step runs the service with an unwritable `OB1_SLEEP_DUMP_DIR` that holds a space, and reads the scheduler's own refusal.

**What changed: decide version 4 (PR 2b).**

Its `--self-check` holds the pass's embedded text equal to the ADR's, step A to the pass's question byte for byte, and the eval's link reading to the pass's.

**What changed: the measurement (PR 2a).**
- **`evals/eval-mediated-pairs.ts`** (new). It asks the pass's own models the test's question about the same 100 pairs, rendered as prompt 4 renders them (`pairContext`).
  - **Version 1, one question:**
    - the tier (H2O-Lightning-4B, ob1-jev/1) gets one choice of the six labels, worded from the test;
    - the writer (the pass's chat model) gets the test's text, read word for word from the ADR, and answers in strict JSON.
  - **Version 2, two steps:** the link (D, H, F or N), then, for an H or F link, whether the subject's state is given. It runs three ways:
    - the tier for both steps;
    - the writer's link with the tier's yes or no on the subject the writer names;
    - the writer for both steps.
  - **Scoring:** against the pairs both raters agree on, with the bar fixed on SMD-3203 before each round.
  - **Its `--self-check`** holds the mapping of labels to rows 2 and 3, both versions' questions (the writer's read from the ADR), the gold, κ, the scoring and the bar.
- **`.github/workflows/fork-checks.yml` and `db/ci-parity.sh`** run that self-check, next to the other decide-mode evals.
- **`docs/subject-anchored-claims.md`:** the test is now version 2. That is version 1, word for word, plus five rules for the points its raters flagged. Rule 1 defines a state as an explicit status or a current or measured value. The ADR records both rounds and the maintainer's choice.
- **`evals/README.md`:** the eval's section, with both rounds' results.

**Why.** Decide version 4 applies the ADR's rows 2 and 3, which act on the test's labels. A pass that labels pairs unlike the raters would cap, or make distinct, the wrong relations, so the measurement came first, as the ADR's path asks. On the canary's sample, the two raters found 43 and 44 of the 80 stored evolves and related pairs mediated: 34 and 33 through a third subject (F), and 9 and 11 half pairs (H). By their labels, 18 and 19 of the F-name and half pairs were stored as evolves, a continuation, which row 2 says they are not.

**What it found.** No candidate meets all three bars:
- κ ≥ 0.6 on mediated or not;
- F-state precision ≥ 0.8, with at least 5 predicted;
- F-state recall ≥ 0.5.

| Round | Candidate | Mediated κ | F-state predicted / right (of 11) |
|---|---|---|---|
| version 1 | tier, one choice of six | 0.04 | 1 / 0 |
| version 1 | writer, the test's text | 0.51 | 0 / 0 |
| version 2 | tier, both steps | 0.21 | 1 / 0 |
| version 2 | writer's link, tier's state | **0.64** | 10 / 4 |
| version 2 | writer, both steps | **0.64** | 5 / 0 |

The writer's link step finds mediated pairs. No state step meets the bars for F-state:
- the writer's own state step called 10 of the 11 gold F-state pairs F-name, as round 1's writer had;
- the tier, asked about the subject the writer named, got 4 of its 10 F-state calls right.

The writer's link step alone takes a median 14.3 s a pair.

So the maintainer chose to build the cap (2026-10-22). Decide version 4 asks the writer's link step of a pair it would store as evolves or propose as outdates. An H or F link is then related at most, with no supersession. Row 2's distinct waits for a state step that meets its bar.

**Not taken.**
- **Building the pass on version 1's numbers.** Its best mediated κ was 0.51, under the bar.
- **Re-rating the gold under version 2.** Only rule 1 is known to match both raters' working definitions. Rules 4 and 5 come from one rater each, and one gold F-state pair rests on a fixed spec counted as a value, which rule 1 calls a name. With every bar missed widely, no conclusion turns on it.
- **Reading the test from the ADR inside the pass.** The pass runs in a container without `docs/`, so decide version 4 embeds the text and holds it equal to the ADR's.
- **Moving the rest of H2O's mass onto a capped relation,** so that it would be stored at 1 − P(unrelated). On a mediated pair, much of that mass is about the subject that mediates it: an F-state pair's outdates is the third subject's later status. So a capped outdates stores a relation only where H2O's relation words clear the floor, as a declined proposal does.
- **Asking the link step of a duplicate, related or distinct verdict.** A duplicate states the same claims, so its two thoughts share their subject, and related and distinct are already within the cap. The maintainer's choice names evolves and outdates.
- **Leaving an unread link answer uncapped.** The guard's own answer is F, the safe side, and an answer naming no link gets the same.

**Operators.**
- **A brain in decide mode starts a fresh pass under `@p4d4`** once it runs this tree, as `@p4d3` did after SMD-3151 (its Operators note on the old key's claims holds here too). The sleep scheduler starts it at its first sleep.
- **The cost:** the link step adds a call to the judge's model on each pair H2O calls outdates at the floor, and on each evolves the pass would store. That is a median 14.3 s of the 27B a pair on SMD-3203's 100. The canary held 1,050 standing `@p4d3` evolves when its sample was drawn, so expect hours more of the 27B beside the tier's pass.
- **The deadline:** one `--timeout` (120 s by default) bounds a pair's three calls: the tier, the link step and the writer.
- **Proposals made before stand.** A proposed pair is not judged again unless a text moves (the candidate rule, 029 and 063). So a `@p4d3` proposal on a mediated pair waits for review, as before. One whose text moves is judged again, capped, and settled with a note naming the link step.
- **What a capped pair records:** never a proposal. Its evolves or duplicate is stored as related at H2O's P(related) when its relation words clear the floor; a related or unrelated verdict stands. The recipe's `judged.decide.link` says why.
- **A brain in judge mode keeps its key, its proposals and its relations.**
- **The sleep dump (PR 3a)** is off unless `OB1_SLEEP_DUMP_DIR` is set. A stack that runs `sleep` gains the empty `sleep-dumps` volume when the service is next created, set or not.
  - Set, every pass appends without end: unset it and recreate `sleep` once the read is done. The files stay in the volume until removed by name, each created 0600 (the brain's text); copy them out while `sleep` runs, to a directory outside any checkout.
  - A directory the scheduler cannot write is refused at its start (exit 2), and its supervisor tries again on its clock.
