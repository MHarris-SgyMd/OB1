## Changelog

Under the sleep scheduler, a thought whose embedding crashes the provider is now recorded failed at its third outage alone, and the backlog behind it is re-embedded in the same period: re-embed waits out an outage that made the thought in hand a suspect, probing every 5 s, up to 2 minutes a wait and 4 minutes of such waits a period, rather than being set down for the period with its suspects forgotten (SMD-4254). A crashing thought is failed in one period if the provider is back within about 80 s of each crash, and its backlog re-embedded too within about 60 s; the claim passes are held 4 minutes at most a period. No migration.

## Record

Re-embed under the sleep scheduler waits out an outage a thought brought down — the thought crashing the provider is failed and the backlog behind it re-embedded, not stalled every period (SMD-4254)

**Why.** SMD-2641 PR 2 has re-embed's `--follow` follower retry an outage's suspects one at a time and fail a thought that crashes the provider at its third outage alone. Under `db/sleep.ts`, the deployed path, the poll that saw re-embed waiting mid-pass ended it and set it down for the period (`setReembedDown("outage")`), and the run's `ProviderOutage` went with it, suspects and counts. The suspect was handed back at the pool's front, so each period's first claim crashed the provider again and the period re-embedded nothing. SMD-2641 PR 2's review pass 1 measured it under the real scheduler: no isolated drain ever started, the thought stayed pending, and from the second or third period each re-embedded 0 of its backlog, on main as on that branch.

**Decided.** The maintainer's choice (2026-11-18): don't set re-embed down on an outage that made suspects; let the run wait it out and isolate, as extract and consolidate already do under the scheduler. An outage with no suspect (a 429, a 503, the model loading) and backpressure keep the set-down. The bound is the build's: re-embed runs alone at a period's start, so an unbounded wait would hold the claim passes for the period whenever the embedding endpoint is really down with a thought in hand. Review pass 1 found the bound a wait's while the probe schedule carried across an isolation's waits, and no limit on a period's waits; the maintainer's decision (2026-11-18): under the scheduler, probe every 5 s from each wait's start, no doubling and no carry; keep the 2-minute bound a wait and cap a period's such waits at 4 minutes, begun again each period.

**What changed.**

**Not taken.** Keeping one `ProviderOutage` for the scheduler's life (the ticket's first option): the maintainer chose the wait.

**Limits.** A crashing thought takes four waits under the scheduler — its batch's outage, two alone, then the outage it leaves once recorded failed — so with the 4-minute cap it is failed in one period only if the provider is back within about 80 s of each crash, and its backlog re-embedded in that period too only within about 60 s. Longer crashes are set down at the cap (or the 2-minute bound) and the thought is retried at the next period's first claim, forgotten as before; preflight's remedy names that case. While re-embed waits on such an outage the claim passes wait too, 4 minutes at most a period; a crash of a few seconds costs about 20 s once. An endpoint simply down, first met mid-pass with a 5xx, a refused or reset connection or a timeout, makes a suspect and so costs up to the 2-minute bound once (a 300-thought backlog's extraction took a median 132-137 s against 12 s on main in the review's run), before the next period's start finds it down and sets re-embed down at once.
