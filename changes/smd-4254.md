## Changelog

Under the sleep scheduler, a thought whose embedding crashes the provider is now recorded failed at its third outage alone, and the backlog behind it is re-embedded in the same period: re-embed waits out an outage that made the thought in hand a suspect, probing every 5 s, up to 2 minutes a wait and 4 minutes of isolation a period — its waits and the retries between them — rather than being set down for the period with its suspects forgotten (SMD-4254). A crashing thought is failed in one period if the provider is back under about 80 s after each crash, and its backlog re-embedded too under about 60 s; the claim passes are held 4 minutes and a poll at most a period from the first wait's start. One that outlasts the limits backs re-embed off: the next period sets it down at once at such an outage, as before, then 2 periods, 4, … between tries, until re-embed drains or a thought is recorded failed alone. No migration.

## Record

Re-embed under the sleep scheduler waits out an outage a thought brought down — the thought crashing the provider is failed and the backlog behind it re-embedded, not stalled every period (SMD-4254)

**Why.** SMD-2641 PR 2 has re-embed's `--follow` follower retry an outage's suspects one at a time and fail a thought that crashes the provider at its third outage alone. Under `db/sleep.ts`, the deployed path, the poll that saw re-embed waiting mid-pass ended it and set it down for the period (`setReembedDown("outage")`), and the run's `ProviderOutage` went with it, suspects and counts. The suspect was handed back at the pool's front, so each period's first claim crashed the provider again and the period re-embedded nothing. SMD-2641 PR 2's review pass 1 measured it under the real scheduler: no isolated drain ever started, the thought stayed pending, and from the second or third period each re-embedded 0 of its backlog, on main as on that branch.

**Decided.** The maintainer's choice (2026-11-18): don't set re-embed down on an outage that made suspects; let the run wait it out and isolate, as extract and consolidate already do under the scheduler. An outage with no suspect (a 429, a 503, the model loading) and backpressure keep the set-down. The bound is the build's: re-embed runs alone at a period's start, so an unbounded wait would hold the claim passes for the period whenever the embedding endpoint is really down with a thought in hand. Review pass 1 found the bound a wait's while the probe schedule carried across an isolation's waits, and no limit on a period's waits; the maintainer's decision (2026-11-18): under the scheduler, probe every 5 s from each wait's start, no doubling and no carry; keep the 2-minute bound a wait and cap a period's such waits at 4 minutes, begun again each period. Review pass 2 found the cap counting the waits alone — re-embed runs alone between them too, the retry's call and its blame probe, two OB1_LLM_TIMEOUTs a round with a provider that hangs (about 16 minutes a period at the defaults) — and a crasher whose crash outlasts the limits costing the cap every period, where main set it down in a poll. The maintainer's decision (2026-11-18): the cap counts the whole isolation from the first wait's start, the waits and the retries and probes between them, and sets re-embed down between waits too; and a period set down at a limit without the thought failed backs off later periods — set down at once at such an outage, as main does, skipping 1 period, then 2, 4, …, a try between, reset when re-embed drains or a thought is recorded failed alone, for the scheduler's life.

**Not taken.** Keeping one `ProviderOutage` for the scheduler's life (the ticket's first option): the maintainer chose the wait.

**Limits.** A crashing thought takes four waits under the scheduler — its batch's outage, two alone, then the outage it leaves once recorded failed — so with the 4-minute cap on the isolation it is failed in one period only if the provider is back under about 80 s after each crash, and its backlog re-embedded in that period too only under about 60 s; the retries between the waits count, so a provider slow to fail them lowers those edges. Longer crashes are set down at the cap (or the 2-minute bound), the thought retried at a later period, forgotten as before, and re-embed backs off — set down at once at such an outage the next period, then 2, 4, … between tries — so a crash that never ends costs the cap once, then a poll a period with a try at the 2nd, 4th, 8th, … period after; preflight's remedy names that case, and its fix the back-off. The back-off is the scheduler's: a restart tries at once. While re-embed isolates such an outage the claim passes wait too, 4 minutes and a poll at most a period from the first wait's start; what comes before that wait — the call that met the outage, and its probe, each up to OB1_LLM_TIMEOUT with a provider that hangs — is as before SMD-4254. A set-down between waits aborts the retry's call or probe in hand and sets its thought back in the queue, behind the backlog. A crash of a few seconds costs about 20 s once. An endpoint simply down, first met mid-pass with a 5xx, a refused or reset connection or a timeout, makes a suspect and so costs up to the 2-minute bound once (a 300-thought backlog's extraction took a median 132-137 s against 12 s on main in the review's run), before the next period's start finds it down and sets re-embed down at once.
