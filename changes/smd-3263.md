## Changelog

Fork Checks' "The REST core's routes and authorization" step is about 29 s faster: test-rest's nine telemetry stop cases run side by side, each with its own collector and `bun api.ts`, where they ran one after another (SMD-3263).

## FORK

test-rest [12]'s stop cases run side by side (SMD-3263)

**What changed.**
- **The nine `stopWith` cases start at once** and are awaited together: slow, hung, crowded, trickled, rejected, perSignal, noSpans, hungFirst and pushedBack. Their rows follow in the order they had, each reading its case's result. The three collectors written for them (trickle, partial, pushback) are defined above the starts. Each case's comment stays with its rows.
- **A case that throws is said, not thrown.** Its error goes into the case's `missed` and a line of its own. Its rows then fail on its empty output. Thrown inside `Promise.all`, it would have left the other eight children running.
- **noSpans' row names every "not sent" line, its stop line and what was missed.** It named only the lines it matched, so a failure read "()".

**Why.** The step took 29–30 s on main until OB1 PR 473 (SMD-2902 PR 2) added four cases to [12], then 57–58 s. SMD-3201 overlapped [16]'s idle probes (58 → 43 s), which left [12] as nearly all of the suite: 39 of its 44 s locally. Nothing is shared between the cases but the suite's tally.

**Measured locally**, Bun 1.4.3:

| | main (31a74e8c) | This change |
|---|---|---|
| [12] | 38.3 s | 9.2 s |
| test-rest | 480 assertions, 43.7 s | 480, 14.3 s |

test-rest's floor is now [16]'s 14 s idle probes, which start at the top.

**Under load.** Runs used macOS `yes` burners on an 18-core machine, at the suite's own priority unless marked starved.

| Condition | Runs | Runs with a failure | main |
|---|---|---|---|
| 14 burners | 10 | 0 | — |
| 30 burners | 28 | 1 | 0 of 5 |
| 60 burners | 10 | 0 | 0 of 10 |
| 16 burners, starved (`taskpolicy -b`) | 17 | 4 | 6 of 13 |

- **Equal priority:** no timing bound moved. hung's flush was cut at 2999–3000 ms and its stop said 3 s in every run. The one failure was noSpans, before its message named what came instead. Its filter shows only lines that say "rejected by the collector". A collector answered by a foreign listener would read exactly that way; see the 404 below.
- **Starved:** the starved suite runs below the burners. That fails main's serial cases as often, in the same rows: pushedBack's first answer late for its Retry-After, trickled and perSignal timed out, crowded's tally, and [16]'s probe. No margin was widened. Nine children starting at once can also run out `serveChild`'s 10 s SDK wait there, which fails every case at once (seen once).
- **A barrier was tried and dropped.** Every child started before any case hit its own. Starved, 6 of its 10 runs had a failure, against 4 of 17 without it, so the cases run without one.

**Mutants**, each run against the concurrent cases:
- the flush's bound the pool's second: slow's and hung's rows;
- no cut said (`unsent` dropped from api.ts's drain): hung's, crowded's and perSignal's rows;
- every signal given the spans' deadline: perSignal's row;
- a partial success not counted: rejected's and noSpans' rows;
- the deadline at the timeout plus 2 s: pushedBack's row;
- no deadline: trickled's and perSignal's rows;
- the log batch's cut counting nothing held: crowded's row.

**Seen, not this change's:**
- The span batch's cut can count nothing held (`watched.cut(kind)` for spans in otel-setup.ts) with every suite passing, main's serial cases included. Crowded's span batch holds nothing at the stop, so only the log batch's held count is checked (caught: mutant).
- Twice in about 100 runs, a server on port 0 in the suite was answered by something else: the lone collector's exports got HTTP 404, where it only answers 500, and [16]'s probe got 404 under one mutant. Both listen dual-stack on `*:port`. A foreign IPv4 listener on the same loopback port, such as another session's suite on this Mac, would answer that way (caught: run-it).
